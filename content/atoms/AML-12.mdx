“Perpetual” or continuous KYC takes ongoing diligence further: banks strive to have real-time or event-driven updates of customer data. Instead of waiting a year for the next review, the bank’s systems continuously screen for changes.  For example, automated processes may re-run name screening daily or alert staff if a customer appears in news or sanctions feeds. The goal is to “maintain a living view” of each client. ACAMS describes this as “perpetual KYC,” where client data is kept current through near-real-time monitoring of risk indicators.

This trend away from calendar-based KYC means banks invest in technology: integrated data sources (legal registries, watchlists, media monitors) and trigger-based workflows. For instance, India’s CKYCR (central KYC registry) facilitates sharing updates across banks, reducing redundant KYC. Video KYC or e-KYC (Aadhaar-based) allow faster identity checks. The result is that if a customer’s situation changes (new BO, sanction hit, etc.), compliance is updated immediately, not at the annual review.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Implement systems that alert compliance staff to material changes (sanctions, adverse news, ownership changes) so KYC can be updated immediately. Use centralized KYC registries (like India’s CKYCR) and re-screen customers periodically (or continuously for high risk).
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Relying purely on periodic checks and treating KYC reviews as routine paperwork. In today’s dynamic world, waiting six months for the next review can miss emerging risks.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Periodic Review" b="Perpetual KYC">
**Periodic Review** — scheduled KYC refresh (e.g. annual).
---
**Perpetual KYC** — continuous, event-triggered updates. The confusion arises as both aim to keep KYC current; the difference is periodic is fixed schedule while perpetual is on change-driven alerts.
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="IN">
— RBI’s CKYCR allows banks to update a central record; it mandates updates on PAN/Aadhaar info changes. E-KYC (Aadhaar) and Video KYC are accepted for quick onboarding and re-verification.
</Delta>

<Delta j="UK">
— The FCA expects firms to have “systems to identify PEP status changes throughout the relationship”. The trend is to more frequent reviews beyond the regulatory minimum.
</Delta>

<Delta j="EU">
— Anti-Money Laundering Package (2024) pushes for digitisation of KYC data and envisages an EU KYC “data house” for continuous updates.
</Delta>

<Delta j="US">
— No centralized KYC, but banks re-run watchlist screening at least daily/weekly. Regulatory focus is on whether changes (like home ownership or employment) should prompt updates under the RBA principle.
</Delta>
