In a plain fixed-for-floating interest-rate swap, one party pays a fixed rate and receives a floating rate; the other does the opposite. Both legs are calculated on a common notional amount under defined day counts, payment frequencies, business-day rules and reference-rate conventions.

The notional is normally a calculation base, not principal that changes hands. If a company has floating-rate debt and pays fixed in a swap while receiving a matching floating reference rate, the combination can approximate fixed-rate funding, subject to basis, credit, timing and documentation differences.

At inception, the fixed rate on an at-market swap is chosen so the present values of the two legs are approximately equal, making initial fair value near zero before transaction costs and credit/funding adjustments. Afterwards, market rates move and the swap acquires positive value to one party and negative value to the other.

A swap is not one “interest rate”. The fixed leg has a coupon schedule; the floating leg references a rate definition and reset method; discounting uses an appropriate collateral/funding framework; separate curves may be used for projecting floating coupons and discounting cash flows. This multi-curve setup became standard after the global financial crisis and benchmark reform.

Risk can be expressed through DV01/PV01 by tenor bucket. A pay-fixed swap generally loses value when the relevant swap rates fall and gains when they rise, from the pay-fixed party’s perspective, all else equal. However, exact exposure depends on curve shape, reset dates and collateral terms.

Operationally, the trade must preserve notional, currency, effective date, maturity, fixed rate, floating-rate option, reset frequency, payment frequency, day count, calendars, compounding method and clearing status. “Five-year swap” is not sufficient to reconstruct economics.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
An IRS exchanges interest cash flows; do not assume the notional principal itself is funded or transferred.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Treating the floating leg, projection curve and discount curve as the same concept.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Fixed leg" b="Floating leg">
**Fixed leg** — contractual interest payments based on a predetermined fixed rate.

---

**Floating leg** — payments determined from a specified floating-rate option and calculation method.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.**
</Rule>

<Recall
  q="Why can a borrower use a pay-fixed/receive-floating swap?"
  a="To offset floating-rate exposure and approximate fixed-rate funding."
/>

<Recall
  q="Does a vanilla single-currency IRS normally exchange the notional principal?"
