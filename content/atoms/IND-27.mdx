### Core mechanism

| Tool | What changes | What can remain |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Compromise settlement | Contractual recovery outcome is agreed for less than or differently from the original dues | Governance, sacrifice recognition and any cooling-off conditions |
| Technical write-off | Accounting carrying amount is removed after applicable provisioning/write-off process | Legal claim and recovery action may continue |
| Waiver/forgiveness | Creditor intentionally gives up part of the legal claim | Only the residual claim, if any |
| Recovery after write-off | Cash is collected after accounting removal | Legal enforcement remains possible if claim survives |

RBI's 2023 framework formalised governance for compromise settlements and technical write-offs, including board-approved policies, delegation and treatment of fraud or wilful-default cases under the applicable conditions.

A write-off is therefore not evidence that the borrower was "forgiven". Nor does a compromise erase the need to record the economic sacrifice and follow applicable credit-information and re-lending rules.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Failed or negotiated resolution
This:       Compromise / technical write-off
Downstream: Recovery · CIC update · Legal enforcement
```

### Permanent rule

Technical write-off changes accounting presentation, not automatically the borrower's legal liability.

### Never confuse [Technical write-off] and [Debt waiver]

**Technical write-off** — accounting removal while recovery can continue.

---

**Debt waiver** — creditor relinquishment of the legal claim to the waived amount.

### Permanent trap

Telling a borrower or stakeholder that "written off" means "no longer owed". That conclusion depends on the legal settlement, not the accounting entry.

### Jurisdiction variants

#### Banks and NBFCs
Both apply the RBI governance framework subject to their entity-specific policy and prudential treatment.
