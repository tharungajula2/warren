ASC Topic 326’s **current expected credit losses (CECL)** methodology measures expected credit losses on financial assets carried at amortised cost using information about historical experience, current conditions and reasonable and supportable forecasts. An allowance is established when an asset is originated or acquired; for assets in CECL scope, the objective is the net amount expected to be collected over the relevant contractual term.

The structural contrast with IFRS 9 is the allowance horizon. IFRS 9 starts a non-credit-impaired asset in Stage 1 with 12-month ECL and moves to lifetime ECL after SICR. CECL generally starts at **lifetime expected loss**. Consequently, CECL has no SICR test, no Stage 1-to-Stage 2 allowance cliff and no 30-DPD staging presumption. Deterioration still changes the allowance, but it changes a lifetime estimate that already exists.

CECL is also method-neutral. Topic 326 does not require one prescribed technique; acceptable approaches can include loss-rate, roll-rate, vintage, discounted-cash-flow and PD/LGD methods when they reasonably estimate expected collectability and are consistently supported.

The lifetime concept is not identical to IFRS 9’s revolving-facility rule. CECL generally measures expected losses over the contractual term, considering expected prepayments but not expected extensions, renewals or modifications unless specified by the guidance. For off-balance-sheet commitments, expected losses are measured over the contractual period of exposure, but no allowance is recorded for commitments the institution can unconditionally cancel.

Purchased credit-deteriorated assets use PCD gross-up accounting: the initial allowance is added to purchase price to establish amortised cost rather than charged as an ordinary acquisition-date provision expense.

```text
IFRS 9
+---------+   SICR   +---------+
| Stage 1 | -------&gt; | Stage 2 |
| 12m ECL |          | Life ECL|
+---------+          +---------+

CECL
+------------------------------+
| Initial recognition          |
| Lifetime expected credit loss|
+------------------------------+
               |
               v
+------------------------------+
| Re-estimate lifetime loss    |
| every reporting date         |
+------------------------------+
```

### Permanent rule

The difference between CECL and IFRS 9 is structural measurement architecture, not merely terminology.

### Permanent trap

Recreating IFRS 9 stages inside CECL and using them to decide whether lifetime ECL applies imports a threshold ASC 326 does not contain.

### Never confuse

**CECL lifetime allowance** — lifetime expected credit loss recognised from initial recognition for in-scope amortised-cost assets.

---

**IFRS 9 lifetime ECL** — lifetime expected loss that ordinarily applies after SICR, to credit-impaired assets, and in specified other cases.

### Jurisdiction variants

**IN** — CECL does not govern Indian statutory bank provisioning. Indian scheduled commercial banks remain under RBI IRACP through March 31, 2027 and transition to RBI’s own three-stage ECL framework from April 1, 2027; relevant NBFCs may apply Ind AS 109, not ASC 326. [RBI 2025 IRACP; RBI 2026 ECL Directions; RBI 2020 NBFC guidance]

**UK** — UK-adopted IFRS 9 uses staging and therefore differs structurally from CECL. [UK-adopted IFRS 9]

**EU** — EU-adopted IFRS 9 likewise retains 12-month versus lifetime staging. [Regulation (EU) 2023/1803]

**US** — ASC 326 CECL is the applicable US GAAP expected-loss model for in-scope amortised-cost financial assets; it recognises lifetime expected losses from initial recognition without IFRS 9 staging. [ASU 2016-13; Interagency CECL FAQ]
