Organizations undertake major change programs (new core banking system, mergers, or regulatory projects). Each such change carries its own risk of introducing errors or disruption. Change management risk includes schedule overruns, technical glitches, data errors, or poor training leading to failures once the change is live.  For example, a system upgrade might inadvertently disable a payment batch. Banks thus need rigorous change-control processes: testing in a risk-free environment, phased rollout, fallback plans, and impact assessment.

Change risk is operational, not strategic: it arises during the execution of projects. It is often managed under project management and IT risk frameworks, but should be integrated with ops risk (e.g. scenario thinking about “failed change” events).

### Permanent rule

Apply strict change controls: use version control, testing, sign-off at each stage, and rollback procedures. Involve the risk function in major changes and log any near-misses or incidents during implementation.

### Permanent trap

Don’t treat project issues as only project risk.  Ignoring a change failure can lead to large operational loss later.  Also, avoid “big bang” rollouts without pilot testing – history shows those often go wrong.

### Never confuse

**Change management risk** — risk of problems due to the change process itself.
---
**Project Risk** — risks around delivering the project objectives (time/cost). (There’s overlap, but change risk focuses on aftermath after deployment.)

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Good change management is a universal best practice (mentioned in Basel principles, IT governance guidelines, etc.). Some regulators (e.g. RBI on IT governance) specifically require robust change controls.
