### Core mechanism

| Layer | Typical contents | Governance purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Business glossary | Term, definition, scope, exclusions, owner, effective date | One governed business meaning |
| Business metadata | CDE status, sensitivity, quality requirement, source authority, consumer | Control and accountability context |
| Technical metadata | Field name, datatype, length, table, interface, code set, refresh | Precise implementation context |
| Operational metadata | Load time, record count, control result, version | Evidence that the process ran as expected |

```text
Governed business term
    ↓
Business metadata and owner
    ↓
Technical fields and allowed values
    ↓
Lineage and mappings
    ↓
Reports, controls and consumers
```

Definitions need version control. If "defaulted exposure" changes meaning because policy or regulation changes, the bank needs the old and new definitions, effective dates, impacted reports and corresponding technical changes rather than silently overwriting the glossary entry.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Business definitions · CDEs · Lineage
This:       Glossary and metadata governance
Downstream: Reference data · DQ rules · Requirements · Mapping · Impact analysis
```

### Never confuse [Business glossary] and [Data dictionary]

**Business glossary** - governed business language: what a term means and where its scope begins and ends.

---

**Data dictionary** - field-level description of how data is represented technically. In this pack, dictionary detail is carried through metadata and mapping rather than a separate atom.

### Permanent rule

A field name is not a definition. Meaning must be explicit enough that two independent teams would classify the same case the same way.

### Permanent trap

Publishing a glossary without ownership or effective dates. A dictionary of prose that nobody is accountable for does not control semantic change.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Common definitions and metadata are enterprise controls; jurisdiction-specific definitions enter through the governed terms, not through different metadata mechanics.

<Recall
  q="Why must metadata include ownership and effective dates?"
  a="Those attributes show who can decide the meaning and which version applies to a given reporting period, preventing silent semantic drift."
