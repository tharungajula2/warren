Banks must monitor customers and transactions throughout the relationship. FATF Recommendation 10 requires “ongoing due diligence” – monitoring transactions against the customer’s known profile and keeping CDD information up-to-date.  In practice this means reviewing accounts at regular intervals (e.g. annually for PEPs, every 2–5 years for normal accounts, with shorter intervals for higher risk). Periodic KYC refresh ensures information (such as employment, address or BO structure) hasn’t changed. For example, if a private company customer suddenly has a new director who is a PEP, the bank should detect this in a review.

Ongoing checks also include watching for unusual activity via transaction monitoring (Atom 13). If alerts trigger, analysts re-assess the customer. Regulatory guidance (UK MLR, EU AMLD) explicitly mandates systems to flag when customer risk increases (winning elections, media scandals, sanctions hits).  Essentially, one-time KYC is insufficient; institutions should treat compliance like continuous management of customer risk.  ACAMS even coins “perpetual KYC” to describe updating client data in near-real time as events occur.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Establish a schedule to review each customer’s risk and KYC at least annually for higher-risk and every few years for others. Immediately update any material change (address, BO, adverse media, sanction status). Document reviews.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
“Set and forget” KYC. Believing that a customer’s risk profile never changes. Failing to catch that an ordinary account became a PEP or involved a sanctioned country months after onboarding leads to regulatory failure.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Ongoing due diligence" b="Transaction monitoring">
**Ongoing due diligence** — the continuous program of monitoring and updating customer information and risk rating.
---
**Transaction monitoring** — the automated system scanning transactions for anomalies. Ongoing due diligence includes transaction monitoring but also periodic KYC refresh and watchlist rescreening, which some mistakenly equate only to transaction monitoring.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Global AML standards (FATF Rec.10) demand ongoing monitoring. Local rules differ only in specifics: UK MLR 2017 requires periodic reviews (Reg 35) and immediate action on any red flags; RBI mandates yearly KYC updates for high-risk customers and EDD refresh; US FinCEN similarly expects risk-based ongoing monitoring though not prescriptive on frequency.
</Rule>
