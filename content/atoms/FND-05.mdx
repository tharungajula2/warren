### Core mechanism

| Question | Concept | Failure mode |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Can losses be absorbed? | Capital | Losses exhaust loss-absorbing resources |
| Can obligations be paid on time? | Liquidity | Usable cash/settlement resources are insufficient when needed |
| Are assets worth enough relative to obligations? | Solvency | Economic asset value falls below obligations |

Accounting equity and regulatory capital are related but not identical. Prudential frameworks apply eligibility criteria, deductions, and adjustments when deciding what counts as regulatory capital.

A solvent bank can still be illiquid: it may own valuable long-dated assets but lack immediately usable settlement resources. An institution can also hold cash and still be insolvent if total asset value is below total obligations.

### Flow

```text
Asset loss
    ↓
Equity / eligible capital absorbs loss
    ↓
Loss capacity falls

Payment outflow
    ↓
Liquid resources used
    ↓
Payment capacity falls
```

### Permanent rule

A balance-sheet resource can improve more than one resilience dimension, but the dimensions remain conceptually separate.

### Never confuse [Capital] and [Liquidity]

**Capital** — loss-absorbing financial resources defined under accounting or prudential rules.

---

**Liquidity** — capacity to meet cash and settlement obligations when they fall due.

### Never confuse [Equity] and [Regulatory capital]

**Equity** — the accounting residual.

---

**Regulatory capital** — the prudential measure after eligibility rules, deductions, and adjustments.

### Permanent trap

Calling a bank “well capitalised” because it has a large cash balance. Cash is an asset; capital describes loss absorption.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Calibration and legal definitions vary, but capital, liquidity, and solvency remain distinct concepts in every jurisdiction.
