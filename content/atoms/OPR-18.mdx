**Conduct risk** stems from staff behaviors and culture that lead to poor customer outcomes or market abuse.  Unlike pure “financial” risks, conduct events (mis-selling, fraud, negligence) may not cause an immediate loss to the bank but can cause reputational and regulatory costs.  Regulators stress that conduct risk is fundamentally about **culture and incentives**.  The Basel Committee notes that mis-selling, compliance breaches, and control failures all fall under operational risk, driven by poor conduct.  

Banks must therefore embed “tone from the top”: fair treatment of customers, clear values, and aligned incentive schemes.  For example, incentive plans that reward volume over quality are red flags. Boards are expected to understand if compensation, sales practices, or product design could create conduct risk. In practice, strong conduct risk management means close oversight by compliance and risk units, review of customer outcomes, and control functions that test for unethical behavior (e.g. forced sales).  Supervisors may intervene if conduct culture is lacking (e.g. FCA’s “consumer duty”).

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Embed conduct risk management into the risk framework.  This includes board accountability, clear ethical standards, and monitoring of behaviors that might harm customers. Treat compliance breaches and misconduct as operational risk incidents.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Focusing only on rule compliance misses the point.  The trap is to assume “if there’s no explicit rule violation, behavior is okay.”  Conduct risk is broader – it can arise from aggressive sales pressures or conflicts of interest even before any rule is broken.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Conduct Risk" b="Compliance Risk">
**Conduct Risk** — the risk that unethical or improper behavior by firm or staff causes customer/market harm.  
---  
**Compliance Risk** — the risk of failing to meet regulatory requirements. (Conduct encompasses compliance but also relates to culture and customer fairness.)
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="UK">
— FCA and PRA explicitly require firms to manage conduct risk (e.g. Principles for Business, Financial Services Act 2021).
</Delta>

<Delta j="EU">
— EBA consumer protection rules, MiFID II/IDD sales rules address conduct; EBA has guidelines on culture.
</Delta>

<Delta j="IN">
— RBI’s Fair Practice Codes and recent Financial Sector reforms emphasize customer interests (e.g. embedding fair treatment).
</Delta>

<Delta j="US">
— CFPB, FINRA, etc. handle conduct at retail level; Fed/OCC enforce risk culture via SCB (Supervisory Complexity and Culture) reviews.
</Delta>
