### Core mechanism

| Behavioural option | Customer incentive | Bank effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Fixed-rate loan prepayment | market rates fall / refinancing becomes cheaper | high-yield asset returns early; reinvestment yield falls |
| Term-deposit break | market rates rise above deposit rate | funding leaves early; replacement funding may cost more |
| NMD withdrawal | confidence or competing rates deteriorate | assumed stable funding shortens |
| Contractual cap/floor | rate crosses option boundary | coupon sensitivity becomes non-linear |

The model therefore cannot use one prepayment or redemption rate across all interest-rate scenarios. Exercise probability should respond to economic incentive, customer friction, seasoning, product terms and stress conditions.

### Flow

```text
Rate / market condition changes
    ↓
Customer incentive changes
    ↓
Option exercise probability changes
    ↓
Effective cash-flow timing changes
    ↓
ΔNII + ΔEVE + funding/liquidity profile change
```

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Behavioural balance sheet · IRRBB sources · NMD behaviour
This:       Scenario-dependent customer optionality
Downstream: Pipeline/commitments · IRRBB stress · hedging · FTP
```

### Permanent rule

When customer exercise depends on the scenario, the projected cash flow must also depend on the scenario.

### Never confuse [Contractual option] and [Behavioural option]

**Contractual option** — the legal right or payoff exists in the instrument terms.

---

**Behavioural option** — the timing and probability of exercise must be estimated from customer behaviour.

### Permanent trap

Using a long-run average prepayment rate in every rate scenario. A historical mean destroys the very non-linearity the option creates.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta in mechanism. Supervisors differ in prescribed standardised assumptions, floors, caps and validation expectations.
