The Basel Committee on Banking Supervision develops common standards to strengthen banking regulation and promote comparability. Its membership includes central banks and supervisory authorities, but the Committee is not a legislature with direct jurisdiction over banks.

Its Charter is explicit: the BCBS “does not possess any formal supranational authority” and its decisions “do not have legal force”. Implementation relies on members’ commitments and domestic action.

That creates three levels a practitioner must keep separate.

First is the **Basel text**: the international benchmark. Second is **jurisdictional implementation**: legislation, regulations, directions or supervisory rules that transpose, modify, defer or supplement Basel. Third is the **firm-specific requirement**: the actual rule, permission, buffer or supervisory decision applicable to a particular institution.

Those layers explain why implementation dates diverge. The EU began applying major CRR3 elements from 1 January 2025; the UK’s Basel 3.1 final rules begin 1 January 2027; India’s revised standardised credit-risk Directions begin 1 April 2027; and the United States’ March 2026 package remains proposed as at 27 August 2026.

A sentence such as “Basel requires this bank to hold 8%” is therefore legally incomplete. Basel sets a global standard; the applicable jurisdictional rule determines the bank’s binding requirement and may be higher or structurally different.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
For a bank, the binding rule is the applicable domestic or regional legal framework, not the Basel publication standing alone.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Quoting a Basel minimum as though it automatically overrides a higher local minimum can understate the bank’s actual regulatory requirement.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Basel standard" b="Binding capital rule">
**Basel standard** — internationally agreed prudential benchmark issued by the BCBS.

---
**Binding capital rule** — requirement made legally effective by the competent national or regional authority.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Basel’s non-binding supranational legal status is stated in the BCBS Charter itself.
</Rule>
