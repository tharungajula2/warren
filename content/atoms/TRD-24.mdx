A central counterparty, or CCP, becomes the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer for transactions accepted for clearing. In a common legal mechanism, the original bilateral contract is replaced or transformed so that each participant has an obligation to the CCP rather than directly to the original counterparty.

This concentration changes the shape of counterparty risk. Bilateral exposure across many trading partners can be multilateral-netted within the clearing framework. The CCP collects margin, maintains a default-management process and holds financial resources designed to absorb losses under defined scenarios. Participants may contribute to a default fund in addition to posting initial and variation margin.

Variation margin transfers current mark-to-market gains and losses. Initial margin is intended to cover potential future exposure during the period needed to close or hedge a defaulted position. A CCP may also apply concentration, liquidity or add-on charges.

Clearing therefore reduces some bilateral risks but creates dependence on the CCP and its members. Liquidity demands can rise sharply when volatility increases and margin calls grow. A participant can be economically hedged but still fail if it cannot meet cash margin on time.

Novation must also be distinguished from trade affirmation. Matching confirms that both sides agree trade economics; novation changes the legal counterparty relationship. Not every clearing model uses identical legal wording, so system design should not hard-code one legal mechanism globally.

The PFMI set international standards for systemically important CCPs and other financial market infrastructures. For a BA, the key data chain is original trade → clearing eligibility → clearing submission → acceptance → cleared position → margin and settlement. A rejected clearing submission may leave the original bilateral risk alive, depending on the legal arrangement.

```text
Before clearing:
Party A &lt;--------&gt; Party B

Accepted by CCP:
Party A &lt;--------&gt; CCP &lt;--------&gt; Party B

Risk controls:
initial margin
variation margin
default fund
default management
```

### Permanent rule

CCP clearing redistributes and manages counterparty risk; it does not abolish credit or liquidity risk.

### Permanent trap

Assuming a matched trade is already novated to the CCP before clearing acceptance.

### Never confuse

**Novation** — replacement of an original contractual relationship with new obligations involving another counterparty.

---

**Netting** — combining multiple obligations into reduced net obligations under enforceable rules.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.**
