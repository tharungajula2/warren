### Core mechanism

| Object | ALM question | Key assumptions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Balance | How much remains, for how long? | core share, decay, attrition |
| Rate | How does customer pricing follow market rates? | beta, lag, asymmetry, floors |
| Segment | Which balances behave alike? | retail/wholesale, transactional/rate-shopping, concentration |

Balance life drives effective tenor and EVE; beta and lag drive repricing and near-term NII. Legal product labels cannot replace behavioural segmentation.

### Flow

```text
NMD accounts
    ↓
Behavioural segmentation
    ↓
Balance persistence model + repricing model
    ↓
Scenario-specific cash flows and rates
    ↓
ΔNII · ΔEVE · liquidity · FTP
```

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Behavioural balance sheet · gap analysis · duration
This:       NMD balance and rate behaviour
Downstream: IRRBB stress · liquidity runoff · behavioural FTP
```

### Formula

```text
Deposit beta = Δdeposit rate / Δreference rate

Beta ↑ → more market-rate movement passes to customer rate → funding cost is more rate-sensitive.

Lag ↑ → pass-through occurs later → near-term NII response is slower, all else equal.
```

### Permanent rule

NMD contractual maturity does not determine its ALM tenor, and balance life must be modelled separately from rate beta and lag.

### Permanent trap

Giving a deposit a long behavioural maturity because it has historically been stable while ignoring that digital access, competition or stress can change both balance decay and repricing behaviour.

### Jurisdiction variants

#### IN (RBI)
RBI’s 2025 forthcoming IRRBB framework includes prescribed constraints for NMD treatment in the standardised methodology. **As at August 2026 it is not yet in force**; the Directions state that the implementation date will be communicated separately.

#### UK (PRA)
No economic delta. PRA firms must support behavioural assumptions within IRRBB and liquidity risk management; firm-specific supervisory expectations sit within the PRA framework.

#### EU
EBA/GL/2022/14 and Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/857 specify detailed behavioural constraints for IRRBB standardised approaches. The Delegated Regulation has applied since 14 May 2024.

#### US
US interagency IRR guidance expects assumptions to be reasonable, supportable and commensurate with the institution’s risk; no EU-style universal NMD calibration is imposed through that guidance.
