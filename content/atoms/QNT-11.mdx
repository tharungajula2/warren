  **Weight of Evidence (WOE):** A transformation of categorical/binned variables

Banks commonly use WOE because it yields linear relationships in log-odds space and deals well with missing values. Example: Suppose credit score binned:
- Score 1-300: 10% defaults (bads), 90% good =&gt; WOE = ln(0.90/0.10)=2.2.
- Score 301-600: 5% defaults, 95% good =&gt; WOE = ln(0.95/0.05)=2.94.
Higher WOE means a bin has relatively more goods (lower default risk). The logistic regression will use these WOE values to model default odds linearly.

**IV** is used to rank variable strength: higher IV implies stronger separation between goods/bads. Rough guide: IV&lt;0.02 not useful; 0.1-0.3 medium; &gt;0.5 extremely predictive. It sums the “divergence” of distribution. It’s not regulatory but common industry practice for feature selection.

**Why binning:** Continuous variables may have non-linear PD relationships. Binning (e.g. by quantile) and computing WOE enforces piecewise-constant relationship, simplifying model. It also handles missing by treating missing as a separate bin. A caveat: bins should have enough data to avoid volatility.

### Permanent rule

When using WOE, ensure monotonicity: bins should be sorted so that WOE moves consistently (e.g. increasing WOE with score). This avoids non-monotonic risk segments which can cause logistic coefficients to be unstable or non-intuitive.

### Permanent trap

Creating too many bins or bins with small counts. This inflates IV artificially and causes overfitting. Also, forgetting to check monotonic trend: if WOE jumps up then down across bins, it suggests your model may overfit bin boundaries rather than true risk.

### Never confuse

**WOE** — numeric encoding of bins: ln(%goods/%bads).

---

**IV** — aggregate measure of how well a variable’s distribution separates goods/bads. High IV implies big differences in rates across bins. Unlike WOE, IV is a single number per variable.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** WOE and IV are industry conventions, not mandated by regulators. All jurisdictions use similar methods. However, if under IRB, RBI expects documented binning rationale. IFRS 9 does not specify this detail.
