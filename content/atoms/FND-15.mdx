For a simple fully drawn loan, the first approximation to **credit exposure** is often the amount currently owed. If 80 of principal remains outstanding, 80 is an obvious starting point for asking what is exposed to borrower default.

But exposure and loss are different quantities. If the bank is exposed to 80 and ultimately recovers 60, the realised loss is 20, not 80. Collateral, guarantees, repayments and recoveries can reduce loss relative to exposure. Conversely, a facility can contain an undrawn amount that may be drawn before default, causing the exposure at the risk event to exceed today's drawn balance.

The same distinction extends beyond loans. A derivative can create exposure when its replacement value becomes positive to the bank. A security position creates exposure to market-value changes. A foreign-currency position creates exposure to exchange-rate movements. Operational processes can create exposure to losses even without a conventional financial claim. "Exposure" therefore needs both a **risk type** and a **measurement date or horizon**.

Practitioners should ask: exposed to **what event**, over **what horizon**, measured using **which amount**, and after **which recognised risk mitigants**? Without those qualifiers, an exposure number can look precise while referring to different economic concepts.

Later credit-risk measures may distinguish current exposure, exposure at default, committed amounts and conversion of undrawn commitments. The foundational discipline comes first: the amount at risk must be identified before probability or severity can be calculated.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Exposure is an input to loss measurement, not a synonym for loss.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Using today's outstanding principal as exposure for an undrawn or revolving facility without asking whether further drawings can occur before default.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Exposure" b="Loss">
**Exposure** — the amount or position subject to adverse change from a specified risk.
---
**Loss** — the adverse economic outcome after the risk event and relevant recoveries or offsets; exposure can exist without an equal loss.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Regulatory definitions of particular exposure measures can differ, but the conceptual distinction between amount exposed and realised loss does not.
</Rule>

<Recall
  q="If exposure is 80 and recovery is 60, must loss equal 80?"
  a="No; in the simple example the loss is 20."
/>

<Recall
  q="Why can an undrawn facility still create credit exposure?"
  a="The borrower may draw funds before the relevant default event."
