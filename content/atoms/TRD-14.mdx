A forward obliges two parties to transact an underlying asset or cash equivalent at a future date for a price agreed today. The contract can be tailored for notional, date, underlying and settlement terms. Because it is bilateral, the parties face each other subject to their legal agreement, collateral arrangement and any applicable clearing requirement.

A futures contract expresses similar directional economics but is standardised by an exchange. Contract size, delivery months, eligible deliverables or cash-settlement method and trading rules are predefined. The clearing house becomes central to performance through the market’s clearing structure. Positions are marked to market and variation margin moves gains and losses through cash over the life of the contract.

That daily settlement creates an important difference. A forward usually realises most value at maturity or close-out, while a future transfers P&amp;L through daily margin. If interest rates are correlated with the underlying, the timing of those cash flows can make futures and otherwise comparable forwards have slightly different prices.

Futures require initial margin or equivalent performance resources and are subject to exchange price limits, position limits or other market rules where applicable. Standardisation improves fungibility and liquidity but removes some customisation. Forwards can match a bespoke exposure exactly but may be less liquid and more operationally bespoke.

Neither contract is “safer” in all senses. Central clearing materially changes counterparty and liquidity risk, but it also creates margin liquidity requirements and dependence on clearing infrastructure. Bilateral forwards can be collateralised and may also be centrally cleared in some markets.

The BA test is to separate economic exposure from processing model: contract economics, trading venue, clearing status, margining, settlement method and legal documentation are distinct data attributes.

### Permanent rule

Separate payoff equivalence from infrastructure differences.

### Permanent trap

Assuming an exchange-traded future and a bilateral forward with the same maturity must have identical operational cash flows.

### Never confuse

**Forward** — future transaction under bilaterally agreed terms.

---

**Future** — standardised future transaction traded on an exchange and processed through its clearing framework.

**No delta.**
