The principle of **Accuracy and Integrity** (Principle 3) requires that risk data reconcile with the general ledger and management reports, and that automated checks minimize manual errors. Any manual adjustments must have documented rationale and approval. **Completeness** (Principle 4) means capturing data across all material business lines, legal entities and geographies. Supervisors typically expect banks to cover at least 95% of risk sources; any gaps trigger examination findings. **Timeliness** (Principle 5) demands that consolidated risk metrics be available fast enough for decision-making in stress; for example, daily VaR and credit portfolio reports as a baseline, potentially intraday for key measures. Finally, **Adaptability** (Principle 6) requires systems to produce new, ad-hoc reports quickly – for instance, answering a one-off supervisory query without months of coding.

Banking institutions implement these requirements by building data warehouses or data lakes that collect source-system data nightly (or more frequently) and standardizing it. They set quality rules so that any discrepancy (e.g. mismatched values) is flagged. Importantly, the infrastructure must operate in crisis scenarios as well (e.g. with market freezes or system outages) to ensure data is still deliverable. The perpetual struggle is that legacy siloed systems and manual processes often fall short of these standards, which is why many banks have ongoing data improvement programs. Nonetheless, under BCBS 239, the **permanent requirement** is clear: all material risk data must be consolidated accurately and on time.

### Permanent rule

Risk aggregation must cover all material data and reconcile to accounting — incomplete or unverified data is unacceptable.

### Permanent trap

Assuming that old processes (e.g. monthly spreadsheet closes) are sufficient; BCBS 239 expects daily or intraday capabilities and automation.

### Never confuse

**Completeness** — capturing *all* relevant risk data (nearly 100% coverage).
---
**Consistency (or validity)** — ensuring data adheres to business rules. While related, completeness means no gaps, consistency means matching definitions.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Data aggregation requirements (accuracy, completeness, etc.) are consistent under BCBS 239 across jurisdictions, as these are quantitative targets not subject to local modification.
