### Core mechanism

| Instrument | Question it answers | Breach means | It cannot prove |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| AUC / Gini / KS | Does the model separate or rank outcomes effectively? | Ranking power has weakened or is inadequate for use | Probability levels are accurate |
| Calibration test / plot | Do predicted levels align with observed levels? | Systematic over- or under-prediction may exist | Cases are ranked well |
| Backtesting | Did prior forecasts correspond to realised outcomes over the test period? | Forecast behaviour differs from expectation | Future validity under changed conditions |

```text
AUC → ranking discrimination, not calibration
Gini / KS → alternative ranking instruments, not proof of level accuracy
Calibration → predicted level versus realised level
Backtesting → forecast versus subsequent outcome
```

```text
Threshold breach
    ↓
Check data and test design
    ↓
Assess magnitude, persistence and business materiality
    ↓
Root-cause
    ↓
Continue with observation · Restrict use · Adjust · Recalibrate · Redevelop
```

Thresholds should be set for the model and use, not copied as universal regulatory numbers. Statistical significance also does not automatically equal economic significance; sample size, data quality and consequence matter.

### Permanent rule

Ranking and level accuracy are different properties. A model can rank cases correctly while consistently predicting the wrong absolute level.

### Never confuse [Discrimination] and [Calibration]

**Discrimination** - ability to order observations so relatively higher-risk cases receive relatively higher-risk model outputs.

---

**Calibration** - alignment between predicted levels and observed outcome frequencies or values.

### Permanent trap

Teaching a fixed AUC, KS or calibration threshold as a universal pass mark. Thresholds are model- and use-specific governance choices unless a particular applicable rule states otherwise.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta. Regulators differ in model scope and supervisory detail, but discrimination, calibration and backtesting are technical validation instruments; the mathematics does not change by jurisdiction.

<Recall
  q="Why can a model with strong AUC still be dangerous?"
  a="AUC addresses ranking only. The model may systematically understate or overstate the absolute level of risk and therefore support poor pricing, provisioning or limits."
/>

<Recall
  q="What should happen after an outcomes threshold breach?"
  a="Validate the data and test first, assess persistence and materiality, determine root cause, then choose a proportionate response such as closer monitoring, restriction, recalibration or redevelopment."
/>

<Recall
  q="What does backtesting fail to prove?"
  a="It cannot prove future validity when the population, relationships or operating environment have changed; it is evidence about realised historical performance."
/>
