Conceptually:

`NSFR = Available Stable Funding / Required Stable Funding`

**Available Stable Funding (ASF)** assigns greater value to liabilities and capital expected to remain available over the relevant horizon. Long-term debt, capital and stable deposit categories generally provide more ASF than very short or unstable wholesale funding.

**Required Stable Funding (RSF)** reflects how much stable funding the bank’s assets and certain off-balance-sheet exposures are judged to require. Cash and highly liquid short-term assets require relatively little stable funding; longer-dated or less-liquid assets require more. The Basel standard requires NSFR of at least 100%, meaning ASF must be no less than RSF.

The structural question is different from LCR. A bank could carry enough HQLA to pass a 30-day LCR while financing a large portfolio of long-dated assets with funding that matures shortly after that horizon. NSFR constrains that reliance on unstable maturity transformation. Conversely, a bank can have strong long-term funding and still face a severe immediate run that threatens its LCR.

NSFR is also not the same as contractual one-year matching. ASF and RSF are weighted prudential constructs. A liability maturing inside a year can still receive some stable-funding recognition, while an asset’s RSF depends on more than contractual maturity.

India’s final NSFR rules were issued earlier but implementation was deferred during the pandemic; RBI made them effective from 1 October 2021 for covered commercial banks. The US final rule became effective 1 July 2021 and applies on a tailored basis to large banking organisations.

### Permanent rule

LCR addresses short-term liquidity stress; NSFR addresses structural funding stability.

### Permanent trap

Calling NSFR a “one-year LCR”; its numerator and denominator are stable-funding weights, not one-year cash inflows and outflows.

### Never confuse

**ASF** — regulatory measure of funding expected to be sufficiently stable.

---

**RSF** — regulatory measure of the stable funding required by assets and specified exposures.

### Jurisdiction variants

**IN** — RBI NSFR guidelines for covered commercial banks have applied since 1 October 2021. [RBI/2020-21/95]

**UK** — the UK prudential framework implements stable-funding requirements alongside PRA liquidity supervision; current scope must be checked against the PRA Rulebook. [PRA SS24/15]

**EU** — CRR contains the NSFR in Part Six, including Articles 413 and 428b. [Regulation (EU) No 575/2013, as amended]

**US** — the interagency NSFR rule is tailored to large banking organisations, has a one-year structural focus and became effective 1 July 2021. [Federal Reserve final rule, 2020]
