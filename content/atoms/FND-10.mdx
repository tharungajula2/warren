### Core mechanism

| Pass | Question | What to inspect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1. Assets | What does the bank own or have claims on? | Cash, securities, loans, other assets |
| 2. Funding | Who finances those assets and on what terms? | Deposits, wholesale funding, other liabilities |
| 3. Loss absorption | What residual / eligible capital sits beneath liabilities? | Equity and prudential-capital disclosures |
| 4. Quality | How reliable are the carrying values and cash flows? | Credit quality, impairment, valuation |
| 5. Maturity | When do assets turn into cash versus liabilities fall due? | Contractual and behavioural maturity |
| 6. Concentration | What could fail together? | Counterparty, sector, geography, product, funding source |

Equal totals can conceal radically different risk. Two banks with the same loan balance can have different borrower quality; two banks with the same deposits can have different deposit stability; two banks with the same equity can have different risk intensity.

### Flow

```text
Assets
    ↓
Funding
    ↓
Equity / capital
    ↓
Quality
    ↓
Maturity
    ↓
Concentration
    ↓

```

### Permanent rule

Never infer resilience from a balance-sheet total without reading composition, quality, timing, and concentration.

### Never confuse [Book equity] and [Regulatory capital]

**Book equity** — accounting residual.

---

**Regulatory capital** — prudentially eligible capital after regulatory definitions and adjustments.

### Permanent trap

Reading “large deposits” as “large liquidity”. Deposits are liabilities; the liquidity question concerns assets and funding behaviour under payment pressure.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Line-item names and disclosure templates differ, but this analytical sequence is jurisdiction-neutral.
