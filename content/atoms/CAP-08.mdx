### Core mechanism

```text
IRB regulatory expected loss
           versus
Eligible provisions assigned to IRB exposures
              ↓
      +-------+-------+
      |               |
EL &gt; provisions   Provisions &gt; EL
      |               |
      ↓               ↓
Shortfall         Excess
      |               |
      ↓               ↓
Deduct from       Limited Tier 2
CET1              recognition, subject
                  to Basel conditions/cap
```

Basel CRE35 defines total eligible provisions for IRB exposures and requires the comparison with total regulatory EL. CAP30 requires the full shortfall, where regulatory EL exceeds eligible provisions, to be deducted from CET1. Where provisions exceed EL, the excess may receive limited Tier 2 recognition subject to the framework.

This is not an accounting true-up. Regulatory EL and accounting ECL can differ in horizon, parameter calibration, default treatment and objective.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   IRB expected loss · accounting provisions
This:       EL-versus-provision comparison
Downstream: CET1 deduction · Tier 2 recognition · regulatory capital ratios
```

### Formula

```text
Provision shortfall = max(0, regulatory EL − eligible provisions)

Provision excess = max(0, eligible provisions − regulatory EL)

Only the treatment permitted by the capital framework applies to those amounts.
```

### Permanent rule

A provision shortfall reduces CET1 because the prudential EL amount is not fully covered by eligible provisions.

### Never confuse [Regulatory expected loss] and [Accounting ECL]

**Regulatory expected loss** — prudential quantity defined inside the IRB capital framework.

---

**Accounting ECL** — loss allowance measured under the applicable accounting or prudential provisioning architecture.

### Permanent trap

Comparing lifetime accounting ECL directly with one-year IRB EL as though the two should reconcile mechanically ignores different definitions and objectives.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta in the Basel IRB mechanism. It is relevant only where the jurisdiction permits IRB for the exposure concerned.
