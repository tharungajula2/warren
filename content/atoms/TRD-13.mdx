A derivative is a contract whose value depends on another price, rate, index, credit event or market variable. Derivatives can transfer risk without requiring the full underlying asset to change hands at trade date. Common families include forwards, futures, swaps and options.

The first useful classification is payoff shape. A linear derivative has a payoff that is approximately a straight-line function of the underlying variable over the relevant range. A long forward on an asset gains when the underlying rises above the contracted forward price and loses when it falls below it. An interest-rate swap is more complex in cash-flow construction, but its value is still driven largely by linear rate sensitivities around current market conditions.

Options are non-linear. A purchased call has limited downside to the premium but increasing upside above the strike. Its sensitivity to the underlying, delta, changes as the underlying price moves; gamma measures that change. Non-linearity means a hedge that is correct now may not remain correct after a market move.

Derivative notional is not the same as market value or maximum loss. Notional is a reference amount used to calculate payments. A swap with a very large notional can have a small current market value, while future exposure can still be material. For options, the premium, strike, notional, expiry and volatility jointly shape risk.

Derivatives also introduce counterparty, collateral, liquidity, legal and operational risks. Exchange trading or central clearing can change those risks without changing the basic economic payoff.

The practical starting point for any unfamiliar derivative is therefore to draw its contractual cash flows and payoff. Product names are less reliable than answering: what is referenced, what is exchanged, when, under what condition, and how does the payoff change when the market variable moves?

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Draw the payoff before naming the risk.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Using notional as if it were current fair value or a direct measure of maximum loss.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Linear derivative" b="Non-linear derivative">
**Linear derivative** — sensitivity is approximately constant with respect to the underlying over small moves.

---

**Non-linear derivative** — sensitivity itself changes as the underlying or other inputs move.
</NeverConfuse>

**No delta.**
</Rule>
