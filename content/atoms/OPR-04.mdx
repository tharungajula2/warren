Risk and Control Self-Assessment (RCSA) is a core risk management process.  In an RCSA, each business unit workshops its processes, risks, and controls.  Teams identify “what can go wrong” (risk scenarios) and evaluate whether existing controls are adequate.  The result is a self-assessed risk rating and list of control gaps, all owned by the first-line managers.  The second line (Operational Risk or Risk Management functions) facilitates the methodology, reviews results, and consolidates findings across the bank.  

RCSA ensures that risks are identified close to the business activity and that managers own their residual risks.  It helps populate the risk register and guides where to improve controls.  However, to be effective, RCSA must be more than a checklist: it needs rigorous facilitation, calibration (e.g. via benchmarking or past loss data), and challenge.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Perform RCSAs regularly (at least annually) and for all material processes. Assign clear ownership: 1st line owns the process and control design, 2nd line owns the RCSA framework and validation.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Do not treat RCSA as a tick-box audit.  A common mistake is letting units fill out forms without truly analyzing controls or linking to loss data.  Ensure RCSA drives real risk improvement.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="RCSA" b="Internal Audit">
**RCSA** — a proactive self-review by the business of its own risks and controls.   
---  
**Internal Audit** — an independent audit function that tests processes after the fact. RCSA is done by the process owner; internal audit is 3rd-line assurance.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** The RCSA concept is a best-practice standard worldwide.  Banks everywhere use first-line self-assessment to uncover op risk.
</Rule>
