### Core mechanism

PSI is usually applied to a model score or population-level distribution; CSI applies the same mathematical structure to an individual characteristic. Naming differs across institutions, but the core calculation compares bin shares between a reference sample and a comparison sample.

| Driver | Effect on PSI or CSI |
| :--- | :--- |
| Larger bin-share shift | Increases contribution |
| More extreme relative shift | Log ratio magnifies divergence |
| Different bin boundaries | Can materially change the index |
| Tiny or zero bin shares | Can create unstable or undefined log ratios without treatment |
| Large sample size | Makes small shifts precisely measurable but does not make them economically important |

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Fixed bins · Reference sample · Comparison sample
This:       PSI · CSI · Distribution shift
Downstream: Root-cause analysis · Performance monitoring · Model review
```

### Formula

```text
Bin contribution = (comparison share - reference share) x ln(comparison share / reference share)
PSI or CSI = sum over bins of bin contribution
```

### Permanent rule

PSI and CSI detect distribution change, not its cause and not automatically model-performance deterioration.

### Never confuse [Population drift] and [Concept drift]

**Population drift** - distribution of model inputs or scores changes.

---

**Concept drift** - relationship between predictors and the credit outcome changes. Input distribution can move while performance remains sound, or relationships can deteriorate with little visible input drift.

### Permanent trap

Treating a PSI increase as proof the model is wrong. The shift may reflect a deliberate acquisition strategy, seasonality or benign mix change; performance and calibration must be checked separately.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta. PSI and CSI mathematics are universal; MRM owns governed thresholds and escalation.

<Recall
  q="Why can the same two populations produce different PSI values under different binning schemes?"
  a="PSI is computed from bin shares, so changing boundaries changes how distribution movement is discretized and therefore changes the summed divergence."
/>

<Recall
  q="What does PSI establish when it rises?"
  a="It establishes that the comparison distribution differs from the fixed reference distribution under the chosen bins; it does not establish why the shift occurred or whether predictive performance deteriorated."
/>

<Recall
  q="Why are zero-share bins technically awkward?"
  a="The calculation contains a logarithm of a share ratio, and a zero numerator or denominator makes that ratio or its logarithm undefined without a stated smoothing treatment."
/>
