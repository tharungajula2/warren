### Core mechanism

Let L be a loss variable. A 99th-percentile loss quantile is a level at or below which approximately 99% of modelled loss outcomes fall under the stated convention, leaving approximately 1% beyond it in a continuous setting.

```text
Lower loss                                      Higher loss
0 ----------------------------------------------------→
|&lt;---------------- about 99% ----------------&gt;| tail |
                                               ^
                                               |
                                         99% quantile
```

Two distributions can share the same 99% quantile while having radically different outcomes in the worst 1%. The quantile therefore answers **where the tail begins for the chosen probability level**, not **how bad every tail outcome is**.

| Change | Effect on interpretation |
| :--- | :--- |
| Confidence level changes | Threshold changes |
| Horizon changes | Distribution changes |
| Model/data changes | Estimated threshold can change |
| Tail shape changes | Severity beyond threshold can change even if quantile is similar |

### Permanent rule

A quantile is a distribution threshold, never a hard cap on possible outcomes.

### Never confuse [Quantile] and [Maximum loss]

**Quantile** — threshold associated with a cumulative probability.

---

**Maximum loss** — greatest possible or defined loss under a separate assumption; it is not implied by a percentile.

### Never confuse [High confidence] and [Low probability of any loss]

**High-confidence quantile** — leaves a small probability of outcomes beyond the stated threshold.

---

**Probability of any loss** — probability that loss is greater than zero, which is a different question.

### Permanent trap

Saying a 99% loss quantile means “there is only a 1% chance of losing money”. It means roughly 1% of modelled outcomes exceed that particular loss threshold.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Quantiles and probability tails are mathematical concepts.

<Recall
  q="What lies beyond a 99% loss quantile in a continuous model?"
  a="Approximately 1% of modelled outcomes."
/>

<Recall
  q="Is the 99% quantile the maximum loss?"
  a="No. Outcomes beyond the quantile remain possible and can be much more severe."
/>

<Recall
