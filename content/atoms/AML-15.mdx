Once an alert survives triage and the bank decides a transaction is suspicious, it must file a report to the FIU/regulator. In the US, banks file **SARs** to FinCEN under 31 CFR 1020; in India, they file **STRs** to FIU-IND within 7 working days of detection. The UK and EU also use the term **SAR** (or Suspicious Transaction Report in EU parlance) reported to the local FIU (NCA in the UK, FIUs in EU).  Banks must include details of the transaction, customer, and why it’s suspicious. Meeting deadlines is critical (e.g. 30 days in US after suspicion).  

Importantly, the customer must **not** be told a report is filed. FATF and all AML laws forbid “tipping off.” For example, UK law (POCA) imposes up to 5 years jail for tipping off or prejudicing an investigation. The bank’s duty is to report in secret. No employee may hint to the customer or third parties that the transaction is under scrutiny.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
File a comprehensive SAR/STR to the designated authority whenever you know or suspect money laundering or terrorism financing, regardless of the transaction amount. Keep all SAR/STR filings confidential.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Accidentally revealing an investigation. For instance, sending a collection notice to a client or closing their account without explanation can be construed as tipping off. Always refuse or delay suspicious transactions without explanation, rather than alerting the customer.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="SAR/STR" b="Currency Transaction Report (CTR)">
**SAR/STR** — formal report to authorities of *suspected* illicit activity.  
---  
**Currency Transaction Report (CTR)** — an automatic report of large cash transactions (e.g. $10K+ in US). Not every big transaction is suspicious; CTRs are just monitoring filings, not judgment calls. Newcomers sometimes think a CTR is the same as an STR (it is not).
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="IN">
— Banks submit Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs) to FIU-IND (via the FINnet portal) within 7 working days of detecting suspicion. In addition, Cash Transaction Reports (CTRs) are required for any cash above ₹10 lakh in a month (even if not suspicious).
</Delta>

<Delta j="UK">
— Regulated firms file SARs to the UKFIU/NCA. They have 2 working days from the date of suspicion to report (usually via the SAR Online portal). Tipping off and prejudicing investigations are criminal offences under POCA (up to 5 years prison).
</Delta>

<Delta j="EU">
— EU AML law requires filing a Suspicious Transaction Report to the national FIU. Member states also mandate large transaction reports (e.g. €10,000 cash threshold).
</Delta>

<Delta j="US">
— US banks file SARs to FinCEN if they detect funds derived from illegal activities or intended for illicit purposes. The CTR (Currency Transaction Report) is filed for cash over $10,000 (whether suspicious or not). SARs in the US do not have a tipping-off prohibition, but bank employees must not notify customers.
</Delta>
