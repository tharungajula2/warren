A useful mental model for ECL starts with cash rather than risk parameters. IFRS 9 defines a **credit loss** through cash shortfalls: compare all contractual cash flows due under the contract with all cash flows the entity expects to receive, then discount the difference using the relevant effective interest rate. Timing matters as well as amount; receiving the full principal materially late can create a credit loss because delayed cash is worth less at the reporting date.

This definition explains why the common formula `PD × LGD × EAD` is a method rather than the accounting definition. Probability of default (PD) estimates the chance of default, loss given default (LGD) estimates loss severity conditional on default, and exposure at default (EAD) estimates the amount exposed when default occurs. A well-built parameter model should reproduce the expected discounted cash shortfall required by the accounting framework. If the parameterisation conflicts with that measurement objective, the model—not the accounting definition—must yield. IFRS 9 deliberately does not prescribe one modelling technique.

Expected recoveries affect the shortfall. Cash expected from the borrower, collateral realisation, guarantees or other credit enhancements can reduce expected loss where those recoveries are appropriately included under the applicable measurement requirements. Their **timing**, costs and uncertainty still matter. A nominal recovery expected after several years is not equivalent to the same amount received today.

The distinction also explains why market-price movements are not automatically credit losses. Credit impairment focuses on expected contractual collection. Market value may change because risk-free rates, liquidity premiums or other non-credit factors move even when expected contractual collection is unchanged.

### Permanent rule

The economic object being measured is expected non-collection of contractual value, including the effect of timing.

### Permanent trap

Using `PD × LGD × EAD` mechanically without checking whether it reproduces discounted expected cash shortfalls can produce a technically elaborate but accounting-inconsistent allowance.

### Never confuse

**Credit loss** — the expected discounted contractual cash shortfall.

---

**Fair-value loss** — a change in market value that may contain credit, interest-rate, liquidity and other effects.

### Jurisdiction variants

**IN** — Current scheduled-commercial-bank IRACP provisioning is rule-based rather than an Ind AS 109 cash-shortfall ECL regime; RBI’s bank ECL Directions effective April 1, 2027 introduce an ECL framework with PD/LGD mechanics and EIR discounting. Relevant Ind AS NBFCs already operate under Ind AS impairment requirements. [RBI 2025 IRACP; RBI 2026 ECL Directions; RBI 2020 NBFC guidance]

**UK** — UK-adopted IFRS 9 uses the IFRS cash-shortfall definition. [UK-adopted IFRS 9]

**EU** — EU-adopted IFRS 9 uses the same cash-shortfall measurement principle. [Regulation (EU) 2023/1803]

**US** — CECL defines the allowance for amortised-cost assets as the amount needed to reflect the net amount expected to be collected; it permits multiple estimation methods rather than requiring the IFRS 9 cash-shortfall technique in every case. [ASC 326; Interagency CECL FAQ]
