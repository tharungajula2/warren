**UAT Coverage:** The BA must confirm that UAT scenarios collectively cover all key requirements and risk areas. Typically, this is demonstrated by linking each requirement (and sub-case) to one or more UAT scenarios.  In practice, teams track *scenario coverage percentage*. For example, Yethi’s core banking UAT practice uses end-to-end business scenarios (account opening, loan origination, payments) to ensure coverage. They measure how many of those planned scenarios have been executed successfully. To argue coverage is sufficient, present traceability: “Requirement X is covered by test cases TC1, TC2.” Focus on risk: prioritize testing of critical flows first.

**Defect Management:** Each defect discovered in testing is logged with details. Importantly, classify it by **Severity** (the technical impact: *Critical* = system-blocker, *Major*, etc.) and **Priority** (how urgent the fix is to the business: high=must-fix, low=can wait). For example, a calculation error in a capital ratio (high severity) might also be high priority. Lowering defect list by severity/priority and reaching “0 critical open” is usually a go-live criterion.  Track defects transparently: who fixes them, retest after fixes, and ensure no showstoppers remain before sign-off.

### Permanent rule

Build UAT scenarios that cover *every* business requirement, especially regulatory ones. Use traceability and coverage metrics to justify adequacy. Classify and fix defects based on agreed severity/priority rules.

### Permanent trap

Overconfidence that “we tested enough” without proof. Always use coverage measures or sign-offs on critical scenarios. Also, don’t mix up severity and priority – a minor typo may be low severity but could be high priority if it affects reporting.

### Never confuse

**Severity** vs **Priority**:
*Severity* gauges the impact of a defect on system functionality. *Priority* indicates how quickly the defect should be fixed (a business decision). They are separate measures, not synonyms.

### No delta

The need for clear UAT coverage and defect triage is the same in all banks. Regulatory or not, rigorous UAT and defect classification prevent missed issues.
