Suppose a qualifying exposure amount is 100 and the applicable standardised credit risk weight is 50%. Its credit RWA is 50. If a minimum total-capital ratio were applied to that RWA, the capital requirement would be calculated from 50, not directly from the 100 exposure. This two-stage structure separates the **risk measurement** step from the **capital ratio** step.

Risk weights therefore act as regulatory scaling factors. Higher weights make a unit of exposure consume more capital; lower weights make it consume less. Under the standardised approach, weights are specified through exposure classes, counterparty characteristics, collateral treatment, external credit assessments where permitted, loan-to-value measures and other criteria. Basel’s revised standardised approach deliberately increases granularity and risk sensitivity while retaining supervisor-prescribed rules.

A 100% risk weight does not mean a 100% probability of default or a 100% expected loss. It means one unit of exposure creates one unit of RWA before other applicable adjustments. Similarly, a 20% weight does not mean that 20% of the exposure is expected to disappear.

**RWA density** is a useful derived intuition: RWA divided by a relevant exposure measure. A portfolio with 40 of credit RWA against 100 of exposure has 40% RWA density. But density is an output of regulatory measurement, not an independent risk parameter.

Risk weights also differ from provisions. Provisions reduce the carrying value or recognise expected loss under the applicable framework; risk weights determine the capital denominator used to support unexpected risk.

### Permanent rule

A risk weight is a prudential scaling factor used to calculate RWA; it is not PD, LGD or expected loss.

### Permanent trap

Reading a 150% risk weight as “the regulator expects to lose 150% of the loan” confuses a capital calibration with an economic loss forecast.

### Never confuse

**Risk weight** — the regulatory percentage or formula output applied to an exposure for RWA purposes.

---
**Capital ratio** — qualifying capital divided by RWA; it is applied after RWA has been determined.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Jurisdictions prescribe different detailed weights, but the standardised arithmetic of exposure amount multiplied by applicable risk weight is common.
