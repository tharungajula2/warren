Each trading desk that uses IMA must calculate a daily ES measure.  The modeler computes 10-day ES at 97.5% for each desk.  Crucially, risk factors have assigned *liquidity horizons* (LH) reflecting how long it takes to exit or hedge.  ES is initially computed for a 10-day base then scaled: e.g. a factor with a 20-day LH has its 10-day ES multiplied by √(20/10) to obtain a 20-day ES.  Table 1 of MAR33 defines five LH categories: 10, 20, 40, 60, 120 days (higher LH for less liquid products).

IMA capital equals the sum of these liquidity-adjusted ES contributions across risk factors in the desk.  Desks must have regulators’ approval to use IMA.  Each desk must also pass backtesting and P&amp;L attribution (next atom).

### Permanent rule

IMA-based capital is the liquidity-adjusted 97.5% ES at desk level.

### Permanent trap

Using VaR in place of ES, or omitting the liquidity horizon adjustment (treating all factors as 10-day).

### Never confuse

**Expected Shortfall (ES)** in IMA vs **VaR** (older metric): IMA requires ES at 97.5%, not VaR.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** The use of ES and liquidity horizons is mandated by the Basel FRTB standard for IMA globally (subject to local implementation delays).
