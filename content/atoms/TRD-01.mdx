Equity represents ownership rather than a contractual promise to repay principal. A common shareholder usually has voting rights, a residual claim on assets after creditors, and potential participation in dividends and capital appreciation. Unlike a bond coupon, a dividend is generally discretionary: the board may reduce or omit it, subject to applicable law and the company’s circumstances.

The economic value of an equity can be viewed as the present value of cash flows ultimately available to shareholders. In practice, market prices move because expectations change about revenue, margins, investment, financing, competitive position, interest rates and risk. A company can report rising profits while its share price falls if the market had expected even stronger results or if the discount rate applied to future cash flows rises.

Primary and secondary markets serve different functions. In a primary issuance, the company raises capital by selling newly issued shares. In the secondary market, investors trade existing shares with one another; the company normally does not receive the trade proceeds. Market capitalisation equals share price multiplied by shares outstanding, but it is not the same as enterprise value, which also reflects net debt and other claims.

Equity risk is multi-dimensional. Market risk moves the whole price; idiosyncratic risk comes from the issuer; liquidity risk affects the ability to transact near observed prices; corporate actions such as dividends, splits, rights issues and buybacks change positions or economics. For a banking professional, the important operational point is that the security identifier, listing venue, currency, settlement cycle, corporate-action treatment and legal owner can all matter independently of the headline share price.

### Permanent rule

Equity is a residual claim: higher upside comes with lower contractual seniority than debt.

### Permanent trap

Treating a listed share as if it promises repayment at a fixed date. It does not have a contractual maturity like a plain bond.

### Never confuse

**Market capitalisation** — share price multiplied by shares outstanding; an equity-value measure.

---

**Enterprise value** — a broader value of the operating business that adjusts equity value for debt, cash and other claims.
