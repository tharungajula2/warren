Consider a flat FTP curve. A five-year loan and a three-month loan receive the same funding charge. The longer asset earns more customer spread without paying for its extra funding commitment, so the business is incentivised to originate long-duration assets. The balance sheet accumulates structural mismatch even if every individual transaction passes its profitability hurdle.

Now suppose all deposits receive the same FTP credit. A highly rate-sensitive deposit that disappears quickly in stress can look as valuable as a stable operating balance. The business is then rewarded for volume rather than funding quality.

A third distortion appears when undrawn commitments receive no contingent liquidity charge. The business earns fees while treasury bears the cost of liquidity needed if customers draw during stress. Rational behaviour is to create more commitments than the bank would choose if their liquidity cost were visible.

FTP can also distort behaviour through stale curves. If market funding costs rise sharply but FTP updates slowly, businesses continue originating assets at yesterday’s internal cost. The reported margin becomes an accounting artefact. Federal Reserve guidance specifically warns that failure to apply FTP effectively can misalign business incentives and lead to excessive on-balance-sheet asset growth or off-balance-sheet commitments because funding and contingent liquidity are mispriced.

FSI research similarly found that average-rate transfer pricing failed to penalise longer-term asset commitments, failed to reward longer-term funding and encouraged risky maturity transformation.

The diagnostic question is therefore not merely whether the FTP calculation is mathematically correct. It is: **what transaction would a rational business produce more of because of this price?** If that behaviour is inconsistent with the bank’s risk appetite, the FTP design is wrong or incomplete.

### Permanent rule

The quality of FTP should be judged partly by the behaviour it incentivises, not only by the elegance of its calculation.

### Permanent trap

Treating an FTP model as a finance-allocation exercise and failing to test which products become artificially profitable under its assumptions.

### Never confuse

**FTP accuracy** — economic faithfulness of the internal funding and liquidity signal.

---

**Accounting allocation accuracy** — whether internal revenue and cost mechanically reconcile; reconciliation alone cannot prove incentives are correct.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Incentive distortion from mispricing is an economic mechanism independent of jurisdiction.
