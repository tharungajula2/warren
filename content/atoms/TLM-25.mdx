### Core mechanism

| Funding dimension | More resilient pattern | Fragile pattern |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Tenor | staggered maturities | maturity wall / cliff |
| Source | diversified deposits and wholesale channels | one market or counterparty type |
| Currency | natural or durable currency funding | repeated FX-swap rollover |
| Secured/unsecured | multiple executable channels | dependence on one collateral class |
| Investor base | broad and tested | concentrated / confidence-sensitive |

Rollover risk mechanism:

Long-lived asset
    ↓ funded with
Shorter liability
    ↓
Liability matures before asset returns cash
    ↓
Need to refinance
    ↓
Market unavailable or spread ↑
    ↓
Liquidity shortfall or margin compression

A funding plan therefore looks forward: expected asset growth, deposit behaviour, debt maturities, planned issuance, collateral capacity and stress alternatives must fit together rather than be managed one maturity at a time.

### Permanent rule

A funding source is not stable merely because it is available today; resilience depends on its tenor, concentration and behaviour when the bank or market is under stress.

### Never confuse [Funding concentration] and [Maturity concentration]

**Funding concentration** — excessive dependence on a source, counterparty, market, currency or instrument type.

---

**Maturity concentration** — too much funding comes due in the same time window, even if raised from several sources.

### Permanent trap

Optimising current funding cost by repeatedly issuing at the shortest cheap tenor. The apparent margin gain can be payment for taking hidden refinancing risk.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta in mechanism. Supervisory limits, reporting and acceptable concentration metrics vary.

<Recall

  a="The risk that funding matures before the asset and cannot be replaced on acceptable terms when needed."
