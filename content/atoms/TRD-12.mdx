A non-deliverable forward, or NDF, is economically similar to an FX forward but differs in settlement. The notional amount of the restricted or non-deliverable currency is used to calculate the payoff; it is not delivered. On the fixing date, an agreed reference rate is observed. The contract then settles a net cash amount, commonly in US dollars, on the settlement date.

A simplified payoff depends on the difference between the contracted NDF rate and the fixing rate, adjusted for the quotation convention and notional. Because quote direction changes the formula, the safe operational method is to define the two currencies, settlement currency, notional currency, forward rate, fixing source, fixing date and payment date explicitly.

NDFs developed particularly where capital controls, convertibility restrictions or market access constraints made offshore deliverable forwards difficult. They allow hedging and price discovery without requiring offshore delivery of the underlying currency. As currencies liberalise, deliverable and NDF markets can coexist, converge or shift in relative importance.

The NDF rate is not simply the expected fixing. It reflects interest-rate differentials, market positioning, liquidity, credit and regulatory segmentation. The offshore NDF market and onshore deliverable market can therefore price the same currency differently, especially under stress.

NDF lifecycle risk is concentrated around fixing and settlement. A wrong fixing source, observation time, holiday calendar or disrupted-rate fallback can cause a material cash difference. Confirmation terms are therefore essential.

The Indian rupee is a major NDF currency in offshore markets, but access to INR non-deliverable derivative contracts is governed by RBI rules for authorised dealers and user categories. The universal product mechanic remains cash settlement against a fixing.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
An NDF uses the underlying currency to calculate value but does not require its principal delivery at maturity.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Building an NDF payoff without locking down quote direction and fixing source.
</Trap>
