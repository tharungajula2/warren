### Core mechanism

| Concentration form | Common failure source | Example diagnostic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Single name | One obligor | Exposure / capital or portfolio share |
| Connected group | Control or economic dependency | Aggregated group exposure |
| Sector | Common industry conditions | Sector share and stressed sector loss |
| Geography | Local economy / law / property market | Regional exposure share |
| Product / collateral | Common underwriting or recovery mechanism | Product or collateral-type share |
| Hidden factor | Shared commodity, customer, currency or funding driver | Factor mapping / scenario analysis |

Ten equal exposures to genuinely independent obligors diversify idiosyncratic failure better than one exposure of the same total size. One thousand borrowers can still be concentrated if most depend on the same property market or commodity price.

### Flow

```text
Shared obligor / group / sector / geography / factor
    ↓
Common exposure dependence
    ↓
Concentration ↑ → Diversification benefit ↓
    ↓
Tail-loss sensitivity ↑
```

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Aggregated exposure · Connected groups · Sector/geography/factor tags
This:       Identify common loss drivers → measure concentration → compare with limits/appetite
Downstream: Limit actions · Portfolio steering · Stress testing · Pillar 2 / capital assessment
```

### Ownership

| Role | Responsibility |
| :--- | :--- |
| Produces | Risk data and portfolio teams aggregate exposures and common drivers |
| Approves | Board/senior risk governance sets appetite and concentration limits |
| Consumes | Underwriting, limits, strategy, stress testing and capital planning |
| Challenges | Independent risk and audit test aggregation, taxonomy and limit exceptions |

### Permanent rule

Diversification must be judged by independence of loss drivers, not by nominal borrower count.

### Never confuse [Concentration] and [Large exposure]

**Concentration** — dependence on a common source of loss, which may be name, sector, geography, collateral or another factor.

---

**Large exposure** — an exposure or connected-group exposure meeting a defined regulatory or internal size threshold; concentration is broader.

### Permanent trap

Splitting one economic group across subsidiaries or one common sector across narrow industry codes can improve a report while leaving the underlying loss concentration unchanged.

### Jurisdiction variants

#### IN (RBI)
RBI’s Large Exposures Framework governs prudential single-counterparty and connected-counterparty aggregation and limits. Internal concentration management must still capture sector and common-factor risks outside the legal large-exposure perimeter.

#### UK (PRA)
PRA large-exposure rules and SS3/25 govern connected-client identification; concentration risk also feeds broader supervisory and Pillar 2 assessment.

#### EU
CRR large-exposure rules constrain single clients and groups of connected clients; broader concentration risk remains a risk-management and supervisory concern.

#### US
US legal lending limits use US-specific aggregation rules. Internal sector and factor concentration management is broader than statutory single-borrower limits.

<Recall
  q="Can 1,000 borrowers still be highly concentrated?"
  a="Yes, if their repayment depends on the same sector or economic driver."
/>

<Recall
  q="Why is connected-group concentration different from simple legal-entity counting?"
  a="Separate entities can fail together through control or economic interdependence."
/>

<Recall

  a="No. Sector, geography and common-factor concentrations may be material without any single name breaching a large-exposure threshold."
