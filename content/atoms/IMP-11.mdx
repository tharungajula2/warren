**Purchased or originated credit-impaired (POCI)** financial assets are credit-impaired at initial recognition. A purchase at a deep discount because the borrower is in severe financial difficulty is a common economic pattern, although the accounting test is credit impairment at initial recognition rather than the size of the discount alone.

POCI accounting avoids recognising a conventional Day-1 loss allowance on top of a purchase price that already reflects expected credit losses. Instead, expected credit losses at initial recognition are incorporated into expected cash flows used to calculate the **credit-adjusted effective interest rate**. Interest revenue is subsequently based on that credit-adjusted EIR.

After initial recognition, the entity recognises the cumulative change in **lifetime ECL** as an impairment gain or loss. This can produce an impairment gain if expected collections improve relative to the credit-adjusted expectations embedded at purchase or origination. The asset does not first enter “Stage 1” merely because it has just been recognised; the ordinary staging model assumes an initially non-credit-impaired asset and therefore does not fit POCI economics.

The concept is particularly important when comparing purchased distressed assets with loans that deteriorate after origination. Two assets can have identical current expected cash flows but different accounting histories because one was purchased already impaired and the other became impaired after initial recognition.

### Permanent rule

For POCI assets, initial credit deterioration is part of the asset’s starting economics rather than a post-origination stage migration.

### Permanent trap

Booking a normal Day-1 Stage 3 allowance on top of a purchase price already reflecting credit impairment can double-count the initial expected loss under IFRS 9.

### Never confuse

**POCI** — an asset credit-impaired when first recognised by the reporting entity.

---
**Stage 3 after origination** — an asset that was not credit-impaired initially but later became credit-impaired.

### Jurisdiction variants

**IN** — Current scheduled-commercial-bank IRACP does not operate the Ind AS 109 POCI model. RBI’s final bank ECL framework from April 1, 2027 separately identifies POCI, embeds initial ECL in a credit-adjusted EIR and recognises subsequent cumulative lifetime-ECL changes; relevant Ind AS NBFCs follow applicable Ind AS requirements. [RBI 2026 ECL Directions, paragraphs 24 and related POCI provisions]
**UK** — UK-adopted IFRS 9 applies the POCI and credit-adjusted-EIR model. [IFRS 9 Appendix A, 5.4.1, 5.5.13]
**EU** — EU-adopted IFRS 9 applies the same POCI treatment. [Regulation (EU) 2023/1803]
**US** — ASC 326 uses **purchased credit deteriorated (PCD)** accounting rather than IFRS 9 POCI: the acquisition-date allowance is added to purchase price to establish the initial amortised-cost basis, producing a gross-up rather than IFRS 9’s POCI credit-adjusted-EIR model. [ASC 326; Interagency CECL FAQ]
