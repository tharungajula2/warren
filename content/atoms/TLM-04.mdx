**Repricing risk** arises when assets and liabilities reset at different times. A fixed-rate asset funded by a frequently repricing liability is the simplest example. **Yield-curve risk** arises because rates at different maturities do not necessarily move in parallel: the short end can rise while long rates barely move, or the curve can steepen, flatten or invert.

**Basis risk** appears where two rates have similar tenor but reference different pricing indices or otherwise move imperfectly together. A floating-rate asset and floating-rate liability therefore do not constitute a perfect hedge merely because both reset every three months. RBI’s forthcoming framework defines basis risk as the effect of changes in rates for instruments of similar tenor priced from different interest-rate indices.

**Optionality risk** comes from rights that change cash-flow timing when rates change. Borrowers may prepay fixed-rate loans; depositors may redeem term deposits; customers may leave non-maturity deposits in place or withdraw them; contractual caps and floors alter instrument sensitivity. Options can be explicit in contracts or behavioural in customer actions. Their defining feature is non-linearity: the cash-flow profile itself changes with the rate scenario.

These mechanisms can offset or reinforce one another. A bank may close its simple repricing gap with a derivative but retain basis risk if the hedge references a different index. It may match contractual maturities yet remain exposed because customers prepay assets when rates fall. A parallel-rate hedge can still lose under a curve twist.

Basel’s IRRBB framework therefore expects identification of gap, basis and option risks and uses multiple standardised shock shapes rather than a single parallel rate shock. RBI’s forthcoming framework likewise requires scenarios capturing parallel and non-parallel gap risks and consideration of basis and embedded-option behaviour.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Matching reset dates eliminates only part of IRRBB; basis, curve and option risks can remain.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Declaring a floating-rate asset “hedged” because its funding is also floating-rate without checking whether the indices, reset conventions and embedded options match.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Repricing risk" b="Basis risk">
**Repricing risk** — sensitivity created by different reset timing.
---
**Basis risk** — sensitivity created by imperfect co-movement of different reference rates even when reset timing is similar.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Basel and major national frameworks recognise the same fundamental economic sources, although prescribed scenario calibrations can differ.
</Rule>

<Recall
  q="What four mechanisms generate IRRBB?"
  a="Repricing, yield-curve, basis and optionality risk."

<Recall
  q="Why can two three-month floating rates still create IRRBB?"
