### Core mechanism

Suppose orderly asset value = 110 and liabilities = 100. Economic net worth is positive 10.

But if 30 must settle today and immediately available cash is 10:

Cash shortfall = cash due now − immediately available cash

Cash shortfall = 30 − 10 = 20

The bank must bridge 20 through borrowing, collateralised funding, asset sale, incoming cash or another executable source.

### Flow

```text
Payment obligation due
    ↓
Usable cash insufficient
    ↓
Borrow / repo / sell / transfer liquidity
    ↓
Market or operational access available?
    ├─ Yes → payment settles
    └─ No  → missed payment / resolution risk
                 ↓
          forced-sale losses + confidence loss
                 ↓
          liquidity stress can become solvency stress
```

### Formula

```text
Cash shortfall_t = required cash outflows_t − usable cash inflows/resources_t

Cash shortfall_t — amount that must be funded by time t.

Required cash outflows_t ↑ → liquidity need ↑.

Usable resources_t ↓ → liquidity need ↑.
```

### Permanent rule

Positive net worth cannot substitute for cash at the moment an obligation must settle.

### Never confuse [Capital] and [Liquidity]

**Capital** — loss-absorbing resources protecting against value losses; see FND-05 and CAP.

---

**Liquidity** — the capacity to produce the right cash at the right time without unacceptable loss.

### Permanent trap

Answering “we are well capitalised” to a liquidity question. The next payment is settled with cash or central-bank money, not with a capital ratio.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta. The failure mechanism is economic; access to facilities, resolution tools and supervisory triggers differ by jurisdiction.
