### Core mechanism

Use closed-open boundaries for reporting periods so timestamps at the next boundary do not leak backward:

```sql
SELECT account_id, payment_date, payment_amount
FROM payments
WHERE payment_date &gt;= '2026-08-01'
  AND payment_date &lt; '2026-09-01';
```

Vintage is normally the origination period, not the snapshot period. If the database supports date truncation, derive a month label from `origination_date`:

```sql
SELECT
    facility_id,
    DATE_TRUNC('month', origination_date) AS vintage_month
FROM facilities;
```

Months-on-book measures elapsed month boundaries between origination and observation. Exact syntax differs by SQL engine, so keep the business definition stable even when the function changes. In a dialect with `DATEDIFF` by month:

```sql
SELECT
    s.account_id,
    s.reporting_date,
    f.origination_date,
    DATEDIFF(month, f.origination_date, s.reporting_date) AS mob
FROM loan_snapshots s
JOIN loan_accounts a ON a.account_id = s.account_id
JOIN facilities f ON f.facility_id = a.facility_id
WHERE s.reporting_date = '2026-08-31';
```

Date arithmetic breaks when strings are mistaken for dates, timestamps are compared to inclusive end dates, or day differences are divided by 30 and called months. A calendar month is not a fixed number of days.

### Never confuse [Vintage] and [Reporting period]

**Vintage** - the origination cohort to which the facility belongs.

---

**Reporting period** - the observation period in which balance or delinquency is measured.

### Permanent rule

Express reporting periods with explicit lower and upper boundaries, and define MOB as a calendar-period concept rather than an approximate day count.

### Permanent trap

Using `BETWEEN '2026-08-01' AND '2026-08-31'` on a timestamp and assuming every 31 August record is included. Values after midnight can be missed depending on the stored type and literal conversion.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta. Date calculations may differ by SQL engine, but not by regulator.
