### Core mechanism

Move a score cutoff from strict to permissive. At each cutoff compute the true-positive rate and false-positive rate, then trace the ROC curve. AUC is the area under that curve and can also be interpreted as the probability that a randomly selected default receives a riskier model score than a randomly selected non-default, with ties handled by convention.

| Metric | Measures | Does not measure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ROC curve | Sensitivity versus false-positive rate across cutoffs | Probability accuracy |
| AUC | Overall ranking discrimination across cutoffs | Calibration level |
| Gini | Linear rescaling of AUC | Economic value at one operating cutoff |

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Untouched predictions · Binary outcomes · Score ordering
This:       ROC · AUC · Gini discrimination
Downstream: KS · Threshold metrics · Challenger comparison
```

### Formula

```text
True-positive rate = true defaults identified / all actual defaults
False-positive rate = non-defaults classified as default / all actual non-defaults
AUC = area under the ROC curve
Gini = 2 x AUC - 1
```

### Permanent rule

Any strictly monotonic transformation of a score preserves ranking and therefore preserves AUC, even if the probability scale changes completely.

### Never confuse [Discrimination] and [Calibration]

**Discrimination** - whether riskier outcomes are ranked ahead of safer outcomes.

---

**Calibration** - whether predicted probability levels match observed event frequencies.

### Permanent trap

Claiming a high AUC proves predicted PDs are accurate. The same ranking can be paired with probabilities that are systematically too high or too low.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta. ROC, AUC and Gini mathematics are universal; MRM owns governance use of the resulting numbers.

<Recall
  q="Why does multiplying every model score by a positive constant leave AUC unchanged?"
  a="The transformation preserves the ordering of every default and non-default pair, and AUC depends on ranking rather than the score scale."
/>

<Recall
  q="What does AUC measure under the pairwise interpretation?"
  a="It is the chance that a randomly selected default is assigned a riskier score than a randomly selected non-default, subject to the tie convention."
/>

<Recall
  q="Can a model have strong AUC and poor calibration?"
  a="Yes. It can rank borrowers correctly while assigning probability levels that are consistently too high, too low or otherwise distorted."
