### Core mechanism

| Distortion | Lending example | Direction of risk bias can be |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Selection bias | Only approved applicants have repayment outcomes | Usually makes applicant risk look safer than the full application pool |
| Survivorship bias | Only currently active accounts are analyzed | Drops repaid, defaulted or exited paths |
| Left truncation | Portfolio extract begins after some loans were originated | Early failures may never enter the observed sample |
| Attrition bias | Customers leaving the bank disappear from later snapshots | Remaining customers may differ systematically |
| Acquisition bias | Purchased portfolio lacks original underwriting fields | Missingness depends on source book |

```text
Original application or origination cohort
    ↓
Approval and booking filter
    ↓
Performance and exit events
    ↓
Portfolio snapshot date
    ↓
Only surviving observable records remain
```

The critical issue is conditional observation. If inclusion depends on borrower risk or a downstream outcome, then sample averages and fitted coefficients describe the selected population, not automatically the population that generated the original applications.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Sampling frame · Label construction · Portfolio history
This:       Selection · Survivorship · Truncation
Downstream: Reject inference · Vintage analysis · Generalisation testing
```

### Formula

```text
Observed event rate = observed events / observed eligible cases
```

### Permanent rule

Define cohorts from an entry event such as application or origination, then follow all permitted exits. Do not define the cohort from who happens to remain visible later.

### Never confuse [Selection bias] and [Survivorship bias]

**Selection bias** - systematic inclusion or exclusion at any stage of sample formation.

---

**Survivorship bias** - the special case where cases are visible because they survived long enough to remain in the observed set.

### Permanent trap

Using a current portfolio snapshot to estimate lifetime borrower behavior without reconstructing closed and failed accounts. The snapshot conditions on survival and therefore changes the population being measured.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta. These are statistical consequences of conditional observation, not jurisdiction-specific rules.

<Recall
  q="Why can an active-account extract understate risk even if every active record is correct?"
  a="Accounts that defaulted, prepaid or exited may be missing, so the extract represents survivors rather than the original lending cohort."
/>

<Recall
  q="What distinguishes left truncation from ordinary right-censoring?"
  a="Left truncation means some cases enter observation only after surviving an earlier period; right-censoring means observation begins correctly but ends before the final outcome is known."
/>
