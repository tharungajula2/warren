Principles 12–14 instruct how supervisors should review the data framework and require remediation if the bank’s practices are deficient. They encourage cross-border coordination for global groups, and detail potential supervisory measures (e.g. requiring capital add-ons for data weaknesses). In effect, they push banks to treat risk data as a first-class issue. Recent Basel progress reports note that almost a decade after BCBS 239’s publication, no major bank is fully compliant. The press release observes “banks are at different stages of alignment” and “additional work is required at all banks to attain or sustain full compliance”. Supervisors worldwide (Fed, ECB, PRA, RBI, etc.) have been pressing banks via Stress Test frameworks and Pillar 2 dialogues to close gaps.

A critical point: BCBS 239 itself has no formal legal force (it relies on member commitments). In the UK, the PRA explicitly incorporates its expectations into SREP for major banks. The ECB can impose Pillar 2 guidance for data issues, and the Fed expects high-quality data for its FR Y-14 stress tests. Thus, compliance is enforced locally. Globally, the common narrative is that risk data deficiencies have been a repeat finding in audits and reviews. The **permanent expectation** is that any material data issue must be escalated and fixed, and supervisors will hold senior management accountable.

### Permanent rule

BCBS 239 principles must be embedded institution-wide and are enforced by national regulators. Formal remediation plans are expected when issues are found.

### Permanent trap

Treating audit comments on data as mere “to-dos” rather than core risk-control failures. Senior management must lead remediation.

### Never confuse

**BCBS 239** — A supervisory standard, not a law.
---
**Accounting standard** — (e.g. IFRS) Rules for financial statements. BCBS 239 is about data systems, not accounting treatment.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** All major jurisdictions (UK PRA, ECB, Fed, RBI etc.) regard BCBS 239 outcomes as required for SIBs, though enforcement mechanisms vary.
