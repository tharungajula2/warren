For each CDE or data domain, an owner is formally assigned (often at executive or business-unit level). The owner’s responsibilities include approving definitions and prioritizing data initiatives. A data steward (usually in risk or finance department) is then responsible for monitoring data quality, investigating issues, and coordinating fixes with IT and business teams. The Reserve Bank recommends clarifying these roles: assigning ownership of key data elements and requiring data stewards to update the data governance committee.

In practice, confusion between these roles is a trap. If nobody is accountable, issues languish; if too many claim ownership, governance muddles. Clear role definitions – documented in a data governance policy – are essential. For example, the data owner might be the Head of Credit Risk for loan data, while the data steward is a risk analyst reporting to them. Together they ensure questions like “Who decides loan classification rules?” have an answer.

### Permanent rule

Every critical data element must have a single business owner assigned (with ultimate accountability) and one or more stewards (with operational responsibilities).

### Permanent trap

Assigning ownership to IT or multiple owners by default. Ownership should reside in the business line that “owns” the risk.

### Never confuse

**Data owner** — Business executive responsible for a domain or data set (e.g. loans, market data). Accountable for policies.

---

**Data steward** — Practitioner who enforces data standards and quality, reports to the owner.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Defining data ownership and stewardship is a standard governance practice across banks globally.
