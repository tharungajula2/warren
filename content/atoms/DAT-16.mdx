### Core mechanism

```text
Define two populations and common cut-off
    ↓
Normalise known scope and timing differences
    ↓
Compare records, balances or aggregates
    ↓
Classify breaks
    ↓
Expected difference → evidence and explain
Unexplained difference → investigate and remediate
    ↓
Clear, age or escalate outstanding breaks
```

| Reconciliation | Typical comparison | What it can reveal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Front-to-back | Trading or origination records vs operations/accounting records | Missing, duplicated or mismapped transactions |
| Risk-to-finance | Risk exposure or position totals vs finance/accounting totals | Scope, valuation, timing or transformation differences |
| Source-to-report | Authoritative source population vs reporting extract | ETL, mapping, filter or aggregation defects |
| Period-on-period | Current return vs prior period with explained movement | Unexpected discontinuity or reporting drift |

A difference is not automatically an error. Accounting and risk views can legitimately use different scope or measurement; the control requirement is that differences are defined, quantified and explainable.

### Never confuse [Reconciliation break] and [Data error]

**Reconciliation break** - an observed difference between populations that requires explanation.

---

**Data error** - a confirmed defect. Some breaks are expected timing, scope or measurement differences and should be evidenced rather than "fixed".

### Permanent rule

A reconciliation is complete only when material breaks are explained, owned and dispositioned, not when the comparison file has run.

### Permanent trap

Netting breaks to zero. Equal and opposite errors can make totals agree while individual records remain wrong.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Reconciliation mechanics are universal; particular returns may prescribe additional validations.
