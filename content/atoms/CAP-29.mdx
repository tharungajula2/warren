The first March proposal is aimed primarily at the largest, internationally active banking organisations. The agencies describe it as completing US implementation of final Basel III components while improving risk sensitivity and replacing the existing dual-stack calculation for covered firms with a single risk-based capital calculation if adopted.

The second proposal generally applies more broadly and revises the standardised capital treatment of traditional lending and other exposures. The third, issued by the Federal Reserve, revises the method for measuring systemic risk for the GSIB surcharge. Comments on the proposals were due by 18 June 2026. **A proposal does not change the binding capital rule merely because its intended implementation is described in detail.**

The US architecture must also be separated from the European combined-buffer/MDA model. Since 2020 the Federal Reserve has integrated supervisory stress testing with non-stress capital requirements through the **stress capital buffer**. The buffer is forward-looking and firm-specific because supervisory stress results affect the applicable capital requirement for covered large banks.

For the 2026 cycle, the Federal Reserve decided to maintain then-current stress capital buffer requirements until 2027 while it considers public feedback on supervisory stress-test models. Thus “US Basel finalisation proposal” and “current US SCB requirement” are separate objects.

### Permanent rule

A US notice of proposed rulemaking remains non-binding until the agencies complete the legally required rulemaking process and issue a final rule with an effective date.

### Permanent trap

Teaching the March 2026 reproposal as “the US Basel rule” converts an unsettled proposal into law and can produce the wrong capital calculation.

### Never confuse

**March 2026 capital reproposal** — proposed revisions to the US regulatory capital framework.

---

**Stress capital buffer** — existing Federal Reserve large-bank capital mechanism tied to supervisory stress testing.

### Jurisdiction variants

**US** — The 19 March 2026 package remains a proposal as at 27 August 2026; current SCB requirements were maintained until 2027 while stress-test-model feedback is considered. [Federal Reserve 19 March 2026 joint release; Federal Reserve 4 February 2026 stress-test release]
