The exercise begins with **contractual cash flows** and then replaces normal assumptions with stressed behaviour. Retail deposits may run faster, wholesale funding may cease to roll, committed facilities may be drawn, secured-funding haircuts may rise, derivatives may require collateral, asset sales may incur discounts and some contingent funding sources may disappear.

Scenarios should separate **idiosyncratic stress**, where confidence in the bank deteriorates; **market-wide stress**, where funding and asset markets are disrupted generally; and a **combined stress**, where both happen together. Basel liquidity principles expressly call for severe stress scenarios and contingency funding plans linked to the resulting liquidity needs.

For each day or time band, treasury calculates stressed inflows, outflows and available liquidity. Cumulative net outflow is compared with the liquidity buffer and other credible counterbalancing capacity. The **survival horizon** is the point at which available resources cease to cover cumulative needs under the assumptions.

The word “credible” matters. A funding source should not be counted merely because it exists in normal markets. Monetisation capacity must consider haircuts, settlement timing, operational readiness, legal-entity location and whether several actions compete for the same collateral. Management actions can be incorporated only where they are sufficiently executable within the stress horizon.

Stress testing is therefore more informative than a single ratio. It shows the path to failure: which day, currency, entity or funding source causes the breach. It can also expose cliffs—a stable profile for ten days followed by a large wholesale maturity on day eleven.

### Permanent rule

Survival horizon is scenario-dependent; there is no meaningful survival-horizon number without stated assumptions.

### Permanent trap

Counting an asset sale, secured borrowing and central-bank borrowing simultaneously when all three rely on the same collateral.

### Never confuse

**LCR horizon** — a standardised 30-calendar-day regulatory stress window.

---

**Survival horizon** — the institution-specific time until liquidity resources are exhausted in a defined stress.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Supervisory specifications vary, but institution-specific cash-flow stress and survival analysis are common liquidity-risk tools.
