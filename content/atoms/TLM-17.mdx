### Core mechanism

| Gate | What must hold before value counts as usable liquidity |
| :--- | :--- |
| Availability | eligible · unencumbered · legally available |
| Execution | positioned/transferable · settlement arrives in time |
| Value | credible market/repo depth · manageable concentration · haircut applied |
| Location | cash emerges in required currency and legal entity |

Fail any gate → nominal value overstates liquidity.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Funding/market liquidity · structural FX
This:       Inventory of usable liquidity resources
Downstream: Liquidity ladder · LCR · stress · collateral
```

### Formula

```text
Usable liquidity value ≈ market value × (1 − applicable haircut)

Market value ↑ → potential liquidity ↑, subject to eligibility and caps.

Haircut ↑ → usable liquidity ↓.
```

### Permanent rule

Liquidity value is what can actually become spendable cash in the required place and currency within the horizon.

### Never confuse [HQLA] and [All monetisable assets]

**HQLA** — assets meeting applicable LCR eligibility and composition rules.

---

**All monetisable assets** — broader assets that may generate cash but with larger haircuts, delays or uncertainty.

### Permanent trap

Counting the same security at full value in several funding channels. Once pledged, sold or committed, residual capacity changes.

### Jurisdiction variants

#### IN (RBI)
Specified government securities within mandatory SLR can count as Level 1 HQLA through MSF/FALLCR. **As at August 2026: 2% of NDTL through MSF + 16% through FALLCR = 18% total carve-out.** RBI’s revised government-security haircut treatment has been in force since **1 April 2026**.

#### UK (PRA)
UK firms follow PRA liquidity/LCR rules plus Pillar 2 liquidity assessment. No economic delta.

#### EU
EU HQLA eligibility and composition follow CRR and Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/61, as amended.

#### US
US LCR defines HQLA for covered firms; applicability is tailored rather than universal.
