### Core mechanism

| Layer | Credit meaning | Failure mode |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Target population | Borrowers or facilities about which an inference is required | Defined too broadly to match actual deployment |
| Sampling frame | Cases the bank could observe and retrieve | Excludes rejects, closed accounts, acquired books or older vintages |
| Realised sample | Records surviving filters, joins and cleaning | Further loss through missing data or eligibility rules |
| Deployment population | Cases scored after implementation | Mix shifts away from development data |

Sampling error is random variation from observing only part of the frame. Sampling bias is systematic distortion because some kinds of borrowers are more or less likely to appear. More data reduces sampling error but does not repair a biased frame.

```text
Target population
    ↓
Observable lending frame
    ↓
Extracted and cleaned sample
    ↓
Estimated relationship
    ↓
Applied to deployment population
```

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Lending scope · Product rules · Data availability
This:       Population · Sampling frame · Realised sample
Downstream: Bias analysis · Outcome construction · Model development
```

### Formula

```text
Sample mean = (sum of observed values) / n
Sampling error = sample estimate - population quantity
```

### Permanent rule

Precision cannot rescue representativeness. A precisely estimated quantity from the wrong lending population is still the wrong quantity.

### Never confuse [Sampling error] and [Sampling bias]

**Sampling error** - random difference caused by observing a finite sample.

---

**Sampling bias** - systematic difference caused by who can enter or remain in the sample.

### Permanent trap

Treating the bank's available dataset as the population. Availability is a property of the data estate, not proof that the observed borrowers represent the cases the model will score.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta. Population, sampling-frame and bias concepts are mathematical and do not change by regulator.

<Recall
  q="Why can a million loan records still produce a biased estimate?"
  a="Sample size reduces random noise, but it does not restore borrowers systematically excluded by approval, retention or extraction rules."
/>

<Recall
  q="What is the practical difference between a target population and a sampling frame?"
  a="The target population is where the inference is meant to apply; the sampling frame is the subset the lender can actually observe and sample from."
/>

<Recall
  q="What should be checked before any model metric is interpreted?"
  a="Check whether the realised development sample and the intended deployment population are aligned in product, borrower mix, time and observation rules."
