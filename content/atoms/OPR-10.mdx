Under Basel II (and prior Basel I transitional norms), simple pillar 1 methods existed before SMA.  **BIA** is the simplest: it sets the operational risk capital charge equal to *α* × (average annual gross income), with α=15%.  For example, if a bank’s average gross income was €100m, BIA charge = 0.15×100m = €15m. (Years with negative or zero income are dropped.)

**Standardised Approach (TSA)** refines this by business line. Basel defined 8 lines (e.g. retail, corporate finance, trading, etc.).  Each line’s gross income is multiplied by a line-specific factor (β) – typically 12%-18% depending on line.  For example, corporate finance β=18%, retail banking β=12%. The capital charge = average over 3 years of sum(β_i × GI_i).  Each negative income can offset positive in the same year.

These approaches were “standardised” because any bank could calculate them from simple P&amp;L data. They were retired by 2022 under Basel 3.1, but some regimes still use BIA/TSA for domestic banks or under-transition.

<Flow>
+------------------------+
| Basic Indicator (BIA)  |
| Charge = 15% × avg GI  |
+------------------------+
           vs
+-------------------------------+
| Standardised (TSA/ASA)        |
| Split into 8 lines with betas |
| Charge = avg over 3y of Σ(β_i×GI_i) |
+-------------------------------+
</Flow>

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
If using TSA, the 8 business lines and associated beta factors (12–18%) must be applied.  National regulators may specify these in detail (EBA or local law).  Board approval and risk framework standards are required even under TSA.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Do not assume these approaches reflect current risk (they were proxies).  Under TSA, a bank with complex products might hold too little capital.  Also, don’t confuse “Standardised” with the newer “SMA” – they are completely different.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Basic Indicator (BIA)" b="Standardised (TSA)">
**Basic Indicator (BIA)** — capital = α×(bank’s gross income).
---
**Standardised (TSA)** — capital = weighted sum of each business line’s GI (β factors).
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** These formulas were global under Basel II. However, note:
- **UK/EU (pre-Basel3.1):** SMEs or retail banks often used TSA with same factors.
- **India:** RBI followed Basel II on paper; BIA (15%) and TSA lines were defined in Master Directions.
- **US:** US banking regulators allowed these approaches earlier but also introduced standardized (ASA) that resembled TSA.
</Rule>
