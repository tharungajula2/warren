### Core mechanism

```text
Choose credit outcome and forecast horizon
    ↓
Align macro variables available at each forecast date
    ↓
Choose contemporaneous and lagged relationships
    ↓
Fit using history available at that origin
    ↓
Forecast next period or horizon
    ↓
Advance origin and repeat
    ↓
Compare errors with simple baseline
```

| Design issue | Credit consequence |
| :--- | :--- |
| Publication lag | Revised macro data may not have been known at forecast time |
| Lag choice | Borrower stress may respond months after macro movement |
| Regime dependence | Relationship estimated in benign years may change in recession |
| Scenario extrapolation | Stress values may lie outside historical support |
| Baseline comparison | Complex model may not beat persistence or seasonal naive forecasts |

Rolling-origin testing preserves chronology: each forecast is generated using only information that would have existed at that historical forecast date.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Credit time series · OOT design · Macro drivers
This:       Macro conditioning · Lag alignment · Rolling-origin evaluation
Downstream: Forecast error · Scenario sensitivity · ECL model inputs
```

### Formula

```text
Forecast error at time t = actual at t - forecast made for t
RMSE = square root of [ sum over forecast errors of error squared / number of forecasts ]
MAE = sum over forecasts of absolute error / number of forecasts
```

### Permanent rule

A historical macro model must be evaluated using the information set that was actually available at each historical forecast origin.

### Never confuse [Scenario conditioning] and [Forecast accuracy]

**Scenario conditioning** - model response given an assumed macro path.

---

**Forecast accuracy** - how close predictions made from information available at the time came to subsequently observed outcomes.

### Permanent trap

Backtesting with revised macroeconomic series that were unavailable when the historical forecast would have been made. This creates look-ahead information even when credit outcomes themselves are correctly time-split.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta. The statistical mechanics do not vary; accounting use of forward-looking information belongs to IMP.
