### Core mechanism

| Situation | Accounting effect | Cash effect at that moment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Interest earned but not yet received | Income and an asset/carrying-amount effect can arise | None yet |
| Expense incurred but not yet paid | Expense and liability can arise | None yet |
| Cash paid before service is consumed | Asset/prepayment can arise | Cash decreases now |
| Borrowing received | Cash and liability increase | Cash increases without profit |
| Non-cash impairment recognised | Expense / carrying-value effect | No equal current cash payment |

Accrual accounting does not mean “book whatever is expected”. Recognition and measurement must still follow the applicable accounting framework.

### Flow

```text
Economic event
    ↓
Recognition criteria met?
    ↓
Accounting entry recorded
    ↓
Cash may occur earlier · same time · later
    ↓
Cash-flow statement records actual cash timing
```

### Permanent rule

The absence of a cash movement does not prove that no income, expense, asset, or liability should be recognised.

### Never confuse [Accrual] and [Cash settlement]

**Accrual** — accounting recognition based on the economic event and applicable recognition rules.

---

**Cash settlement** — transfer of cash; its date can differ from the recognition date.

### Permanent trap

Rejecting a valid non-cash expense because “nothing has been paid yet”. Accounting can recognise deterioration or consumption before the related cash consequence is realised.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Specific recognition rules differ between accounting frameworks, but the distinction between accrual recognition and cash timing is universal.
