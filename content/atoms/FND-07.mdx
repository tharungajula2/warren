A useful first pass starts with **assets**. Cash and central-bank balances provide settlement capacity. Securities can provide income, liquidity or investment exposure depending on their nature and classification. Loans represent claims on borrowers and are commonly a major source of interest income and credit risk. Other financial and non-financial assets complete the asset side.

Next read **liabilities** as funding and payment obligations. Customer deposits can differ substantially in stability, interest rate and maturity. Wholesale borrowings are funds obtained from financial markets or institutional counterparties. Other liabilities can include settlement obligations, derivatives and accrued expenses. The maturity and behavioural characteristics of liabilities matter because an asset may remain economically sound while funding against it becomes unavailable.

Then read **equity** and, separately where relevant, prudential capital disclosures. Equity is the accounting residual. Regulatory capital applies regulatory eligibility, deductions and other adjustments and therefore should not be assumed to equal book equity.

A bank balance sheet should also be read by **quality**, not merely size. Two loan portfolios with equal carrying amounts may have different credit quality. Two security portfolios may have different market sensitivity or liquidity. Two deposit bases of equal size may behave differently under stress. Balance-sheet analysis therefore combines amounts with information about maturity, concentration, valuation, impairment and contractual terms.

This differs from the intuitive reading of many non-financial corporates. Inventory and physical plant may dominate a corporate asset side, while bank assets are heavily financial claims. Debt is funding for both, but customer deposits are distinctive because they simultaneously represent funding to the bank and money-like assets to customers.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Never assess a bank balance sheet from totals alone; composition, quality, maturity and funding structure determine what the totals mean.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Treating a large deposit balance as evidence of a large liquid-asset buffer. Deposits are funding liabilities, not liquid assets.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Book equity" b="Regulatory capital">
**Book equity** — the accounting residual of assets minus liabilities.
---
**Regulatory capital** — capital recognised under prudential rules after eligibility criteria and adjustments; it can differ from book equity.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Reporting templates differ, but the analytical sequence of assets, funding, residual loss absorption and balance-sheet quality is jurisdiction-neutral.
</Rule>
