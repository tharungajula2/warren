Under the gatekeeper model, banks (and other regulated firms) have primary responsibility for spotting and reporting financial crime.  Laws typically mandate that banks implement AML programs – including KYC checks, transaction monitoring, and reporting – so that the private sector polices the system.  For example, RBI’s AML guidelines explicitly instruct banks to establish AML policies (KYC, STR reporting, etc.) with Board approval. This means if a terrorist or money launderer tries to move money, the bank’s controls should catch it first. Criminals must insert illicit money into banks to spend it, so banks are critical checkpoints.

If suspicious, banks escalate by filing SARs/STRs with authorities (see Atom 15).  Senior management appoints a Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO) or compliance officer who ensures internal policies match regulations.  Regulators (like RBI, FCA, FinCEN) then audit and examine banks’ AML controls for adequacy. Non-bank institutions (casinos, real estate, fintech) also play gatekeeper roles where they handle large transactions.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Always assume customers are subject to AML rules. No matter how lucrative, a customer or transaction that fails KYC or looks unusual must be vetted or reported. Banks *must* file an STR/SAR if money laundering is suspected.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Thinking AML is optional or “just a paperwork exercise.” In reality, failing AML duties (like ignoring a big cash deposit warning sign) can lead to penalties and even criminal charges for the bank and its officers.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Gatekeeper role (banks)" b="Regulator/Supervisor">
**Gatekeeper role (banks)** — duty to enforce AML by monitoring and reporting.
---
**Regulator/Supervisor** — government agencies (RBI, FinCEN, FCA) that write rules and enforce them. Banks are *enforcers*, not regulators.
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="IN">
— Under India’s PMLA, banks are explicitly required to follow RBI’s AML Master Directions (CIP, STRs, MLRO).
</Delta>

<Delta j="UK">
— The Money Laundering Regulations impose KYC/AML duties on “regulated institutions” (including banks); the FCA supervises banks’ AML programs.
</Delta>

<Delta j="EU">
— Each Member State’s law (per AML Directives) tasks banks/financial institutions with CDD and STR reporting. The EBA provides joint guidance, but local regulators enforce compliance.
</Delta>

<Delta j="US">
— US banks must comply with BSA (e.g. CIP Rule, CTR/SAR filing) under FinCEN oversight. Bank regulators (OCC, Fed, FDIC) examine AML controls, and FinCEN has broad enforcement powers.
</Delta>
