Conceptually:

`LCR = stock of HQLA / total net cash outflows over 30 days`

Under the Basel architecture, the ratio is calibrated around a 30-calendar-day stress horizon and a 100% minimum in ordinary conditions. Net outflows are not simply contractual maturities. Liability and off-balance-sheet categories receive regulatory **run-off rates** representing assumed stressed withdrawals or draws, while eligible inflows receive prescribed recognition subject to limits.

The question is therefore: after applying the rulebook’s stress assumptions, can the recognised liquid-asset stock cover the resulting net cash requirement for 30 days?

A stable retail deposit may receive a lower assumed run-off than a less stable or certain wholesale balance because their stressed behaviour is assumed to differ. An undrawn commitment can generate a contingent outflow even though no cash is owed today. Contractual receivables can generate inflows, but the framework prevents a bank from treating unlimited expected inflows as a substitute for a liquidity buffer.

LCR is intentionally stylised. An actual crisis can last longer than 30 days, run-off rates can exceed regulatory assumptions, liquidity can be trapped by currency or legal entity and intraday settlement demands can arise even when the end-of-day LCR looks comfortable. Basel explicitly treats intraday liquidity separately from the original LCR calibration.

India’s current framework follows the Basel architecture but has local calibrations. RBI’s April 2025 amendments, effective 1 April 2026, add 2.5 percentage points to run-off rates for internet/mobile-enabled retail deposits, producing 7.5% for qualifying stable balances and 12.5% for less-stable balances, and revise government-security HQLA valuation.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
LCR measures standardised 30-day stressed liquidity coverage, not long-term funding stability.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Reading a 110% LCR as “the bank can survive exactly 33 days”; the ratio is based on prescribed stress assumptions, not a linear survival clock.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="LCR" b="Survival horizon">
**LCR** — HQLA relative to standardised net stressed cash outflows over 30 days.
---
**Survival horizon** — the modelled time until available liquidity is exhausted under a particular internal stress scenario.
</NeverConfuse>
