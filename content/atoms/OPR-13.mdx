Two banks with the same size (BI/BIC) might hold different operational risk capital if their loss histories differ.  Under SMA, capital = BIC×ILM. If Bank A had higher losses over the last 10 years, its LC/BIC ratio is bigger, yielding a larger ILM (and thus capital) than Bank B.  For instance, riskhub showed that raising average loss from €80m to €150m with the same BIC increased ILM from 0.94 to 1.13, raising capital by 21%.  Thus, historical operational performance influences capital.

Critically, jurisdictions can blunt this disparity: Basel lets national regulators set ILM=1 if desired.  The UK PRA decided to neutralize ILM entirely.  As a result, two UK banks of equal size will hold identical capital regardless of their loss experience.  But in regimes that keep ILM active (or in Pillar 2), differences persist. So, a big US bank still using an AMA-like model or a European bank implementing Basel SMA with ILM could end up with capital quite different from a UK peer.

<Flow>
Same BIC  -&gt; Bank A LC high -&gt; ILM&gt;1 -&gt; Higher ORC
           Bank B LC low  -&gt; ILM&lt;1 -&gt; Lower ORC
</Flow>

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Always check the local rule on ILM.  If ILM=1 is mandated (as in UK), capital is just BIC. Otherwise, ensure the ILM (and LC calculation) is correctly applied.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
The mistake is to assume size alone dictates capital.  Even small banks (low BI) with a bad loss track record can have high capital under SMA.  And believing UK banks hold higher capital when actually PRA’s ILM=1 means only BI matters.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**BIC (size-based capital)** vs **LC (loss component)**.  Two banks could have identical BIC, but if one has LC much bigger, its ILM will be higher, leading to higher final capital.
</NeverConfuse>
