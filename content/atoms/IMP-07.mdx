This is one of the most persistent IFRS 9 misunderstandings. Stage 1 does **not** estimate only cash shortfalls occurring during the next year. IFRS 9 defines 12-month ECL as the portion of lifetime expected credit losses representing losses resulting from default events that are possible within twelve months after the reporting date.

Consider a five-year loan. A default could occur nine months from today, followed by restructuring and collateral recovery over the next three years. If that default event lies within the relevant twelve-month default horizon, the ECL calculation includes the full discounted loss consequences of that default, including cash shortfalls occurring after month twelve. Conversely, a default expected only in year four is outside Stage 1’s 12-month default-event horizon even though it may enter lifetime ECL after SICR.

This also means 12-month ECL is not simply “lifetime ECL for loans expected to default within a year”. Every exposure can contribute through the probability that a default event occurs in the next twelve months, multiplied by the loss consequences if it occurs. IFRS 9’s application guidance explicitly distinguishes these concepts.

For an instrument with less than twelve months remaining, the relevant period cannot exceed the remaining exposure period. The term “12-month” therefore describes the maximum default-event horizon for Stage 1, not a requirement to manufacture risk beyond contractual exposure.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
“Twelve months” limits the **default-event window**, not the timing of subsequent cash shortfalls caused by those defaults.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Truncating recoveries and losses at month twelve systematically misstates Stage 1 ECL on instruments whose post-default workout extends beyond a year.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="12-month ECL" b="Twelve months of cash loss">
**12-month ECL** — lifetime loss consequences of defaults possible during the next twelve months.

---
**Twelve months of cash loss** — cash shortfalls physically occurring during the next year; this is not the IFRS 9 definition.
</NeverConfuse>

<Recall
  q="Does 12-month ECL ignore losses occurring after month twelve?"
  a="No, if those losses result from a default event possible within the twelve-month default horizon."
/>

<Recall
  q="What does the twelve-month horizon constrain?"
  a="The set of possible default events included, not the post-default loss timeline."
/>
