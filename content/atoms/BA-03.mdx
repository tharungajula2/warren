A bank change program involves many stakeholders.  **Business owners** (line-of-business managers, product owners) define requirements. **Risk management** teams (credit, market, operational risk) must approve changes affecting risk profile. **Finance** stakeholders (finance, accounting) need to assess cost, budget impact, and reporting changes. **Technology/IT** teams design and build the solution.  **Data owners** or **model owners** must be involved when data or risk models change, to align logic and validation.  **Internal Audit** should be aware early – auditors will later review that controls and procedures were properly implemented. Finally, the **Regulator** (or compliance team, if working with external regulators) often signs off on regulatory changes or reviews evidence to ensure requirements are met.

A BA bridges all these groups, collecting input and keeping them aligned.  For example, a new risk-reporting requirement needs review by business (for accuracy), risk (for methodology), finance (for impact on reports), IT (for system changes), and model validation (for calculations).  Missing a key stakeholder can derail a project; for instance, if IT or audit are not included until late, critical requirements (like data lineage or evidence logs) might be missed.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Include all relevant stakeholders early and often. Document each group’s inputs and approvals.  If a group (e.g. internal audit, model validation) is required to sign off on compliance, involve them from the start to avoid rework.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Assuming one person can “speak for” multiple stakeholder groups or ignoring a group’s needs.  A common mistake is treating the business as the only stakeholder and failing to involve risk or audit until late in the project.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Internal Audit" b="Regulator">
**Internal Audit** — the bank’s own independent check on controls and procedures (internal governance function).
---
**Regulator** — the external authority enforcing compliance.
Both review the project, but Internal Audit is part of the bank’s governance, whereas the Regulator is an external enforcer.
</NeverConfuse>
