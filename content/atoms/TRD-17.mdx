Optionality is broader than a listed option. Any contract that gives one party a meaningful right to change timing, quantity or price contingent on future conditions contains optionality. A callable bond gives the issuer a right to redeem early. A puttable bond gives the investor a right to sell back. A mortgage borrower may have a prepayment option. A swap may contain cancellation rights, caps, floors or extension features.

The owner of an option benefits from discretion: exercise when favourable, decline when unfavourable. The other party is short that discretion. This asymmetry changes valuation because future cash flows are no longer fixed. Expected exercise depends on market levels, volatility, time, transaction costs and behavioural or contractual constraints.

Embedded optionality can reverse otherwise simple risk. A fixed-rate bond without options usually rises when rates fall. A callable bond may stop rising as quickly once refinancing becomes attractive to the issuer. The investor is effectively short a call on the bond, so positive duration alone no longer tells the whole story.

Optionality also appears operationally. A cancellation right may require notice by a specific cut-off. A barrier option may activate or terminate when a price is observed under defined market-source rules. A wrong event date, fixing source or exercise status can therefore produce a valuation and settlement break even when the model is correct.

For analysis, identify four elements: who owns the choice, what action can be taken, when it can be taken, and what market state makes exercise valuable. Then ask how that choice changes expected cash flows and sensitivities.

The durable insight is that products with the same headline maturity, coupon or notional can carry radically different risks if optionality differs. The option owner’s flexibility is an economic asset; somebody else is paying for it.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
If future cash flows can change because one party has discretion, model the option before trusting linear risk measures.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Treating a callable or prepayable instrument as a plain fixed-cash-flow bond merely because its coupon is fixed.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Explicit option" b="Embedded option">
**Explicit option** — standalone contract whose main purpose is the exercise right.

---

**Embedded option** — exercise right contained inside another instrument or financing contract.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.**
</Rule>
