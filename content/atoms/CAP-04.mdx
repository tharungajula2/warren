### Core mechanism

CET1 begins with qualifying common equity instruments, associated share premium, retained earnings and other eligible disclosed reserves, then applies prudential criteria and adjustments.

| CET1 property | Why it matters |
| :--- | :--- |
| Perpetual | No maturity that forces repayment |
| Most subordinated claim | Losses reach common equity before senior creditors |
| Distributions discretionary | Bank is not forced to pay a coupon or dividend |
| Fully available for loss absorption | Capital can fall as losses are recognised |
| Clear issue and repayment rules | Prevents capital disappearing through redemption |

A common share can fail regulatory eligibility if its terms weaken permanence or loss absorption. Conversely, an accounting equity line is not automatically CET1 merely because it sits within shareholders' equity.

### Permanent rule

CET1 is defined by prudential eligibility and loss-absorption characteristics, not by the accounting label "equity".

### Never confuse [Common equity] and [CET1]

**Common equity** — an accounting or corporate-law description of ownership interests.

---

**CET1** — the subset of highest-quality capital recognised after Basel eligibility criteria and regulatory adjustments.

### Permanent trap

Using total shareholders' equity as the CET1 numerator without checking eligibility, filters and deductions can overstate prudential capital.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta in the core Basel characteristics. National company law and local transposition determine the exact legal instruments that can meet those characteristics.
