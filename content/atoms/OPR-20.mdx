Personal data (customer information) is heavily regulated.  Rules like the EU’s GDPR (and UK GDPR) require banks to collect data lawfully (usually with customer consent or legitimate interest), use it only for specified purposes, protect it securely, and honor customer rights (access, correction, erasure). Infringements (e.g. data breaches, misuse) incur steep penalties – up to €20M or 4% of global turnover. Similarly, India’s new DPDP Act (effective 2023) enforces data fiduciary principles: consent, minimization, data security, and data subject rights. Violations can lead to heavy fines (up to ₹250 crores, ~$30M).  

Banks must therefore govern privacy at an enterprise level: assigning a Data Protection Officer (DPO), maintaining privacy notices, and integrating “privacy by design” into processes. While technical (encryption, access controls) is critical, oversight and policies are also key. For example, GDPR requires breach notification within 72 hours.  Compliance failures can lead to both regulatory fines and reputational/legal losses.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Handle personal data only as allowed by law: get valid consent or legal basis, limit access, and implement strong security (encryption, etc.).  Always comply with notification rules for breaches.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Don’t assume data security (e.g. encryption) alone solves privacy.  Privacy obligations also demand proper data usage (transparency to customers, data minimization).  Ignoring the “legal basis” or consent aspect is a frequent oversight.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Data Privacy" b="Cybersecurity">
**Data Privacy** — legal rights and rules about how personal data is used (e.g. GDPR consent).  
---  
**Cybersecurity** — technical measures to protect data (e.g. firewalls, anti-malware).
</NeverConfuse>

**EU/UK** — GDPR (and UK DPA 2018) require strict data protection principles and enforce through DPIAs and high fines.

<Delta j="IN">
— The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP) applies broadly; banks must follow its consent, notice, and security norms. Penalties in DPDP Act: up to ₹250 Crore.
</Delta>

<Delta j="US">
— No unified federal banking privacy law (GLBA exists for financial privacy, HIPAA for health, etc.), but some states (e.g. California) have robust laws.
</Delta>
