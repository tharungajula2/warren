IFRS 9 requires expected credit losses to reflect an **unbiased and probability-weighted amount** determined by evaluating a range of possible outcomes. The aim is neither a worst-case provision nor a best-case provision. Where credit losses can arise in some possible outcomes but not others, the probability of both loss and no-loss outcomes matters even when the most likely single outcome would show no loss.

Suppose three internally coherent macroeconomic scenarios produce ECL estimates of 40, 100 and 250, with probabilities of 20%, 60% and 20%. Probability-weighted ECL is `0.20×40 + 0.60×100 + 0.20×250 = 118`. The calculation does not mean the entity predicts a loss of exactly 118. It means 118 is the expectation across the modelled distribution.

A critical modelling issue is **non-linearity**. Credit risk often deteriorates disproportionately when unemployment, property values or borrower cash flows pass stress thresholds. Consequently, ECL evaluated under a single “average” macro path may not equal the weighted average of ECL calculated separately under multiple scenarios. IFRS 9 does not prescribe a fixed number of scenarios; the number and construction must be sufficient to capture material non-linear effects and satisfy the unbiased probability-weighted objective.

Scenario probabilities are themselves estimates. They should correspond to coherent economic narratives, not be selected backwards to obtain a desired allowance. Governance therefore needs to address scenario design, probability setting, model sensitivity and changes from the previous reporting date.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Probability weighting applies to possible credit-loss outcomes; it is not permission to select whichever scenario gives a comfortable provision.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Running ECL once on average macroeconomic variables and calling the result “probability weighted” can miss material non-linear loss behaviour.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Most-likely scenario" b="Expected loss">
**Most-likely scenario** — the individual scenario with the highest assigned probability.

---
**Expected loss** — the probability-weighted result across relevant possible outcomes.
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="IN">
— Current bank IRACP is not a probability-weighted ECL accounting regime. RBI’s final 2026 bank ECL framework, effective April 1, 2027, explicitly requires multiple scenarios and probability weights informed by historical experience and expert judgement; Ind AS NBFCs already apply expected-loss accounting. [RBI 2026 ECL Directions, paragraphs 47–49]
</Delta>

<Delta j="UK">
— IFRS 9 probability weighting applies; PRA’s 2025 thematic work additionally emphasises governance over model limitations and adjustments. [IFRS 9 5.5.17; PRA thematic feedback]
</Delta>

<Delta j="EU">
— EU-adopted IFRS 9 applies the same probability-weighted objective, with EBA supervisory expectations around robust ECL practices. [Regulation (EU) 2023/1803; EBA/GL/2017/06]
</Delta>

<Delta j="US">
— CECL also incorporates reasonable and supportable expectations about future conditions, but Topic 326 does not impose the IFRS 9 scenario architecture or prescribe one estimation method. [ASU 2016-13; Interagency CECL FAQ]
</Delta>

<Recall
  q="Is ECL the loss under the scenario with the highest probability?"
  a="No. It is an expectation across relevant possible outcomes."
/>

<Recall
  q="Why can an “average macro scenario” be insufficient?"
