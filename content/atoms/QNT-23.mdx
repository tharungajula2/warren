### Core mechanism

| Feature | Credit example | Statistical implication |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Trend | Defaults rise through a downturn | Mean changes over time |
| Seasonality | Application volume repeats annual pattern | Fixed-period structure recurs |
| Autocorrelation | This month's arrears resemble last month's | Observations are serially dependent |
| Structural break | Underwriting rule changes suddenly | One historical process becomes another |
| Stationarity | Mean, variance and lag structure stable enough over time | Past dynamics can be estimated more coherently |

```text
Observed credit series
    ↓
Inspect level, trend and seasonal pattern
    ↓
Assess stationarity and breaks
    ↓
Transform or difference if needed
    ↓
Study lag dependence with ACF and PACF
    ↓
Fit parsimonious time-series structure
```

ACF measures correlation between the series and lagged versions of itself. PACF isolates the relationship at a particular lag after accounting for shorter lags, helping distinguish autoregressive from moving-average structure.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Calendar effects · Portfolio aggregates · Serial observations
This:       Stationarity · Trend · ACF/PACF · ARIMA structure
Downstream: Macro conditioning · Rolling forecasts · Scenario paths
```

### Formula

```text
First difference at time t = value at t - value at t-1
AR(1): value at t = constant + phi x value at t-1 + error at t
```

### Permanent rule

Do not interpret lag relationships until trend, seasonality and structural breaks have been considered; non-stationary level movement can create misleading autocorrelation.

### Never confuse [Seasonality] and [Credit cycle]

**Seasonality** - pattern repeating at a fixed calendar frequency.

---

**Credit cycle** - broader expansion and contraction in credit conditions that need not repeat at fixed intervals.

### Permanent trap

Randomly shuffling a time series before evaluation. That removes the very chronological dependence and regime change the forecast must survive.
