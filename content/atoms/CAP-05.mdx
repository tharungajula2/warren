### Core mechanism

| Feature | AT1 | Tier 2 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Place in stack | Tier 1 | Total Capital below Tier 1 |
| Maturity | Perpetual under Basel eligibility | Minimum original maturity requirements; recognition amortises near maturity |
| Distributions | Fully discretionary, non-cumulative where required | Contractual servicing can exist subject to eligibility |
| Subordination | Deeply subordinated | Subordinated to depositors and general creditors |
| Loss absorption | Going-concern mechanisms plus non-viability requirements | Principally non-viability / gone-concern |
| Redemption | Subject to regulatory conditions and no expectation of repayment | Subject to eligibility and supervisory conditions |

Basel requires detailed instrument terms because a security that looks equity-like economically may still contain redemption incentives, step-ups, payment obligations or other features inconsistent with regulatory capital.

### Permanent rule

An instrument counts only if its legal terms satisfy the applicable regulatory-capital criteria throughout the period for which recognition is claimed.

### Never confuse [AT1] and [Tier 2]

**AT1** — additional going-concern Tier 1 capital that does not qualify as CET1.

---

**Tier 2** — lower-ranking capital recognised toward Total Capital and designed principally for gone-concern loss absorption.

### Permanent trap

Assuming every perpetual bond is AT1 or every subordinated bond is Tier 2 ignores the eligibility tests that make the instrument genuinely loss absorbing.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta in the Basel categories. Instrument documentation, tax treatment, resolution powers and national eligibility rules create implementation differences.

  q="Why can AT1 count in Tier 1 but not CET1?"
  a="It meets going-concern capital criteria without meeting the stricter common-equity criteria."
