IFRS 9 requires the loss allowance to revert from lifetime ECL to 12-month ECL when the criterion for lifetime ECL is no longer met. That makes staging two-way: exposures can deteriorate and later improve.

The apparent asymmetry arises from the reference point. Suppose a borrower entered Stage 2 after a sharp internal-rating downgrade. Six months later, the rating improves by one grade. The original trigger has partly reversed, but current risk may still be substantially worse than at origination. Stage 2 therefore remains appropriate. “Cure” is not simply the opposite movement in the latest indicator; it requires reassessing current credit risk against the original benchmark.

The same principle prevents automatic cure immediately after an arrears balance is paid. Payment can be strong evidence of improvement, but the borrower may remain financially weak, recently restructured or exposed to adverse forward-looking conditions. All relevant reasonable and supportable information remains part of SICR assessment. Conversely, IFRS 9 does not establish a universal mandatory probation period for every Stage 2-to-Stage 1 transfer. Policies that require sustained performance can be evidential tools, but the accounting test remains whether SICR persists.

Stage 3 cure adds another layer: first determine whether the asset remains credit-impaired; then determine whether SICR relative to origination still exists. A cured Stage 3 exposure need not jump directly to Stage 1 if credit risk remains significantly elevated.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
A stage reversal must reapply the same underlying credit-risk assessment objective used for deterioration.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Automatically curing Stage 2 when DPD returns to zero can move an exposure to Stage 1 while its lifetime default risk remains materially above origination.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Symptom cure" b="SICR cure">
**Symptom cure** — disappearance of a particular warning signal such as arrears.

---
**SICR cure** — evidence that credit risk is no longer significantly increased relative to initial recognition.
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="IN">
— Current bank IRACP has its own NPA-upgradation rules rather than IFRS stage cure. RBI’s ECL Directions effective April 1, 2027 contain specific Stage 3 upgradation and restructuring conditions alongside SICR assessment, so their prudential cure mechanics must be applied on their own terms. [RBI 2025 IRACP; RBI 2026 ECL Directions, paragraphs 78–81]
</Delta>

<Delta j="UK">
— IFRS 9 permits reversion to 12-month ECL when lifetime-ECL criteria are no longer met; PRA expects robust monitoring rather than mechanical cures. [IFRS 9 5.5.7; PRA 2025]
</Delta>

<Delta j="EU">
— EU-adopted IFRS 9 follows the same accounting reversal principle, supplemented by EBA supervisory guidance. [Regulation (EU) 2023/1803; EBA/GL/2017/06]
</Delta>

<Delta j="US">
— CECL has no Stage 2-to-Stage 1 cure because it has no staged allowance horizon; improved credit expectations reduce the lifetime allowance through remeasurement. [ASC 326]
</Delta>
