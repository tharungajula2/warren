Market risk arises when positions are marked‐to‐market, so portfolio value changes with market movements.  It includes price risk on instruments from six risk categories: general interest rates, credit spreads (non-securitized and securitized), foreign exchange, equity, and commodity risk.  Each of these risk factor classes drives the market value of trading positions (e.g. rising rates reduce bond values, widening credit spreads hurt credit-sensitive instruments).

A market risk manager communicates risk in terms of sensitivities (delta, vega, curvature) to these factors (see MRK-03).  For example, a bond portfolio’s delta sensitivity quantifies loss per basis-point move in rates.  Each risk class is handled separately but then aggregated under capital rules.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Market risk is always about price changes in trading positions, not credit defaults.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Treating credit spread risk simply as credit default risk (credit risk capital) rather than as a market risk factor.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Interest rate risk** — loss from interest rate moves (fixed income valuation); **Credit spread risk** — loss from widening credit spreads on debt (a market risk factor).
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** The definitions of market risk and its factor categories are uniform across jurisdictions.
</Rule>
