Under SA, each risk factor (e.g. interest rate, FX, equity) has pre-set weight tables.  A bank computes portfolio delta, vega and curvature sensitivities for each risk factor.  Those sensitivities are multiplied by risk weights and summed according to prescribed buckets (e.g. grouping interest rate tenors into zones, currency pairs into FX buckets).  Buckets allow intra- and inter-bucket correlations: SA defines two scenarios (medium and low correlation) for combining risk, and takes the larger capital requirement from those.

After summing delta, vega, and curvature charges for all classes under the worst-case scenario, SA also adds two extra charges: the **Default Risk Charge** (DRC) for jump-to-default credit risk and the **Residual Risk Add-On** (RRAO) for exotic/basis exposures.  These components ensure known market and credit risks are covered even without an internal model.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
SA capital = max(scenario capital) + DRC + RRAO.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Forgetting to apply the correct correlation scenario (always take the higher) or omitting the RRAO for exotic positions.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Delta risk** (linear sensitivity) vs **Vega risk** (volatility sensitivity) — both are treated separately in SA and not interchangeable.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** The SA framework is a standardized Basel formula applied globally (though local regulators may adjust some risk weights).
</Rule>

<Recall
  q="What components make up SA market risk capital?"
  a="Sensitivity-based capital (taking worst of correlation scenarios) plus Default Risk Charge plus Residual Risk Add-On."
/>

<Recall
  q="Why two correlation scenarios?"
  a="To bound capital under different assumed risk correlations; the final capital is the larger of the two."
/>
