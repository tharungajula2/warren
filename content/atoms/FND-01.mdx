A **balance sheet** is a snapshot of financial position at a particular date. An **asset** is a present economic resource controlled by an entity as a result of past events; a **liability** is a present obligation to transfer an economic resource; **equity** is the residual interest after liabilities are deducted from assets. Those definitions make the basic accounting identity unavoidable rather than optional.

For a bank, the labels can initially feel reversed. A loan made to a customer is normally an **asset of the bank** because the customer owes future payments to the bank. A customer deposit is normally a **liability of the bank** because the bank owes the depositor money and must honour valid withdrawals or payments. Cash, balances with central banks, securities and loans are common asset categories; deposits and wholesale borrowings are common liability categories. Equity sits beneath them as the residual claim.

Suppose a bank has assets of 110 and liabilities of 100. Its equity is 10. If an asset subsequently loses 4 in value and nothing else changes, assets become 106 and equity becomes 6. The loss does not disappear because it was non-cash; it reduces the residual available to absorb losses. This is the mechanical reason capital matters to a bank.

The identity does **not** say that every asset is safe, every liability is payable immediately, or equity is a pile of cash. It only says that recognised resources and obligations must balance through the residual interest. Accounting recognition and measurement determine the amounts assigned to each item; prudential regulation may then make additional adjustments when determining regulatory capital.

```text
+-----------------------+
|        ASSETS         |
| cash, securities,     |
| loans, other claims   |
+-----------------------+
            |
            | financed by
            v
+-----------------------+
|      LIABILITIES      |
| deposits, borrowings  |
+-----------------------+
            |
            | plus residual
            v
+-----------------------+
|        EQUITY         |
| assets - liabilities  |
+-----------------------+

Assets = Liabilities + Equity
```

### Permanent rule

Every recognised balance-sheet amount must fit the identity **Assets = Liabilities + Equity**. A loss that reduces assets without reducing liabilities reduces equity.

### Permanent trap

Reading a customer's loan as the bank's liability because the bank "gave the money". The remaining repayment claim belongs to the bank, so the loan is the bank's asset.

### Never confuse

**Asset** — a recognised economic resource controlled by the bank.

---

**Equity** — the residual after liabilities are deducted from assets; it is not another asset or a dedicated cash reserve.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** The accounting identity and the asset-liability-equity relationship do not depend on jurisdiction.
