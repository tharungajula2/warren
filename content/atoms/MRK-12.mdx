Prior to FRTB, banks had wide discretion to classify positions, often moving illiquid bonds into the banking book to reduce capital.  FRTB closes these gaps by requiring documented intent and specific criteria for trading book inclusion.  For example, a position must be held with intent to trade (or hedge another trading position) to go in the trading book.  Otherwise it must stay in the banking book.  This “marked-to-market requirement” ensures capital reflects actual risk.  

The revised rules (Basel RBC25) replaced the previous “principles-based” boundary with prescriptive rules and governance requirements.  Banks must justify each trading book position with approvals and documentation.  This reduces risk-shifting between books and promotes consistency.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Any position not held with a trading intent or as a hedge of trading risk must be classified as banking book.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Designating long-term or illiquid instruments as trading positions simply to use potentially lower market risk capital.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Trading book** — positions held for short-term resale or hedging trading exposures; **Banking book** — held for earning interest or fulfilling contractual relationships (not for active trading).
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** All regimes that implement FRTB impose stricter boundaries (UK/EU via RBC, US via OCC guidelines, etc).
</Rule>
