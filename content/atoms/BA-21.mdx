BAs often help decide what to do first in a change program. Common prioritisation methods include *weighted scoring* (scoring features by value, cost, risk), *MoSCoW* (Must/Should/Could/Won’t), or simple *risk-first* (fix highest compliance risk first). For regulatory projects, items explicitly tied to required dates are usually top priority. Always document how you prioritized: for example, “Requirement A scored 9/10 on impact because it directly addresses a mandatory regulation” provides rationale. This transparency makes prioritisation defensible to management and auditors.

For estimation, acknowledge that BAs and teams are naturally optimistic (the “planning fallacy”). Use historical data or three-point estimates (best/likely/worst) to capture uncertainty. Include a buffer for testing and integration complexities. For instance, if developing a new report took 10 days in a past project, plan 15 days for similar complexity. Clear estimation methods (e.g. story points, function points, or t-shirt sizes) help set realistic schedules and avoid last-minute scope cuts.

### Permanent rule

Use objective criteria (value to business, compliance risk, regulatory deadline) in a documented prioritisation model. When estimating, always allow contingencies – it’s normal to be optimistic, so plan for known unknowns.

### Permanent trap

Letting “priority” be decided by the loudest stakeholder or an incorrect assumption that all items are equally urgent. Also, giving point estimates (e.g. “5 days”) without ranges ignores uncertainty; this often leads to schedule overrun.

### Never confuse

**Urgency** vs **Importance**:
An *Urgent* task has a tight deadline (often regulatory or external). An *Important* task has high business value or impact. They can differ (e.g. a bug might be urgent but low importance). Prioritisation should consider both, not mistake one for the other.

### No delta

Banks use the same prioritisation logic everywhere. CFOs and regulators expect clear rationale for why one feature was delayed and another accelerated.
