### Core mechanism

Move from defect description to disposition decision.

```text
Observed failure -&gt; Current requirement -&gt; Business impact -&gt; Workaround/control -&gt; Release consequence -&gt; Owner decision
```

| Question | Why it matters |
| :--- | :--- |
| What requirement or expected behaviour failed? | Separates defect from enhancement |
| Which process or output is affected? | Establishes business consequence |
| Is mandatory scope compromised? | Determines whether release can satisfy mandate |
| Is there a controlled workaround? | Tests temporary operability |
| What control or evidence is weakened? | Makes hidden risk visible |
| By when can it be fixed and retested? | Connects defect to release timing |
| Who can accept the residual exposure? | Prevents project team from self-accepting risk |

Severity and priority can inform the discussion, but labels do not make the decision. A technically minor issue can be a release blocker if it invalidates a required disclosure; a severe issue in an unused deferred component may have a different release consequence.

If the proposed "fix" changes approved behaviour, it is also a change request and must follow change control.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Test evidence · Requirements · Defect record
This:       Business impact · Release consequence · Residual risk
Downstream: Retest · Acceptance · Go/no-go · Post-go-live action
```

### Ownership

| Role | Responsibility |
| :--- | :--- |
| Produces | Test team records defect; BA links it to requirement and business impact |
| Approves | Product, business, control or release authority decides disposition within mandate |
| Consumes | Delivery, testing, operations and go/no-go governance consume the decision |
| Independently challenges | Risk or control functions challenge unacceptable residual exposure |

### Permanent rule

A project team cannot make risk disappear by changing a defect status. Release with a known limitation requires an authorised owner for the resulting business or control exposure.

### Never confuse [Defect] and [Change request]

**Defect** - the delivered solution does not meet the current approved expectation.

---

**Change request** - proposes to alter the approved expectation or scope.

### Never confuse [Severity] and [Release consequence]

**Severity** - a classification of the defect's impact under the defect scheme.

---

**Release consequence** - the decision about whether and under what conditions the change may proceed to production.

### Permanent trap

Using "workaround available" as automatic permission to release. The workaround itself has capacity, control, evidence, training and duration consequences that must be assessed.

  a="When release is proposed while its consequence remains, requiring authorised acceptance of the exposure."
