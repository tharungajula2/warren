  The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (2016) provides a structured process for
  resolving stressed corporate debts. If lenders collectively decide (typically
  75% by value) that a default requires legal resolution, they can initiate
  corporate insolvency. A *Resolution Professional* then manages the company’s
  affairs, and creditors vote on a revival plan. The IBC has priority over
  SARFAESI in that once an insolvency petition is admitted, SARFAESI proceedings
  halt. For personal or small debtors, formal bankruptcy via IBC may not apply;
  instead, DRTs (for bank debts above a threshold) and debt recovery tribunals
  handle lower-value cases, including unsecured and smaller claims.

Under IBC, any financial creditor (bank, NBFC, or even a supplier if admitted) can initiate proceedings when payment default occurs. A strict timeline (330 days max) forces quick action. Successful resolution under IBC often involves structural changes (equity dilution, takeover by new promoters, etc.). Recoveries under IBC have exceeded SARFAESI recoveries historically because of its collective mechanism. But IBC is mostly for corporate debt. For individual/business borrowers, RBI relies on SARFAESI and DRT (established by RDDBFI Act, 1993) and lok adalats. 

Debt Recovery Tribunals handle all debts of ₹10 lakh and above to banks/financial institutions. They can entertain cases on any credit, secured or not, and can order recovery from a debtor’s assets. There are separate DRTs for circular references from the RBI. The DRT process is formal (like a tribunal court), but faster than regular civil court. DRTs have been strengthened by digital filing and “DRAT” appeals.  

Banks usually prioritize SARFAESI for quick value, then DRT if borrower defends. IBC is the last resort for viable corporates or when persuasion fails. RBI’s provisions require reclassification of accounts when IBC is filed (they become NPA on petition filing). The IBC can wipe out shareholders; in one instance, leading to EMI refinance rather than further lending.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Under IBC, when a corporate borrower defaults, lenders may trigger insolvency proceedings with RBI filing specified asset classification. DRTs handle recovery suits for any loan above ₹10 lakh.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Missing the priority: initiating SARFAESI on a company after IBC has been filed – not allowed, since IBC is a binding multilateral process once admitted. Also, dismissing smaller claims (below 10 lakh) thinking only big debts go to DRTs – DRT jurisdiction starts at ₹10 lakh for banks.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**IBC (Corporate)** — structured insolvency process for companies / FIs (time-bound, collective).  
**DRT (Debt Recovery)** — tribunal for creditor claims on individuals or businesses (₹10L+), including enforcement of guarantee by guarantors.
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="IN">
— The IBC is India’s consolidated insolvency law (2016). Countries like the UK/US have different corporate insolvency regimes (Chapter 11, administration, etc.), but the IBC’s speed and creditor primacy are quite distinctive.
</Delta>
