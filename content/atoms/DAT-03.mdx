BCBS 239’s purpose is to ensure banks can identify their risk exposures at any time. It mandates that the board of directors oversee data quality, and that the Chief Risk Officer is accountable day-to-day. Principle 1 demands “strong governance” consistent with other Basel standards, and Principle 2 requires a data architecture that supports aggregation in normal and stress conditions (avoiding last-minute spreadsheets). The second group (Principles 3–6) then focuses on aggregation: data must reconcile with accounting, cover all material sources (at least ~95%), be updated rapidly (daily or intraday for critical metrics), and support ad hoc queries under stress. The third group (Principles 7–11) covers reporting: risk reports must be “accurate, comprehensive, and written for their intended audience,” delivered at appropriate frequency, and distributed along the approved chain of command. Finally, Principles 12–14 describe supervisory review: they guide how regulators should evaluate compliance, require remediation plans for deficiencies, and address home/host coordination for global banks.

BCBS 239 is principles-based: it does not prescribe specific IT architectures but sets outcomes. National supervisors (e.g. PRA in the UK, Fed in the US, RBI in India) expect banks to meet these outcomes, potentially imposing Pillar 2 add-ons for serious shortcomings. The standard has no formal legal force by itself; compliance is enforced through local regulation and supervision.

### Permanent rule

The board of directors must ultimately own the risk data framework, not just delegate it to IT – strong governance is mandatory.

### Permanent trap

Treating BCBS 239 as optional guidance; in practice, major regulators treat it as mandatory for significant banks.

### Never confuse

**BCBS 239** — Basel Committee principles for risk data aggregation and reporting. Its focus is data and reporting quality.
---
**Basel III/CRR** — Regulatory framework for capital and liquidity. While related to risk, Basel III addresses capital adequacy, not data architecture.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** The 14 principles and their objectives are identical globally; they were adopted for G-SIBs with the same intent by all major regulators.
