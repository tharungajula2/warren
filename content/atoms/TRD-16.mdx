Option value depends on multiple variables simultaneously, so a single P&amp;L number does not explain the risk. The Greeks approximate how value changes when specific pricing inputs move.

Delta is the change in option value for a small change in the underlying. A call generally has positive delta and a put negative delta from the holder’s perspective. Delta is often used to construct a local hedge in the underlying.

Gamma is the rate at which delta changes as the underlying moves. Long plain options generally have positive gamma: large moves in either direction can improve the delta-adjusted payoff relative to a static hedge, though the option holder pays for that convexity through premium and time decay.

Vega measures sensitivity to implied volatility. Long plain options generally benefit when implied volatility rises, all else equal. Despite the name, vega is not a Greek letter. Theta measures the passage of time, commonly expressed as expected value decay per day with other inputs held constant. Long plain options often have negative theta because the opportunity for favourable future movement shrinks as expiry approaches.

Rho measures sensitivity to interest rates and can matter more for long-dated options. Cross-Greeks and higher-order sensitivities become important for complex books, but delta, gamma, vega and theta provide the core mental model.

The Greeks are model-dependent and local. A delta measured now can change after a price move, volatility change or passage of time. They also depend on conventions such as spot versus forward delta and volatility quoting practice.

A trader may describe a position as “delta neutral” while still carrying substantial gamma and vega. Neutralising first-order direction is therefore not equivalent to eliminating option risk.

&lt;Flow&gt;
Option value
   |
   +--&amp;gt; underlying move ------&amp;gt; Delta
   |                              |
   |                              +--&amp;gt; change in Delta -&amp;gt; Gamma
   |
   +--&amp;gt; implied volatility ---&amp;gt; Vega
   |
   +--&amp;gt; passage of time ------&amp;gt; Theta
   |
   +--&amp;gt; interest rates -------&amp;gt; Rho
&lt;/Flow&gt;

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Greeks are local derivatives of value; always ask the unit, sign convention, model and valuation point.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Calling a delta-neutral book risk-free. Gamma, vega, theta, basis and model risks remain.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Delta" b="Gamma">
**Delta** — first-order option-value sensitivity to the underlying.

---

**Gamma** — sensitivity of delta to changes in the underlying.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.**
</Rule>

<Recall
  q="What risk remains after a book is delta hedged?"
  a="Among others, gamma, vega, theta, basis and model risk."
/>

<Recall
  q="Why can yesterday’s delta be a poor hedge ratio today?"
  a="The underlying, volatility, time and other inputs may have changed, and gamma changes delta."
