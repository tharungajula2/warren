The **trading book** contains positions assigned to the regulatory trading perimeter under applicable capital rules. The **banking book** is the non-trading portion of the balance sheet. The distinction matters because market risk in the trading book and interest-rate risk in the banking book are governed through different prudential machinery.

A fixed-rate bond illustrates the point. Economically, its value changes when interest rates change regardless of where it is booked. If it is a trading-book position, that risk is generally captured through the market-risk capital framework. If it sits in the banking book, the structural interest-rate exposure enters IRRBB measures such as economic value of equity and net interest income. The economic sensitivity exists before regulation; the boundary determines how that sensitivity is classified and supervised. Basel defines IRRBB as risk to capital and earnings from adverse interest-rate movements affecting banking-book positions.

This prevents a common conceptual error: accounting classification and prudential-book classification are related operationally but are not synonyms. A position's accounting measurement basis does not, by itself, answer which prudential risk framework applies. Nor can a bank freely relabel positions merely because one treatment appears more favourable; the boundary is governed by regulatory assignment criteria and controls.

For ALM, the consequence is practical. Structural customer loans, deposits, non-maturity balances, term funding and relevant off-balance-sheet positions feed the banking-book risk system. Treasury must identify which cash flows belong there, how they reprice and which embedded options affect them. RBI’s 2025 forthcoming IRRBB Directions similarly define IRRBB around banking-book positions and require measurement of both ΔEVE and ΔNII.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Book classification determines the applicable prudential risk framework; economic interest-rate sensitivity exists irrespective of the label.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Using “banking book” to mean “held to maturity” or assuming an accounting category automatically determines the prudential book.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Prudential book classification" b="Accounting classification">
**Prudential book classification** — regulatory allocation between trading and banking books for risk and capital purposes.
---
**Accounting classification** — the financial-reporting basis used to recognise and measure an instrument.
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="UK">

<Delta j="EU">

</Delta>

<Delta j="US">
— US capital and supervisory frameworks separately address trading-market risk and structural interest-rate risk; precise trading-book assignment must be verified against the current US capital rule.
</Delta>
