Consider a ₹10 lakh loan co-lent at 80:20. The bank disburses ₹8L (possibly via the NBFC’s escrow), and the NBFC ₹2L. The NBFC may use its credit underwriting. When the borrower repays, the NBFC collects full EMI and must immediately allocate ₹8 of every ₹10 repayment to the bank’s account. RBI requires this flow to be documented, often via escrow or trustee arrangements. Both lenders then account: the bank books ₹8L asset, NBFC books ₹2L asset (showing ₹8L as a loan to NBFC if it was an advance).

Reconciling these transactions is crucial each month. Banks and NBFCs often have joint MIS (Management Information System) to track co-lending pools. Differences (timing, rounding) must be resolved, or interest mismatches occur. RBI guidelines mandate that co-lending contracts clearly define default risk-sharing: if the borrower defaults, the bank and NBFC write off the respective shares in NPA. The NBFC’s share is often fully provided since the borrower mostly owes NBFC’s smaller part last (depending on waterfall, if any).

Operational burdens include dual KYC process (both lenders verify the borrower), dual documentation, and regulatory reporting. For example, both lenders report their part of the loan in returns (one sees an inter-company advance, the other the borrower advance). Oversight demands that both sides have audit rights and controls over the loan pool.

Despite the paperwork, co-lending is encouraged because it leverages NBFC outreach while using cheaper bank funds. Analysts must however note that it creates many micro-contractual relationships. Staff in banks and NBFCs must ensure cash flows match contractual splits exactly – failure can lead to compliance flags (like undisclosed bridging finance if the NBFC temporarily covers the bank’s share).

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Each co-lent account must be recognized on both the bank’s and NBFC’s balance sheets in the agreed ratio. Collections must be split immediately per that ratio.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Neglecting reconciliation: assuming the NBFC’s collection is the end. In fact, the bank’s share must be remitted precisely each payment cycle. Failing to transfer promptly can violate regulations and trigger funding issues.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Disbursement ratio** — split of funds lent (e.g. bank:NBFC 80:20).
**Risk ratio** — same as disbursement split (each bears losses in proportion). These two are identical in RBI’s co-lending rules.
</NeverConfuse>
