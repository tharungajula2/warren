Specifically, U.S. rules now use SA-CCR in place of the previous Current Exposure Method. The FFIEC and Basel rules define the calculation. U.S. guidance also harmonizes with global standards for netting and margin. For CVA capital, banks currently use the standardized CVA approach (Basel-aligned) as per existing regulations, but agencies have sought comment on further changes.

### Permanent rule

Large U.S. banks must compute CCR RWA under SA-CCR. For capital, counterparty exposures use SA-CCR EAD, and CVA risk uses the standardized CVA approach.

### Permanent trap

Misapplying older Basel I/II rules. Also, small U.S. banks had the option to use the simpler exposure method but even those are encouraged to adopt SA-CCR.

### Never confuse

**Fed/OCC rules** — U.S. regulations implementing Basel (SA-CCR, CVA).

---

**FRB’s Exchange Act rules** — Another set of U.S. rules (generally consistent).

### Jurisdiction variants

**US** — SA-CCR has been mandatory since Jan 2022. U.S. regulators are aligning CVA standards with Basel 3.1 (proposed in 2022). No separate CVA regime in India; UK/EU have updated theirs as noted above.
