### Core mechanism

Separate the observed failure from the condition that allows it to recur.

```text
Symptom -&gt; Immediate cause -&gt; Contributing condition -&gt; Controllable root cause -&gt; Required change
```

| Layer | Reporting example | BA test |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Symptom | Late manual adjustment | What failed or needed intervention? |
| Immediate cause | Source arrived after freeze | What directly produced it? |
| Contributing condition | Source timing owner unclear | What allowed it to persist? |
| Root cause | No accountable cutoff control | What would prevent recurrence? |
| Required outcome | Source completion owned and evidenced before freeze | What must become true? |
| Evidence | Logs, steps, exceptions, reconciliations, decisions, workarounds | Does evidence support causality? |
| Multiple causes | Several conditions contribute | Preserve the chain; do not force one neat cause |
| Remediation | Today's output corrected | Was the enabling process or control weakness removed? |

"Five whys" can prompt inquiry but does not prove causality. Correction restores today's output; remediation removes the recurring condition.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Mandate · Incident · Finding · User pain
This:       Problem statement · Cause · Required outcome
Downstream: As-is discovery · Scope · Requirements
```

### Ownership

| Role | Responsibility |
| :--- | :--- |
| Produces | BA structures the problem and evidence chain |
| Approves | Business or control owner confirms the problem definition and required outcome |
| Consumes | Delivery and design teams use it to avoid solving the wrong problem |
| Independently challenges | Risk, compliance, operational risk or audit challenge root cause where relevant |

### Permanent rule

A root cause must explain why the symptom occurred and identify something the change can act on. A label such as "human error" is not sufficient if the process made the error likely or undetectable.

### Never confuse [Symptom] and [Root cause]

**Symptom** - the visible failure, delay, error or workaround that triggered attention.

---

**Root cause** - the underlying controllable condition whose correction materially reduces recurrence.

### Permanent trap

Writing the desired solution into the problem statement. "The problem is that we do not have platform X" closes solution space before the actual process or control failure has been established.

No delta.

<Recall
  q="Why is a manual adjustment not automatically the root cause?"
  a="It may be the recovery action; the cause can sit in timing, ownership, data, control or upstream process."
/>

<Recall
  q="What should support root-cause analysis?"
  a="Process evidence, exceptions, logs, reconciliations, decisions and observed workarounds must support the causal chain."
/>

<Recall
  q="When is remediation incomplete?"
  a="When today's output is corrected but the condition that allowed recurrence remains."
