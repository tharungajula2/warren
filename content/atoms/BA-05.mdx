When interviewing an SME who “does not have time,” preparation is key. Before the meeting, research the topic, study any existing documentation, and send a concise agenda or questionnaire in advance. Start by explaining the project’s purpose and how their input will be used; this shows respect and gains their cooperation.

In the meeting, ask clear, focused questions. Prioritize questions so the most critical ones get asked first. If possible, schedule short, focused sessions (e.g. 30 minutes) and stick to that time. Always come with an agenda and demonstrate that you’ve “done your homework” — SMEs should not hear questions that could have been answered by your own prep.  Use techniques like the *5 Whys* or scenario walkthroughs to get deep insight quickly. If an SME can’t meet face-to-face, consider email questions or quick confirmations to capture their knowledge.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Treat every SME meeting as valuable and limited. Go in prepared and respectful of their time. Always document answers immediately, and confirm understanding before ending.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Entering an SME meeting without preparation, or hoping to get detailed answers by casually catching them off guard. Another mistake is not validating their time constraints; for example, scheduling an hour when only 15 minutes is feasible.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Project Sponsor** vs **Subject Matter Expert (SME)**:
A *Sponsor* is the executive or business champion who funds the project. An *SME* is a technical or functional expert providing detailed knowledge. A sponsor’s role is strategic; an SME’s is informational. They are often mixed up by new BAs.
</NeverConfuse>

### No delta

These interpersonal techniques are universal. Bank SMEs in any country have limited time, so the approach to interviewing them is the same.
