The Financial Stability Board's **risk appetite framework (RAF)** links four ideas: risk capacity, risk appetite, risk limits and risk profile. **Risk capacity** is the maximum level of risk the institution can bear before breaching constraints such as capital, liquidity, regulatory or operational viability. **Risk appetite** is how much and what types of risk the institution chooses to take within that capacity to pursue strategy. **Risk limits** translate appetite into practical boundaries. The **risk profile** is the institution's actual risk exposure at a point in time.

Capacity should therefore sit outside appetite, not the other way round. A bank might have the capacity to tolerate a particular level of credit concentration but choose a lower appetite because its strategy favours diversification. Limits can then constrain exposures by sector, counterparty, geography, product or another measurable dimension.

A useful appetite statement combines quantitative and qualitative dimensions. Some risks can be expressed directly through ratios or limits. Others require statements about activities the institution will avoid, control conditions that must be satisfied, or circumstances requiring escalation.

Risk appetite is not a forecast. A statement that a bank is willing to accept a certain level of loss does not mean that loss is expected to occur. Nor is appetite simply a regulatory minimum. A regulatory boundary is an external constraint; internal appetite should define chosen risk-taking within the institution's capacity and applicable constraints.

The framework closes through monitoring. Actual risk profile is compared with appetite and limits. Approaching or breaching a limit should trigger the predetermined escalation, remediation, acceptance or governance response rather than silent relabelling of the boundary.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Risk appetite must remain within risk capacity and must connect to measurable or otherwise enforceable decision boundaries.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Raising a limit after a breach solely so that the reported position becomes compliant, without treating the change as a governed risk-appetite decision.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Risk capacity" b="Risk appetite">
**Risk capacity** — the maximum risk the institution can bear subject to its binding constraints.
---
**Risk appetite** — the risk the institution chooses to accept within that capacity; willingness and ability are not the same.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Supervisory expectations vary, but the capacity-appetite-limits-profile architecture is a cross-jurisdictional governance principle.
</Rule>
