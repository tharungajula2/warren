### Core mechanism

| Decision | Golden-source question |
| :--- | :--- |
| Scope | Which data element, entity, product or population does this authority cover? |
| Authority | Which system or controlled record is the official source? |
| Timing | At what event time, effective date or reporting cut-off is it authoritative? |
| Control | How are updates, corrections and overrides governed? |
| Consumption | Which downstream processes must source from it or reconcile to it? |

```text
Business event
    ↓
Authoritative capture or approved master
    ↓
Controlled distribution
    ↓
Warehouse, risk engine and reporting copies
    ↓
Reconcile downstream use to governed authority
```

A bank can legitimately have different authoritative sources for different concepts: customer legal name, product classification, accounting balance and risk exposure may each belong to different controlled domains. The rule is not one database for the bank; it is one governed authority per defined purpose and scope.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Data architecture · Business events · Ownership
This:       Golden-source designation
Downstream: CDE governance · Lineage · Mapping · Reconciliation
```

### Never confuse [Golden source] and [Data warehouse]

**Golden source** - the governed authority for a defined datum or population.

---

**Data warehouse** - a repository that may contain integrated copies from many sources. It becomes authoritative only where governance explicitly assigns that role.

### Permanent rule

When two systems disagree, governance must determine which source is authoritative and why; report teams must not choose whichever value makes the reconciliation easier.

### Permanent trap

Using the phrase "single source of truth" without defining scope. Different data domains can have different authoritative sources without violating the golden-source principle.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** The concept is an enterprise data-governance mechanism, not a jurisdiction-specific return rule.

<Recall
  q="Can a warehouse be a golden source?"
  a="Yes, but only if governance makes it authoritative for the defined purpose; storage or consolidation alone does not create authority."
/>

<Recall
  q="Why can one bank have multiple golden sources?"
  a="Authority is assigned by data concept and scope. Customer identity, accounting balances and market reference data can each have different governed systems of record."
