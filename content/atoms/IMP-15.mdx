**Loss given default (LGD)** is the proportion of exposure lost if default occurs. In accounting ECL models, LGD is a modelling component used to represent expected discounted cash shortfalls conditional on default. It should therefore capture expected recoveries, their timing, collateral and guarantee effects where appropriate, workout costs and forward-looking conditions consistently with the accounting framework.

Regulatory-capital LGD answers a different question. Under the Basel internal-ratings-based framework, a bank’s LGD must reflect economic-downturn conditions where necessary to capture relevant risk. Basel capital parameters also operate within prudential calibration rules designed to protect solvency against unexpected loss.

That difference is not semantic. A downturn-calibrated regulatory LGD may be deliberately more conservative than a neutral probability-weighted accounting estimate at a particular reporting date. Basel analysis has explicitly noted that regulatory-capital models can be useful starting points for accounting ECL but may require adjustment because regulatory PD and LGD have different objectives and inputs; for example, Basel regulatory LGD reflects downturn economic conditions, whereas accounting ECL is intended to produce an unbiased expectation rather than a permanently stressed estimate.

Other mismatches can arise from prescribed regulatory floors, definitions of default, cure treatment, collateral eligibility, recovery costs and discounting conventions. Therefore, a mapping from regulatory LGD to accounting LGD needs an explicit bridge showing which prudential adjustments remain relevant and which must be removed or recalibrated.

### Permanent rule

A risk parameter inherits the objective and calibration rules of the framework for which it was built.

### Permanent trap

Copying downturn regulatory LGD directly into accounting ECL without adjustment can embed prudential conservatism twice—once in the parameter and again in scenario weighting or overlays.

### Never confuse

**Accounting LGD** — expected loss severity calibrated to the financial-reporting ECL objective.

---

**Regulatory LGD** — loss severity calibrated under prudential capital rules, potentially including downturn requirements and regulatory constraints.

### Jurisdiction variants

**IN** — Current scheduled-commercial-bank IRACP uses prescribed prudential provisioning rather than accounting LGD-based ECL. RBI’s ECL Directions from April 1, 2027 introduce PD/LGD modelling but overlay it with RBI prudential ECL floors, so modelled accounting-style loss and regulatory minimum provision remain distinct concepts. [RBI 2026 ECL Directions, paragraphs 82–95]

**UK** — IFRS 9 ECL measurement and PRA capital-model requirements serve different objectives; regulatory parameters should not be imported into accounting without an accounting-consistent bridge. [IFRS 9; BCBS guidance]

**EU** — EU banks applying IFRS 9 also operate under prudential capital requirements; EBA has separate guidance for IRB PD/LGD estimation, reinforcing the distinction between accounting and regulatory parameter frameworks. [EBA PD/LGD Guidelines; EBA ECL Guidelines]

**US** — CECL likewise measures accounting expected losses under Topic 326, while US regulatory-capital parameters and capital rules have separate prudential purposes. [ASC 326; Interagency ACL policy]
