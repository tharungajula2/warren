Under SARFAESI, upon default, an asset reconstruction company (or the bank itself) can issue a notice to the borrower (and guarantor) demanding payment within 60 days. If not paid, the creditor can auction the pledged assets (e.g. sell the mortgaged factory). The sale proceeds pay off the debt. Borrowers can appeal before DRTs (Debt Recovery Tribunals) if they believe wrongful action. SARFAESI’s enforcement is therefore final, with limited appeals – it is potent. Banks often syndicate bad loans to ARCs (Asset Reconstruction Companies) which use SARFAESI to reorganize or sell assets.

For lenders, SARFAESI provides collateral realisation. Practically, banks must follow RBI’s “change of management” norms if a corporate account being taken over exceeds a threshold. RBI also requires lenders to classify any SARFAESI-induced partial recovery appropriately (e.g. as standard if current, else further NPA reduction). In stress scenarios, SARFAESI is often a precursor to invocation of IBC or other legal recovery if needed.

### Permanent rule

A secured lender can take possession of pledged collateral if the borrower defaults and does not repay after notice. The process must follow SARFAESI procedures (notice, sale, use funds to settle debt).

### Permanent trap

Trying to use SARFAESI on unsecured loans – it only applies to secured advances. Also, failing to issue the proper notice period (60 days) or not following RBI’s change-of-management rules when seizing a company.

### Never confuse

**SARFAESI** — enforcement law letting secured creditors seize and sell collateral after default (without courts).
**Debt Recovery Tribunal (DRT)** — quasi-judicial body that borrowers can approach to challenge SARFAESI or other recovery actions.

### Jurisdiction variants

**IN** — Unique to India (introduced 2002). No direct analog in US/UK; other countries rely on normal foreclosure laws.
