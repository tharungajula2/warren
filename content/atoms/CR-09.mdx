Default need not be permanent. A borrower may restore arrears, recover financial capacity and resume sustainable contractual performance. A **cure** occurs when the applicable framework permits the exposure or obligor to return from default to non-default.

Cure is not simply "DPD became zero". A payment may eliminate current arrears while the obligor remains financially distressed, under restructuring, or otherwise unlikely to pay. Robust frameworks therefore require evidence that the original default conditions have ceased and that renewed performance is credible. Regulatory regimes may impose probation or monitoring periods before return to non-default.

A **re-default** occurs when a cured exposure subsequently satisfies the default definition again. Re-default matters because a superficially high cure rate can conceal unstable cures. A portfolio in which half of cured borrowers fail again soon afterwards behaves differently from one in which cures are durable.

The default lifecycle must therefore preserve dates and states rather than only a current flag. At minimum, useful history distinguishes first default, cure, subsequent re-default and recovery/write-off outcomes. Destroying prior-default history when an account cures biases default-rate estimation, obscures restructuring performance and weakens behavioural models.

India illustrates the distinction between mere payment activity and prudential upgradation. RBI's commercial-bank rules state that, where a borrower has multiple facilities with a bank, NPA accounts are upgraded to standard only when entire arrears of interest and principal relating to all credit facilities have been repaid, subject to additional rules for specified restructuring cases.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Cure must be defined independently of the original default trigger and must preserve the historical fact that default occurred.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Resetting the customer's record to "never defaulted" after cure creates survivorship bias in PD, migration and recovery analysis.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Cure** — a qualifying return from default to non-default.

**Temporary arrears clearance** — a payment that removes overdue amounts but may not satisfy broader cure or viability criteria.
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="IN">
— RBI commercial-bank upgradation from NPA to standard generally requires full repayment of arrears of interest and principal; where several facilities exist with the bank, arrears across all such facilities must be cleared, with special restructuring rules applying where relevant. </Delta>

<Delta j="UK">
— PRA default guidance requires policies for return to non-default and continued consistency with the applicable regulatory definition. [PRA default framework]

<Delta j="EU">
— EBA definition-of-default Guidelines prescribe return-to-non-default criteria and probation requirements. The Guidelines were amended in May 2026; the current scenario-specific probation requirement must therefore be read from EBA/GL/2016/07 as amended by EBA/GL/2026/05 rather than from superseded versions. [EBA/GL/2026/05]
</Delta>

<Delta j="US">
— under Federal Reserve Advanced Approaches rules, a wholesale obligor remains in default until the institution has reasonable assurance of repayment and performance for contractual principal and interest; corresponding retail rules use an exposure-level standard. [12 CFR Part 217 Subpart E]
</Delta>
