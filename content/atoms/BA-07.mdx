When a process fails or a requirement isn’t met, RCA helps prevent recurrence by fixing the core issue. The BA collects data about the problem, then uses a structured approach: for example, the **5 Whys** technique simply asks “Why?” repeatedly until the fundamental cause is found.  Or use a **Fishbone (Ishikawa) diagram**, where the team brainstorms possible causes in categories (People, Process, Systems, etc.) and traces them back to root factors. 

For instance, if a loan payment is missed, an RCA might reveal that data mismatches caused a calculation error (one of many possible answers). The BA should lead the team through questioning until the real cause (e.g. a misconfigured data field) is identified.  Document findings in an action plan to resolve the root issue and not just the symptom.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Always go deeper: fix the root cause, not just the immediate error. Use structured RCA (5 Whys, fishbone, flow analysis) to uncover underlying factors.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Stopping the analysis when a temporary fix is found. For example, just patching a software bug (symptom) without discovering that it was caused by a flawed business rule mapping.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Symptom** vs **Root Cause**:  
A *Symptom* is the visible error (e.g. a broken report). The *Root Cause* is the underlying reason (e.g. a missing data mapping rule). They are often mistakenly treated as the same.
</NeverConfuse>
