Customer Identification is the front-line of AML. Under law, banks cannot open a new account without verifying who the customer is. For example, India’s RBI KYC guidelines require banks to follow strict customer identification procedures when establishing relationships. In the US, the BSA’s CIP Rule mandates collecting at least name, date of birth, address and government ID number for each new customer. Common documents used include passports, government IDs (like PAN in India), and for companies – incorporation papers.  

The KYC process typically involves: customer provides identity documents; bank checks authenticity (e.g. original ID); and records a copy. For non-residents or politically exposed persons, additional checks may apply (see Atom 8 on PEP). If identity cannot be verified, the bank must refuse the account or transaction. All this information is documented. For example, RBI explicitly mandates that banks verify identity and maintain copies of proof-of-identity and address for every customer before business is undertaken.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Collect at minimum name, address, date of birth, and official ID when opening any account. Always record this information and keep it updated.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Relying solely on customer-supplied documents without independent verification. Always compare the presented ID to official records or via KYC registries (like India’s CKYCR) to avoid forgeries.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="KYC/CIP (Customer ID)" b="Customer Due Diligence (CDD)">
**KYC/CIP (Customer ID)** — initial verification step using IDs and documents.  
---  
**Customer Due Diligence (CDD)** — broader process including ongoing risk assessment and monitoring (not just initial ID). Novices often think KYC and CDD are identical, but CDD encompasses the whole relationship lifecycle.
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="IN">
— RBI’s Master Direction: banks must complete Customer Identification Procedure (CIP) and KYC before any transaction. Uses Aadhaar or documents.
</Delta>

<Delta j="UK">
— MLR 2017 requires regulated firms to verify customer identities (CIP) at account opening and to identify BOs.
</Delta>

<Delta j="EU">
— AML Directives mandate customer identification for all new accounts and significant transactions.
</Delta>

<Delta j="US">
— The BSA’s CIP rule requires US banks to collect name, DOB, address and TIN/ID number for each new account holder.
</Delta>
