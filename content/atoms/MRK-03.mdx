Instead of revaluing portfolios for every risk shock, traders and risk managers express exposure in sensitivities to risk factors.  **Delta risk** is the linear change in value per unit move in the underlying (e.g. bond yield or stock price).  **Vega risk** measures the change in instrument value for a change in implied volatility (important for options or caps).  **Curvature risk** (sometimes called gamma/volga) captures the additional non-linear loss beyond the linear delta approximation when large shocks or optionality is present.

For example, an at-the-money option has high vega and curvature: a spike in volatility or a large price move causes loss beyond the delta estimate.  FRTB explicitly models separate charges for delta, vega, and curvature in each risk class.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Key market risk sensitivities are delta, vega, and curvature; all must be quantified and aggregated.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Ignoring vega and curvature in an options portfolio and using only delta risk (underestimating true risk).
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Delta** — linear sensitivity to a small move in the underlying price; **Curvature** — additional non-linear effect (the loss beyond delta when larger shocks or optionality occur).
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** The definitions of delta, vega, and curvature risk are set by the Basel framework and apply universally.
</Rule>

<Recall
  q="What is delta risk in market risk?"
