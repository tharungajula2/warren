### Core mechanism

Two superficially “off-balance-sheet” objects create different risks:

| Object | Uncertainty | Main treasury risk |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Fixed-rate loan commitment / pipeline | whether, when and how much business closes | rate moves before booking; hedge may over/under-shoot |
| Undrawn credit commitment | whether and when customer draws | stressed cash outflow and funding need |

**Pipeline risk** appears when treasury hedges expected future originations but actual completion differs. If 100 of expected fixed-rate loans are hedged and only 60 close, the hedge can become a 40 excess position.

**Drawdown risk** is different. CR-08 → commitments create exposure when customers use available limits. In liquidity stress, draw behaviour can rise exactly when market funding becomes scarce.

### Flow

```text
Commitment / forecast exists
    ↓
Behavioural probability: close? draw? when? amount?
    ↓
Scenario-specific future balance
    ↓
Interest-rate hedge need + contingent cash need
    ↓
IRRBB / liquidity stress / FTP
```

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Behavioural optionality · credit commitments
This:       Uncertain future balance-sheet formation
Downstream: IRRBB scenarios · liquidity stress · contingent FTP
```

### Formula

```text
Expected funded amount = committed amount × expected draw/close proportion

Expected funded amount is a planning quantity, not a promise that the same proportion will hold in stress.

Draw proportion ↑ → liquidity need ↑.

Pipeline fallout ↑ after anticipatory hedging → residual hedge exposure ↑.
```

### Permanent rule

An unfunded position can still create a current risk if the bank has committed a price, capacity or future cash payment.

### Never confuse [Pipeline risk] and [Commitment drawdown risk]

**Pipeline risk** — future business may not book as forecast, leaving a rate or hedge mismatch.

---

**Commitment drawdown risk** — a customer may convert an undrawn facility into a funded cash requirement.

### Permanent trap

Using base-case drawdown to size stress liquidity. Contingent facilities often become most valuable to customers during the same conditions in which the bank’s own funding is under pressure.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta in mechanism. Regulatory LCR and stress frameworks prescribe different drawdown factors by exposure type.
