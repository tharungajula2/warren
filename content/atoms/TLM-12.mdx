A bank has **funding liquidity** when it can meet payment obligations as they come due. Failure can occur because deposits leave, wholesale funding does not roll, collateral calls rise, committed credit lines are drawn or other cash needs exceed available resources. Basel defines bank liquidity as the ability to fund asset increases and meet obligations when due without unacceptable losses.

**Market liquidity** describes the ease with which an asset can be sold or monetised. An asset may be economically sound but difficult to sell quickly except at a steep discount. Bid-ask spreads can widen, market depth can disappear, secured lenders can increase haircuts and transactions large relative to market capacity can move prices.

The risks interact. A funding outflow forces the bank to raise cash. If external borrowing is unavailable, it may sell securities. Forced selling into an illiquid market produces losses. Those losses can weaken confidence, trigger more funding withdrawals or reduce collateral capacity, creating a feedback loop.

The distinction also explains why “assets exceed liabilities” does not settle a liquidity question. Solvency is principally about whether asset value is sufficient relative to obligations and capital. Liquidity is about whether cash can be produced at the required time. A bank can own valuable long-term assets and still be unable to convert them to immediate settlement cash without severe loss.

Treasury therefore manages both sources of cash and the monetisability of assets. A liquidity buffer must not merely look valuable on a normal-day balance sheet: assets need sufficient market depth, credit quality, operational availability and lack of encumbrance to generate cash in stress. Basel’s HQLA and liquidity principles are built around this distinction.

### Permanent rule

Funding liquidity concerns obtaining cash on time; market liquidity concerns converting positions into cash at tolerable value.

### Permanent trap

Calling a security “liquid” solely because it has a quoted market price in normal conditions.

### Never confuse

**Funding liquidity risk** — inability to meet cash obligations when due without unacceptable loss.
---
**Market liquidity risk** — inability to transact or monetise an asset quickly at a price close to its expected value.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** The economic distinction is universal.
