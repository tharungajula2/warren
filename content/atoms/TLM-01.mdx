A corporate treasury primarily raises funding, manages surplus cash, foreign-exchange exposures and financing risks created by a non-financial operating business. A bank treasury sits inside a financial intermediary whose assets and liabilities are largely financial claims. Deposits arrive with one pattern of tenor, optionality and repricing; loans and securities create another. Treasury must make the aggregate balance sheet fundable and liquid while controlling the resulting risks.

The exact organisational split differs, but four functions recur. **Funding** obtains wholesale or internal funding and manages the liability structure. **Liquidity management** ensures cash obligations can be met, maintains liquid-asset buffers and manages collateral. **Asset-liability management (ALM)** measures structural mismatches in maturity and interest-rate repricing. **Markets activity** executes transactions used to invest liquidity, raise funding or hedge exposures. Basel liquidity principles explicitly require banks to identify the full range of liquidity risks, maintain a liquid-asset cushion and allocate liquidity costs, benefits and risks to significant business activities.

Treasury therefore sees customer businesses differently from the customer-facing unit. A lending desk sees an asset earning a customer rate. Treasury sees a stream of cash flows requiring funding for a particular behavioural tenor, consuming liquidity under stress and potentially creating interest-rate risk. A deposit business sees a product margin. Treasury sees a funding source whose stability, repricing behaviour and withdrawal risk must be estimated.

Treasury normally acts within limits rather than trying to eliminate every mismatch. Some maturity transformation is intrinsic to banking. The objective is to decide which risks the bank is willing and able to retain, which should be transferred internally through funds transfer pricing, and which should be hedged or funded externally.

### Permanent rule

A bank treasury manages the consequences of the whole balance sheet, not merely a portfolio of securities.

### Permanent trap

Treating treasury profit as the objective and forgetting that treasury is also the bank’s central allocator and controller of funding and liquidity risk.

### Never confuse

**Bank treasury** — the function managing structural funding, liquidity, balance-sheet risk and related market execution.

---

**Corporate treasury** — the function financing and managing financial risks of a primarily non-financial operating business.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Organisational charts vary, but the economic role arises from banking intermediation rather than jurisdiction-specific mathematics.
