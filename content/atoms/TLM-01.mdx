### Core mechanism

FND-01 → balance sheet = assets funded by liabilities and equity.

| Customer activity creates | Treasury sees | Treasury response |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Fixed-rate loan | funding need + repricing mismatch | fund, hedge, price through FTP |
| Non-maturity deposit | uncertain tenor + administered-rate behaviour | model stability, beta and lag |
| Undrawn facility | contingent cash need | hold/price standby liquidity |
| Foreign-currency asset | currency funding + FX mismatch | fund/hedge by currency |
| Securities portfolio | liquidity buffer + collateral + rate sensitivity | optimise usability and risk |

Treasury does not try to remove every mismatch. Banking earns partly by transforming maturity and liquidity; the control problem is deciding which mismatches are intended, priced and supportable, and which must be transferred or hedged.

### Ownership

| Role | Owns |
| :--- | :--- |
| Business lines | customer pricing and origination within limits |
| Treasury / ALM | aggregate funding, liquidity and structural positions |
| ALCO | balance-sheet risk decisions and limits within delegated authority |
| Independent risk | challenge, limit framework and independent monitoring |
| Board / board risk committee | risk appetite and ultimate oversight |

### Permanent rule

Treasury manages the consequences of the whole balance sheet, not merely a securities portfolio or a dealing desk.

### Never confuse [Bank treasury] and [Corporate treasury]

**Bank treasury** — manages structural risks created by financial intermediation itself.

---

**Corporate treasury** — finances and manages financial risks of a primarily non-financial operating business.

### Permanent trap

Judging treasury only by its own profit. A central treasury can maximise its desk result while mispricing funding or allowing business lines to create risks that damage the bank.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta. Organisational charts differ, but the economic control problem comes from banking intermediation.
