### Core mechanism

```text
Risk inventory
    ↓
What Pillar 1 captures
    ↓
Residual / under-captured material risks
    ↓
Quantification and stress
    ↓
Internal capital adequacy assessment
    ↓
Board-approved capital plan
    ↓
Supervisory review and challenge
    ↓
Capital / governance / risk-management response
```

Typical Pillar 2 concerns include concentration, interest-rate risk in the banking book, weaknesses in governance or risk management, model limitations and risks that are material but not fully captured by Pillar 1.

The order prevents double counting: identify the risk, identify the Pillar 1 coverage already present, then assess the residual. Pillar 2 is not a generic percentage added because "Pillar 1 is conservative".

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Pillar 1 minimum · concentration · risk appetite
This:       ICAAP and supervisory completeness check
Downstream: Firm-specific capital · buffers · stress capital planning
```

### Ownership

| Role | Produces / owns |
| :--- | :--- |
| Business and risk owners | Risk identification and credible management actions |
| Finance / capital | Capital projections and resources |
| Risk function | ICAAP methodology and challenge |
| Board | Approves risk appetite and capital adequacy |
| Supervisor | Independently reviews and intervenes |
| Internal audit | Assesses governance and control effectiveness |

### Permanent rule

Management owns ICAAP; the supervisor reviews it. Supervisory review does not transfer responsibility for capital adequacy away from the bank.

### Never confuse [ICAAP] and [Pillar 1 calculation]

**ICAAP** — forward-looking assessment of overall capital adequacy for the bank's actual risks.

---

**Pillar 1 calculation** — prescribed minimum capital methodology.

### Permanent trap

Starting ICAAP with a desired capital percentage and inventing risks to justify it reverses the process.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta in the Basel principles. Local frameworks determine the legal form of Pillar 2 requirements, expectations and supervisory communications.
