A **Business Requirements Document (BRD)** is the formal specification of business needs. It typically contains an executive summary, background, business objectives (e.g. compliance goals), scope, assumptions, and a list of requirements from the business perspective. Requirements in a BRD may include functional needs (e.g. “system shall generate monthly reports by day 5”) and non-functional needs (e.g. performance, audit trails). Importantly, the BRD states *what* is needed, not *how* to build it. It avoids technical solution detail (that belongs in a functional spec).  

Each requirement in the BRD should be clear and include acceptance criteria (conditions for success). For instance, instead of saying “system should be fast,” a BRD requirement would say “System must respond to user queries within 2 seconds” (with that criterion). This precision avoids ambiguity and ensures testability. Also include references to regulatory rules if applicable, and note any impacts (e.g. compliance reporting).  

&lt;Flow&gt;
Sample BRD section:
Objective: Improve AML screening efficiency.
Requirement: System should screen 100% of new accounts for PEP status within 1 hour of creation (Acceptance: Demonstrate 1-hour turnaround in test).
&lt;/Flow&gt;

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
A BRD defines *business needs and constraints*. It must not prescribe the solution. Document each requirement with its own acceptance criteria to make it verifiable.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Mixing requirements with design. For example, writing “the system shall use rule X to calculate fee” (that’s a design decision) instead of “calculate fee per business rule.” Also avoid low-level detail or UI mockups in the BRD.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Business Requirements** vs **Functional Specification**:  
A *Business Requirement* describes what the business must have (high-level need or rule). A *Functional Specification* details how the system will fulfill those needs. They are different deliverables; the BRD should not become a development spec.
</NeverConfuse>

<Recall
  q="What should you never include in a BRD?"
  a="Solution design or technical details. For example, omit UI designs or code logic."
/>

<Recall
  q="What makes a requirement in a BRD testable?"
  a="Including clear acceptance criteria (conditions for success)."
/>
