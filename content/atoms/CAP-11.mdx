**Credit risk RWA** captures the risk that borrowers, counterparties or issuers fail to perform as required. It is usually the largest RWA category in a traditional banking book and can be calculated through standardised or, where permitted, internal-ratings-based methods.

**Market risk RWA** addresses losses arising from movements in market risk factors for positions within the applicable trading-book and market-risk perimeter. Its measurement is not the same as simply risk-weighting a loan.

**Operational risk RWA** addresses losses arising from inadequate or failed processes, people and systems or from external events under the applicable operational-risk framework. The Basel finalisation replaced prior multiple operational-risk approaches with a standardised approach in the global standard.

**Credit valuation adjustment risk**, or CVA risk, concerns changes in the market value of counterparty-credit adjustments for covered derivatives and similar transactions. It is related to counterparty credit risk but is not identical to the default-loss capital charge: one concerns mark-to-market changes in credit valuation, the other the counterparty’s failure to perform. Basel contains a separate CVA capital framework.

Basel’s minimum-capital calculation aggregates the prescribed RWA components to produce the denominator against which capital ratios are measured. The calculation should therefore be visualised as several risk engines feeding one capital ratio, not as one universal risk-weight formula.

```text
+------------------+   +------------------+
| Credit RWA       |   | Market RWA       |
+------------------+   +------------------+
          \              /
           v            v
         +------------------+
         | Total RWA        |
         +------------------+
           ^            ^
          /              \
+------------------+   +------------------+
| Operational RWA  |   | CVA RWA          |
+------------------+   +------------------+
```

### Permanent rule

The four RWA components measure different risk types before being aggregated into the common risk-based capital denominator.

### Permanent trap

Treating CVA risk as merely another name for counterparty default risk misses the mark-to-market credit-spread risk that the CVA framework is designed to capture.

### Never confuse

**Counterparty credit default risk** — risk of loss because the counterparty fails to perform.

---
**CVA risk** — risk of loss from changes in the credit valuation adjustment before or without counterparty default.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Detailed implementation dates and methods vary, but credit, market, operational and CVA risk are the core risk-based capital components in the Basel finalisation architecture.
