### Core mechanism

```text
Exposure amount
    ↓
Applicable regulatory treatment
    ↓
Risk weight
    ↓
RWA
    ↓
Capital ratio denominator
```

| Quantity | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| Exposure amount | Regulatory amount to which the capital treatment applies |
| Risk weight | Prudential scaling factor |
| RWA | Exposure translated into the risk-based denominator |
| RWA density | RWA divided by a chosen exposure measure |

A 100% risk weight means one unit of exposure creates one unit of RWA before other adjustments. It does not mean 100% PD, 100% LGD or 100% expected loss. Likewise, a low risk weight can reduce risk-based capital without reducing the leverage exposure.

### Formula

```text
RWA = exposure amount × applicable risk weight

RWA density = RWA / exposure measure

Higher risk weight → higher RWA → lower capital ratio, all else equal.
```

### Permanent rule

Risk weights calibrate prudential capital intensity; they do not estimate expected credit loss.

### Never confuse [Risk weight] and [Probability of default]

**Risk weight** — regulatory scaling factor used in calculating RWA.

---

**Probability of default** — likelihood of default over a governed horizon.

### Permanent trap

Reading a 150% risk weight as "the regulator expects a 150% loss" confuses a capital calibration with a loss forecast.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta in the arithmetic. Exposure classes, prescribed weights and permitted methodologies differ by rulebook.

  q="What does a 50% risk weight do to an exposure of 100?"
  a="It produces 50 of RWA before other applicable adjustments."
