A loan can contain more economics than its headline coupon. There may be an initial price different from principal, fees integral to the yield, transaction costs, instalment timing and a final repayment. The **effective interest rate** expresses those relevant cash flows as one rate that solves a present-value equation.

In simplified form, for an initial carrying amount \(P\) and future relevant cash flows \(CF_t\), EIR \(i\) solves:

\[
P=\sum_t\frac&#123;CF_t&#125;&#123;(1+i)^t&#125;
\]

This makes EIR an **internal rate of return** for the specified cash-flow definition.

Suppose 100 is advanced and the only contractual receipt is 110 one year later. The EIR is 10%. If the initial amount and contractual cash flows differ because of qualifying fees or transaction costs, the EIR can differ from the stated coupon even when contractual interest payments use that coupon.

Under IFRS 9, the effective interest method uses an EIR determined through the standard's specified estimated cash flows and incorporates fees, transaction costs and other premiums or discounts that are integral to that rate. The accounting definition controls which items enter the calculation. The general mathematical concept nevertheless remains the same: solve for the rate that equates a present value to the relevant carrying amount.

EIR is therefore not merely "the interest rate printed on the contract". Nor is it automatically the market yield observed today. Once an accounting EIR has been established, the relevant framework determines when it remains fixed and when special rules apply.

```text
+----------------------+
| Initial carrying     |
| amount               |
+----------------------+
          ^
          |
          | find rate i such that
          |
+----------------------+
| PV of relevant       |
| future cash flows    |
| discounted at i      |
+----------------------+

PV = initial amount
=&gt; i = effective interest rate
```

### Permanent rule

EIR is defined by a present-value equality across a specified set of cash flows; changing the cash-flow definition can change the rate.

### Permanent trap

Using the contractual coupon as the EIR without checking fees, premiums, discounts, transaction costs and timing.

### Never confuse

**Coupon rate** — the contractual rate or formula used to determine stated interest payments.
---
**Effective interest rate** — the yield solving the relevant present-value equation; it can differ from the coupon.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** The rate-solving mathematics is universal; accounting frameworks determine the precise cash flows included and subsequent accounting use.
