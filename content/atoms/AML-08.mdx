Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs) are individuals who hold or have held important government or international positions (e.g. heads of state, ministers, senior military or judiciary).  The FATF warns that such positions carry a higher risk of bribery or corruption, as PEPs could abuse power for personal gain. Therefore banks must apply additional scrutiny to PEPs.  This includes not only the PEP, but also close family members and known associates (they can be vehicles for laundering).  

For example, banks usually collect detailed information and source-of-funds for PEPs. Enhanced due diligence (EDD) means higher risk ratings, senior approval for maintaining the account, and more frequent transaction monitoring.  Many rulebooks (e.g. UK, India, EU) require annual reviews of PEP accounts.  Overlooking a customer’s PEP status is a common compliance mistake.  FATF Recommendation 12 explicitly calls for risk-based measures for all foreign PEPs (with many countries extending it to domestic PEPs).

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Identify all customers who are PEPs (domestic or foreign as required by law) and require senior management approval to onboard them. Apply EDD: verify source of wealth, increase monitoring frequency, and record approvals.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Ignoring *former* officials or relatives. PEP status often persists after leaving office, and close family can be just as risky. Never assume “just because a PEP is friendly, he’s low risk.”
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="PEP (Politically Exposed Person)" b="Important public figure">
**PEP (Politically Exposed Person)** — someone currently or formerly holding a high government/party position.  
---  
**Important public figure** — e.g. a celebrity or top banker. They are not PEPs unless they have held public office. People often erroneously think any prominent person is a PEP.
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="IN">
— PEPs are defined under FIU-IND guidelines (aligned with PMLA Rule 9(13)) and include domestic officials and judiciary. Banks in India treat both Indian and foreign PEPs as high-risk.
</Delta>

<Delta j="UK">
— The Money Laundering Regulations define PEP to include all domestic and foreign PEPs and their families. EDD is mandatory for all PEP relationships under Reg 12.
</Delta>

<Delta j="EU">
— 4AMLD/5AMLD cover domestic, foreign, and international PEPs, including relatives; firms must apply EDD as per FATF guidance.
</Delta>

<Delta j="US">
— Under FinCEN, only *foreign* PEPs (and their relatives/associates) are explicitly defined for mandatory EDD. (Domestic politicians aren’t separately covered by CIP, though banks may still flag them internally.)
</Delta>
