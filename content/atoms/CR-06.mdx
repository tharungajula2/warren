**Days past due (DPD)** is a clock. It counts how long a contractual payment has remained unpaid after its due date under the applicable convention. **Arrears** is an amount: the sum of required payments that remain unpaid. An account can therefore have ₹1 of arrears and 40 DPD, or a large arrears balance and a different DPD count.

A **delinquency bucket** converts continuous DPD into categories such as current, 1–30, 31–60 and 61–90 days. Bucketing makes operational monitoring, collections and migration analysis easier, but the cut-offs are conventions rather than laws of nature.

The starting point of the clock matters. RBI requires borrower accounts to be flagged overdue through the day-end process for the due date. Its current commercial-bank directions illustrate an amount due on 31 March becoming overdue on that date if not received before the day-end process, then reaching SMA-1 after 30 continuous days and SMA-2 after 60.

Revolving facilities require different logic because there may be no single scheduled principal instalment. RBI therefore uses an **out-of-order** concept for cash-credit and overdraft accounts, including conditions involving persistent excess over sanctioned limit/drawing power, absence of credits and insufficient credits to cover interest.

DPD should be treated as an observed behavioural state, not an estimate of ultimate loss. A borrower at 60 DPD may cure; another borrower at 0 DPD may already be unlikely to pay because of insolvency, fraud or another serious credit event.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
DPD is a time-since-missed-payment measure; default status requires the applicable default rule.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
A common operational error is to calculate DPD from statement date, reporting date or month-end instead of the contractual due date and specified day-end convention.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Arrears** — the unpaid contractual amount.

**Days past due** — the elapsed delinquency time associated with an unpaid obligation.
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="IN">
— RBI commercial-bank monitoring uses SMA-0 up to 30 days overdue, SMA-1 more than 30 and up to 60, and SMA-2 more than 60 and up to 90; revolving facilities use corresponding out-of-order logic. [RBI IRACP Directions 2025]
</Delta>

<Delta j="UK">
— PRA default treatment uses material past-due obligations and a 90-day default criterion under the current CRR-derived framework; arrears reporting buckets are not themselves the complete definition of default. [PRA PS7/19]
</Delta>

<Delta j="EU">
— CRR reporting distinguishes performing and non-performing exposures across past-due bands, including exposures that are unlikely to pay even when no more than 90 days past due. [CRR Article 178; Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2021/637]
</Delta>

<Delta j="US">
— US regulatory frameworks use multiple delinquency thresholds depending on purpose; Advanced Approaches retail default, for example, is not uniformly a 90-day rule. [12 CFR Part 217 Subpart E]
</Delta>
