Challenger and benchmark models serve to guard against model errors.  For example, a bank’s existing PD model (the “incumbent”) might be compared to a newly-developed simpler model or an industry benchmark.  This comparison can be part of validation or periodic review.  The challenger might use a subset of the data or a different algorithm.  The goal is not necessarily to replace the primary model, but to ensure the primary model is still competitive.

Regulators encourage such comparisons.  The PCA pillar 2 common terminology refers to “benchmarking” as a supervisory tool.  While SR 26-2 does not mandate challengers, it lists ongoing monitoring and outcome analysis as good practices.  RBI’s draft also mentions “benchmarking” in its requirements.  In practice, a challenger model can help identify when a model has become stale.  If performance metrics of the challenger are significantly better on hold-out data, this may trigger a model review or redevelopment.

Benchmarking can also refer to comparing to industry models or historical performance.  The key is to not rely blindly on one model.  Banks should have a process to re-run or update challengers periodically for major models.  (Note: having challengers is not a universal requirement; smaller banks may simply revalidate the main model more often.)

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Maintain at least one independent benchmark (or challenger) for each material model to verify it still performs well.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Treating the primary model’s validation in isolation.  Example: **believing a long-used model is fine without testing a new approach**.  A challenger might reveal weaknesses not caught by routine checks.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Benchmark model" b="Primary model">
**Benchmark model** — an external or internally developed reference used for comparison.
---
**Primary model** — the model in actual use.
They get mixed up when a benchmark is incorrectly used for decisions; remember the primary is the one governing business.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Challenger/benchmark practice is recommended by regulators worldwide as part of model governance.  (ECB’s internal model guidelines mention the use of simpler collateral models, for example.)
</Rule>
