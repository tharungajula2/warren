Value at Risk (VaR) is a statistical risk measure.  For a chosen confidence level α (e.g. 99%) and horizon (e.g. 10 days), VaRα is the loss L such that there is a (1−α) probability the actual loss will exceed L.  In practice, a bank says “99% 10-day VaR” of $X means there is a 1% chance the 10-day loss will exceed $X.  

Banks typically compute VaR daily for trading books to summarize total market risk exposure. Basel rules historically used 10-day 99% VaR (12-month window) for capital. FRTB replaces VaR with ES, but VaR remains key for backtesting and internal limits.  

&lt;Flow&gt;
VaR computation (conceptual):
1. Gather P&amp;amp;L distribution over horizon (simulation or historical returns).
2. Sort losses from smallest (best) to largest (worst).
3. Identify the 1% quantile (for 99% VaR).
4. VaR = loss at that quantile (not exceeded with 99% confidence).
&lt;/Flow&gt;

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
VaRα is always defined as the (100−α)th percentile loss over the chosen horizon.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Treating VaR as the maximum possible loss or ignoring that beyond-VaR losses can be larger.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Value at Risk (VaR)** — the loss not exceeded at the confidence level (loss threshold); **Expected Shortfall (ES)** — the expected loss *beyond* the VaR threshold (average tail loss).
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** The mathematical definition of VaR is universal across regulatory regimes.
</Rule>

<Recall
  q="What is VaR at confidence level α?"
  a="The largest loss not expected to be exceeded with probability α; i.e., the loss at the (1−α) quantile of the loss distribution."
/>

<Recall
  q="How is 99% VaR interpreted?"
  a="There is a 1% chance of losing more than the VaR amount over the specified horizon."
/>
