Operational resilience goes beyond traditional op risk. It focuses on ensuring that critical services (e.g. payments, trading systems, ATM networks) continue or quickly recover during major disruptions. Regulators in the UK define it as avoiding “intolerable harm to consumers and threats to market integrity when services are disrupted”. The cornerstone is mapping **Important Business Services (IBS)** – those that if disrupted would harm customers or the market. For each IBS, the bank sets an **impact tolerance** (e.g. max downtime or data loss beyond which harm becomes intolerable).

Firms must map all people, processes, technology, and third parties that support each IBS. Then they run severe-but-plausible scenario tests (e.g. cyberattack, data center fire, supplier outage) to see if they can stay within tolerances. For instance, recent examples like AWS and Azure outages are exactly the kind of severe scenarios firms must prepare for. Meeting impact tolerances might involve backup sites, rapid failover, or manual workarounds. Operational resilience is about systemic continuity planning and is a regulatory requirement (especially in UK’s PRA/FCA regime and under EU’s DORA) – it is a distinct discipline from day-to-day op risk management.

### Permanent rule

Identify and document each Important Business Service. For each, define a clear impact tolerance (not just time, but level of harm). Test resilience with scenario exercises at least annually, adjusting plans as needed.

### Permanent trap

Don’t confuse resilience with mere disaster recovery. Resilience is holistic (including customer harm assessment).  A common error is to plan only IT backups (DR) without considering how long customers can be without the service or what customer harm might occur.

### Never confuse

**Operational Resilience** — maintaining essential services during major events (focus on continuity/impact).
---
**Operational Risk** — managing losses from failures (focus on losses incurred).

### Jurisdiction variants

**UK** — PRA/FCA rules (PS21/3) mandate mapping IBS and setting impact tolerances.
**EU** — The Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) covers ICT resilience, requiring testing of critical functions.
**IN** — RBI issued Operational Resilience guidelines and mandated core business continuity requirements.
**US** — Regulators (Fed/OCC/SEC) encourage resilience planning (e.g. emergency preparedness rules) but no unified regime yet.
