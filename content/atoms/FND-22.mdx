### Core mechanism

| Layer | Owns | Test |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Policy | Intent, principles, mandatory outcomes, boundaries | What must be true? |
| Standard | Detailed mandatory criteria and control requirements | What exact requirement must be met? |
| Procedure | Operational sequence | How is the requirement performed? |
| Evidence / record | Proof of execution and decision | Can performance be demonstrated? |

A procedure cannot legitimately authorise something prohibited by its governing policy. A standard cannot silently widen a board-approved risk boundary. Lower-level documents implement higher-authority requirements; they do not override them.

### Flow

```text
External law / regulation
    ↓
Internal policy
    ↓
Standard / control requirements
    ↓
Procedure
    ↓
Execution
    ↓
Evidence / records
    ↓
Monitoring / assurance
```

### Ownership

| Role | Typical responsibility |
| :--- | :--- |
| Governing body / delegated authority | Approves highest-level policies within governance |
| Policy owner | Maintains content, scope, and review cycle |
| Control / standard owner | Defines testable mandatory requirements |
| Process owner | Maintains procedures and executes controls |
| Risk / compliance | Challenges framework and monitors within remit |
| Internal audit | Independently assesses design and operation |

### Permanent rule

Authority flows downward; evidence flows upward. Execution must be traceable to a valid requirement, and assurance must be able to trace the requirement to evidence.

### Never confuse [Document title] and [Document authority]

**Document title** — label such as policy, standard, framework, or manual.

---

**Document authority** — who approved it, what it governs, and whether it is mandatory.

### Never confuse [Procedure] and [Evidence]

**Procedure** — steps that should be performed.

---

**Evidence** — proof that the steps and required decisions actually occurred.

### Permanent trap

Treating a beautifully written procedure as proof of control effectiveness. A procedure describes intended execution; evidence and testing show whether it happened.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Institutions and regulators use different terminology, but no universal statutory taxonomy fixes these four labels across jurisdictions.
