A **policy** states what the institution requires and why. It usually establishes principles, scope, responsibilities, risk boundaries or mandatory outcomes. For example, a credit-risk policy might require independent approval above defined thresholds and prescribe high-level principles for acceptable lending.

A **standard** converts policy into more specific mandatory requirements. It might define minimum information needed for an approval, required control frequencies, quantitative thresholds, modelling conventions or documentation fields. A standard should be sufficiently precise that compliance can be tested.

A **procedure** describes how people or systems execute the requirement. It normally specifies a sequence: obtain information, perform checks, record evidence, obtain approvals, book the transaction and retain documentation. Procedures are closest to day-to-day operations and usually change more often than governing policy.

This three-level hierarchy is a governance convention, not a globally fixed legal taxonomy. One institution may call a document a "policy" where another calls similar content a "standard", "framework", "manual" or "rulebook". Regulatory texts can also use these words differently. The reliable test is therefore the document's **authority, mandatory force, scope, owner and approval route**, not its title.

The hierarchy should flow downward without contradiction. A procedure cannot legitimately authorise activity prohibited by the governing policy. A standard cannot silently widen risk appetite beyond an approved boundary. Where lower-level documents conflict with higher-authority requirements, the conflict must be resolved through governance rather than by choosing whichever text is operationally convenient.

Version control matters for the same reason. Users need to know which document is effective, who approved it, what it superseded and how exceptions are authorised.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Lower-authority documents may implement higher-authority requirements but cannot validly contradict them.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Treating the word "policy" as proof of board approval, or "procedure" as proof of low authority, without checking the institution's actual governance taxonomy.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Standard" b="Procedure">
**Standard** — a set of specific mandatory criteria translating higher-level requirements into testable controls.
---
**Procedure** — the operational sequence used to perform those requirements; detailed steps do not themselves establish higher authority.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** There is no universal statutory taxonomy fixing these three document labels; institution-specific governance and applicable local rules determine formal authority.
</Rule>
