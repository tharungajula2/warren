### Core mechanism

UTP trigger met first → Default now

Past-due clock reaches applicable material backstop first → Default now

Neither met → Continue non-default monitoring

The backstop constrains optimism. It preserves an objective point beyond which prolonged material non-payment cannot remain outside default merely because judgement has not acted.

### Flow

```text
Qualifying UTP → Default now
    ↓
OR
    ↓
Past-due clock → Material backstop reached → Default now
```

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   DPD · Materiality · UTP evidence
This:       Earlier of qualifying UTP or applicable mechanical backstop
Downstream: Default date · Cure clock · Model labels · Workout and reporting
```

### Permanent rule

The backstop is the latest mechanical trigger under its rule, not a grace period that overrides earlier qualitative default evidence.

### Never confuse [Backstop] and [Definition of default]

**Backstop** — an outer mechanical trigger based on elapsed delinquency under a specified rule.

---

**Definition of default** — the full set of qualitative and quantitative conditions governing entry to default.

### Permanent trap

Writing “default = 90 DPD” into data dictionaries suppresses early defaults, mixes products with different rules and trains models on a delayed target.

### Jurisdiction variants

#### IN (RBI)
For commercial banks, a term loan is classified NPA when interest and/or principal remains overdue for **more than 90 days**. RBI also prescribes distinct tests for OD/CC, working-capital irregularity, bills, agricultural exposures and other categories; asset classification is borrower-wise subject to specified exceptions.

#### UK (PRA)
The current UK CRR-derived default framework uses past-due and materiality rules alongside UTP. The updated PRA Basel 3.1 package, including the January 2026 SS3/24 version, takes effect on 1 January 2027.

#### EU
CRR Article 178 uses a past-due limb alongside UTP, supported by materiality rules. The EBA’s May 2026 amended Guidelines become applicable on 19 October 2026.

#### US
Advanced Approaches wholesale default includes more than 90 days past due on a material obligation, while specified retail categories use different mechanical thresholds; “90 days everywhere” is therefore wrong.

<Recall
  q="Why is 90 DPD called a backstop rather than the definition?"
  a="Because default can occur earlier through UTP and some categories use different mechanical rules."
/>

<Recall
  q="What logical operator joins the two Basel wholesale default limbs?"
  a="OR: either qualifying UTP or the material past-due trigger is sufficient."
/>

<Recall
  q="Why must data retain the actual trigger date?"
  a="Using a later 90-day date for an earlier UTP default distorts PD, cure and LGD timing."
/>
