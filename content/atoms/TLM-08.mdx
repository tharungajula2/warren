A **non-maturity deposit (NMD)** has no fixed contractual maturity at which the entire balance must be repaid. Transaction and savings balances are typical economic forms. Contractually, customers may be able to withdraw quickly. Behaviourally, a portion of an aggregate portfolio can remain persistent.

Two models are required. The **balance model** estimates how much funding is stable and for how long. The **repricing model** estimates how the administered deposit rate responds when market rates change. A useful concept is deposit **beta**: the proportion of a market-rate change eventually passed through to the customer rate. **Lag** describes how quickly that pass-through occurs.

Suppose market rates rise 100 basis points and a deposit rate historically rises only 40 basis points after a delay. Treating the deposit as instantly repricing by 100 basis points overstates funding-cost sensitivity. Treating the entire balance as fixed for five years can make the opposite error. Stability and repricing must be estimated separately.

Segmentation matters. Operational transaction balances can behave differently from rate-sensitive surplus balances; retail behaviour can differ from wholesale behaviour. Models therefore group accounts with sufficiently similar economic characteristics, estimate decay or stability and test behaviour under changing rate environments.

The danger is false precision. Historical stability observed during calm periods may not survive intense competition, digital withdrawals or customer concentration. Behavioural assumptions must therefore be governed, validated and stressed rather than treated as contractual facts. Basel IRRBB standards specifically recognise NMD optionality. RBI’s forthcoming framework prescribes behavioural treatment and, for its standardised methodology, caps core-deposit proportions and average maturities by category.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
An NMD’s contractual overnight maturity is not its behavioural ALM tenor.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Using a single “average life” for NMDs without separately modelling rate beta, lag and stressed balance decay.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Core deposit assumption" b="Contractual maturity">
**Core deposit assumption** — the estimated stable portion of an NMD portfolio used for behavioural modelling.
---
**Contractual maturity** — the legal withdrawal horizon, which for an NMD may be immediate despite behavioural stability.
</NeverConfuse>
