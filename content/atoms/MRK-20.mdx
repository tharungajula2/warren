A strong valuation control environment is vital.  Banks establish independent valuation committees to review model inputs and outputs.  **Independent Price Verification (IPV)** means having separate systems or personnel to compare book prices against external sources (vendors or brokers) daily.  Significant discrepancies trigger investigation and adjustments.  

Regulators (via accounting standards IFRS13/ASC820 and risk rules) define a fair value hierarchy:  
- *Level 1:* quoted market prices in active markets (highest reliability).  
- *Level 2:* observable inputs (e.g. dealer quotes, similar instruments).  
- *Level 3:* unobservable inputs (derived from models).  

Level 3 instruments (illiquid or complex) carry the highest valuation uncertainty and often the largest AVAs (see MRK-19).  Banks must document assumptions for Level 3 models, regularly backtest them against any transaction prices, and escalate issues.  In essence, valuation models fall under model risk management (MRM) frameworks, requiring validation and governance.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
All significant mark-to-market positions must be independently priced or verified each day.  Any Level 3 (non-observable) inputs require extensive model validation.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Using stale or self-generated prices without challenge, especially for illiquid positions.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Independent Price Verification (IPV)** — reconciling book prices with third-party data; **Model output** — output of a valuation model (which may feed Level 3 valuation).
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** These valuation controls and hierarchy concepts derive from accounting standards and are universally expected in sound practice.
</Rule>
