### Core mechanism

```text
Tier 1 capital
      ÷
Leverage exposure measure
      ↓
Leverage ratio
```

The denominator is not simply total accounting assets. Basel includes prescribed treatment for on-balance-sheet exposures, derivatives, securities financing transactions and off-balance-sheet items.

| Portfolio | Risk-based lens | Leverage lens |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| High-risk assets | Often high RWA | Exposure still enters denominator |
| Very low-risk assets | May create low RWA | Still consume leverage capacity |
| Model changes | Can alter RWA | Cannot remove the underlying exposure merely by lowering a risk weight |

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Tier 1 capital · exposure measurement
This:       Non-risk-based capital backstop
Downstream: Binding capital constraint · stress planning · local leverage requirements
```

### Formula

```text
Leverage ratio = Tier 1 capital / leverage exposure measure

Basel minimum = 3% under LEV20, before jurisdiction-specific higher requirements or buffers.
```

### Permanent rule

The leverage ratio complements risk-based capital; neither can be inferred from the other.

### Never confuse [Total assets] and [Leverage exposure measure]

**Total assets** — accounting balance-sheet quantity.

---

**Leverage exposure measure** — regulatory denominator including prescribed on- and off-balance-sheet exposures.

### Permanent trap

Using accounting assets as an exact denominator omits derivative, securities-financing and off-balance-sheet measurement rules.

### Jurisdiction variants

#### IN (RBI)
**As at August 2026:** RBI's 2025 capital directions prescribe **4% for D-SIBs and 3.5% for other covered commercial banks**. This position is in force.

#### UK, EU, US
Local leverage frameworks and overlays apply by firm category. The permanent concept is the same; exact requirements must be read from the live domestic rulebook.
