**Prudential regulation** concerns the financial resilience and safe operation of regulated institutions. Its tools can include capital, liquidity, large-exposure, governance and risk-management requirements. The Basel framework is an international prudential standard architecture, while national and regional authorities implement their own legally applicable regimes.

**Conduct regulation** concerns behaviour: how firms treat customers, design or distribute products, communicate, manage conflicts and comply with applicable market or consumer-protection obligations. The UK illustrates the institutional distinction clearly: the PRA is the prudential regulator for firms within its remit, while the FCA regulates conduct across a broad population of financial-services firms.

**Accounting standards** answer another question: how transactions and economic events enter general-purpose financial statements. They establish recognition, measurement, presentation and disclosure rules. The FASB's mission, for example, is financial accounting and reporting standard-setting for nongovernmental US entities, not prudential bank supervision.

The systems meet at important boundaries. Accounting values can be inputs into prudential measures, but supervisors can impose regulatory definitions, deductions and adjustments. A credit-loss allowance can affect accounting equity, while prudential rules determine how provisions interact with regulatory capital. A firm can therefore comply with an accounting standard yet breach a prudential requirement, or satisfy a capital rule yet breach a conduct obligation.

India provides the strongest warning against collapsing the categories. RBI has deferred Ind AS implementation for banks until further notice, so scheduled commercial-bank provisioning remains governed by RBI prudential norms rather than Ind AS 109. Relevant NBFCs applying Ind AS occupy a different accounting position.

### Permanent rule

Accounting compliance does not prove prudential compliance, and prudential compliance does not prove conduct compliance; each framework answers a different regulatory or reporting question.

### Permanent trap

Using an accounting label as though it determines prudential treatment automatically—for example, assuming an IFRS or Ind AS impairment allowance is identical to the regulatory provision or regulatory-capital deduction.

### Never confuse

**Prudential regulation** — requirements and supervision directed principally at institutional safety, soundness and financial resilience.

---

**Accounting standards** — financial-reporting rules governing recognition, measurement, presentation and disclosure; they supply important numbers but do not themselves constitute prudential supervision.

### Jurisdiction variants

**IN** — RBI combines major prudential and banking-conduct regulatory responsibilities within its statutory remit. Scheduled commercial banks remain outside Ind AS implementation following RBI's deferment and use RBI prudential provisioning norms; relevant Ind AS-applying NBFCs are treated separately. [RBI DBR.BP.BC.No.29/21.07.001/2018-19; RBI prudential directions; RBI NBFC Ind AS guidance]

**UK** — The PRA is the principal prudential regulator for banks and other firms within its statutory remit; the FCA is the principal conduct regulator across financial services. Applicable UK-adopted international accounting standards form a separate financial-reporting framework. [PRA prudential-regulation mandate; FCA mandate; UK Endorsement Board]

**EU** — The CRR/CRD architecture and EBA Single Rulebook form core elements of EU banking prudential regulation. The EBA also has consumer-protection responsibilities, while conduct supervision and enforcement can involve national and sector-specific authorities. EU-adopted IFRS under the IAS Regulation is a separate accounting framework for accounts within its scope. [EBA Single Rulebook; EBA Consumer Protection; Regulation (EC) No 1606/2002]

**US** — Prudential supervision is distributed among agencies according to charter and statutory remit; the Federal Reserve and OCC are major examples. Banking agencies also enforce specified consumer and compliance requirements, while FASB establishes US GAAP for nongovernmental entities rather than prudential rules. [Federal Reserve supervision framework; OCC mandate; FASB Mission]
