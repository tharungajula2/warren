### Core mechanism

| Framework | Question | Outputs |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Prudential | Can the institution remain safe, sound, resilient? | Capital · Liquidity · Governance · Risk requirements |
| Conduct | Is behaviour proper toward customers and markets? | Product · Disclosure · Sales · Market-conduct obligations |
| Accounting | How are economic events reported? | Recognition · Measurement · Presentation · Disclosure |

One item can cross all three: a credit-loss allowance affects accounting profit/equity; prudential rules determine regulatory treatment; conduct rules can govern the underlying product or servicing.

### Flow

```text
Economic activity
    ↓
Accounting → Financial reporting
Prudential → Resilience / supervision
Conduct → Behaviour / supervision
    ↓
Interacting outputs · Different questions
```

### Permanent rule

Never carry a label or treatment from one framework into another without checking the governing rule.

### Never confuse [Prudential regulation] and [Accounting standards]

**Prudential regulation** — safety, soundness, resilience, supervision.

---

**Accounting standards** — financial-statement recognition, measurement, presentation, disclosure.

### Never confuse [Prudential regulation] and [Conduct regulation]

**Prudential regulation** — whether the institution can safely bear and manage risk.

---

**Conduct regulation** — whether behaviour meets customer and market obligations.

### Permanent trap

Assuming an accounting allowance, reserve, or valuation label fixes regulatory-capital treatment. Prudential rules can adjust accounting inputs.

### Jurisdiction variants

| Jurisdiction | Boundary |
| :--- | :--- |
| IN | RBI carries major banking prudential and conduct-related roles. Scheduled commercial-bank reporting/provisioning follows the RBI framework; Ind AS 109 should not be assumed. |
| UK | PRA is principal prudential regulator within remit; FCA is principal conduct regulator and also has prudential responsibilities within remit; accounting standards are separate. |
| EU | CRR/CRD/EBA form core prudential architecture; ECB/SSM and national authorities supervise by remit; EU-adopted IFRS is separate for accounts within scope. |
| US | Federal/state prudential oversight is split by charter/activity; banking agencies also enforce specified consumer rules; FASB sets US GAAP, not bank supervision. |

<Recall
  q="What does prudential regulation primarily ask?"
  a="Whether the institution is safe, sound, and resilient under the applicable prudential framework."
/>

<Recall
  q="What does accounting primarily determine?"
  a="How economic events are recognised, measured, presented, and disclosed in financial statements."
/>

<Recall
