### Core mechanism

```text
Capital resources
    ↓
CET1 / Tier 1 / Total Capital
                    \
                     → Capital ratio
                    /
Credit + market + operational + other prescribed RWA
```

| Change | Ratio effect, all else equal |
| :--- | :--- |
| CET1 decreases | CET1 ratio decreases |
| RWA increases | All RWA-based ratios decrease |
| New eligible capital issued | Relevant numerator increases |
| Exposure runoff / lower risk weight | RWA may decrease |
| Provision expense | CET1 may decrease through retained earnings |
| Credit migration | RWA may increase |

The Basel minima are expressed as ratios to RWA: 4.5% CET1, 6% Tier 1 and 8% Total Capital before buffers and local additions. Buffers do not change the denominator; they raise the amount of CET1 the bank must maintain above the minimum stack.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Eligible capital · regulatory adjustments
This:       Numerator / denominator mechanics
Downstream: Risk weights · RWA engines · Pillar 1 · buffers
```

### Formula

```text
CET1 ratio = CET1 / RWA

Tier 1 ratio = Tier 1 capital / RWA

Total capital ratio = Total Capital / RWA

Ratio movement is driven by both numerator and denominator.
```

### Permanent rule

A capital ratio cannot be interpreted from the numerator alone; RWA is part of the prudential measurement.

### Never confuse [Capital amount] and [Capital ratio]

**Capital amount** — currency value of qualifying regulatory capital.

---

**Capital ratio** — qualifying regulatory capital divided by the prescribed RWA denominator.

### Permanent trap

Explaining a falling CET1 ratio only by "losses reduced CET1" can miss denominator inflation from credit migration, market moves or model changes.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta in the ratio mechanics. Binding minima, buffers and RWA methodologies vary by jurisdiction.

<Recall
  q="How can a bank's CET1 ratio fall without a CET1 loss?"
  a="RWA can rise while CET1 stays unchanged."

<Recall
