The **capital conservation buffer** creates a standing cushion above minimum capital. Under the Basel standard it is 2.5% of RWA and must be met with CET1. Its purpose is usability: a bank can enter the buffer during stress, but doing so increasingly constrains distributions so earnings are conserved and capital can be rebuilt.

The **countercyclical capital buffer** is macroprudential rather than permanently fixed. Authorities can raise it when system-wide credit conditions indicate excessive risk build-up and release it when conditions deteriorate. Basel sets an international framework in which the bank-specific CCyB reflects relevant jurisdictional rates and cross-border exposures.

**Systemic buffers or surcharges** address the additional damage that failure of a systemically important institution could cause. Their purpose is different from the conservation buffer: the added requirement reflects systemic externality rather than merely providing the same fixed cushion to every bank.

This produces a layered target. Management should distinguish minimum requirements from buffers because the consequences of breaching them differ. Entering a buffer can trigger distribution restrictions while the institution remains above minimum capital; breaching a minimum is a more severe prudential condition.

The exact stack is jurisdiction-specific. The United States, in particular, uses a stress capital buffer architecture for large firms rather than simply replicating the European combined-buffer structure.

```text
+-------------------------+
| Systemic / other buffer |
+-------------------------+
            |
+-------------------------+
| Countercyclical buffer  |
+-------------------------+
            |
+-------------------------+
| Conservation buffer     |
+-------------------------+
            |
+-------------------------+
| Regulatory minimums     |
+-------------------------+
```

### Permanent rule

A buffer sits above a minimum because it is intended to be usable before the minimum is breached.

### Permanent trap

Calling the conservation buffer an additional “minimum” obscures the graduated consequences that make a buffer usable in stress.

### Never confuse

**Minimum capital requirement** — the hard baseline prudential requirement.

---

**Capital buffer** — additional loss-absorbing capacity above the minimum, normally coupled with consequences that intensify as the buffer is used.

### Jurisdiction variants

**IN** — RBI’s 2025 commercial-bank capital directions prescribe a 2.5% CET1 capital conservation buffer and a countercyclical framework above India’s higher domestic minima. [RBI Capital Adequacy Directions 2025, Table 1 and buffer provisions]

**UK** — The PRA framework uses a combined-buffer architecture for firms in the ordinary regime; the separate SDDT regime taking effect on 1 January 2027 introduces a simplified capital-buffer structure. [PRA SS6/14 framework; PS4/26]

**EU** — CRD-based combined-buffer requirements include conservation and applicable countercyclical/systemic components, with distribution restrictions linked to failure to meet the combined buffer. [CRD Article 141 / EBA Single Rulebook]

**US** — Large-bank requirements incorporate a firm-specific stress capital buffer informed by supervisory stress testing; this differs structurally from simply adding the European combined-buffer stack. [Federal Reserve Annual Report, Supervision and Regulation]
