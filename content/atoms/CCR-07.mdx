When a counterparty defaults, trades do not immediately settle; the surviving party needs time to revalue or replace trades. The MPR covers this lag. For example, if a bank assumes a 10-day MPR, it calculates potential exposure assuming markets move during those 10 days. IM posted is meant to cover losses that could arise in that period. A longer MPR means greater PFE (since more days of potential move), so higher IM.

Other collateral terms: **Threshold** is the uncollateralized exposure allowed (e.g. $0 for major banks), **Minimum Transfer Amount (MTA)** is a floor below which no margin is transferred, and **Independent Amount (IA)** is an extra fixed IM buffer. These terms are specified in the CSA. A bank must model the MPR correctly; underestimating it can leave exposure when collateral is stale.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
When computing PFE (in SA-CCR or IMM), include an appropriate margin period of risk. Regulators mandate conservative MPR assumptions (often 10–20 days for non-cleared).
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Forgetting that collateral calls aren’t instantaneous. A default may occur during periods when collateral isn’t updated (e.g. weekend or holiday), so exposure can grow.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Margin Period of Risk (MPR)" b="Maturity (T)">
**Margin Period of Risk (MPR)** — The assumed time to close out positions after default, used in PFE calculation.  
---  
**Maturity (T)** — The time until a trade’s normal expiration. These get mixed up but MPR is usually much shorter than T.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Basel SA-CCR and IMM define MPR parameters; implementations are consistent across major regulators.
</Rule>
