An **application scorecard** supports an origination decision. Its predictors must be information legitimately available at or before the decision point: applicant characteristics, verified financial information, credit history and characteristics of the proposed facility.

A **behavioural scorecard** is used after the account has seasoned. It can incorporate information generated by the lending relationship: payment behaviour, utilisation, excesses, delinquency, balance trends and other account activity.

The distinction is fundamentally temporal. A variable observed after approval cannot legitimately improve an origination model because the lender did not possess it when the decision was made. Using such information is **target leakage**.

Scorecards usually produce a numerical score that ranks risk. A score is not automatically PD. To become a PD, the score must be mapped or calibrated to a defined default frequency over a defined horizon. A model can rank borrowers accurately while being poorly calibrated if predicted probabilities are systematically too high or too low.

Behavioural models also need a clear **observation window** and **performance window**. For example, information may be collected over a defined past period and used to predict default over the next twelve months. Mixing periods can allow early signs of the future default to leak into predictors.

Statistical scorecards do not replace policy. Affordability, fraud controls, legal eligibility, product limits and manual review can operate alongside credit-risk scoring. Conversely, overrides should be monitored because systematic human overrides can destroy model rank-ordering or conceal bias.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
A model may use only information available at the point in time at which its prediction is supposed to be made.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Including later delinquency information in an application model creates apparently excellent discrimination by allowing the future to predict the past decision.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Application score** — risk ranking based on information available around origination.

**Behavioural score** — risk ranking using subsequent account-performance information.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** The temporal modelling distinction is universal; local law and supervisory rules govern permissible data, fairness, governance and model use.
</Rule>
