### Core mechanism

| Information source | Observation point | Typical content | Limitation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Application | Origination | Declared/verified income, financials, demographics permitted by law, requested facility | No future account behaviour exists yet |
| Internal behaviour | Post-origination | Payments, utilisation, arrears, balance trends, excesses | Requires account seasoning and clean observation windows |
| Bureau / credit information | External reported history available at observation date | Other obligations, balances, enquiries, repayment history | Coverage, reporting lag, errors and thin files |
| Policy / affordability | Decision-time rules | Eligibility, repayment capacity, limits | Not itself a statistical score |

A bureau score or internal score is not automatically a PD. It must have a defined target, population and calibration. A “clean” thin file is also weaker evidence than a long clean history: absence of observed problems can mean absence of observation.

### Flow

```text
Application data + bureau data available at origination
    ↓
Application score → Calibration → PD / credit decision

Account opens
    ↓
Internal behaviour + refreshed bureau data
    ↓
Behavioural score → Calibration → PD / monitoring
```

### Where it sits

Upstream:   Decision date · Application data · Bureau snapshot · Internal account history
This:       Define observation window → build score/ranking → calibrate if probability needed
Downstream: Approve/decline · Limit/pricing · Behavioural monitoring · PD · Collections

### Permanent rule

A model may use only information that was available at the point in time at which the prediction is supposed to be made.

### Never confuse [Application score] and [Behavioural score]

**Application score** — risk ranking from information available around origination.

---

**Behavioural score** — risk ranking from subsequent account-performance information.

### Never confuse [Clean bureau] and [Strong repayment capacity]

**Clean bureau** — no adverse event observed in the covered reported history.

---

**Strong repayment capacity** — sustainable ability to meet future obligations from cash flow or resources; bureau history is only one input.

### Permanent trap

Allowing later delinquency, post-decision bureau updates or other future information into an origination model creates target leakage and makes model performance look unrealistically strong.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta in the modelling logic.** Data availability, permissible variables, consumer rights, credit-reporting frequency and automated-decision requirements vary materially by law and jurisdiction.

<Recall
  q="What is the defining difference between application and behavioural models?"
  a="The information date: pre/origination information versus post-origination account behaviour."
/>

<Recall

  a="Little adverse behaviour may have been observed because little credit history exists."
