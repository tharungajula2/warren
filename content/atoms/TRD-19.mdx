Interbank offered rates such as LIBOR were historically embedded in loans, bonds, swaps and valuation systems. Benchmark reform replaced them because panel-based unsecured term borrowing became less robust as the underlying market shrank. Alternative reference rates are generally grounded more directly in overnight transactions or robust market data.

The transition was not a simple label substitution. LIBOR contained bank credit and term components. Overnight risk-free rates such as SONIA for sterling and SOFR for US dollars have different economic bases. A contract moving from a forward-looking term IBOR to an overnight rate may need compounding in arrears, observation shifts, lookbacks, payment delays or a published term-RFR convention. Spread adjustments may be needed to preserve economic value during fallback.

Fallback language is therefore a core contract feature. It defines what happens if the benchmark is unavailable, non-representative or permanently ceases. Derivatives markets used ISDA fallback frameworks; cash products may have different statutory or contractual solutions. Basis can remain between instruments that transition under different rules.

**Perishable knowledge — checked 28 August 2026:** all 35 LIBOR settings have permanently ceased. The final synthetic US dollar LIBOR settings ceased after publication on 30 September 2024. References to “current LIBOR fixing” in a 2026 production system should therefore trigger a legacy-data or contract-state question, not a request for today’s rate.

India’s benchmark landscape differs by product; MIBOR and other domestic benchmarks should not be casually called “Indian LIBOR”. Benchmark family, administrator, tenor, calculation method and fallback must be stored explicitly.

For BAs and risk teams, the main migration lesson is data lineage: every affected trade needs the original benchmark, fallback hierarchy, spread adjustment, effective trigger, calculation method and downstream valuation/reporting treatment.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Benchmark migration changes economics and calculation conventions; never perform a ticker-only replacement.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Assuming LIBOR still has live settings because legacy contracts or database fields still contain the word LIBOR.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="IBOR" b="RFR">
**IBOR** — interbank offered-rate family historically intended to reflect term unsecured bank funding.

**RFR** — risk-free or near-risk-free reference rate, commonly based on overnight markets.
</NeverConfuse>

**IN — No universal one-for-one LIBOR replacement exists across all INR products; use the benchmark specified by the instrument and applicable Indian market rules.**UK — Perishable, checked 28 August 2026.** SONIA is the principal sterling RFR. **EU — EURIBOR remains a separate benchmark and should not be described as LIBOR. Euro derivatives also use €STR-based conventions.

**US — US dollar LIBOR has ceased; SOFR is the principal US dollar RFR used in the transition framework.**
