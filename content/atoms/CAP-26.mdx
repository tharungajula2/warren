### Core mechanism

```text
Adverse scenario
    ↓
Macroeconomic / market risk factors
    ↓
Credit losses · market losses · operational losses
    ↓
Revenue and expense effects
    ↓
Balance-sheet and exposure changes
    ↓
CET1 movement + RWA movement
    ↓
Stressed capital ratios
    ↓
Credible management actions
```

Reverse stress testing runs backwards:

```text
Defined failure / non-viability condition
    ↓
What losses, funding events or risk concentrations could cause it?
    ↓
Which vulnerabilities permit that path?
```

Internal tests are bank-owned and tailored to the institution. Supervisory tests are authority-run or authority-specified and can support cross-bank comparison or formal capital consequences under local rules.

### Ownership

| Role | Responsibility |
| :--- | :--- |
| Risk | Scenario and risk transmission |
| Finance | Earnings, balance sheet and capital projection |
| Business | Credible behavioural assumptions |
| Board / senior management | Challenge and action decisions |
| Validation / audit | Independent testing of models and controls |
| Supervisor | Challenge or prescribe supervisory exercises |

### Permanent rule

A capital stress conclusion is incomplete until both numerator and denominator effects have been transmitted.

### Never confuse [Scenario stress test] and [Reverse stress test]

**Scenario stress test** — begins with adverse conditions and projects outcomes.

---

**Reverse stress test** — begins with failure and identifies conditions that could cause it.

### Permanent trap

Deducting one loss amount from CET1 while holding RWA and earnings constant can materially understate stress transmission.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta in the mechanism. Scenarios, governance, supervisory consequences and management-action rules vary.
