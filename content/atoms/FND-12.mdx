With annual compounding, an amount \(P\) invested for \(n\) years at annual rate \(r\) grows to:

\[
FV=P(1+r)^n
\]

where **FV** is future value. Reversing the operation gives:

\[
PV=\frac&#123;FV&#125;&#123;(1+r)^n&#125;
\]

Compounding and discounting are therefore inverse operations under the same assumptions.

Rates can be quoted under different **compounding conventions**. If a nominal annual rate \(r_&#123;nom&#125;\) is compounded \(m\) times per year for \(n\) years, one common representation is:

\[
FV=P\left(1+\frac&#123;r_&#123;nom&#125;&#125;&#123;m&#125;\right)^&#123;mn&#125;
\]

A quoted annual rate of 12% compounded monthly is therefore not mechanically identical to 12% compounded annually. More frequent compounding changes the effective annual growth when the nominal quote is held constant.

Timing conventions matter just as much. A cash flow received today has no one-period discounting. A payment received at the end of year one receives one period of discounting. A payment occurring halfway through a year needs timing that reflects that half-period under the chosen convention. In real financial contracts, day-count conventions and payment calendars can further affect the calculation.

The most common modelling error is not sophisticated mathematics; it is **unit mismatch**. A model may combine annual probability assumptions, monthly cash flows and quarterly discount rates without converting them to a common time basis. Another error is comparing rates whose quotation conventions differ and treating the numerical percentage as sufficient.

The safe method is to identify the cash-flow dates first, state the rate convention explicitly, convert rates consistently where necessary, then apply the corresponding factors. Only after that should results be aggregated.

```text
Present value
     |
     | compound forward
     v
+----------------------+
| P x (1+r)^n          |
+----------------------+
     |
     v
Future value
     |
     | discount backward
     v
+----------------------+
| FV / (1+r)^n         |
+----------------------+
     |
     v
Present value
```

### Permanent rule

Rate frequency and cash-flow frequency must use the same time basis before compounding or discounting.

### Permanent trap

Treating a nominal 12% rate compounded monthly as economically identical to an effective 12% annual rate.

### Never confuse

**Nominal annual rate** — a quoted annualised rate whose economic effect depends on its compounding convention.

---

**Effective annual rate** — the one-year growth rate after the specified intra-year compounding has been reflected.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Compounding identities and the need for consistent conventions are mathematical rather than jurisdictional.
