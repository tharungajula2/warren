A loan may remain a legally valid claim for its full contractual amount while its accounting carrying amount is reduced by a **loss allowance**. Impairment is therefore not cancellation of the borrower’s obligation. It is recognition in the financial statements that some contractual cash flows are not expected to be collected. Under IFRS 9, the impairment model recognises expected credit losses (ECL) and updates the allowance as expectations and credit risk change. The standard replaced the IAS 39 incurred-loss approach; the move towards forward-looking loss recognition responded directly to concerns exposed by the financial crisis about delayed credit-loss recognition.

The change is fundamental. An incurred-loss model asks whether evidence of impairment has already emerged. An expected-loss model asks what credit loss is expected given information available at the reporting date, including relevant forecasts. Under IFRS 9, performing instruments generally carry an allowance from initial recognition: 12-month ECL where credit risk has not increased significantly, and lifetime ECL after a significant increase in credit risk.

Expected loss does not mean recognising every adverse economic possibility as though it were certain. IFRS 9 requires an unbiased, probability-weighted estimate using reasonable and supportable information and the time value of money. The allowance is therefore an estimate under uncertainty, not a hidden reserve and not a forecast of one inevitable outcome.

The US reached the same broad destination—earlier expected-loss recognition—but with different architecture. ASC 326 CECL recognises lifetime expected credit losses for financial assets measured at amortised cost from initial recognition rather than using IFRS 9’s Stage 1/Stage 2 threshold.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Impairment changes the accounting measurement of expected recoverability; it does not by itself extinguish the contractual claim.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Treating “performing” as equivalent to “zero impairment” is wrong under expected-loss accounting: credit loss can be expected before default or delinquency appears.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Impairment" b="Default">
**Impairment** — accounting recognition of expected credit loss on an asset or exposure.

---
**Default** — a credit-risk event concerning the obligor or facility; impairment can exist before default.
</NeverConfuse>
