### Core mechanism

For a future cash flow CF_t, annual discount rate r, and t annual periods:

### Formula

```text
PV = CF_t / (1 + r)^t
PV = present value
CF_t = cash flow at future time t
r = discount rate per stated period
t = number of consistent periods

DF_t = 1 / (1 + r)^t
DF_t = discount factor to the valuation date

PV = CF_t × DF_t
PV = future cash flow translated to the valuation date

If 110 is received in one year and r is 10% annually, PV is 100. The same 110 due later has a lower PV at the same positive rate.
```

### Flow

```text
Future cash flow
    ↓
Match rate to timing convention
    ↓
Apply discount factor
    ↓
Present value at common date
```

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   None
This:       Time value · Present value · Discount factor
Downstream: Compounding · EIR · NPV · Discounted loss measures
```

### Permanent rule

Cash flows at different dates are not economically comparable until their timing is put on a common basis.

### Never confuse [Discount rate] and [Discount factor]

**Discount rate** — the rate input used to translate value through time.

---

**Discount factor** — the numerical multiplier generated from the rate and timing.

### Never confuse [Discounting] and [Probability weighting]

**Discounting** — adjusts for timing.

---

**Probability weighting** — adjusts for uncertainty across outcomes.

### Permanent trap

Using an annual rate as though each monthly cash flow were one full annual period. Correct algebra with mismatched units still produces a wrong valuation.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Present-value mathematics is universal; frameworks differ only in which cash flows and rate they instruct the user to apply.

<Recall
  q="What is the one-period discount factor at rate r?"
  a="1/(1+r)."
/>

<Recall
  q="Does discounting itself model default probability?"
  a="No. Discounting adjusts value for timing; probability weighting addresses uncertainty."
