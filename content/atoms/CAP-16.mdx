The calculation requires two views of risk. First, the bank calculates RWA using the approaches it is authorised to use, including internal models where permitted. Second, it calculates a **standardised total** under the rules specified for the floor. The regulatory RWA relevant to the floor becomes the higher of the model-based amount and the applicable floor percentage of the standardised amount.

Example: model-based total RWA is 60; standardised RWA is 100; fully phased floor is 72.5. The floor raises regulatory RWA from 60 to 72.5. If model-based RWA were 80, the floor would not bind.

The purpose is not to declare models wrong. Basel preserves modelling incentives above the floor while limiting how far model outputs can diverge downward from a common standardised benchmark. The PRA describes the 72.5% multiplier as a backstop that maintains incentives to pursue model approval.

**Perishable — phase-in is jurisdiction-specific.** In the EU the floor is 55% during 2026, rises to 60% in 2027, 65% in 2028, 70% in 2029 and 72.5% from 1 January 2030. The UK starts its Basel 3.1 implementation on 1 January 2027 and is scheduled to reach the fully phased level by January 2030. The US has not implemented the final Basel output floor as a binding rule as at 27 August 2026; the March 2026 package remains proposed.

```text
+----------------------+   +----------------------+
| Model-based RWA      |   | Standardised RWA     |
| 60                   |   | 100                  |
+----------------------+   +----------------------+
             \             /
              v           v
          +----------------------+
          | 72.5% x 100 = 72.5  |
          +----------------------+
                     |
                     v
          +----------------------+
          | Higher of 60 / 72.5 |
          | = 72.5              |
          +----------------------+
```

### Permanent rule

The 72.5% figure applies to the standardised RWA benchmark at full phase-in; it is not a universal asset risk weight.

### Permanent trap

Multiplying each individual IRB exposure by 72.5% misunderstands an aggregate RWA comparison as an exposure-level risk weight.

### Never confuse

**Output floor** — aggregate lower bound on model-based RWA relative to standardised RWA.

---

**Risk weight** — exposure-level or formula-derived scaling factor used inside an RWA methodology.

### Jurisdiction variants

**IN** — RBI has selected the Standardised Approach for covered commercial-bank banking-book credit risk from 1 April 2027; an IRB-versus-standardised credit output-floor architecture is therefore not the domestic Indian credit regime. [RBI 2026 SA Directions]

**UK** — Basel 3.1 takes effect 1 January 2027; the UK output floor begins then and is scheduled to reach full 72.5% calibration by January 2030. [PRA PS1/26; European Commission 2026 comparative implementation table]

**EU** — CRR3 output floor is **55% in 2026**, then 60%, 65%, 70%, and **72.5% from 1 January 2030**. [CRR Article 465(1); European Commission SWD 2026]

**US** — The final Basel output floor is not a binding US rule as at 27 August 2026; the March 2026 capital package remains proposed. [Federal Reserve proposal; European Commission comparative implementation review]
