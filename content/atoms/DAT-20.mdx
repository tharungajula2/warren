In practice, attestation is typically documented via a cover letter or checkbox in the submission portal. It holds senior officers legally responsible if the data is faulty. Restatements usually follow a defined protocol: e.g. notify the regulator, submit a corrected report, and explain the cause. Each restatement is logged to avoid repeat mistakes.

Reporting changes (like new BCBS rules or formats) require project management. For instance, moving from manual to XBRL filing or adding a new COREP schedule. Such projects demand coordination between IT, data teams, and regulators. They produce new mapping specs and update test packs. A common trap is underestimating the regression testing needed across old reports. Compliance requires that all related reports still match after change.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
No report is final until attested. All corrections must be documented: one should never “forget” a restatement or leave an unexplained discrepancy.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Thinking of reporting change as just a format switch; in reality it often involves changing data definitions, so deep validation is needed.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Attestation" b="Audit">
**Attestation** — Management’s certification of a report’s accuracy.
---
**Audit** — Independent verification by internal or external auditors. Attestation is self-certification; audit is independent checking.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Attestation/sign-off processes and requirements for restatements are universal (though e.g. Indian banks may align with Companies Act and RBI letters).
</Rule>
