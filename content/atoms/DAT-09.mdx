Business lineage answers “what data needed to produce a report and why.” It’s often documented in a business requirements document (BRD): e.g. a line item in a report is defined as “sum of loan commitments by country.” Technical lineage, by contrast, traces exactly how that sum is calculated: which tables in the core banking system, what transformations (aggregations, currency conversion) were applied, and how it moved into the data warehouse. Regulators and auditors require lineage evidence to trust reports. For instance, if a regulator sees a credit risk report, they may ask the bank to show the chain of systems and transformations leading to each number.

Clear lineage helps identify root causes of data discrepancies. If a report number is wrong, technical lineage lets data engineers pinpoint where the anomaly occurred in the pipeline. Business lineage helps managers understand the impact (e.g. which products are affected). Both together provide a “complete picture.” Under BCBS 239, documenting lineage is part of demonstrating data governance; many banks include lineage diagrams in their data management plans.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Always document both business and technical lineage when creating data flows or reports. This double perspective ensures clarity for both business and IT stakeholders.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Providing only high-level lineage (“loans come from system X”) without the technical detail needed for audit.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Business lineage" b="Technical lineage">
**Business lineage** — A high-level mapping (for example, “Loan system ⇒ (sum) ⇒ monthly risk report”), understandable by non-IT stakeholders.  
---  
**Technical lineage** — A precise record of ETL steps, database tables and transformations that produce the data.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Both forms of lineage are part of global data governance expectations (e.g. BCBS 239 Principle 2’s call for documented architecture).
</Rule>
