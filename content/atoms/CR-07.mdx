In the Basel regulatory default architecture, the 90-day test sits beside an unlikeliness-to-pay test. Default occurs when either relevant condition is met. The two tests therefore form an **OR**, not a sequence requiring a bank to wait until both occur.

That distinction is operationally important. Consider an obligor whose loan is only 20 days overdue but which has entered a severe insolvency process and cannot meet its obligations without a distressed concession. An unlikeliness-to-pay assessment may identify default immediately. Conversely, an obligor may pass through earlier delinquency buckets without yet satisfying another default indicator; the mechanical backstop prevents an indefinitely optimistic judgement once material arrears persist.

A **backstop** therefore constrains judgement rather than replacing it. It creates an outside mechanical trigger while permitting earlier recognition when substantive evidence warrants it.

Materiality matters as well. Basel and CRR formulations refer to a material credit obligation. Without a materiality threshold, tiny operational underpayments could mechanically create default; with an excessively generous threshold, genuine delinquency could be hidden. Jurisdictions consequently prescribe or supervise how materiality operates.

India requires particular care because the banking prudential concept is **NPA classification**, not simply a direct transcription of Basel's IRB definition. RBI's commercial-bank rules make more than 90 days overdue a core NPA criterion for term loans, but they also prescribe different tests for cash-credit/overdraft facilities, crop loans, bills and other categories.

The US demonstrates why "90 days everywhere" is unsafe. Under Federal Reserve Advanced Approaches rules, wholesale default includes more than 90 days past due on a material obligation, while residential mortgage and revolving retail exposures use 180 days and other retail exposures 120 days for specified mechanical default triggers.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
A past-due backstop is the latest mechanical trigger under that rule, not permission to postpone an earlier qualitative default trigger.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Calling 90 DPD "the definition of default" causes banks to label severe credit failures as performing until an arrears clock happens to mature.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Backstop** — a mechanical outer trigger that limits how long an exposure can escape classification.

**Definition of default** — the complete set of conditions, including qualitative and quantitative triggers.
</NeverConfuse>
