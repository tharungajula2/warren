## Definition and Estimation Process

Probability of Default measures the empirical likelihood that an obligor experiences a default event within 12 months <Source id="1" />.

## Regulatory Rules and Traps

<Rule label="12-MONTH BASELINE horizon">
Regulatory IRB models require estimating PD over a minimum 12-month horizon following the rating date.
</Rule>

<Trap label="ZERO-DEFAULT PORTFOLIO FALACY">
Having zero historical defaults in a low-default portfolio does not justify a 0% PD assignment. Floor limits apply.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Point-in-Time (PiT) PD" b="Through-the-Cycle (TtC) PD">
Reflects current macroeconomic condition and obligor status. Fluctuate significantly over the credit cycle.

---

Reflects long-term average obligor rating across full credit cycle. Remains stable during economic shocks.
</NeverConfuse>

## Jurisdiction Variants

<Delta j="IN">
Reserve Bank of India (RBI) mandates a 90-day past due threshold for NPA classification across commercial banks under Master Circular on Prudential Norms.
</Delta>

<Delta j="UK">
Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA) requires UK firms to apply a hybrid PiT/TtC approach with explicit stress testing under SS11/13.
</Delta>

<Delta j="EU">
European Banking Authority (EBA) RTS on Definition of Default requires strict 90-day materiality thresholds (1% relative and EUR 100/500 absolute). <Source id="2" />
</Delta>

<Delta j="US">
Federal Reserve and OCC mandate CECL lifetime loss estimation rules alongside US Basel III Advanced Approaches.
</Delta>
