### Core mechanism

| Component | Question that must be answered |
| :--- | :--- |
| Unit | Does default apply to an obligor, facility, account or retail pool? |
| Event | Which qualitative and quantitative conditions trigger default? |
| Materiality | Which past-due amounts are material enough to count? |
| Propagation | Does one facility’s default affect other facilities? |
| Exit | What permits return to non-default? |
| History | Which date and state are preserved for modelling and recovery? |

Basel’s IRB reference definition uses two routes for an obligor: **unlikeliness to pay in full without recourse to actions such as realising security**, or **more than 90 days past due on a material credit obligation**. The routes are alternatives; later atoms own their mechanics.

### Flow

```text
Performing
    ↓
Qualifying credit deterioration
    ↓
UTP trigger OR material past-due trigger
    ↓
Default state
    ↓
Cure test / recovery lifecycle
```

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Legal obligor · Contract · Payment history · Credit information
This:       Apply governed default definition → default / non-default state
Downstream: PD labels · LGD sample · Cure · Recovery · Capital · Reporting
```

### Permanent rule

Define default before measuring default risk, then preserve that definition across development, calibration, validation and reporting.

### Never confuse [Default] and [Loss]

**Default** — entry into a defined credit-risk state.

---

**Loss** — the economic or accounting shortfall arising from outcomes around or after default; default can still produce substantial recovery.

### Permanent trap

Combining historical “bad” flags that mean 90 DPD, charge-off, bankruptcy or NPA creates a target variable with no stable economic meaning.

### Jurisdiction variants

#### IN (RBI)
RBI commercial-bank asset classification is built around prudential **NPA** rules, not a word-for-word Basel IRB default definition. The IRACP Directions also cross-reference “default” in RBI’s stressed-asset resolution framework; mappings must state which definition is used.

From **1 April 2027**, RBI’s 2026 Asset Classification, Provisioning and Income Recognition Directions mandate an expected-credit-loss provisioning framework for commercial banks; that future provisioning regime does not make the Basel IRB default definition and RBI prudential classifications interchangeable.

#### UK (PRA)
The current UK framework remains CRR-derived. The January 2026 PRA SS3/24 version is **future policy effective 1 January 2027**, not the framework in force on 29 August 2026.

#### EU
CRR Article 178 uses UTP and past-due criteria. EBA’s May 2026 amended Guidelines have an **application date of 19 October 2026** and are future-effective on 29 August 2026.

#### US
Advanced Approaches rules distinguish wholesale and retail default definitions; specified retail categories use different mechanical thresholds.
