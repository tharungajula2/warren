The context: unregulated loan apps led to harassment and exorbitant rates. In Feb 2022, RBI’s Master Directions on Digital Lending standardized the space. **Lending Service Providers** (non-bank fintechs who process applications) must follow KYC standards, get Permanent Account Number (PAN)/Aadhaar, and have a formal tie-up with a bank/NBFC. Every digital loan app must display, before disbursal, a *Key Fact Statement* summarizing the rate of interest, all fees and charges (incl. late payment fees), total amount payable, EMI/tenor etc. This ensures transparency. Borrowers must consent to these terms electronically.

**Disbursal and repayment:** Loans must be disbursed directly to the customer’s bank account or their PSP wallet. Use of any “pass-through” account (e.g. an agent’s account) is banned. Similarly, repayments cannot flow through third-party accounts: they must go to the lender directly. This severed the practice of routing funds through shell accounts.

**Fee and guarantee rules:** Fintech LSPs often offered “first loss default guarantees” to banks (promising to cover initial losses). RBI clarified these are not true securitizations but credit guarantees, and capped them at 20% of exposure or INR 5 cr. Also, LSPs cannot charge any fee at loan start except as explicitly disclosed – no pre-check fees.

**Grievance redress:** Digital lenders must list a nodal grievance officer. Any user complaint must be addressed per RBI’s turnaround times. Ombudsman schemes also cover digital loans. Unfair collection by aggressive agents is prohibited under the Recovery Agent Code (see Consumer section).

The RBI guidelines distinguish digital lending as a mechanism: *the loan contract is between lender and borrower*, while LSPs act as technology intermediaries. This “platform neutrality” means fintech apps cannot circumvent banking norms – borrowers can always refer to the original lender’s terms. Overall, these rules aim for consumer protection without stifling innovation: licensed entities can still use tech for underwriting (including data analytics, Aadhar/KYC), but must do so under RBI oversight.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Digital lending platforms must issue a clear *Key Fact Statement* before loan disbursement, showing all charges and repayment terms. Loans must disburse directly to the customer’s own bank/wallet account, and no pass-through accounts are allowed.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Loan sharks often created tech “platforms” outside regulation. A pitfall is thinking a fintech app can lend on its own terms. RBI’s rules require the actual loan to be from a bank/NBFC with digital co-lending only. Also, ignoring the KFS requirement can lead to non-compliance – every loan must show fully loaded APR and fees upfront.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Lending Service Provider (LSP)** — a non-bank platform or fintech that facilitates loan origination on behalf of a bank/NBFC. Not a lender itself.
**Fintech loan** — an RBI-licensed lender’s loan disbursed via a digital channel. (Not simply “getting money from a random app”.)
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="IN">
— RBI was among the first to regulate digital lending apps directly in such detail (re: KFS, pass-through ban). In UK/US, digital lending is allowed but individual states or laws handle disclosures (e.g. Truth-in-Lending Act) differently, and there is no blanket ban on pass-through accounts.
</Delta>
