Historical default and recovery experience is a starting point, not the destination. IFRS 9 requires ECL to incorporate reasonable and supportable information available without undue cost or effort, including forecasts of future economic conditions. Forward-looking information (FLI) can affect the probability of default, expected exposure, recovery rates, staging, or several of these simultaneously.

Relevant variables depend on the portfolio. Employment conditions may be important for unsecured household credit; property prices and interest rates may affect mortgage affordability and collateral recoveries; output, commodity prices or sector-specific demand can matter for corporate borrowers. The modelling question is not “Which macro variables are fashionable?” but “Which variables have a defensible causal or predictive relationship with the credit outcomes being estimated?”

The **forecast horizon** and the **credit horizon** are different. A five-year loan can require lifetime ECL even when detailed macro forecasts are credible for only a shorter period. Beyond a reasonable and supportable forecasting horizon, models need a disciplined method for extrapolation or reversion rather than pretending to possess precise long-range forecasts. Under US CECL, Topic 326 expressly contemplates reversion to historical loss information for periods beyond the reasonable-and-supportable forecast horizon.

FLI also influences staging. A borrower can experience a significant increase in credit risk before missing a payment when forward-looking evidence indicates material deterioration. That is why a purely arrears-driven staging system defeats the purpose of expected-loss accounting.

### Permanent rule

Forward-looking information belongs in ECL only through a defensible connection to expected credit outcomes.

### Permanent trap

Adding a generic “macroeconomic buffer” after already incorporating the same macro deterioration in modelled PD or LGD can double-count the risk.

### Never confuse

**Forecast horizon** — the period over which specific forward-looking economic estimates remain reasonable and supportable.

---

**ECL horizon** — the period over which expected credit losses must be measured; it can extend beyond the detailed forecast horizon.

### Jurisdiction variants

**IN** — Current bank IRACP remains primarily classification- and provisioning-rule based. RBI’s ECL Directions effective April 1, 2027 require macroeconomic variables and multiple scenarios, including documented treatment beyond standard forecast horizons; Ind AS NBFC ECL already requires forward-looking measurement. [RBI 2026 ECL Directions; RBI 2020 NBFC guidance]

**UK** — IFRS 9 governs FLI; PRA’s 2025 review stressed monitoring model boundaries and risks not adequately captured by existing models. [IFRS 9 5.5.17; PRA 2025]

**EU** — EU-adopted IFRS 9 applies, with EBA guidance and monitoring focused on sound forward-looking ECL implementation. [Regulation (EU) 2023/1803; EBA/GL/2017/06]

**US** — CECL uses historical experience, current conditions and reasonable and supportable forecasts, with reversion to historical information when forecasting beyond the supportable period. [ASC 326; FASB Staff Q&amp;A Topic 326 No. 2]
