The **Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process**, or ICAAP, begins with the bank’s risk inventory. Management identifies material risks arising from the balance sheet, business model, concentrations, operations and forward strategy. The bank then assesses how those risks could create losses or capital depletion and determines the capital resources needed to remain adequately capitalised.

The process is broader than a point-in-time calculation. Basel expects capital assessment to be connected to risk management, governance and strategy. Supervisors review whether the bank has a sound process for assessing overall capital adequacy relative to its risk profile and whether the resulting capital position is sufficient.

Stress testing is a central input. A base-case capital plan may show ample headroom, yet an adverse scenario can reveal that losses, lower earnings and rising RWA consume that headroom quickly. Basel’s supervisory-risk-management framework therefore links stress testing with internal capital assessment.

The **supervisory review process** then challenges assumptions, risk coverage, governance, models, management actions and capital conclusions. A supervisor need not accept the amount management considers adequate. It may require remedial action, stronger controls or additional capital under the applicable domestic framework.

ICAAP is therefore an argument supported by evidence: these are the risks, this is how severe they can become, these are available loss absorbers, this is the resulting capital need, and these are the actions available if conditions deteriorate.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
ICAAP must start from the institution’s actual material risks, not from the desire to reverse-engineer a predetermined capital number.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Copying Pillar 1 capital into an ICAAP and adding a generic percentage defeats the process because it never identifies which residual risks require capital.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="ICAAP" b="Supervisory review">
**ICAAP** — management’s internal assessment and governance process for capital adequacy.

---
**Supervisory review** — the authority’s independent evaluation of that assessment and the bank’s resulting prudential position.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** “ICAAP” and the formal supervisory process are implemented differently in national law, but the Basel sequence of internal assessment followed by supervisory review is common.
</Rule>
