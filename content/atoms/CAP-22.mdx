### Core mechanism

```text
Eligible regulatory capital
           versus
Prescribed RWA
    ↓
Minimum ratios
    ↓
CET1 minimum
Tier 1 minimum
Total Capital minimum
    ↓
Then Pillar 2 and buffers are considered
```

Under the Basel global standard, before buffers, the minimum ratios are:

| Ratio | Basel minimum |
| :--- | ---: |
| CET1 / RWA | 4.5% |
| Tier 1 / RWA | 6.0% |
| Total Capital / RWA | 8.0% |

These are nested quality requirements. The 8% Total Capital requirement includes the capital already satisfying the CET1 and Tier 1 minima; it is not an additional 8% on top of them.

Pillar 1 also depends on the permitted approaches used to generate RWA. Standardised approaches create common benchmarks; internal-model approaches may be permitted for specified risks and exposures subject to constraints.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Capital quality · RWA engines · three-pillar map
This:       Formulaic minimum capital
Downstream: Pillar 2 completeness check · buffers · capital headroom
```

### Formula

```text
Minimum CET1 amount = 4.5% × RWA under the Basel global minimum

Minimum Tier 1 amount = 6.0% × RWA

Minimum Total Capital amount = 8.0% × RWA

Local binding ratios may be higher.
```

### Permanent rule

Pillar 1 is the common quantitative baseline, not proof that the bank has enough capital for every material risk.

### Never confuse [Basel minimum] and [Bank's binding requirement]

**Basel minimum** — international standard agreed by BCBS members.

---

**Bank's binding requirement** — requirement under its jurisdictional rulebook plus applicable firm-specific decisions.

### Permanent trap

Quoting 8% as the bank's full required capital ignores CET1 quality constraints, Pillar 2, buffers and higher local minima.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta in the conceptual role. Numerical binding minima can differ materially; India is a clear example in CAP-29.
