**Maturity** matters because credit risk has more time to change over a longer horizon. A borrower that is sound today has more opportunity to deteriorate over five years than over three months, all else equal.

The simplest maturity measure is **remaining final maturity**: time until the last contractual payment. **Effective maturity (M)** can instead reflect the timing of all expected contractual cash flows. Under Basel IRB rules, specified wholesale exposures with a determined cash-flow schedule use a cash-flow-weighted measure, subject to regulatory specifications, floors and caps. US Advanced Approaches regulation similarly defines effective maturity for ordinary wholesale exposures using the weighted-average remaining maturity of expected contractual cash flows, with undiscounted cash-flow amounts as weights and separate rules for specified transaction types.

Consider two five-year loans. Loan A amortises heavily in the first two years; Loan B makes almost all principal repayment at year five. They have the same final legal maturity but not the same temporal exposure profile. A cash-flow-weighted effective maturity will generally be shorter for Loan A.

Effective maturity is not EAD. EAD measures how much is exposed at default; maturity describes the time structure over which credit risk operates. Nor is it the **average life** used in every pricing or asset-liability calculation: formulas can look similar while serving different regulatory purposes.

Contractual options, derivatives, netting sets and certain short-term transactions can require specialised treatment, so production implementations should never replace the applicable regulatory formula with a generic spreadsheet average.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
A maturity measure is meaningful only when its cash-flow convention and purpose are specified.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Using final contractual maturity as effective maturity for every instrument ignores amortisation and specialised regulatory rules.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Final maturity** — time until the final contractual payment date.

**Effective maturity** — a risk measure reflecting the timing of contractual cash flows under a defined methodology.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** The economic concept is universal; Basel-derived regulatory frameworks can prescribe different floors, caps and special-case formulas.
</Rule>

<Recall
  q="Can two loans with the same final maturity have different effective maturities?"
