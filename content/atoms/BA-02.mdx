### Core mechanism

A usable mandate separates what must be achieved from what delivery may still trade.

| Element | Mandate must establish |
| :--- | :--- |
| Required outcome | Condition that must be true after change |
| Trigger | Regulation, finding, strategy, incident or dependency |
| Deadline | Setter and authority to move it: fixed external, internal commitment or target |
| Mandatory scope | Work without which the outcome fails |
| Discretionary scope | Improvement that can be traded without defeating the outcome |
| Constraints | Calendar, platform, control, funding or resource limits |
| Evidence | What must be demonstrable, and by whom |
| Delivery pressure | Consequences for scope, resources, sequence and residual risk |

```text
Ambiguous mandate -&gt; False assumptions -&gt; Mis-scoped requirements -&gt; Late conflict
Clear mandate -&gt; Explicit non-negotiables -&gt; Defensible requirements -&gt; Controlled trade-offs
```

Delivery difficulty does not move a date. Only the authority that set an external, executive or strategic commitment can change it.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Regulation or commitment · Finding · Strategy · Operational need
This:       Mandate · Deadline · Non-negotiables
Downstream: Stakeholders · Scope · Requirements · Delivery trade-offs
```

### Ownership

| Role | Responsibility |
| :--- | :--- |
| Produces | BA converts the initiating statement into explicit outcome, constraints and open questions |
| Approves | Sponsor, business owner or accountable control owner confirms the mandate |
| Consumes | Governance, delivery, architecture, testing and operations use it to judge later choices |
| Independently challenges | Risk, compliance or internal audit challenge where the initiating obligation falls within their mandate |

### Permanent rule

A deadline is fixed only because an authority outside the delivery team has made it fixed. The project cannot silently redefine the obligation to fit its plan.

### Never confuse [Mandate] and [Solution]

**Mandate** - the outcome, obligation and constraints that must be satisfied.

---

**Solution** - one proposed way of satisfying them.

### Permanent trap

Starting detailed requirements from a solution name. "Build a new engine" can hide the real objective, preserve avoidable design assumptions and make later alternatives look like scope changes when the mandate never required that design.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta. A specific regulator may impose a particular implementation date or evidence obligation, but the change discipline for interpreting it is the same.

<Recall
  q="What must be clear before detailed requirements begin?"
  a="Outcome, trigger, deadline authority, mandatory and discretionary scope, constraints and evidence expectations."
/>

<Recall
  q="Why separate mandatory from discretionary scope?"
  a="Delivery pressure then has a defensible trade space without weakening the required outcome."
/>

<Recall

  a="Only when the authority that owns the date changes it; delivery difficulty alone does not."
