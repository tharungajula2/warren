Bank change programs fall into categories: **Strategic** (business-driven goals like new services or cost reduction), **Remediation** (fixing problems found by audit), and **Regulatory** (mandated by laws or regulators). Regulatory changes are unique: their deadlines are *unmovable* and non-negotiable, and failure to comply can incur penalties and license risk.  These projects carry high visibility, involve many functions, and have huge impact. Because regulators require proof of compliance, regulatory programs demand rigorous documentation, traceability, and audit trails.  For example, a compliance program like IFRS 9 transition is closely monitored by supervisors; banks often run parallel regimes to validate results before the fixed effective date.

Strategic initiatives, by contrast, prioritize internal business value and have more flexible timelines. Remediation projects respond to issues (e.g. a control failure) and may have urgency but usually less external scrutiny. Regulatory projects behave differently: teams often use waterfall governance and stage gates to meet the firm deadlines.  Cutting corners or missing details is dangerous; regulators expect complete traceability from each regulatory requirement to the implemented solution. Audit findings often arise when banks discover a gap between knowing the rule and effectively applying it.

### Permanent rule

Treat regulatory change as mandatory, with fixed deadlines and senior oversight. Plan for full documentation and testing of compliance. Never assume you can shift a regulatory date to accommodate project delays.

### Permanent trap

Treating a regulatory deadline like a usual internal deadline (i.e. deferring it or deprioritizing compliance tasks). Missing that fixed deadline invites exam findings and fines.

### Never confuse

**Strategic Change** — voluntary, business-driven initiatives (may adjust scope/deadlines based on ROI).
---
**Regulatory Change** — mandatory compliance projects, externally imposed with firm deadlines and audit requirements.

### No delta

Across jurisdictions, the nature of regulatory projects is the same: non-negotiable effective dates and mandatory compliance. Each regulator may set different dates, but the principle of fixed deadline and required audit evidence does not change.
