The cycle starts with positions and behavioural assumptions. Assets, liabilities and off-balance-sheet exposures are mapped by maturity, repricing date, currency, rate basis and option characteristics. Non-maturity deposits, prepayments and contingent draws require models because contractual schedules alone do not describe their economic behaviour.

The same balance sheet is then viewed through multiple lenses. **IRRBB** produces EVE and NII sensitivities. **Liquidity analysis** produces cash-flow gaps, LCR, NSFR, intraday requirements and internal stress survival. **Capital analysis** asks how losses and valuation changes interact with available loss-absorbing resources. **FTP** converts those system-level costs and benefits into business-level prices.

Management then compares the outputs with risk appetite, regulatory minima, internal limits and strategic objectives. A breach or undesirable trend can be addressed through several levers: change product pricing, alter origination mix, attract a different funding tenor, issue wholesale funding, change liquid-asset holdings, hedge interest-rate exposure, reduce commitments or adjust business limits.

The key is to analyse side effects. Raising long-term funding can improve structural liquidity but reduce margin. Selling liquid assets to repay funding can change LCR and market-risk capacity. Extending deposit behavioural tenor may improve one measure only if the assumption is genuinely supportable. A derivative hedge can reduce EVE while altering NII or basis risk.

Basel’s liquidity principles require liquidity costs and risks to enter pricing, stress testing and contingency planning, while IRRBB standards require both economic-value and earnings views. Integrated ALM is the governance mechanism that forces these views to reconcile before action is taken.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
No ALM action should be judged on a single metric when it materially changes another part of the balance-sheet risk profile.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Fixing the metric that appears on the current committee dashboard while shifting the underlying risk into another horizon, legal entity, basis, liquidity bucket or accounting presentation.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="ALM optimisation" b="Single-metric optimisation">
**ALM optimisation** — choosing a balance-sheet configuration that jointly respects risk appetite, liquidity, capital, earnings and strategic constraints.
---
**Single-metric optimisation** — improving one ratio or sensitivity without accounting for the risk transferred elsewhere.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Regulatory constraints differ, but the integrated decision logic is universal: measure the same balance sheet from multiple risk perspectives, act, and remeasure.
</Rule>

<Recall
  q="Why should an ALM action be recalculated across EVE, NII and liquidity?"
  a="The action can improve one measure while worsening another because all are generated by the same balance sheet."
/>

<Recall
  q="What connects business pricing to ALM?"
  a="FTP, which transmits funding and liquidity economics from the central balance sheet into product-level decisions."
