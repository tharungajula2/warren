### Core mechanism

| Driver | What it captures |
| :--- | :--- |
| Inherent risk | Complexity, assumptions, input quality, data constraints |
| Purpose | Importance of the decision or regulatory use |
| Exposure | Scale of balances, customers, transactions or business affected |
| Materiality | Significance of purpose plus exposure |
| Consumer / operational impact | Harm beyond direct financial exposure |
| Changeability | Dynamic behaviour or frequent updating |
| Dependency | Whether other important models rely on it |

```text
Assessment
    ↓
Risk tier
    ↓
Scaled validation · Approval authority · Monitoring · Documentation · Change control
```

A simple model can be high risk because its output affects a huge portfolio. A complex model can be lower materiality if it has narrow, non-critical use. Complexity and materiality therefore answer different questions.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Inventory · Model risk sources · Business exposure
This:       Materiality and risk tier
Downstream: Validation scope · Approval level · Monitoring intensity
```

### Permanent rule

Tiering scales controls; it never removes accountability. A lower tier means proportionate MRM, not exemption from all governance.

### Never confuse [Complexity] and [Materiality]

**Complexity** - difficulty of understanding, estimating, implementing or challenging the model.

---

**Materiality** - significance of the model's output to decisions and exposures. A model can be simple but highly material.

### Permanent trap

Building a score that lets one benign factor cancel a critical one. A highly material model should not become "low risk" merely because its technique is simple.

### Jurisdiction variants

#### US (Federal Reserve / OCC)
SR 26-2 distinguishes inherent risk, model exposure and model purpose. Purpose together with exposure determines materiality; overall model risk reflects inherent risk in the context of materiality. Higher materiality warrants more rigorous oversight, while immaterial models may receive more limited controls.

#### UK (PRA)
SS1/23 expects firms to classify model risk and use proportionality so the level of MRM activity reflects the model's risk. The PRA does not require every firm to use the same numerical tiering scale.

#### IN (RBI)
As at August 2026, the RBI draft proposes risk-based tiering for all models, with materiality, complexity and other relevant factors considered collectively. The proposed tier drives validation, approval, monitoring, reporting and business-continuity intensity; high-risk models receive stronger governance. It remains draft.
