The ISDA Master Agreement is a standard framework for OTC derivatives relationships. The 2002 form updates the 1992 architecture and includes provisions for payment, representations, events of default, termination events, close-out amount and set-off. Parties negotiate elections and amendments in a Schedule.

Its central risk-management feature is the relationship between the “single agreement” concept and close-out. In normal conditions, individual transactions continue with their own cash flows. If an Event of Default or relevant Termination Event occurs and the contractual steps are satisfied, transactions can be terminated in accordance with the agreement. Their replacement or close-out values are calculated and combined into one Early Termination Amount under the applicable terms.

Close-out netting reduces gross bilateral replacement obligations into a net claim or payable. If one party is owed 100 on some terminated transactions and owes 80 on others, enforceable netting can leave a single 20 claim rather than separate gross obligations. This can materially reduce counterparty exposure and capital requirements where regulatory conditions are met.

But netting is a legal conclusion, not an arithmetic feature. Enforceability depends on governing law, counterparty type, insolvency regime, branches and transaction scope. Banks therefore rely on legal opinions and eligibility controls before recognising netting benefits. A database “netting set” flag does not create legal enforceability.

Resolution regimes can also impose temporary stays on termination rights for certain financial institutions. ISDA’s close-out framework highlights the interaction among default notices, stays and collateral enforcement.

Set-off and close-out netting should not be treated as identical. Close-out netting operates through termination and valuation of covered transactions under the contractual framework; set-off may apply to separate payment obligations under different legal conditions.

The operational control is document-to-netting-set lineage: every trade included in a net exposure calculation must be legally in scope.

```text
Normal state:
Trade 1
Trade 2  --&gt; one ISDA relationship
Trade 3

Default / termination trigger
          |
          v
terminate covered trades
          |
          v
value each terminated trade
          |
          v
close-out netting
          |
          v
single net payable / receivable
```

### Permanent rule

Netting benefit exists only if the contractual mechanism is legally enforceable for the relevant parties and transactions.

### Permanent trap

Assuming a system-generated net amount proves legal close-out netting eligibility.

### Never confuse

**Close-out netting** — termination, valuation and combination of covered transactions into a single net amount.

---

**Set-off** — legal combination of mutual payment obligations that may arise under a different basis and scope.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.**
