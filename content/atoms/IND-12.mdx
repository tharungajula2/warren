**Agriculture (18% target):** Loans to farmers (individual or joint) for agriculture and allied activities. This includes crop loans, agricultural equipment loans, irrigation loans, and loans to agri-related services. Within agri, there is an 8% target specifically for small and marginal farmers (holding &lt;2ha). Also loans to agri corporations, horticulture, and some farm sector supply chain are included.

**MSMEs (7.5% target):** Loans to micro, small enterprises (as per MSME Act definitions) for business purposes. Also very small businesses (like retail shops, crafts). Recently, RBI expanded the definition to include loans to MSMEs up to ₹5 crore (and with turnover criteria). Loans to larger SMEs (beyond ₹5 cr) are out of PSL.

**Weaker Sections (10% target):** This is a portion of PSL loans to small borrowers: it includes small farmers, microenterprises, borrowers from marginalized communities, and loans up to ₹50,000 (whichever sector).  Essentially, a subset of agri, micro, and other small loans targeted at the bottom of economic pyramid (ST/SC, economically backward, etc.).

Other PSL categories include education loans, housing loans (to EWS/LIG segments), renewable energy, and loans to specific small-scale service industries. Banks must use RBI’s PSL guidelines booklet to classify loans correctly. Only loans falling in these categories count; e.g. a big corporate loan does not.

Overall, PSL shapes balance sheets: banks often build rural branches and specialized MSME credit teams to meet these targets. Satisfied PSL requirements can improve regulatory standing (e.g. risk weight relief), while shortfalls incur penalties. As RBI notes, PSL fosters socio-economic goals but also forces banks into lower-yielding lending.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Only loans to RBI-defined priority categories count toward PSL. Agriculture loans, small enterprise finance, and weaker section lending have separate sub-targets.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Misclassifying loans. E.g., making a small business loan to an urban store and hoping it counts as agriculture PSL – it does not. Each category has strict definitions. Also, banks sometimes ignore the ‘weaker sections’ requirement thinking general MSME lending covers it – but weaker sections is a distinct sub-target.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Agriculture credit** — includes crop/pump loans etc. (part of 18% PSL).
**Weaker sections** — a subset of priority sector covering loans to marginalized and poorest borrowers, under the 10% sub-target of ANBC.
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="IN">
— These exact sectoral categories (e.g. the 18% agri target) are unique to India’s RBI framework.  Other countries may have development lending programs, but not tied to bank quotas.
</Delta>
