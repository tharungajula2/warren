### Core mechanism

```text
Borrower applies through DLA
    ↓
RE/LSP collects only permitted data with required consent
    ↓
Offer and KFS delivered before binding acceptance
    ↓
Loan disbursed through permitted direct flow
    ↓
Repayments move through permitted direct flow
    ↓
Servicing, cooling-off and grievance rights continue
```

| Control | Why it exists |
| :--- | :--- |
| KFS and offer disclosure | Prevents hidden pricing and lender ambiguity |
| Explicit consent | Restricts unnecessary or covert data access |
| Need-based data collection | Limits data extraction unrelated to lending |
| Direct disbursal/repayment | Reduces uncontrolled pass-through handling by LSPs |
| Cooling-off or look-up period where applicable | Gives the borrower a defined exit route |
| Grievance officer and escalation | Keeps digital delivery inside normal accountability |

Permitted exceptions to direct fund flow exist only where the Directions expressly allow them. The operational default is not "money may pass through the fintech because the fintech sourced the customer."

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   RE/LSP/DLA perimeter · KFS
This:       Digital loan operating controls
Downstream: Servicing · Data governance · DLG · Recovery
```

### Permanent rule

Digital convenience does not displace borrower consent, price transparency or the regulated entity's responsibility.

### Permanent trap

Treating an LSP collection account as the natural centre of the transaction. RBI's design generally keeps loan disbursal and repayment tied directly to the regulated lender and borrower, subject only to specified exceptions.

### Jurisdiction variants

No within-India delta in the core digital control logic; entity-specific prudential treatment continues underneath the digital channel.

  q="What is the regulatory purpose of direct digital loan fund flow?"
