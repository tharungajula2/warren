### Core mechanism

| Recovery control | Required outcome |
| :--- | :--- |
| Agent due diligence and authorisation | Lender knows who acts on its behalf |
| Identity and communication discipline | Borrower can identify the lender/agent and reason for contact |
| Contact controls | Recovery activity occurs within permitted conduct boundaries |
| No harassment or intimidation | Default does not remove dignity and fair-treatment obligations |
| Complaint handling | Borrower can escalate misconduct to the regulated entity |
| Ombudsman route | Eligible unresolved service grievances can move to RBI's external redress framework |

The lender cannot outsource accountability. If an agent breaches conduct rules, the regulated entity faces supervisory and customer-redress consequences.

RBI issued recovery-conduct amendments on 6 August 2026 with an effective date of 1 January 2027. As at 31 August 2026 those future-effective provisions are not yet in force. This atom therefore distinguishes the current conduct framework from the 2027 amendments and does not backdate future requirements.

### Permanent rule

A valid debt does not legalise abusive collection. The lender is accountable for outsourced recovery conduct.

### Never confuse [Recovery right] and [Recovery method]

**Recovery right** — the creditor's lawful claim to collect or enforce the debt.

---

**Recovery method** — the manner in which collection is carried out; it remains subject to RBI conduct rules.

### Permanent trap

Treating a third-party recovery agent as outside the lender's compliance perimeter. Outsourcing the call or visit does not outsource regulatory responsibility.

### Jurisdiction variants

#### Banks, NBFCs and other covered REs
Apply the conduct and outsourcing requirements relevant to their entity class.

#### Future position from 1 January 2027
RBI's 6 August 2026 recovery-conduct amendments become effective; they are future rules as at 31 August 2026 and are not treated as currently operative.
