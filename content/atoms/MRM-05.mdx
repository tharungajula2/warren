A comprehensive model inventory is a foundation of MRM.  Regulators expect banks to maintain a **complete, firm-wide list of all models** – those in use, in development, or even recently retired – with key attributes (purpose, owner, validation status, etc.).  The purpose is to “enable firms to identify the sources of model risk” and prioritize controls.  RBI’s credit-model circular similarly mandates that banks “maintain a Model Inventory of approved models, insourced or outsourced, with critical information on it”.  In practice, each line of business may track its own models, but the firm must compile a consolidated list for oversight and reporting.

Once inventoried, models are usually tiered by risk.  Regulators say this tiering should be **risk-based**, reflecting each model’s materiality.  Factors include financial exposure (e.g. how much money or capital depends on the model) and model purpose (e.g. credit approval vs internal analysis).  For example, the Fed guidance introduces a “materiality-based framework” where “model purpose and model exposure” determine materiality.  UK SS1/23 Principle 1 requires risk-based tiering so that “validation activities and other risk controls” focus on critical models.  RBI draft also requires risk-tiering and annual review of tier levels.  A typical approach is to label models Tier 1 (most critical), Tier 2, Tier 3, etc.  High-tier models trigger the strictest governance (e.g. board approvals, more frequent reviews), while lower-tier models have scaled-back oversight.

```text
+---------------+
| Model Tiers   |
+-------+-------+
| Tier 1 (High) |
+-------+-------+
| Tier 2 (Med)  |
+-------+-------+
| Tier 3 (Low)  |
+---------------+
```

Tiering helps allocate resources.  For instance, a retail-credit scorecard impacting $1B loans (Tier 1) would get priority validation and senior oversight.  A small-scope risk indicator (Tier 3) might be reviewed less often.  Regardless, regulators expect that **no model is left untracked**.  The PRA says even decommissioned models should remain in the inventory for historical lessons.

### Permanent rule

Maintain an up-to-date model inventory and use a risk-tiering scheme.  This is non-negotiable in MRM policies.

### Permanent trap

Thinking only big regulatory models matter.  Banks often miss models used for analytics or financial reporting that still drive decisions.  The rule is: **all** models must be inventoried, not just credit/market capital models.

### Never confuse

**Model inventory** — the list of all models, with details (owner, status, etc.).
---
**Asset inventory** — list of financial assets (loans, etc.).  They serve different purposes: one tracks models, the other tracks financial exposures.

### Jurisdiction variants

**IN** — RBI’s 2024/2026 guidelines require a model inventory of all credit models (including third-party), with board oversight. Tiering by risk is required by draft Guidance (RBI).
**UK** — PRA SS1/23 Principle 1 mandates a formal model inventory and risk-based tiering to focus validation on material models.
**US** — The April 2026 guidance emphasizes a “materiality-based” approach: firms should define material models by purpose and exposure.  It does not prescribe fixed tiers, but many banks adopt a tiering scheme to implement the risk-based approach.
**EU** — EBA/ECB expect banks to inventory all models used for IRB capital or risk management and to apply risk-based oversight. ECB’s internal models guide similarly uses materiality concepts. (No fundamental conflict with other jurisdictions on this point.)
