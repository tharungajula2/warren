IFRS 9 contains a rebuttable presumption that credit risk has increased significantly since initial recognition when contractual payments are **more than 30 days past due**. “Rebuttable” matters: the presumption is strong but not irrebuttable. An entity may rebut it when reasonable and supportable information demonstrates that credit risk has not increased significantly despite the delinquency.

The equally important point is what the rule does **not** mean. An entity must not wait for day 31 when other reasonable and supportable information identifies SICR earlier. IFRS 9 explicitly treats the delinquency presumption as a backstop. A rating downgrade, worsening liquidity, adverse covenant information or relevant macro deterioration can trigger Stage 2 before any payment is missed.

Rebuttal should be evidence-based. A temporary administrative failure may support rebuttal where credible historical and current evidence shows that it does not signal higher lifetime default risk. A blanket policy that all 30–59 DPD cases remain Stage 1 would invert the presumption and is inconsistent with its purpose.

Past-due status also must be computed consistently. Data-quality errors around due dates, payment allocation, moratoria, restructurings and account-level versus borrower-level delinquency can cause false stage transfers or conceal genuine ones. The backstop is simple conceptually but operationally dependent on reliable delinquency data.

+----------------------+
| More than 30 DPD?    |

### Permanent rule

A backstop catches deterioration missed by earlier indicators; it does not replace those indicators.

### Permanent trap

Calling 30 DPD “the Stage 2 trigger” causes late recognition because it turns an outer backstop into the primary staging rule.

### Never confuse

**Rebuttable presumption** — a default conclusion that stands unless supported evidence demonstrates otherwise.

---

**Optional indicator** — a factor that may simply be ignored; the 30-DPD presumption is not optional.

### Jurisdiction variants

**IN** — For Ind AS-implementing NBFCs, RBI reiterates the 30-DPD rebuttable presumption and additionally says exposures overdue beyond 60 days must not have SICR recognition deferred. Current scheduled-commercial-bank IRACP is different. RBI’s bank ECL Directions effective April 1, 2027 adopt a more-than-30-DPD rebuttable presumption, with a specific revolving-facility backstop. [RBI 2020 NBFC guidance; RBI 2026 ECL Directions, paragraphs 33–36]

**UK** — UK IFRS 9 applies the more-than-30-DPD rebuttable SICR presumption. [IFRS 9 5.5.11]

**EU** — EU-adopted IFRS 9 applies the same presumption. [Regulation (EU) 2023/1803; IFRS 9 5.5.11]

**US** — CECL has no 30-DPD staging backstop because there is no Stage 1-to-Stage 2 lifetime-loss switch. Delinquency remains relevant credit information within allowance estimation and credit administration. [ASC 326]
