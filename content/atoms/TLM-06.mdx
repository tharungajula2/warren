### Core mechanism

| Mechanism | What is mismatched | Typical failure of intuition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Repricing / gap risk | reset timing | fixed asset funded by fast-reset liability |
| Yield-curve risk | movement at different tenors | hedge works for parallel move but not twist |
| Basis risk | different indices / bases | both sides float, but not one-for-one |
| Optionality risk | cash-flow amount/timing changes with behaviour | prepayment or deposit behaviour changes in scenario |

A three-month floating asset and three-month floating liability can have almost no timing gap and still have basis risk if one references a different benchmark or reprices with different conventions. A five-year fixed loan and five-year fixed funding can appear maturity matched but retain optionality if the borrower can prepay.

### Flow

```text
Banking-book positions
    ↓
Repricing + curve + basis + option exposures
    ↓
Scenario changes cash flows / discount rates / margins
    ↓
NII and economic value change
```

### Permanent rule

Matching reset dates removes only timing gap risk; curve, basis and option risks can remain.

### Never confuse [Repricing risk] and [Basis risk]

**Repricing risk** — rates reset at different times.

---

**Basis risk** — rates reset on different reference bases that do not move together, even when timing is similar.

### Permanent trap

Calling two floating-rate positions a hedge because they share the same tenor. Index, spread behaviour, floors, caps and reset conventions can leave material residual exposure.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta. Basel and major national frameworks recognise the same underlying mechanisms; prescribed shocks and measurement constraints vary.
