Two aspects of a control must be tested: its design and its operating effectiveness.  **Design effectiveness** asks, “Is this control logically sufficient to address the risk?” For example, a password policy (design) aims to prevent unauthorized access; it is well-designed if it covers all critical systems.  **Operating effectiveness** asks, “Does the control actually work consistently?” Even a well-designed control fails if people ignore it or it’s poorly executed (e.g. employees sharing passwords).

In practice, auditors or risk managers often test design by inspection (is the rule documented? covers all cases?) and test operation by sampling actual instances (did staff follow it every time in the sample?). For robust risk management, both must be in place. A common failure is to assume that having a written policy implies complete coverage. In reality, lack of operating effectiveness means the risk is uncontrolled.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Test each control along two dimensions.  First ensure the control’s design is appropriate for the risk.  Then test that the control operates effectively (e.g. performs correctly and consistently).  Use samples, walkthroughs, and reconciliations to evidence operation.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Never assume a strong design implies it’s working.  A classic mistake is having a good procedure on paper but failing to enforce it.  For example, a fraud-check process might look solid, but if staff skip steps under pressure, the control is ineffective.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Control Design" b="Operating Effectiveness">
**Control Design** — the theory and setup of the control (e.g. policy, procedure).
---
**Operating Effectiveness** — the actual execution of the control.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** This distinction is part of basic internal control frameworks everywhere (Basel/SSOX/SOX). Regulatory guidance (e.g. Basel and audit standards) explicitly require both aspects to be tested.
</Rule>
