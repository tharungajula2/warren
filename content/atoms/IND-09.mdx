Restructuring allows lenders to modify loan terms (extension of tenor, interest rate cut, etc.) for a viable borrower under stress. RBI has periodically issued frameworks: e.g. in 2014–15 the CDR mechanism (mostly phased out), and “5/25” for infrastructure. Post-COVID, RBI issued Resolution Frameworks (2020-21) letting banks restructure specified stressed loans with a benefit of preserving asset classification (subject to strict criteria). RBI also had schemes specifically for MSMEs (e.g. Mudra loans) and one-time restructuring packages for legacy stressed sectors.

Under RBI policy, when a loan is restructured, it can be upgraded out of NPA only if it meets specific performance criteria over a period. RBI’s circular in 2015 clarified that a restructured advance must perform for at least one year (or two quarters) after restructuring to be considered Standard again. This avoids immediate upgrade fraud. If those conditions aren’t satisfied, the account remains an NPA.

When restructuring occurs, banks must increase provisions (e.g. a minimum 5% on restructured standard loans, rising on restructured NPA). RBI’s norms spell out extra provisioning for restructured assets. Also, restructured loans carry enhanced reporting and are tagged on balance sheets.

RBI’s resolution frameworks now overlap with the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC). Banks can opt for IBC proceedings for large defaulters, or choose RBI’s guidelines for out-of-court settlement. Both routes have specific timelines and safeguards to protect creditors and equitable treatment of all lenders. Resolution under IBC requires strict timelines (270 days) and NCLT oversight. RBI’s preference is that viable debt should not require formal insolvency if banks can resolve it cooperatively.

### Permanent rule

When loans are restructured, they remain NPAs until strict conditions (specified by RBI) are met. Absent that, the classification stays NPA even if payments resume. Upgrading only happens after sustained performance post-restructuring.

### Permanent trap

Assuming a restructured loan is automatically ‘standard’. In India, a loan modified under a restructuring scheme must still run successfully for a mandatory period before being considered standard. Banks often mistakenly revert classification immediately.

### Never confuse

**Restructured loan** — a loan whose terms are modified to help a stressed borrower. It may still be NPA; upgrading depends on post-restructure performance.

### Jurisdiction variants

**IN** — RBI’s circulars on restructuring (e.g. Resolution Frameworks) are India-specific. Other countries have workout rules, but RBI’s emphasis on performance post-restructure is a local regulatory detail.
