### Core mechanism

Challenge the design through business consequences.

| Design question | BA challenge |
| :--- | :--- |
| Coverage | Which approved requirements does this option satisfy, and which does it not? |
| Assumptions | What business assumption does the design depend on? |
| Exceptions | What happens outside the normal path? |
| Controls | Which existing control is preserved, moved, automated or removed? |
| Dependencies | Which upstream or downstream process changes because of this choice? |
| Operations | How will users support, reconcile and recover the solution? |
| Evidence | What will later demonstrate that the business obligation is met? |
| Trade-off | What business outcome, risk or deadline changes if this option is chosen? |

A BA can describe a business rule in enough detail to remove ambiguity. That does not make the BA owner of SQL, Python, database structures, API design or source-to-target mappings. TEC owns SQL and Python implementation; DAT owns data-control specifications. The BA should ask whether those artefacts preserve the approved business meaning.

When a proposed design cannot satisfy a requirement, do not quietly rewrite the requirement to fit feasibility. Surface the gap as a decision: change solution, change requirement through authorised control, or accept the consequence through the correct owner.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Approved requirements · Constraints · Impact analysis
This:       Business challenge of solution choices
Downstream: Design decisions · Change control · Test governance
```

### Ownership

| Role | Responsibility |
| :--- | :--- |
| Produces | Solution owner produces design; BA produces requirement and impact challenge |
| Approves | Architecture or technology authority approves solution design within mandate |
| Consumes | Build, data, testing and operations teams consume the design |
| Independently challenges | Control functions challenge design impacts within their mandates |

### Permanent rule

The BA may challenge the "how" because the "how" can violate the "what". Challenging a design does not transfer design accountability to the BA.

### Never confuse [Requirement clarification] and [Solution design]

**Requirement clarification** - removes ambiguity from the business obligation without changing its approved meaning.

---

**Solution design** - chooses the technical or operational mechanism used to satisfy that obligation.

### Permanent trap

Accepting "the system cannot do that" as an automatic requirement change. Technical constraint is an input to a business decision, not authority to rewrite the need.
