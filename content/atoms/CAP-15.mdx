Internal models can generate extremely low PDs, LGDs or other parameters for portfolios with benign observed histories. That may reflect genuinely low risk, but it may also reflect limited defaults, favourable economic periods, data scarcity or model specification. Basel therefore imposes **parameter constraints**, including prescribed floors for relevant IRB inputs.

An input floor works before the risk-weight function. If the model estimates a parameter below the applicable regulatory floor, the floor is used for capital purposes. The model estimate may remain useful for internal risk management, but it is not allowed to drive regulatory capital below the prescribed prudential boundary.

Parameter constraints also include rules on estimation horizon, downturn treatment, eligible exposure classes, maturity and the circumstances in which bank estimates may be used. Their collective purpose is to reduce excessive model variability and make capital less dependent on sparse or optimistic data.

The **output floor** operates at a different level. Input floors constrain individual parameters; the output floor compares total internally modelled RWA with a percentage of standardised RWA after the model calculations have been performed. A bank can therefore satisfy every input floor and still be constrained by the output floor.

**Perishable — India’s revised commercial-bank credit framework effective 1 April 2027 is standardised, so IRB input floors are not domestic Indian credit-capital parameters.**

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
An input floor changes or bounds a model parameter before the risk-weight calculation; it does not directly impose a percentage floor on total RWA.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Calling the 72.5% output floor a “PD floor” mixes an aggregate RWA backstop with parameter-level constraints.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Input floor" b="Output floor">
**Input floor** — a lower bound on an IRB risk parameter or model input.

---
**Output floor** — an aggregate lower bound on model-based RWA relative to standardised RWA.
</NeverConfuse>
