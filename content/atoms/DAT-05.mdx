A bank’s risk reports should paint a complete picture: covering all major risk types (credit, market, liquidity, etc.) and including both totals and trend analysis. They must be error-free and understandable; jargon must be explained for non-experts. BCBS 239 (Principle 7–11) says reports should be of high quality (“comprehensive, timely, clear”) and delivered at the mandated frequency. For instance, daily and weekly dashboards for market risk, and periodic reports for portfolios, with exceptions escalated as needed. The reports must include documented definitions (so recipients know what each metric means) and must follow a regular schedule. Distribution (Principle 11) demands reports go to the right people (board, risk committee, regulators) and that escalation paths exist if limits are breached.

In summary, risk reporting is about governance of the report content and process: it involves standard templates, sign-off procedures, and validation checks (ensuring the report matches the aggregated data). A common failure (trap) is producing reports without reconciling them back to source data (so-called “data drift”). Another mistake is listing raw data without analysis. Regulators have noted banks sometimes produce stat sheets but not insightful narrative or exception lists. Under BCBS 239, the **permanent expectation** is that reports translate data into actionable intelligence, validated end-to-end.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Each risk report must be accompanied by context and definition, and approved by the appropriate officer; raw numbers alone are insufficient.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Dumping large tables of figures in a report with no explanatory content or without ensuring they match the underlying data.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Frequency** vs **Response Time**:  
Frequency — how often reports are published (e.g. daily, monthly).  
Response Time (Timeliness) — how quickly updated data is produced during stress. Reporting frequency must align with decision needs but is not the same as intraday data availability.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** These qualitative reporting expectations are set at the global level by BCBS 239; national supervisors typically echo them.
</Rule>
