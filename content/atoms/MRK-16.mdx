The PLA test evaluates how well a desk’s risk model explains actual P&amp;L.  **Risk-theoretical P&amp;L** is the model’s forecast of one-day P&amp;L using the desk’s risk factors. **Hypothetical P&amp;L** is the P&amp;L from revaluing yesterday’s positions with today’s prices.  Basel requires these to be highly correlated over time.  A desk must satisfy PLA on an ongoing basis to retain IMA status.  

Many desks fail PLA because models omit risk factors or simplify valuation.  For example, a desk trading exotic options may not include volatility surface shifts or correlation terms in its model, so the hypothetical P&amp;L (which captures actual instrument repricing) diverges from the model’s P&amp;L.  Common issues include illiquid instruments with no observable prices, or mis-specified static factors.  Failing PLA means the desk’s capital must be calculated using the less favorable standardised approach.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Desks using IMA must continuously satisfy PLA (compare model vs revalued P&amp;L).
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Assuming a desk passes PLA based on backtesting alone; neglecting PLA discrepancies can invalidate the IMA.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Risk-theoretical P&amp;L** — desk’s model-predicted P&amp;L; **Hypothetical P&amp;L** — P&amp;L from revaluing held positions at new market prices.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** PLA requirements are part of the international FRTB IMA criteria.
</Rule>

<Recall
  q="What two P&Ls are compared in the PLA test?"
  a="The risk-theoretical P&L (model) vs the hypothetical P&L (revaluation of positions)."
/>

<Recall
  q="What happens if a desk fails PLA?"
  a="The desk must switch to the standardised approach for market risk capital."
/>
