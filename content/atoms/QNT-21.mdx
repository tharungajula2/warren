ARIMA stands for AutoRegressive Integrated Moving Average. In an ARIMA model, \(p\) is the number of AR terms (lags of the dependent variable), \(d\) is the number of differences to make the series stationary, and \(q\) is the number of MA terms (lags of the forecast error). For example, ARIMA(1,1,1) on default rates means first difference the series (to remove trend), then fit one AR and one MA component on the differenced series. SARIMA (Seasonal ARIMA) adds seasonal orders \((P,D,Q)_m\): \(P\) seasonal AR lags, \(D\) seasonal differences, and \(Q\) seasonal MA lags, with period \(m\) (e.g. 12 for monthly data).

For instance, SARIMA(0,1,1)\(\times\)(0,1,1)\(_&#123;12&#125;\) is a common model for monthly data: it differences once to remove trend and once seasonally (lag 12), with one AR and one MA at lag 12. The forecasting equation is linear in the lagged differenced series and lagged errors. If defaults show both a trend and an annual cycle, SARIMA can capture both. Model orders are typically chosen using ACF/PACF plots and information criteria (AIC/BIC).

```text
Original series Y_t (possibly non-stationary)
    |  (difference d times)
    v
Differenced series Δ^d Y_t
    |  (if seasonal, difference every m)
    v
Stationary series
    |
    v
Fit ARMA(p,q) (plus seasonal ARMA if SARIMA)
    |
    v
Forecasts (by extrapolating lags of series and errors)
```

### Permanent rule

ARIMA(p,d,q) = difference \(d\) times to stationarize, then fit ARMA(p,q). Seasonal ARIMA adds seasonal differences and terms for period \(m\). Always difference non-stationary series before modeling.

### Permanent trap

Forgetting to difference a non-stationary series (taking \(d=0\) when a trend exists) or neglecting seasonality. This leads to poor model fit and biased forecasts. Always test for unit roots and seasonal cycles.

### Never confuse

**ARIMA** — an ARIMA model always includes an integration (I) component; if \(d=0\), it reduces to ARMA.

---

**ARMA** — a special case of ARIMA with \(d=0\). ARMA assumes the series is already stationary (no differencing).

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** The ARIMA framework is a mathematical model. Regulatory constraints (e.g. IRB validation) do not change its formulation, although practitioners may prefer simpler models if interpretability is required.
