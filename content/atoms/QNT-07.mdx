### Core mechanism

| Time axis | Question answered | Typical distortion if ignored |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Origination vintage | Which loans entered under the same underwriting and macro environment? | Mixes changing policies and borrower quality |
| Months on book | How mature is each account? | New accounts receive less opportunity to fail |
| Calendar period | What macro conditions affect all active vintages now? | Confuses cycle effects with origination quality |
| Observation horizon | How long is performance measured? | Compares unequal event opportunities |

```text
Vintage A originated Jan
    ↓
3 months → 6 months → 12 months seasoning
Vintage B originated Jul
    ↓
3 months → 6 months → 12 months seasoning
```

A fair vintage comparison aligns account age. Comparing Vintage A at 12 months with Vintage B at 3 months confounds underwriting quality with exposure time. Conversely, comparing different vintages at the same calendar date can reveal common macro shocks but not pure seasoning effects.

### Formula

```text
Cumulative vintage default rate at month m = defaults observed by month m / originated accounts eligible for that vintage measure
```

### Permanent rule

Compare like seasoning with like seasoning unless the purpose is explicitly to measure calendar-time effects.

### Never confuse [Vintage effect] and [Calendar effect]

**Vintage effect** - difference associated with the cohort's origination conditions, underwriting or borrower mix.

---

**Calendar effect** - condition affecting multiple active vintages at the same time, such as a macroeconomic shock.

### Permanent trap

Calling a recent vintage "better" because its cumulative default rate is lower before it has reached the age at which older vintages generated most of their defaults.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta. Vintage and seasoning mathematics are universal.
