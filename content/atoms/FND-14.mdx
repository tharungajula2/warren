### Core mechanism



### Formula

```text
NPV = sum of [CF_t / (1 + r)^t] across all t
NPV = net present value at the chosen valuation date
CF_t = signed cash flow at time t
r = discount rate per stated period
t = number of consistent periods to each cash flow

Probability weighting and discounting are separate operations: probability changes the expected amount; discounting changes the value of its timing.
```

### Flow

```text
Possible cash shortfalls
    ↓
Probability-weight where methodology requires
    ↓
Place on expected dates
    ↓
Discount where methodology requires
    ↓
Sum at reporting / valuation date
```

### Permanent rule

Do not mix uncertainty and time value into one unexplained adjustment. They are separate operations even when one framework embeds both in a single measurement process.

### Never confuse [Nominal shortfall] and [Present value of shortfall]

**Nominal shortfall** — difference in cash amounts at the future date.

---

**Present value of shortfall** — that amount translated to the valuation date under the applicable discount rule.

### Permanent trap

Exporting one framework's discounting rule into another framework because both use “expected credit loss” language.

### Jurisdiction variants

#### IN (RBI)
Scheduled commercial-bank provisioning follows RBI prudential directions rather than an assumption that IFRS 9 EIR discounting governs bank IRACP provisioning.

#### UK
For entities applying UK-adopted IFRS 9, expected credit losses are discounted to the reporting date using the EIR determined at initial recognition or an approximation, subject to IFRS 9's instrument-specific rules.

#### EU
The same IFRS 9 principle applies where EU-adopted IFRS 9 governs the accounts.

#### US
CECL permits multiple reasonable estimation methods that achieve the Topic 326 objective; a discounted-cash-flow method is not mandatory in every case.
