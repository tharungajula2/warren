Under FATF’s standards, banks are expected to tailor their AML controls to the level of risk. This means identifying each customer’s risk level (high, medium, low) based on factors like customer profile (e.g. PEP vs retail), country of origin (sanctioned vs safe), industry (cash-intensive vs usual), and product (complex instruments vs basic deposits).  The FATF states that firms should “identify, assess and understand ML/TF risk… and take AML measures commensurate to the risk level”.  For example, a high-risk client may have quarterly KYC reviews, while a low-risk retail saver may only need basic onboarding checks.

A customer risk rating typically combines factors: a PEP from a high-risk country doing large wire transfers might be “high risk,” requiring enhanced due diligence. Conversely, a local salaried employee with small deposits is “low risk,” possibly even eligible for simplified due diligence. The product or transaction risk is also considered: certain services (like private banking or crypto trading) raise risk. The key is that higher-risk relationships have more rigorous checks (EDD) while very low-risk customers may get reduced scrutiny (SDD) if regulations allow.

### Permanent rule

Always document a customer’s risk rating and apply the corresponding level of due diligence. Higher risk customers require senior review and more frequent monitoring, as per FATF’s guidance.

### Permanent trap

Treating all customers identically (one-size-fits-all compliance). Also, misjudging risk: e.g. calling a high-net-worth business owner “low risk” just because they’re polite and have an account manager.

### Never confuse

**Customer risk** — the inherent ML/TF risk of the customer’s profile (PEP status, business type, location).
---
**Transaction/product risk** — risk associated with a particular product or transaction (cash deposit, trade finance, etc.). They are often conflated; in fact, a high-risk customer can have safe transactions, and vice versa.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** All AML regimes (US BSA, UK MLR, RBI, EU AMLD) require a risk-based approach. The FATF standard (Rec.1) is implemented universally, though exact categories (what is “high risk”) may differ by country’s assessment.
