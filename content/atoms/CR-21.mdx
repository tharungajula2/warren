A **rating grade** places an obligor into an ordered category of credit quality. Better grades represent lower default risk; weaker grades represent higher risk. A **master scale** is the complete ordered set of grades and their risk meaning, often including one or more default categories.

A rating is not merely a label such as "Grade 4". Its meaning comes from its relationship to other grades, its assignment criteria and its calibrated PD. Basel requires qualifying wholesale rating systems to distinguish borrower default risk from transaction-specific characteristics, reinforcing why obligor rating should not be contaminated by facility collateral when the purpose is to estimate borrower default likelihood.

**Rating migration** is movement between grades over time. An upgrade is movement to better credit quality; a downgrade is movement to worse credit quality. A **transition matrix** records the frequencies or probabilities of movements from each starting grade to each ending grade, including default.

Migration data reveal information that a snapshot cannot. Two portfolios may both contain 10% weak-rated borrowers today, yet one may have been stable while the other has rapidly migrated downward. The latter carries different momentum and validation implications.

Transition analysis needs adequate history. Federal Reserve supervisory work notes that sound rating-transition models require sufficiently robust time series and granular rating systems; sparse grades or too few transitions can make estimated matrices unreliable.

Grades must also avoid artificial stickiness or excessive volatility. A supposedly TTC system that never downgrades before default fails to recognise deterioration; an excessively PIT system may move borrowers several grades for modest cyclical noise. The intended rating philosophy must be explicit.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
A grade has no standalone meaning; it must be interpreted within a documented ordered scale and a defined rating philosophy.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Recalibrating grade PDs while silently changing assignment criteria makes historical migration appear comparable when the grades no longer represent the same risk classes.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Rating migration** — movement of an obligor between risk grades.

**PD calibration** — mapping a grade or score to a probability of default; PD can change even if the grade label does not.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Rating-scale and transition concepts are universal; regulatory rules differ on minimum granularity, estimation and model approval.
</Rule>

<Recall
  q="What does a transition matrix show?"
  a="Movements from starting rating states to ending states, including default, over a defined period."
/>

<Recall
  q="Why should collateral normally not determine an obligor's default grade?"
  a="Collateral principally changes transaction loss severity, while the obligor grade is intended to represent borrower default risk."
