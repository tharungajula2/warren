A borrower can owe 100 contractually while the bank expects to recover less. Credit-loss accounting provides a mechanism for reflecting that deterioration before the final cash loss necessarily occurs. The resulting **allowance** is an accounting adjustment associated with expected credit losses; the related charge ordinarily affects profit or loss under the applicable framework.

The allowance must not be confused with cash placed in a separate account. Under US CECL, for example, the allowance for credit losses on financial assets measured at amortised cost is a **valuation account deducted from amortised cost** to present the amount expected to be collected. Under IFRS 9, expected credit losses are probability-weighted and the impairment model distinguishes 12-month and lifetime expected credit losses according to the applicable stage and conditions.

The Indian banking position requires special discipline. RBI deferred implementation of Ind AS for scheduled commercial banks in April 2019 **until further notice**. Their prudential asset classification and provisioning operate under RBI directions, not an assumption that Ind AS 109 applies to banks. Relevant NBFCs that are required to apply Ind AS have separate RBI guidance dealing explicitly with impairment allowances under **Ind AS 109**.

This distinction matters operationally because "expected loss", "provision", "allowance", "stage" and "NPA" can belong to different rule systems. Similar economic concerns do not make the accounting mechanics interchangeable.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
A credit-loss allowance is a measurement or valuation mechanism, not a segregated pot of cash.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Applying IFRS 9 staging and ECL rules to an Indian scheduled commercial bank merely because Ind AS 109 exists for other Indian financial entities.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Credit-loss allowance" b="Regulatory provision">
**Credit-loss allowance** — an accounting valuation adjustment reflecting credit losses under the applicable reporting framework.
---
**Regulatory provision** — an amount required by prudential rules; it may interact with accounting allowances but need not be calculated on the same basis.
</NeverConfuse>

— RBI deferred Ind AS implementation for scheduled commercial banks until further notice; bank income recognition, asset classification and provisioning therefore operate under RBI prudential directions. <Delta j="UK">
— For entities reporting under UK-adopted IFRS, IFRS 9 contains the expected-credit-loss impairment model. [UK Endorsement Board, UK-adopted IFRS 9]
</Delta>

<Delta j="EU">
— For reporting within the scope of EU-adopted IFRS, IFRS 9 is part of the EU-endorsed accounting framework. [Regulation (EU) 2023/1803]
</Delta>

<Delta j="US">
— ASC Topic 326 uses CECL for applicable financial assets; CECL estimates expected credit losses over the contractual term, considering specified historical, current and reasonable-and-supportable forecast information. [FASB ASU 2016-13; Federal Reserve CECL FAQ]
</Delta>
