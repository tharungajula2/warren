### Core mechanism

| Decision | More granularity gives | Too much granularity causes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Portfolio segmentation | Better fit to genuinely different products or borrower processes | Thin samples and unstable segment estimates |
| Variable binning | Captures nonlinear or threshold-shaped risk | Small-bin volatility and overfitting |
| Merge adjacent bins | More stable event rates | Loss of real local structure |
| Monotonic constraint | Smoother, interpretable risk ordering | Can hide genuine non-monotonic behavior |
| Missing-value bin | Explicit treatment of missingness | Can turn operational missingness into an unstable risk proxy |

```text
Raw continuous predictor
    ↓
Candidate cut points
    ↓
Check counts + defaults + ordering + stability
    ↓
Merge unstable or indistinguishable bins
    ↓
Final bins used as model inputs
```

A cut point chosen after repeatedly inspecting the same default outcomes uses outcome information to optimize the representation. That is legitimate model development only if its optimism is controlled with validation and out-of-time testing.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Rare defaults · Logistic relationship · Portfolio heterogeneity
This:       Segmentation · Cut points · Bin stability
Downstream: WoE · IV · Score transformation
```

### Formula

```text
Bin default rate = defaults in bin / observations in bin
```

### Permanent rule

Every additional segment or bin spends data. Granularity is justified only when the extra structure is stable enough to survive new lending observations and time periods.

### Never confuse [Segmentation] and [Binning]

**Segmentation** - separates populations that may require different model relationships or treatment.

---

**Binning** - groups values of one predictor within a modelling population.

### Permanent trap

Forcing monotonic bins by repeatedly moving cut points on the development sample until the bad-rate pattern looks clean. That can encode sample noise as if it were economic structure.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta. Segmentation and binning mathematics are universal; CR owns how the resulting scorecard is constructed and used.

<Recall
  q="Why can a statistically intuitive narrow bin be dangerous in a low-default portfolio?"
  a="It may contain too few defaults to estimate its risk reliably, so its apparent separation can disappear or reverse out of sample."
/>

<Recall
  q="What is the main trade-off when merging adjacent bins?"
  a="Merging increases sample stability but reduces the model's ability to represent real local differences in the predictor-risk relationship."
