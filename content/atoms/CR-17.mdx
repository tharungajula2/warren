### Core mechanism

| Facility component | Credit question |
| :--- | :--- |
| Current drawn amount | What is already exposed? |
| Undrawn commitment | How much can still be drawn before default? |
| Contingent item | What amount can crystallise into a claim? |
| Accrued interest/fees | What additional amount may be outstanding at default under the chosen basis? |
| Limit cancellation / draw-stop behaviour | How much future drawdown is actually possible? |

For a fully drawn amortising loan, EAD can be close to expected balance at default. For cards, overdrafts and revolving commitments, distressed borrowers may draw further before failure, so current utilisation can materially understate EAD.

### Flow

```text
Current drawn amount + Undrawn / contingent amount
    ↓
Apply drawdown / CCF logic
    ↓
Additional pre-default exposure
    ↓
EAD at default
```

### Flow

```text
Upstream:   Facility limit · Drawn balance · Undrawn amount · Behaviour · Default date
This:       Convert relevant off-balance / undrawn amount → add to default exposure
Downstream: Expected loss · LGD denominator · Regulatory RWA · Limit analysis
```

### Formula

Teaching decomposition:

`CCF = additional pre-default drawing / undrawn amount at reference date`

`EAD = current drawn amount + CCF × undrawn amount`

CCF ↑ → EAD ↑, all else equal. This is not a universal regulatory formula; applicable frameworks prescribe treatment by exposure type and approach.

### Permanent rule

EAD is anchored to the default event. Current balance is an input, not the definition.

### Never confuse [EAD] and [Current utilisation]

**EAD** — exposure expected or prescribed at default.

---
**Current utilisation** — amount drawn now, often expressed relative to the facility limit.

### Permanent trap

Estimating revolving-credit loss on today’s balance while LGD is measured against default-date exposure creates a denominator mismatch and usually understates drawdown risk.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta in the mechanism.** Regulatory conversion factors, modelling permissions and off-balance-sheet exposure rules differ by jurisdiction and prudential approach.
