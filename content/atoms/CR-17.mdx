For a stylised exposure, expected monetary credit loss can be represented as:

`EL = PD × LGD × EAD`

Suppose one-year PD is 2%, LGD is 40%, and EAD is 100. The expected loss is:

`0.02 × 0.40 × 100 = 0.8`

The result is an **expectation across possible outcomes**, not a forecast that this specific exposure will lose 0.8. In reality the borrower might not default and lose nothing, or might default and generate a much larger loss.

The multiplication hides important assumptions. PD must refer to the same default definition that determines the LGD sample. LGD must be conditional on that default. EAD must be measured at the corresponding default event. Horizons must align. Currency and valuation bases must align.

Dependence also matters. The neat product is exact under a conditional decomposition when LGD and EAD inputs are appropriate conditional expectations associated with default. A careless implementation that simply multiplies three independently estimated unconditional averages can be biased when exposure utilisation and recovery severity worsen around default or in common downturn conditions.

For example, revolving borrowers may draw more just before default while collateral recoveries deteriorate in the same recession. Treating PD, LGD and EAD as unrelated constants can conceal this joint behaviour.

Basel's IRB framework separates the risk components PD, LGD, EAD and maturity and calculates regulatory expected-loss amounts under prescribed rules rather than treating a generic spreadsheet identity as the whole capital framework.

&lt;Flow&gt;
+-----------+     +-----------+     +-----------+
| PD        |  x  | LGD       |  x  | EAD       |
| default   |     | severity  |     | amount    |
+-----------+     +-----------+     +-----------+
       \              |               /
        \             |              /
         v            v             v
+---------------------------------------+
| Expected credit loss                  |
| under aligned definitions             |
+---------------------------------------+
&lt;/Flow&gt;

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Never multiply PD, LGD and EAD until their default definition, horizon, observation point and population have been reconciled.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Combining a 12-month regulatory PD, lifetime accounting LGD and today's balance produces a number with units but no coherent risk interpretation.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Expected loss** — probability-weighted average loss over the defined horizon.

**Most likely loss** — the modal or most probable outcome; for a low-PD loan this may be zero even when expected loss is positive.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** The probability decomposition is universal; accounting and regulatory frameworks prescribe different parameter definitions, horizons and calculation rules.
</Rule>

<Recall
  q="Why is the multiplication &quot;not innocent&quot;?"
  a="Because the inputs must be aligned and may be dependent, especially around default and downturns."
