### Core mechanism

```text
Asset loss
    ↓
Retained earnings / CET1 absorb first
    ↓
Further going-concern loss absorption
    ↓
Viability threatened
    ↓
Resolution / non-viability mechanisms
    ↓
Subordinated gone-concern resources absorb loss before senior claims
```

Basel classifies CET1 and AT1 within Tier 1, its going-concern layer. Tier 2 is the lower-quality layer intended principally for loss absorption at non-viability or resolution. The legal trigger and write-down or conversion mechanism depend on the applicable instrument terms and resolution law.

A solvent bank can still be illiquid, and an illiquid bank can temporarily remain solvent. Therefore a strong CET1 ratio does not mean the bank holds enough cash to meet tomorrow's withdrawals.

### Permanent rule

Regulatory quality depends on whether loss absorption is reliably available when required, not on whether an instrument is called equity or subordinated debt.

### Never confuse [Going-concern capital] and [Gone-concern capital]

**Going-concern capital** — absorbs loss while the institution remains viable and operating.

---

**Gone-concern capital** — absorbs loss principally after non-viability, failure or resolution action.

### Permanent trap

Treating Tier 2 as equivalent to CET1 because both count in Total Capital ignores the timing and quality of their loss absorption.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta in the Basel hierarchy. Resolution statutes and contractual non-viability mechanisms differ by jurisdiction.
