### Core mechanism

| Contract | Customer view | Bank view |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Positive deposit balance | Asset: claim on bank | Liability: amount owed to customer |
| Loan made by bank | Liability: amount owed to bank | Asset: claim on borrower |

Funding answers a different question from classification: **what supports the bank's assets?** Customer deposits are one source; wholesale borrowing and equity are others. No rule says each unit of deposit funding must be traceable to a particular loan.

A bank therefore has two simultaneous relationships with many customers: it can owe them deposits while other customers owe it loans. The balance sheet aggregates these positions rather than pairing each depositor with each borrower.

```text
Deposits / Borrowings / Equity
    ↓
Funding base
    ↓

    ↓
Loans · Securities · Cash · Other claims
```

### Permanent rule

Instrument classification follows the reporting entity's rights and obligations; funding describes how the asset side is supported, not where a specific currency unit “came from”.

### Never confuse [Deposit] and [Bank asset]

**Deposit** — normally a liability of the bank because the bank owes the depositor.

---

**Bank asset** — a resource or claim controlled by the bank, such as cash, securities, or a loan receivable.

### Never confuse [Funding] and [Cash]

**Funding** — liabilities and equity supporting assets.

---

**Cash** — one asset on the balance sheet; receiving funding can increase cash, but the concepts are not the same.

### Permanent trap

Saying “deposits are money in the bank, therefore deposits are bank assets”. That description uses the customer's intuition while reading the bank's balance sheet.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** The contractual perspective and the distinction between funding sources and assets are universal mechanics.
