**Interest income** is income generated by interest-bearing assets such as loans and debt securities. **Interest expense** is the cost recognised on interest-bearing liabilities such as deposits and borrowings. Their difference is **net interest income (NII)**. The Federal Reserve uses this definition when analysing bank earnings.

Suppose a bank recognises 8 of interest income and 3 of interest expense during a period. NII is 5. The calculation is simple; interpreting it is not. NII depends on the amount of interest-earning assets, the rates earned on them, the amount and mix of funding, the rates paid for that funding, and how quickly asset and liability rates reprice.

**Net interest margin (NIM)** scales NII relative to earning assets. A common supervisory presentation annualises net interest income and divides it by average earning assets. NII is therefore an amount of income; NIM is a ratio.

A bank's earnings are broader than interest. Fees and commissions can arise from services. Securities and other financial instruments can generate gains or losses. Staff, technology and premises create operating costs. Credit deterioration can create impairment or provisioning expense under the applicable accounting or regulatory framework. Taxes then reduce pre-tax earnings. Consequently, a large NII does not guarantee a large profit.

The economic spread also cannot be read from two headline interest rates alone. Funding and asset balances differ, instruments reprice at different times, some balances pay or earn no interest, and defaults can prevent expected interest from being collected. Bank profitability analysis therefore begins with NII but cannot end there.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
NII measures the accounting difference between interest income and interest expense; it is not the same as total bank profit.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Comparing the average loan rate directly with the average deposit rate and calling the difference NIM without weighting balances, other funding or the denominator.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Net interest income" b="Net interest margin">
**Net interest income** — an amount: interest income minus interest expense.
---
**Net interest margin** — a ratio relating net interest income to earning assets; the similar names conceal different units.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** The economic distinction between interest income, interest expense and their net amount does not change across jurisdictions.
</Rule>

<Recall
  q="What is net interest income?"
  a="Interest income minus interest expense."

<Recall
