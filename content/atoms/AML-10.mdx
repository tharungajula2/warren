Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) is required when a customer or situation is high-risk. FATF and most regulations demand EDD for PEPs, cross-border correspondent banking, and customers from FATF “gray/black” jurisdictions.  For example, RBI directs banks to apply EDD measures (e.g. senior management approval, detailed source-of-funds analysis) for relationships with persons from high-risk countries flagged by the FATF.  Other triggers include private banking services, complex corporate ownership, or any red flags.

Simplified Due Diligence (SDD) is the opposite: lower-risk scenarios. For instance, stable salaried individuals in low-risk areas might only need standard ID verification. Many rules (UK MLR, EU AMLD) allow SDD in limited cases, but usually not in the US. RBI and FATF permit SDD only when risk is demonstrably minimal (e.g. listed companies with public financials). SDD might waive certain documentation if risk is very low, but it must still comply with basic KYC.

### Permanent rule

Apply EDD whenever a customer, product, or country is assessed as high-risk (PEPs, shell companies, high-risk jurisdictions). Use SDD only when explicitly allowed by law and documented as low-risk. Maintain clear policies on what conditions allow simplifications.

### Permanent trap

Mistakenly assuming simplified due diligence can apply just to expedite onboarding. (SDD is the exception, not the norm.) On the flip side, applying EDD inconsistently or without justification, which can delay business and annoy customers.

### Never confuse

**Simplified Due Diligence (SDD)** — reduced compliance measures for *low*-risk customers, permitted by law in narrow cases. It is not a way to cut corners unlawfully.

---

**Inadequate due diligence** — poor or missing checks. Some confuse SDD with simply not doing enough. Proper SDD is documented as permitted by policy, not negligence.

### Jurisdiction variants

**IN** — RBI permits SDD for low-risk customers (e.g. salary accounts with salary credits) and mandates EDD for high-risk ones, including foreign entities from FATF-specified countries.

**UK** — MLR 2017 allows SDD in limited categories (e.g. listed companies) and requires EDD for PEPs, TF, or high-risk scenarios.

**EU** — EU AML law permits optional SDD if national authorities designate low-risk situations, and mandates EDD for PEPs and TF by virtue of FATF Rec 10/12.

**US** — The US does not formally recognize “SDD.” Banks are expected to use at least standard CIP for all customers. However, FinCEN allows some exceptions (e.g. certain trusted accounts). EDD for foreign PEPs was formerly required under FinCEN guidance, but overall US approach is risk-based without a labeled “SDD.”
