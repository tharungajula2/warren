Compare:

`Restricted model`
vs
`Larger model`

<Formula math="\Large \displaystyle LR= -2(\log L_{restricted}-\log L_{full})" />

Tests whether added parameters meaningfully improve likelihood.

## Classification Metrics

## Precision

<Formula math="\Large \displaystyle Precision= \frac{TP}{TP+FP}" />

Of predicted positives, how many were correct?

## Recall

<Formula math="\Large \displaystyle Recall= \frac{TP}{TP+FN}" />

Of actual positives, how many did we catch?

## Specificity

<Formula math="\Large \displaystyle Specificity= \frac{TN}{TN+FP}" />

## F1

<Formula math="\Large \displaystyle F1= 2 \frac{Precision\times Recall} {Precision+Recall}" />

## Probability Calibration

{/* UNVERIFIED CLAIM — check primary source before publishing */}
Suppose model assigns **20% probability** to many cases.

A calibrated model should observe roughly:

{/* UNVERIFIED CLAIM — check primary source before publishing */}
**20% positives** among such cases.

## Metrics / diagnostics

- calibration curve
- Brier Score
- Log Loss
- calibration intercept/slope
- Hosmer-Lemeshow in traditional settings

### Calibration methods

- Platt scaling
- isotonic regression
- intercept recalibration

### Key distinction

> **Discrimination = who is riskier?**

> **Calibration = how risky are they?**

## Threshold Is a Business Decision

Logistic model outputs:

<Formula math="\Large \displaystyle P(Y=1)" />

It does **not inherently require 0.50**.

Choose threshold using:

- cost FP
- cost FN
- capacity
- risk appetite
- precision/recall requirement
- profit/loss function

### Therefore

**Model development**
and
**decision policy**

are related but separate.
