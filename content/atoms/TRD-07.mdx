A yield curve is not one universal object. It is a term structure built from a defined set of instruments or market quotes: government securities, overnight-index swaps, interbank instruments or another consistent basis. The horizontal axis is maturity or tenor; the vertical axis is a yield, zero rate, forward rate or related measure depending on the curve.

An upward-sloping curve has longer rates above shorter rates; an inverted curve has some longer rates below shorter rates; a flat curve has little difference. The shape reflects many forces: expected future short rates, inflation expectations, term premia, supply and demand, liquidity and policy expectations. The curve should therefore be treated as a market pricing object, not a deterministic forecast.

Three broad movements are useful mental models. A level move shifts rates broadly together. A slope move changes the difference between short and long rates. A curvature move changes the middle relative to the ends. Real curves can exhibit combinations of all three.

Pricing requires more than reading a par-yield chart. Many derivatives and bonds are valued using zero-coupon discount factors derived by bootstrapping liquid market instruments. Forward rates can then be implied between future dates. A par rate, zero rate and forward rate at the same headline maturity are not interchangeable.

Risk measures must match this structure. A single duration assumes a parallel move and can hide offsetting exposures. Key-rate DV01 or bucketed sensitivities show how value responds to specific sections of the curve. Curve construction also has operational dependencies: instrument set, day counts, calendars, interpolation, compounding and extrapolation.

The practical test is always: which curve, built from which instruments, for which purpose? “The five-year rate” is incomplete unless the rate family and convention are known.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Always name the curve and the rate type before interpreting a point on it.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Assuming every yield-curve shock is parallel and therefore fully captured by one duration number.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Par yield" b="Zero rate">
**Par yield** — coupon rate that makes a bond price at par for a given maturity under the curve.

---

**Zero rate** — discount rate for a single cash flow at a specific maturity.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.**
</Rule>
