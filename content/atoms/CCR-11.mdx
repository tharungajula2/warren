  A CCP’s default waterfall is its ordered pool of resources to cover a member’s
  losses. Typically, waterfall layers are: the defaulting member’s initial
  margin → the member’s default fund contribution → the CCP’s own capital (“skin
  in the game”) → the mutualized default fund of surviving members. If losses
  exceed all, additional measures (like assessments or equity auction) are used.

For example, if Member X defaults on a large loss, the CCP applies X’s margin first. If that is insufficient, it takes X’s share of the default fund. If still short, the CCP’s own capital absorbs the remainder (to align incentives). Finally, non-defaulting members’ default fund contributions cover any further shortfall. This ensures that members have “skin” in the outcome of defaults.  

Waterfalls vary by CCP, but all include these layers. An incentivized contribution (skin in game) is usually 5–10% of required default fund. Capital regulations may allow partial offset for default fund contributions. The structure ensures losses are contained and predictable.  

Client clearing often has special default procedures: for example, a clearing member’s default may lead to porting client positions to other members or liquidating client trades separately, depending on segregation arrangements.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Only pledged margin and default fund contributions are at risk first. The order of the waterfall must be respected. Clearing members must cover their contributions on demand to ensure the waterfall is fully funded.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Assuming mutualized funds are available immediately. In a crisis, liquidity to cover losses may not be instant, and clawback provisions may differ.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Initial Margin** vs **Default Fund Contribution**.  
Initial margin is collateral posted for each member. Default fund contributions are shared resources by all members. They serve different roles in the waterfall.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Global CCP frameworks (CPMI-IOSCO Principles) require similar default waterfalls. Details (e.g. mandatory skin-in-game) are converging internationally.
</Rule>

<Recall
  q="What is a CCP’s own “skin in the game”?"
  a="It is the CCP’s capital at risk in the waterfall, usually a fixed percentage of the default fund, absorbing losses after a defaulting member’s resources."
/>

<Recall
  q="In a default waterfall, what comes after a member’s margin?"
  a="The member’s own default fund contributions, then the CCP’s capital, then other members’ contributions."
