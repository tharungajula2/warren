  The Account Aggregator (AA) ecosystem enables customers to share financial
  data from banks, investments and tax (GST) authorities with lenders upon
  consent. An AA is an RBI-regulated NBFC that securely fetches a borrower’s
  authenticated bank and other accounts data (like GST returns) to streamline
  credit assessment. In practice, a borrower can allow a prospective lender to
  view her bank statements and GST filings via the AA, replacing the need for
  manual document submission. This enhances underwriting accuracy for cash-flow
  lending.

Under AA rules, banks and NBFCs must obtain explicit consent through the AA network before accessing a customer’s data. For MSMEs, RBI and government agencies are pushing use of GST returns to gauge cash flows. RBI now permits lenders to use GST-tracking (via AAs) as part of their credit appraisal. This is a paradigm shift from collateral-based to data-driven lending: for example, instead of using fixed assets, a bank can underwrite a shop owner’s ₹20 lakh loan by analyzing 6 months of GST invoices and bank inflows via an AA platform. The RBI’s digital lending guidelines implicitly encourage such open data models (as long as privacy is protected).

Regulators (RBI, IBBI, GSTN) have jointly built frameworks: GSTN acts as a KYC and data provider (through Accounts Aggregator or NBFC-Factors). Lenders need to verify GSTIN status and utilization to validate turnover. The RBI’s 2022 standards require customer consent and confidentiality for all shared data. Meanwhile, data validation through GST filters out sham businesses.

The government’s Unified Lending Interface (ULI) is an upcoming initiative to integrate this: ULI would let a lender fetch customer data (from AAs or government sources) through APIs, assess eligibility and disburse quickly. As of 2026, ULI pilots (e.g. with NBFCs and fintechs) are underway under RBI and IBA supervision. The idea is a one-stop-shop for loan origination: borrower consents once, and multiple lenders can exchange standardized credit data.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Customer data (bank statements, GST returns, etc.) may be shared with lenders only through RBI-authorised channels (Account Aggregators) and only with customer consent.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Assuming lenders can scrape GST data directly. In fact, only AAs or designated repositories (with consent) can transmit GST filings to banks. Also, neglecting to update borrower’s risk after changes in their GST activity can misstate credit risk.
</Trap>

<Delta j="IN">

</Delta>
