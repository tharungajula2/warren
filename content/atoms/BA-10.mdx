Once the business requirements are agreed, the next step is often a detailed functional specification (or functional requirements document). In the functional spec, analysts or developers spell out how each business need will be met technically: database fields, algorithms, screen designs, API calls, etc.  For example, a business requirement might be “notify managers of high-risk trades.” The functional spec would describe the notification module, threshold parameters, and user interface.

In practice, keep these separate: the BA focuses on business needs, while solution designers write the functional spec. The BRD should remain at a business level “without excessive technical detail”. Any technical content needed for implementation goes into the functional specification.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Business requirements (in the BRD) and functional specifications are distinct.  The BRD should capture *what and why*. Details of *how* to meet those requirements (logic, UIs, data formats) belong in the functional spec after the BRD is approved.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Allowing design to creep into the BRD or holding back needed functional detail. For instance, adding a database table definition to the BRD is mixing layers.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Business Requirement" b="Functional Specification">
**Business Requirement** — a high-level need or rule that the solution must satisfy (e.g., “report X must include all customers over 60 days delinquent”).
---
**Functional Specification** — the technical design documenting how the system will achieve that requirement (e.g., database queries, user interface fields, business logic).

They get mixed up if a BA starts writing implementation detail in the BRD.
</NeverConfuse>

<Recall
  q="What goes into a functional spec that doesn’t belong in a BRD?"
  a="Technical details like data field mappings, calculations, UAT scripts – things that implement the requirement."
/>

<Recall
  q="Can one document be both the BRD and the functional spec?"
  a="No. The BRD states needs (no tech detail); the functional spec provides solution details."
/>
