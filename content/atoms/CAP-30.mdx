The safest implementation workflow is **jurisdiction → legal status → effective date → institutional scope → methodology**. Starting with a Basel formula and asking later whether it applies reverses the legal hierarchy.

In the **UK**, the PRA has issued final rules. The principal Basel 3.1 commencement is 1 January 2027; FRTB-IMA starts separately on 1 January 2028. Eligible Small Domestic Deposit Takers enter their simplified capital regime on 1 January 2027.

In the **EU**, CRR3 has already applied major finalisation measures since 1 January 2025. During 2026 the output floor is 55%, rising in annual stages to 72.5% from 1 January 2030. Binding FRTB market-risk own-funds requirements are postponed to 1 January 2027.

In **India**, current domestic commercial-bank minima are CET1 5.5%, Tier 1 7% and total capital 9%; the leverage minimum is 4% for D-SIBs and 3.5% for other banks. RBI’s final revised banking-book credit-risk framework uses the Standardised Approach from 1 April 2027. Scheduled commercial banks must not be labelled Ind AS 109 banks: RBI deferred their Ind AS implementation until further notice.

In the **US**, the March 2026 capital package is still a proposal, not the binding final Basel-endgame rule. The existing large-bank architecture continues to include the Federal Reserve stress capital buffer.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Never infer a bank’s binding capital treatment from Basel alone: identify the jurisdiction, current legal status, commencement date, scope and any transitional treatment first.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Putting UK, EU, India and US into a single “Basel 3.1 effective” column erases the most operationally important facts: different dates, different methodologies and, in the US, proposal status.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="International standard" b="Jurisdictional implementation">
**International standard** — BCBS benchmark that jurisdictions commit to implement.

---
**Jurisdictional implementation** — the binding or proposed local rule, with its own scope, dates, transitions and deviations.
</NeverConfuse>
