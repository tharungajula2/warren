### Core mechanism

| Measure | What it answers | Typical mistake |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Arrears | How much required payment is unpaid? | Treating amount as duration |
| DPD | How long has the relevant payment been overdue? | Starting the clock from statement or month-end instead of due date |
| Delinquency bucket | Which monitoring state does the DPD fall into? | Treating a bucket boundary as a universal default rule |
| Out-of-order test | Is a revolving/OD/CC facility persistently irregular under its rule? | Applying instalment logic to revolving credit |

DPD describes payment behaviour; the default definition determines when that behaviour, or separate qualitative evidence, becomes a governed credit state.

### Flow

```text
Contractual due date → Unpaid amount = arrears
    ↓
DPD clock starts → Delinquency bucket advances
    ↓
Collections / early warning → Governed default test
```

### Where it sits

Upstream:   Contractual due date · Required payment · Credits received
This:       Due → Overdue → DPD count → Delinquency state
Downstream: Collections · Early warning · Default test · Roll rates · Vintage analysis

### Formula

`DPD = observation date − relevant unpaid contractual due date`

The exact counting convention belongs to the governing policy or regulation. DPD ↑ → delinquency severity usually ↑, but default still requires the applicable rule.

### Permanent rule

Calculate delinquency from the contractual obligation and the specified counting convention; do not infer default merely from an informal bucket label.

### Never confuse [Arrears] and [Days past due]

**Arrears** — the unpaid amount.

---

**Days past due** — elapsed time associated with an unpaid contractual obligation.

### Permanent trap

Changing due-date logic, allocation of payments or day-end convention can create apparent credit deterioration or improvement without any change in borrower behaviour.

### Jurisdiction variants

#### IN (RBI)
RBI requires overdue, SMA and NPA status to be determined through the day-end process for the relevant date. Its illustration moves a continuously overdue account to SMA-1 after 30 days, SMA-2 after 60 days and NPA after the applicable 90-day progression; OD/CC products use the defined “out of order” test.

#### UK (PRA)
Past-due status and materiality are inputs to the current regulatory default framework; arrears buckets used operationally are not themselves the complete definition of default.

#### EU
CRR/EBA rules distinguish days-past-due mechanics, materiality and unlikeliness-to-pay indicators; reporting past-due bands do not replace Article 178 default.

#### US
Different regulatory purposes and retail categories use different delinquency/default thresholds; there is no safe universal “90 DPD for everything” rule.

<Recall
  q="Can two accounts have the same DPD but different arrears amounts?"
  a="Yes. DPD measures time; arrears measure money."
/>

<Recall
  q="Why does revolving credit need different delinquency logic?"
  a="It may not have a single scheduled principal instalment, so persistent limit/credit behaviour can be the relevant test."
/>

<Recall
  q="What does 60 DPD prove?"
  a="A payment has remained overdue under the specified convention; it does not prove default in every framework."
