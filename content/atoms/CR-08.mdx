**Unlikeliness to pay (UTP)** asks whether the obligor is likely to discharge its credit obligations in full without the lender having to rely on actions such as enforcing collateral. Basel and US wholesale-default rules expressly contain this concept.

UTP exists because payment history is lagging information. A borrower may still be contractually current on the observation date even though events have made full repayment implausible. Waiting for 90 days of arrears in such a case would make default identification systematically late.

Relevant evidence can arise from distressed restructuring, insolvency processes, severe deterioration in repayment capacity, credit-related write-downs or other well-defined indicators under the applicable framework. The point is not the name of the indicator but the inference: full repayment under the original credit obligation is no longer likely without exceptional intervention.

UTP must nevertheless be governed. An internal watch-list flag, a negative news item or a rating downgrade is not automatically default. Those signals may lead to assessment, but the institution needs documented criteria that distinguish increased risk from the threshold for default. Basel minimum requirements and PRA supervisory materials place emphasis on consistency, documentation and defined indicators because uncontrolled judgement would make default rates incomparable through time and across portfolios.

UTP also shows why collateral does not "prevent" default. A fully secured obligor can be unlikely to pay. Good collateral may produce a low LGD after default, but the need to realise security is itself central to the default concept in Basel-style formulations.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
UTP evaluates the likelihood of full payment, not the expected collateral recovery after payment failure.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Treating a well-secured exposure as non-defaulted because collateral should cover the debt confuses default likelihood with loss severity.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Unlikely to pay** — the obligor is not expected to meet the credit obligation in full without qualifying recovery action.

**High LGD** — a large loss severity conditional on default; an obligor can be UTP with either low or high LGD.
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="EU">

</Delta>

<Delta j="US">
— Advanced Approaches wholesale obligor default expressly includes a determination that the obligor is unlikely to pay in full without recourse to actions such as realising collateral. [12 CFR Part 217 Subpart E]
</Delta>
