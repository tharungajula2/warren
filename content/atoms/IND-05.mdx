In practice, an Indian bank is licensed under the BR Act and can grant loans (including overdrafts, credit cards) directly funded by deposits. NBFCs (except certain rare deposit-taking ones) cannot open current accounts or offer cheque books. They can only accept term/lien-marked savings deposits with restrictions, and they must invest most deposits in government securities (e.g. PBs keep 75% in SLR securities). Banks contribute to CRR and SLR at set ratios; NBFC deposit-takers have their own (lower) CRR rates and no CRR if non-deposit NBFC. NBFCs access RBI refinancing schemes (e.g. MSE financing, mortgages) but cannot borrow directly through standard bank routes like repo.

Regulatory burdens also differ: banks need full Basel capital ratios and AML/KYC; NBFCs follow RBI NBFC Directions (now largely converging with Basel norms for larger NBFCs). NBFCs often face extra restrictions on leverage, lending (e.g. sub-limits on unsecured loans), and must maintain Asset-Liability limits. For example, only banks count as “scheduled banks” in inter-bank systems. Moreover, only banks have statutory liquidity injection roles (like government banking), whereas NBFCs do not.

```
+----------+    vs     +------------+
|    Bank (SCB)      |    NBFCs        |
+----------+    +     +------------+
| - Accept current/savings deposits    | - Mostly term deposits (no current a/c)
| - Issue loans (OD, cards)             | - Issue loans but rely on other funding
| - No external deposit cap             | - Regulatory deposit caps (if NBFC-D)
| - Part of payment/clearing networks   | - Cannot directly clear payments
| - Bank Rate refinancing, RBI liquidity| - No direct access to repo window
| - Deposit insurance (DICGC)           | - No government deposit insurance
+---------------------------------------+
```

### Permanent rule

Banks are permitted to accept demand (current) deposits and to extend credit directly; NBFCs cannot accept current account deposits or create payment mechanisms.  Only SCBs hold RBI-guaranteed deposit insurance and use CRR/SLR facilities.

### Permanent trap

Treating an NBFC like a small bank. For example, assuming an NBFC’s deposits are instantly withdrawable (like a current account) – this is false.  Also ignoring NBFC funding limits can lead to compliance errors (NBFCs must invest most deposits in govt bonds, etc.).

### Never confuse

**Bank** — a licensed entity that can take demand deposits (cheque/savings accounts) and provide a full suite of banking services.
---
**NBFC** — a non-bank financial firm that cannot accept demand deposits or issue cheques; funding comes via term liabilities, and it is generally outside central-bank clearing.

### Jurisdiction variants

**IN** — RBI delineates banks (BR Act) vs NBFCs (RBI Act) sharply. NBFCs have limits (no current accounts, limits on deposits) not found in US/UK banking.
**No delta.** The general difference that banks take deposits and NBFCs do not is universal. But India’s deposit cap rules (e.g. ₹100k for PBs) are local details.
