### Core mechanism

| Dimension | Test | Example failure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Accuracy | Does the value correctly represent the governed source or real-world fact? | Outstanding balance differs from authoritative record |
| Completeness | Is the required population and required fields present? | One legal entity is absent from the exposure feed |
| Consistency | Do equivalent concepts agree across systems or reports after allowed differences? | Same customer has conflicting sector classifications |
| Timeliness | Is the data current and available when needed? | Position is accurate for yesterday but used for an intraday stress decision |
| Validity | Does the value conform to defined type, range, format or code rules? | Country code is outside the approved list |
| Uniqueness | Is each entity or event represented without unintended duplication? | One trade loaded twice |

Quality is always tied to use. A reporting field can require exact validity but allow a documented timing tolerance; another field can require near-real-time availability because the risk changes rapidly.

### Never confuse [Accuracy] and [Consistency]

**Accuracy** - the value is correct against the governed fact or authoritative source.

---

**Consistency** - equivalent representations agree after legitimate scope, timing and accounting differences are understood. Two systems can be consistently wrong.

### Permanent rule

Quality is fit for purpose: the required dimensions and tolerances follow material use, not a universal enterprise score applied blindly to every datum.

### Permanent trap

Reporting a high overall DQ percentage that averages away a critical failure. A small number of wrong CDEs can matter more than millions of clean low-impact fields.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Quality dimensions do not change by regulator; return-specific tolerances and validation rules can.
