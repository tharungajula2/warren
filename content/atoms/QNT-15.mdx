### Core mechanism

For each bin, compare the bin's share of all non-defaults with its share of all defaults. A positive WoE under the convention below means the bin contains a larger share of non-defaults than defaults; reversing the convention reverses the sign, so the definition must be fixed before interpreting coefficients.

| Pattern | WoE behavior | Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Equal shares of defaults and non-defaults | Near zero | Little separation in that bin |
| Non-default share exceeds default share | Positive | Safer under this sign convention |
| Default share exceeds non-default share | Negative | Riskier under this sign convention |
| Very small event count | Extreme or unstable | Smoothing or bin merging may be needed |

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Bins · Binary outcome · Logistic model
This:       WoE transformation · IV aggregation
Downstream: Variable selection · Logistic fitting · Stability monitoring
```

### Formula

```text
WoE(bin) = ln( (share of non-defaults in bin) / (share of defaults in bin) )
IV = sum over bins of ( share non-default - share default ) x WoE(bin)
```

### Permanent rule

WoE and IV are properties of a labelled sample and binning scheme, not timeless qualities of a variable.

### Never confuse [Weight of Evidence] and [Information Value]

**Weight of Evidence** - one value per bin describing relative class concentration.

---

**Information Value** - one aggregate number per variable summing separation across its bins.

### Permanent trap

Treating a very high IV as automatic proof of a brilliant predictor. Extreme IV can signal target leakage, post-event information or over-fine bins that memorize the development sample.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta. WoE and IV are statistical conventions, not jurisdiction-specific mathematics.

<Recall
  q="What makes WoE equal to zero under the stated convention?"
  a="The bin contains the same share of all non-defaults as it contains of all defaults, so the bin does not shift the class distribution relative to the sample totals."
/>

<Recall
  q="Why can IV increase when bins become too fine?"
  a="Small bins can exaggerate random differences between default and non-default shares, making sample noise look like predictive separation."
/>

<Recall
  q="Why must the WoE sign convention be stated explicitly?"
  a="Some implementations use non-default over default and others reverse it; the mathematics is equivalent but all signs and coefficient interpretations reverse."
/>
