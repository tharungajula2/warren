A useful conceptual starting point is:

`FTP tenor rate ≈ base/reference rate + funding-liquidity premium`

with a separate or embedded:

`contingent liquidity charge`

The **base rate** represents the time value and benchmark interest-rate component appropriate to the relevant currency and tenor. The **liquidity or funding premium** represents the additional marginal cost associated with obtaining term funding for the bank rather than simply observing a benchmark risk-free curve. This premium can vary substantially by tenor and market condition.

The **contingent liquidity charge** addresses exposures that may require cash in stress even though they are not fully funded today. Undrawn commitments, potential collateral calls and other contingent demands can force the bank to hold standby liquidity. The Federal Reserve’s FTP guidance explicitly describes contingent liquidity risk as the cost of holding standby unencumbered highly liquid assets and expects that cost to be allocated to the businesses creating the contingent funding need.

The curve should be marginal rather than merely an average of historic funding costs. Average cost answers what past liabilities cost; marginal cost is closer to what incremental balance-sheet usage economically requires now. The FSI identifies marginal matched-maturity funding cost as better practice.

Care is required when combining components. Credit spread, liquidity premium, interest-rate risk and contingent charges can overlap if definitions are vague. A well-governed FTP framework states exactly what each component transfers and prevents the same risk from being charged twice.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Every FTP component should correspond to a distinct economic cost or benefit and have a defined risk owner.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Adding a “liquidity spread”, “term premium” and “contingency premium” without checking whether two components charge the same underlying risk.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Funding-liquidity premium" b="Contingent liquidity charge">
**Funding-liquidity premium** — internal compensation for the marginal cost/value of term funding.
---
**Contingent liquidity charge** — allocation for potential stressed cash needs that may arise from currently unfunded or conditional exposures.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Curve construction is institution-specific; the economic components do not depend on jurisdictional naming.
</Rule>

<Recall
  q="Why use marginal rather than average historic funding cost?"
  a="Marginal cost better represents the economics of adding or retaining the next unit of balance-sheet exposure."

<Recall
