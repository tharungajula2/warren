### Core mechanism

Suppose total daily receipts = 100 and total daily payments = 100.

| Time | Cash flow | Cumulative position |
| :--- | ---: | ---: |
| 09:00 | −60 | −60 |
| 11:00 | −20 | −80 |
| 14:00 | +30 | −50 |
| 16:00 | +50 | 0 |
| 17:00 | +20 | +20 |

Daily net flow looks comfortable, yet the bank needs at least 80 of intraday resources before 14:00 in this simplified example.

### Flow

```text
Opening liquidity
    ↓
Time-stamped payments and receipts
    ↓
Cumulative intraday position
    ↓
Peak net outflow
    ↓
Intraday funding/collateral requirement
    ↓
End-of-day position
```

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Cash-timing failure · liquidity inventory · survival stress
This:       Within-day settlement liquidity
Downstream: Collateral/pre-positioning · CFP · operational escalation
```

### Formula

```text
Peak intraday liquidity need = absolute value of the most negative cumulative intraday cash position

Earlier outflows ↑ → peak need ↑.

Earlier incoming payments ↑ → peak need ↓.
```

### Permanent rule

Liquidity must exist at the payment deadline, not merely by the reporting cut-off.

### Never confuse [Intraday liquidity] and [End-of-day liquidity]

**Intraday liquidity** — resources available through the day to settle time-critical obligations.

---

**End-of-day liquidity** — closing cash after all daily flows; it can hide a severe earlier deficit.

### Permanent trap

Using LCR or daily net cash flow as a proxy for intraday capacity. Basel developed separate intraday monitoring tools because the timing problem is different.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta in mechanism. Payment-system structures, central-bank facilities and reporting requirements differ by jurisdiction.
