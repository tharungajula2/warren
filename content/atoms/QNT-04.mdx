### Core mechanism

CR owns the credit approval decision. QNT owns the inference problem produced when that decision determines whose outcomes become observable.

| Approach | Statistical idea | Main assumption or weakness |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Approved-only model | Fit only where outcomes exist | Estimates performance conditional on historical approval |
| Reweighting | Give approved cases weights representing similar rejected cases | Requires observable variables to explain selection sufficiently |
| Augmentation | Infer expected outcomes for rejects from a preliminary model | Can reinforce the preliminary model's errors |
| Parceling-style assignment | Allocate synthetic good/bad outcomes within score bands | Strong assumption about rejected performance inside each band |
| External outcome data | Link rejects to later bureau or market performance | Coverage and outcome definitions may differ |

```text
Applications
    ↓
Historical approval rule
    ↓
Approved → repayment outcome observed
Rejected → repayment outcome missing
    ↓
Inference requires assumptions about missing outcomes
```

The missingness is usually not random. Low-scoring applicants are rejected precisely because the lender believes their risk differs, so treating rejects as if they were a random subset of approved cases is structurally wrong.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Application population · Historical approval rule · Selection bias
This:       Missing rejected outcomes · Inference assumptions
Downstream: Score development · Population calibration · Policy change analysis
```

### Formula

```text
Weighted loss = sum over observed cases of weight x case loss
```

### Permanent rule

Reject inference is assumption management, not hidden-data recovery. State what makes the missing rejected outcomes inferable before trusting the result.

### Never confuse [Missing at random] and [Missing by credit policy]

**Missing at random** - outcome absence can be explained without dependence on the unseen outcome after conditioning on observed information.

---

**Missing by credit policy** - outcome absence is created by a decision rule designed around perceived risk, making naive missing-data assumptions fragile.

### Permanent trap

Calling synthetic bad/good assignments "observed performance". They are model-based imputations and should remain distinguishable from realized repayment outcomes.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta. The identification problem is mathematical; lending and fair-treatment constraints on how models are used sit outside this atom.
