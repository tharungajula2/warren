### Core mechanism

| Regulatory role | What it changes for lending |
| :--- | :--- |
| Prudential supervisor | Asset classification, provisioning, capital-related constraints, governance and supervisory expectations |
| Conduct regulator | Pricing disclosure, fair practices, digital-lending controls, outsourcing and recovery conduct |
| Developmental authority | Priority Sector Lending and inclusion-oriented credit architecture |
| Licensing/perimeter authority | Which banks and non-banks may undertake lending and under what entity-specific rulebook |

RBI's lending estate sits across the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934, the Banking Regulation Act, 1949, entity-specific Directions and subject-matter Directions. The same activity can therefore be governed by more than one instrument: a digital loan by an NBFC, for example, remains an NBFC exposure for prudential purposes while also falling under digital-lending conduct rules.

This pack treats monetary policy only as an external input where a lending rule explicitly references it. Repo transmission, liquidity operations and balance-sheet liquidity are not re-taught here.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Statutory mandate · Licensing perimeter
This:       RBI lending regulation

```

### Permanent rule

The regulated entity remains responsible for the lending activity even when origination, servicing, data collection or recovery is outsourced.

### Permanent trap

Treating an RBI circular as if it applied to every lender identically. Application usually depends on entity type, product and the specific Direction that governs that perimeter.

### Jurisdiction variants

#### Banks
Banking Regulation Act licensing and bank-specific Directions apply.

#### NBFCs and HFCs
RBI Act registration and entity-specific NBFC/HFC Directions apply, with additional layer-based deltas for NBFCs.
