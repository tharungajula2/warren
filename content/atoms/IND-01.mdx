The RBI Act (1934) empowers RBI as monetary authority and bank regulator, but also explicitly tasks it with supporting economic development. For example, RBI regularly revises the Priority Sector Lending norms to “ensure adequate flow of credit” to sectors crucial for socio-economic development. In practice, RBI issues prudential norms (capital, NPA rules, etc.) like any financial regulator, **and** enforces growth-oriented policies (such as mandatory loans to farmers, micro‐businesses, etc.). This dual mandate means RBI-issued guidelines often blend safety with directed targets. Unlike most central banks abroad, RBI’s charter includes credit-promotion – e.g. linking lending rates to an external benchmark to improve transmission – and priority targets for banks.  

The RBI’s remit thus spans monetary policy, foreign exchange, currency issuance, system oversight, and development.  It supervises commercial banks, NBFCs, payments systems and more. The net effect is that Indian banking regulators regularly set both stability rules (asset classification, capital, audit) **and** development quotas (priority sectors, subsidies).  Internationally, counterparts like the U.S. Fed focus on macro‐stability, but RBI also directly steers credit allocation.  For India’s banks, this means compliance is both about risk control and social objectives.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
The RBI Act grants RBI objectives beyond bank supervision – including currency and monetary control **and** socio-economic development (e.g. credit to priority sectors).
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Assuming RBI’s role is *only* prudential oversight.  In reality RBI imposes directed lending programs (priority sectors, subsidized refinancing, financial inclusion schemes) as part of its mandate.  Ignoring these developmental mandates leads to misreading RBI guidelines.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Monetary Authority" b="Developmental Regulator">
**Monetary Authority** — The role of setting interest rates and controlling money supply (e.g. policy repo rate).  
---  
**Developmental Regulator** — RBI’s role in promoting growth (e.g. enforcing priority‐sector lending to agriculture/MSMEs). They often overlap, but the former is macro management, the latter is directed credit policy.
</NeverConfuse>
