Basel III (Chapter 5) provides special capital formulas for CCPs. For example, a clearing member may assign a 2% risk weight to its trade exposure (after netting) to a well-regulated CCP (QCCP). For contributions to a CCP’s default fund, banks may use a haircut approach or even deduction if not performing obligations. The goal is to encourage central clearing by lowering capital. However, non-clearing member (client) exposures can be higher risk – often treated like a bank loan to the member, adjusted for collateral. 

Thus, CCP exposures are not risk-free: banks cannot fully ignore them. Nonetheless, the capital charges are far lower than for bilateral OTC. This incentivizes central clearing of standard products.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Identify CCPs that qualify as “QCCPs” (per supervisory list) to apply favorable capital treatment. Always ensure margin and default fund contributions are included in exposure measures.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Overlooking CCP contributions. For example, thinking collateral to the CCP doesn’t affect capital – regulators usually require including posted margin and default fund contributions in exposure calculations (albeit with reductions).
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Trade Exposure to CCP" b="Default Fund Exposure">
**Trade Exposure to CCP** — The current and future exposure to the clearing house (subject to 2% RW).  
---  
**Default Fund Exposure** — The risk from loss mutualization. Banks must separately calculate capital for this (often higher).
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Global Basel standards treat CCP exposures similarly. National regulators (e.g. SEC/CFTC or PRA/EBA) list recognized CCPs (QCCPs) for capital rules.
</Rule>
