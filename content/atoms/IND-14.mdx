A key mandate: **Direct disbursal and repayment.** Any credit extended via a lending app must be paid into the customer’s own bank/PSP account – no money passes through the fintech or any third party. Similarly, loan repayments (EMIs) must be paid directly to the bank/NBFC. This is to prevent misappropriation. RBI explicitly forbids any “pooling or sweep accounts” that were used by some apps to skim funds.  

**Guarantee arrangements:** Fintechs used to offer to cover the bank’s losses (FLDG) up to some amount to motivate banks to lend. RBI treats an FLDG (capping around 20% of loan principal or ₹5 crore, whichever lower) as a credit enhancement, not traditional securitisation. Such guarantees must be explicit and capped by contract. They differ from securitisation: in a normal securitisation, credit risk is split among investors and not guaranteed by originator. Under RBI’s regime, if a fintech guarantees 20% of a loan’s loss, that fintech is effectively retaining part of credit risk (so regulator counts only 80% transfer as securitization). These agreements must be disclosed to borrowers. RBI’s limit (20%/₹5cr) prevents full risk transfer in a grey-camouflage way. 

RBI’s guidance also affirms that a fintech providing FLDG cannot park consumer funds. If a fintech receives payment from a borrower (say for processing), it must immediately route it to the bank. Collecting money and redisbursing it later is not allowed. Essentially, the fintech’s role is only origination &amp; tech; money flows directly between borrower and bank.

**First Loss Guarantee vs. Securitisation:** RBI emphasises they are distinct. In a securitisation, the originator may sell a pool of loans (and risk) to investors; there is no party assuring losses beyond already sold tranches. In a FLDG, the fintech agrees to compensate the bank for first losses. Because the guarantee can distort borrowers’ incentives and hides the true risk, RBI caps and requires disclosure of FLDGs. A FLDG is treated prudentially – the lender must still provision if a guarantee is in place (it’s not risk-free).

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
No “pass-through” accounts in lending: funds must flow directly between borrower and lender. Any first-loss guarantee by a fintech must be contractually capped (max 20% of loan or ₹5 cr) and is **not** a pure securitisation.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Using agent accounts to disburse or collect loan money.  Some apps tried to appear as platforms but routed payments through their own accounts — RBI now explicitly forbids this. Also, fintechs believing they can guarantee entire losses – RBI caps such guarantees.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**FLDG (First-Loss Default Guarantee)** — a fintech’s commitment to cover an agreed fraction (max 20%) of losses on a loan. This is a credit support, not full securitisation.  
**Securitisation** — selling loan assets and credit risk to investors (no partial guarantee by originator).
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="IN">
— RBI’s treatment of fintech guarantees (FLDG) as limited and capping them is specific. Other countries allow fintech partnerships but often regulate them case-by-case (e.g. US banking regs). No identical cap is universal.
</Delta>
