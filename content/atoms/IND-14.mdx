### Core mechanism

```text
REs enter a co-lending agreement
    ↓
Borrower receives one coordinated credit arrangement
    ↓
Each RE funds and books its agreed share
    ↓
Each RE retains at least 10% of each loan
    ↓
Servicing and collections follow the agreed operating model
```

| Feature | Current rule architecture |
| :--- | :--- |
| Minimum retention | Each participating RE retains at least 10% of each individual loan |
| Effective date | Directions apply from 1 January 2026 |
| Loan ownership | Each RE owns and records its share |
| Customer terms | Agreement must make the co-lending structure and roles transparent |
| Risk | Credit loss follows each RE's exposure, subject to any separately permitted support arrangement |

This replaces the older shorthand that co-lending meant an 80:20 bank/NBFC structure. An 80:20 commercial split can still occur contractually, but it is not the current universal regulatory formula.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Entity eligibility · Joint underwriting
This:       Co-lending structure
Downstream: Booking · Servicing · CIC reporting · SMA/NPA synchronisation
```

### Permanent rule

Co-lending is not a disguised loan from one RE to another; each participating RE must hold a real share of the end-borrower exposure.

### Never confuse [Co-lending] and [Syndication]

**Co-lending** — a regulated joint-lending arrangement governed by RBI's co-lending Directions.

---

**Syndication** — a broader multi-lender financing structure that is not defined by the specific co-lending framework.

### Jurisdiction variants

#### Participating banks and NBFCs
Each applies its own prudential rules to its retained share while complying with the common co-lending framework.
