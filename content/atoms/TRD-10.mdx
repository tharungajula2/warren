Foreign exchange is quoted as one currency in terms of another. In a pair such as EUR/USD, the first currency is the base and the second the quote currency under the stated market convention. Buying one currency necessarily means selling the other.

A spot transaction exchanges agreed amounts on the spot value date for the market spot rate. “Spot” does not always mean same-day settlement; the standard value date depends on the currency pair and market convention. A forward fixes today the exchange rate for a future value date. No principal necessarily changes hands at trade date; at maturity the parties deliver the two currencies unless the contract is non-deliverable.

An FX swap is two linked exchanges in opposite directions on different dates. A typical transaction exchanges currencies on the near leg and reverses the exchange on the far leg at a pre-agreed forward rate. Economically, this creates secured-like funding in one currency against another without leaving a net open FX position if the amounts and dates are matched.

The spot rate and forward rate should not be interpreted as a simple market forecast. Forward points mainly reflect relative interest-rate conditions, while balance-sheet costs, hedging demand and cross-currency basis can also matter.

Operational errors often arise from direction. “Buy USD/INR” may be interpreted differently if a system stores the base currency, pay currency or dealer perspective. Every trade record should therefore identify bought amount, sold amount, currencies, exchange rate, near/far value dates and settlement instructions explicitly.

Settlement itself can create principal risk when one currency is paid before the other is received. Payment-versus-payment arrangements are designed to reduce this risk.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Never infer trade direction from a rate field alone; record which currency is bought, which is sold and on which date.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Treating the forward rate as the market’s pure forecast of the future spot rate.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="FX forward" b="FX swap">
**FX forward** — single future exchange of two currencies at a rate fixed today.

---

**FX swap** — near exchange plus an offsetting far exchange of the same two currencies.
</NeverConfuse>

  a="A forward has one future exchange; a swap has linked near and far exchanges in opposite directions."
