### Core mechanism

Default
    ↓
Original trigger ceases
    ↓
Required arrears / viability conditions met
    ↓
Probation or observation requirement, where applicable
    ↓
Cured / non-default
    ↓
New qualifying trigger?
    ↓
Re-default

A sound data model keeps the entire state history: first default date, cause, restructuring events, cure date, probation, re-default and recovery. Deleting default history after cure produces survivorship bias and makes weak restructurings look successful.

### Flow

```text
Default
    ↓
Original trigger ceases → Arrears / viability conditions met
    ↓
Probation / observation where required
    ↓
Cured / non-default
    ↓
New qualifying trigger → Re-default
```

### Flow

```text
Upstream:   Default state · Payments · Financial recovery · Restructuring performance
This:       Test exit conditions → probation where required → cure
Downstream: Migration · Re-default · PD calibration · Recovery analysis · Monitoring
```

### Permanent rule

Cure removes the current default state; it does not remove the historical fact that default occurred.

### Never confuse [Cure] and [Arrears clearance]

**Cure** — return to non-default under the complete exit criteria.

---
**Arrears clearance** — payment of overdue amounts; UTP or other default conditions may still remain.

### Permanent trap

Resetting a cured customer to “never defaulted” inflates apparent portfolio quality and corrupts PD, transition and restructuring-performance analysis.

### Jurisdiction variants

#### IN (RBI)
Under the current commercial-bank IRACP Directions, an NPA may be upgraded to standard only when the **entire arrears of interest and principal are paid**; where the borrower has more than one facility with the bank, arrears across all those credit facilities must be repaid, subject to additional restructuring rules where relevant.

#### UK (PRA)
Return-to-non-default is governed under the current definition-of-default framework. The updated PRA SS3/24 sets the future Basel 3.1 expectations from 1 January 2027.

#### EU
EBA Guidelines govern return-to-non-default and probation concepts. The May 2026 amended version has an application date of 19 October 2026, so the applicable version must be identified by observation date.

#### US
Advanced Approaches rules provide return-from-default standards for wholesale obligors and retail exposures under the US framework; the relevant unit must be preserved.

  q="Is zero DPD sufficient for cure?"
  a="Not necessarily. Other default conditions and any required exit/probation criteria must also be satisfied."

  a="It reveals whether cures are durable and materially affects calibration and workout assessment."
