A **probability of default (PD)** is a probability attached to a future default event. Four elements must accompany the number.

First is the **unit**: an obligor, facility or homogeneous retail pool. A 2% obligor PD and a 2% facility-level default probability need not describe the same event.

Second is the **default definition**. A PD calibrated to regulatory default is not automatically interchangeable with a probability of 90 DPD, bankruptcy, charge-off or accounting impairment.

Third is the **horizon**. Basel IRB PDs are fundamentally one-year measures, with regulatory estimates required to reflect long-run average one-year default experience subject to framework requirements. A lifetime default probability answers a different question from a 12-month PD.

Fourth is the **information date** or conditioning set. Two identical borrowers observed in different macroeconomic environments may receive different point-in-time probabilities if the model conditions strongly on current conditions.

PD is not a prediction that a particular borrower "will default". If 1,000 genuinely comparable exposures each have a well-calibrated 1% one-year PD, the interpretation is roughly that repeated comparable populations would default at a rate consistent with that probability, not that ten predetermined borrowers have already been identified.

A rating grade can therefore contain many obligors with a common calibrated grade PD even though individual circumstances differ. Conversely, a credit score may rank borrowers well without its raw score being a probability. Calibration is the mapping from score or grade to an observed or estimated default frequency.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
A PD is incomplete unless the event, unit, horizon and conditioning information are known.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Comparing two PDs solely because both are labelled "one-year PD" can be invalid when one uses regulatory default and the other uses a delinquency or accounting-loss target.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**PD** — a calibrated probability of a specified future default event.

**Credit score** — a numerical ranking or predictive output that need not itself be a probability.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Probability mathematics does not change by jurisdiction; regulatory frameworks can prescribe different default definitions, floors, horizons or calibration requirements.
</Rule>
