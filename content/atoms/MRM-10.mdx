Models are dynamic; their performance can drift over time as data or conditions change.  Banks therefore establish an **ongoing monitoring program**.  This typically involves: 
- **Performance tracking:** comparing model predictions to actual results (backtesting). For example, periodically compare predicted default rates to realized defaults.  
- **Data drift checks:** computing stability metrics (PSI/CSI) on input variables or score distributions (see MRM-16).  Significant shifts in data distributions may signal model drift.  
- **Thresholds:** setting quantitative thresholds (e.g. maximum allowable PSI) that, if exceeded, trigger investigation or revalidation.  Common practice is PSI&lt;0.10 (little change), 0.10–0.25 moderate, &gt;0.25 significant.  
- **Periodic reviews:** formal re-validation cycles or ad-hoc reviews when performance degrades.  Regulators discourage fixed cadences; instead, use a risk-based approach to determine review frequency.  

Regulators support strong monitoring.  PRA SS1/23 Principle 5 expects firms to have “model risk mitigants” including performance monitoring and expert overrides.  The PRA details standards for monitoring model performance (criteria, thresholds, back-testing).  RBI’s draft also requires “ongoing performance testing using backward-looking and forward-looking approaches”.  In the US, SR 26-2 lists monitoring as a governance activity (OCC noted monitoring and outcome analysis as sound practice in vendor models).

Senior management should review model performance at least annually or more often for top-tier models.  Reports summarizing key metrics and any breaches of thresholds should be provided to risk committees.  Any persistent performance shortfall must be remediated (perhaps via recalibration or new model).

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Always monitor a model in production.  A model’s validity is not one-time; it requires continuous checking.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Assuming a validated model will stay valid forever.  For example, **ignoring a rising PSI that indicates data shift**, which could make the model obsolete.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Monitoring" b="Validation">
**Monitoring** — ongoing checks of model output/data (e.g. backtests, stability indices).  
---  
**Validation** — formal, periodic re-assessment of the whole model.  
They are related but different stages.  Monitoring is continuous; validation is periodic/developer review (see MRM-08).
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="IN">
— RBI’s draft requires continuous oversight.  It even calls for backward/forward performance testing and benchmarking in monitoring. Excessive model risk must be reported to the Board.
</Delta>

<Delta j="UK">
— The PRA expects firms to monitor model performance and use expert judgment or overrides as needed (SS1/23 Principle 5).  Monitoring standards should include criteria and thresholds.
</Delta>

<Delta j="US">
— Fed/OCC guidance includes “ongoing monitoring and outcome analysis” as key practices (especially for vendor models).  The revised guidance emphasizes risk-based monitoring but is principles-based.
</Delta>

<Delta j="EU">
— ECB supervisors require regular monitoring of internal models and use of backtesting (as in TRIM/ICAAP reviews).  EBA Pillar 2 practices include ongoing performance tracking.
</Delta>
