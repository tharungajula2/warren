The first step is **exposure classification**. A banking-book exposure is assigned to the regulatory class that matches its counterparty and characteristics: for example sovereign, bank, corporate, retail or real-estate categories under the applicable framework. Classification matters because each class has its own rules.

Next comes **risk-weight determination**. Depending on the exposure class and national implementation, the weight may depend on a permitted external credit assessment, loan-to-value measure, counterparty characteristics, repayment dependence or a prescribed fixed weight. Basel’s revised standardised approach does not simply map every loan to an external rating. Some classes use ratings, some use other risk drivers, and due-diligence requirements prevent blind mechanistic reliance where applicable.

The **exposure amount** is then determined after relevant conversion and credit-risk-mitigation rules. Multiplying the adjusted exposure by its applicable weight produces credit RWA.

The Basel finalisation strengthened the standardised approach partly because it also serves as the comparison basis for the output floor. A weak standardised approach would make that backstop ineffective.

### Permanent rule

Standardised does not mean uniform: the regulator standardises the methodology while allowing prescribed risk differentiation within it.

### Permanent trap

Assuming every externally unrated exposure automatically receives the same risk weight ignores exposure-class-specific rules and non-rating risk drivers.

### Never confuse

**Standardised approach** — supervisor-prescribed methodology for exposure classification and risk weighting.

---
**External credit rating** — one possible input to specified standardised risk weights; it is not the standardised approach itself.

### Jurisdiction variants

**IN** — RBI’s final 2026 Directions require the Standardised Approach for covered commercial-bank banking-book credit risk from **1 April 2027**; RWAs are exposure amount multiplied by prescribed standardised weights, using eligible ratings where the Directions specify. [RBI/DOR/2026-27/397, DOR.CRE.REC.5/21.06.201/2026-27]
**UK** — PRA Basel 3.1 final rules revise the credit-risk standardised approach with effect from **1 January 2027**. [PRA PS1/26]
**EU** — CRR3’s revised credit-risk framework has applied from **1 January 2025**, subject to CRR3 transitional provisions. [Regulation (EU) 2024/1623; European Commission banking-package Q&amp;A]
**US** — The **19 March 2026** package proposes revisions to risk-based capital and standardised risk measurement; as at 27 August 2026 it remains a proposal, not a final rule. [Federal Reserve joint proposal; June 2026 Supervision and Regulation Report]
