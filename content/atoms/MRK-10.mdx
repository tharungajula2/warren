To capture potential losses under adverse conditions, banks must compute a *Stressed VaR* (Basel 2.5) or *Stressed ES* (FRTB) using a past period of high volatility.  Each bank selects a recent 250-business-day window of significant market stress that is relevant to its portfolio.  For example, a U.S. dealer might choose the 2008 crisis or a 2011 euro debt stress.  The chosen period’s data are fed into the VaR or ES model instead of a normal period.

Under Basel 2.5, capital was 10-day VaR (99%) plus 10-day Stressed VaR (99%) from a stress window.  FRTB instead uses 10-day ES at 97.5% on the stress window for capital.  The rules ensure banks hold capital that reflects losses in a realistic stress scenario.

### Permanent rule

Always use a continuous one-year period of actual stress for calibrating Stressed VaR/ES.

### Permanent trap

Selecting a stress period that is not relevant to current portfolio risks, or forgetting to update the stress window periodically.

### Never confuse

**Historical VaR** — VaR from recent normal market data; **Stressed VaR** — VaR from a past stress period.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** The concept of calibrating to a stressed period is applied globally in advanced regimes.
