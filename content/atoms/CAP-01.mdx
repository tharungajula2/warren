### Core mechanism

```text
Credit exposures
    ↓
Distribution of possible losses
    ↓
Expected loss
    ↓
Pricing · earnings · allowances
    ↓
Losses beyond expectation
    ↓
Capital absorbs loss
    ↓
Depositors and senior creditors remain protected
```

| Object | Prudential job |
| :--- | :--- |
| Expected loss | Anticipated cost of taking risk |
| Allowance | Recognised reduction in an asset's carrying amount |
| Earnings | First recurring source for absorbing costs and rebuilding capital |
| Regulatory capital | Loss-bearing resource protecting solvency against adverse outcomes |

Basel's IRB architecture makes the split visible: its risk-weight functions are calibrated to unexpected loss, while regulatory expected loss is compared separately with eligible provisions. The split is conceptual, not a claim that accounting allowance must equal regulatory expected loss.

### Permanent rule

Capital is a solvency resource against loss. It is not a substitute for recognising expected credit losses promptly.

### Never confuse [Allowance] and [Capital]

**Allowance** — a measured reduction in the carrying amount of an asset for recognised credit loss.

---

**Capital** — qualifying loss-absorbing resource available to bear losses and preserve solvency.

### Permanent trap

Calling an allowance "capital set aside for bad loans" collapses two different objects. The allowance changes the asset and earnings; capital is the residual loss-bearing claim after those accounting effects.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta. The loss-distribution logic is common; accounting and prudential implementation determine how the two objects interact.

<Recall
  q="What loss does regulatory capital principally protect against?"
  a="Adverse loss beyond the amount already expected, priced or recognised, so the bank can remain solvent."
