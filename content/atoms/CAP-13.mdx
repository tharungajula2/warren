The **internal ratings-based approach**, or IRB, starts from borrower and facility risk estimates rather than a table of standardised risk weights. Basel then feeds those estimates into prescribed regulatory functions. The bank therefore models inputs; it does not freely choose the capital formula.

For many corporate-type exposures under **Foundation IRB (F-IRB)**, an approved bank estimates probability of default while supervisory values govern specified other parameters such as loss given default and exposure at default, subject to the detailed Basel rules. Under **Advanced IRB (A-IRB)**, banks may estimate additional parameters where that approach remains permitted for the relevant exposure class.

Permission is central. IRB is not an accounting method that a bank may elect simply because its internal models are sophisticated. Basel establishes minimum requirements covering rating systems, data, governance, validation and the use of internal ratings in risk management. Supervisors can restrict model scope, impose conditions or withdraw approval.

Basel finalisation also narrows the areas in which advanced modelling may be used and applies input floors and an output floor. Those constraints address excessive variability and model risk.

**Perishable — India’s domestic position changes under final Directions effective 1 April 2027; UK Basel 3.1 restrictions take effect 1 January 2027.** India must not be taught as an IRB domestic regime after that date.

### Permanent rule

IRB means supervisory permission to estimate specified inputs inside a prescribed regulatory framework, not permission to set capital internally.

### Permanent trap

Calling an internal credit scorecard an “IRB model” because it estimates PD ignores the regulatory approval, use-test, data and governance requirements.

### Never confuse

**F-IRB** — IRB variant in which the bank estimates fewer regulatory risk components and supervisors prescribe more inputs.

---

**A-IRB** — IRB variant permitting bank estimates for more risk components where the framework allows and the supervisor approves them.

### Jurisdiction variants

**IN** — RBI has chosen the **Standardised Approach**, effective 1 April 2027, for covered commercial-bank banking-book credit risk; IRB should therefore be treated as international knowledge, not the Indian domestic credit-capital regime. [RBI 2026 SA Directions]

**UK** — Basel 3.1 retains IRB subject to PRA permission and revised modelling-scope restrictions from 1 January 2027. [PRA PS1/26]

**EU** — CRR3 continues a permission-based IRB framework with Basel-finalisation restrictions and the output floor. [Regulation (EU) 2024/1623 / CRR3]

**US** — The March 2026 package proposes a materially revised capital architecture for the largest banks; because it remains proposed, its final treatment of internally modelled credit-risk capital must not be presented as settled law. [Federal Reserve, 19 March 2026]
