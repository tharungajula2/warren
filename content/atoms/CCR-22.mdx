Herstatt risk famously arose when a German bank failed mid-day, causing FX payments to be one-sided. To mitigate settlement risk in FX, most participants now use payment-versus-payment (PvP) systems like CLS, which ensure the final transfer of one currency occurs only if the other currency’s payment is finalized. PvP is mandated or recommended for major FX pairs. Settlement risk can also occur in securities (delivery vs payment), but standard solutions exist (DVP settlement at exchanges or clearinghouses).

In short, pre-settlement (counterparty) risk is before trade completion; settlement risk is the risk of final exchange of value. Both affect CCR practice, but capital for CCR typically concerns pre-settlement exposures.

### Permanent rule

Implement PvP (e.g. through CLS) for FX trades to eliminate Herstatt risk when possible. Use delivery-versus-payment (DVP) for securities.

### Permanent trap

Equating CCR capital with settlement risk. Regulators require CCR capital for trades that are bilateral; settlement risk is operational/liquidity risk, not included in CCR capital.

### Never confuse

**Pre-settlement risk (CCR)** — Counterparty default risk before and up to settlement of cash flows.
---
**Settlement risk (Herstatt)** — Risk of loss due to one party failing to make payment at settlement time (e.g. FX PvP risk).

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Settlement risk management is driven by market infrastructure (CLS, clearinghouses). Basel mandates PvP where available. Regulatory capital for settlement risk is not separate; it is managed via operational standards.
