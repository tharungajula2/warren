A call option gives its holder the right to buy the underlying at the strike price; a put gives the right to sell. The holder pays a premium for that right. The writer receives the premium and assumes the contingent obligation if the option is exercised under its terms.

At expiry, a plain call’s intrinsic value is max(S − K, 0), while a plain put’s is max(K − S, 0), where S is the underlying price and K the strike. Before expiry, the option can be worth more than current intrinsic value because there is still time for favourable market moves. That additional component is time value.

Moneyness is a relative description. A call is in the money when the underlying is above the strike and a put is in the money when the underlying is below the strike. “At the money” is used when spot or the relevant forward is close to strike, depending on market convention. In-the-money does not mean profitable overall: a call can have positive intrinsic value but still fail to recover the premium originally paid.

Time value generally increases with remaining time and expected volatility, all else equal, because more possible future paths can create favourable exercise outcomes. It normally decays toward zero as expiry approaches. Interest rates, dividends, carry and settlement style also affect option value.

American-style options can generally be exercised before expiry; European-style options only at expiry. The labels describe exercise style, not geography.

A complete option definition must include call/put, buyer/seller, underlying, strike, expiry, notional, style, settlement, premium and any barrier or other feature. Without these, “long option” is not a complete economic position.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
In-the-money describes exercise value relative to strike; it does not tell you the trade’s total profit after premium.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Assuming an option with zero intrinsic value before expiry is worthless. It may still have substantial time value.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Moneyness" b="Profitability">
**Moneyness** — relationship between underlying price and strike under the relevant option convention.

---

**Profitability** — economic P&amp;L after premium and all relevant cash flows.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.**
</Rule>

<Recall
  q="What happens to a plain option’s time value at expiry?"
  a="It goes to zero; only intrinsic value can remain."
/>

<Recall
  q="Can an in-the-money option trade be loss-making for its buyer?"
  a="Yes. Its intrinsic value may be smaller than the premium paid."
/>
