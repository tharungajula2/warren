### Core mechanism

Sign-off has four dimensions.

| Dimension | Required question |
| :--- | :--- |
| Authority | Is this person authorised to approve this requirement or baseline? |
| Object | Exactly which version, requirements and assumptions are being approved? |
| Meaning | Is the approver confirming business correctness, control adequacy, funding, design, or something else? |
| Evidence | What durable record proves approval and its conditions? |

A single "approved" status can conceal different commitments. A business owner may approve requirement meaning; a risk function may provide challenge or control approval; technology may confirm feasibility. Do not collapse those into one generic sign-off.

Conditional approval must name the condition. "Approved subject to reconciliation evidence before go-live" is a real state; "approved with comments" is not useful unless the comments and ownership are explicit.

After sign-off, clarification can still occur without reopening the baseline if it does not change meaning. A change to obligation, boundary, acceptance condition or constraint is a scope or requirement change and belongs in change control.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Requirements · Decisions · Assumptions · Authority map
This:       Baseline approval · Approval evidence · Conditions

```

### Ownership

| Role | Responsibility |
| :--- | :--- |
| Produces | BA prepares the baseline, approval statement and evidence trail |
| Approves | Named requirement or control owners approve within their mandates |
| Consumes | Delivery and governance rely on the approved baseline |
| Independently challenges | Independent functions challenge whether required owners or conditions are missing |

### Permanent rule

Sign-off commits an authorised person to a specific object and meaning. An email reaction, meeting attendance or silence is not equivalent unless the agreed governance explicitly makes it so.

### Never confuse [Sign-off] and [Acceptance]

**Sign-off** - approves a requirement, scope, design input or other baseline before or during delivery.

---

**Acceptance** - decides that the delivered change is suitable for business use against agreed evidence and disclosed limitations.

### Permanent trap

Requesting approval with no statement of what approval means. Later disputes are often not about whether someone clicked "approve", but about whether they thought they were approving completeness, wording, feasibility or production readiness.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta. A specific supervisory or internal governance process may impose particular evidence or approval requirements, but the discipline remains the same.
