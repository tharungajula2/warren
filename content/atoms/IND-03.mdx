### Core mechanism

| Dimension | Bank | NBFC |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Core legal status | Banking company or other bank category | Company undertaking regulated non-bank financial business |
| Demand deposits | Banking power | Generally unavailable to NBFCs |
| Payment-system role | Direct banking role, subject to applicable access rules | Not a substitute for a bank account/payment-system participant |
| Funding | Customer deposits plus wholesale/market funding | Primarily borrowings, market funding and permitted deposits where authorised |
| Prudential estate | Bank-specific capital, IRACP and conduct directions | NBFC-specific prudential directions, modified by activity and SBR layer |
| Deposit protection | Bank deposits may fall within the statutory deposit-insurance framework | NBFC liabilities are not bank deposits merely because the entity lends |

The practical consequence is that a loan with identical contractual cash flows can produce different regulatory obligations depending on whether it sits in a bank or NBFC. That affects governance, provisioning, reporting and sometimes customer-facing rules.

One-line reminder: RWA and regulatory capital mechanics belong to CAP; this atom only identifies which Indian rulebook owns the exposure.

### Permanent rule

A non-bank lender does not become a bank because it performs bank-like credit underwriting or uses a bank-like digital interface.

### Permanent trap

Applying a commercial-bank rule to an NBFC simply because both have the same borrower and product. The legal form of the lender determines the prudential starting point.

### Jurisdiction variants

#### Scheduled commercial banks
Full bank prudential estate applies.

#### NBFCs
Scale, activity and deposit-taking status determine additional prudential deltas.
