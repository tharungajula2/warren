Under RBI’s **Scale-Based Regulations (SBR)**, NBFCs are grouped by size and activity. *Base Layer*: mainly non-deposit NBFCs with assets below ₹1,000 crore, plus all NBFC-P2P, NBFC-AA, NOFHCs, etc.. These are subject to simpler rules. *Middle Layer*: all deposit-taking NBFCs and non-deposit NBFCs with assets ≥ ₹1,000 cr; also includes dedicated entities like SPDs, IDF-NBFC, CICs, HFCs, NBFC-IFCs. They face intermediate norms. *Upper Layer*: systemically important NBFCs identified yearly (no fixed list), facing the strictest capital and governance requirements. *Top Layer* is a potential category for any NBFC whose risk (possibly identified from the Upper layer) becomes a serious systemic concern; it is empty at inception. In essence, this regime applies graduated oversight: the smaller/lower-risk NBFCs in the Base Layer follow lighter prudential norms, while large NBFCs or critical ones are tightly regulated.

The SBR came into effect October 2022 (with legal backing under various Acts). It replaced the older NBFC-ND-SI (systemic) vs NBFC-ND (non-systemic) classification. Now, for example, an HFC or CIC automatically goes into the Middle or Upper Layer (not Base).  A group consolidation rule (from Oct 2022) ensures related NBFCs’ assets are summed to decide their layer. This means RBI can tailor capital, provisioning, group exposure rules by layer.

```
   +--------------------------------------+
   |   NBFC Scale-Based Regulation (SBR)  |
   +--------------------------------------+
              |
   +----------+----------+----------+
   |                     |          |
   v                     v          v
Base Layer        Middle Layer    Upper/Top
 (NBFCs-BL)      (NBFCs-ML/UL)    (NBFCs-UL/TL)
 - ND NBFCs       - Deposit         - Systemically
   &lt; ₹1000cr        NBFCs            important NBFCs
 - P2P, AA, NOFHC  - ND ≥₹1000cr     (identified by RBI)
 - Type-I NBFC     - HFC, CIC, IFC, SPD, IDF
```

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
All NBFCs in India must register with RBI (with minimum capital), but RBI now groups them by scale: smaller NBFCs (Base Layer) have lighter rules, while larger or deposit-taking NBFCs (Middle/Upper Layer) have stricter norms.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Ignoring layer classification: applying the same rules to all NBFCs.  For example, assuming an NBFC with ₹500 cr assets is treated like a ₹5,000 cr company is wrong – RBI’s SBR buckets mean they face different requirements.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="NBFC–Base Layer (NBFC-BL)" b="NBFC–Middle/Upper Layer (NBFC-ML/UL)">
**NBFC–Base Layer (NBFC-BL)** — NBFCs with asset size &lt; ₹1,000 crore (and fintech NBFCs like P2P, AA, etc.); subject to least stringent norms.
---
**NBFC–Middle/Upper Layer (NBFC-ML/UL)** — larger or deposit-taking NBFCs (e.g. NBFC-D, HFC, CIC, etc.) with ₹1,000cr+; subject to higher capital and prudential standards.
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="IN">
— RBI’s SBR framework is India-specific. It integrates HFCs and others into NBFC layer definitions.
</Delta>

**No delta.** (No exact counterpart in UK/EU/US; other countries classify banks differently but India’s four-layer NBFC regime is unique.)
