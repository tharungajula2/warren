Any model that produces trade valuations (e.g. Black-Scholes for options, simulation for exotic rates) is subject to model risk.  That risk arises from potential errors in model structure, parameters, or missing risk factors.  For example, a swap valuation using a one-factor curve model may misprice when rates move non-parallel (model risk).  Banks mitigate this through model validation teams, sensitivity analyses, and including AVAs for model risk (see MRK-19).  

Regulators expect firms to track model performance: for instance, comparing model values to actual trade P&amp;L movements or to prices from alternative models/vendors.  Persistent model deficiencies should be captured in governance (e.g. increased capital buffers or additional AVA).  In summary, valuation model risk is a subset of overall model risk management (MRM), applying the same principles of validation, documentation and controls.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Treat valuation models under the bank’s MRM governance: validate them periodically, and adjust capital or AVAs for significant model errors.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Assuming a model is “correct” because it is industry-standard; ignoring its limitations in a stressed scenario.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Model risk** — errors from model inaccuracies; **Market risk** — losses from actual market moves. Valuation models can create market risk if mispriced.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Model risk management requirements are consistent across jurisdictions (BCBS Principles for effective model risk management).
</Rule>
