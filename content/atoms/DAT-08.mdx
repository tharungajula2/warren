### Core mechanism

```text
Identify business or reporting use
    ↓
Assess materiality of wrong, missing or late data
    ↓
Designate CDE where impact is material
    ↓
Assign owner and steward
    ↓
Document definition, source and lineage
    ↓
Apply quality rules, monitoring and change controls
```

| CDE test | Question |
| :--- | :--- |
| Regulatory dependency | Does the element directly determine a required reported value or classification? |
| Risk-management dependency | Could an error distort exposure, concentration, limit or risk decision? |
| Aggregation dependency | Is it a key identifier, amount, date or classification needed to consolidate correctly? |
| Propagation | Would one defect contaminate many reports or downstream calculations? |
| Recovery difficulty | Would late discovery make correction costly or impossible before a decision or filing? |

A CDE is normally a governed field or business concept, not an entire database. "Counterparty identifier" may be critical because it drives legal-entity aggregation; every technical audit column in the same table need not become a CDE.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Business materiality · Golden sources · Reporting obligations
This:       CDE designation and control intensity
Downstream: Ownership · Lineage · Data quality · Change impact assessment
```

### Ownership

| Role | CDE responsibility |
| :--- | :--- |
| Owner | Approves meaning, scope and required quality |
| Steward | Maintains metadata, controls and issue follow-up |
| Producer | Supplies the element through the governed process |
| Consumer | Confirms fitness for the material use |
| Challenge | Tests whether designation and controls are credible |

### Permanent rule

Criticality follows impact, not technical convenience or the number of reports containing the field.

### Permanent trap

Declaring every field critical. If everything receives the same control intensity, criticality no longer helps prioritise scarce remediation and monitoring capacity.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Regulators may use different terminology, but materiality-based scoping is not a country-specific mechanic.

<Recall
  q="Why is a counterparty identifier often a CDE?"
  a="It can determine whether exposures are aggregated to the correct obligor or group; one identifier defect can propagate into concentrations, limits and reports."
/>

<Recall
  q="Does CDE designation mean every non-CDE is uncontrolled?"
  a="No. All data needs appropriate control; CDE status signals stronger governance because the consequence of failure is material."
/>
