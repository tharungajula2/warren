### Core mechanism

Generic stack:

```text
Management headroom
        ↓
Systemic / institution-specific buffers where applicable
        ↓
Countercyclical buffer where applicable
        ↓
Capital conservation buffer
        ↓
Applicable Pillar 2 capital requirement
        ↓
Pillar 1 minimum
```

| Buffer | Risk addressed | Typical character |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Capital conservation buffer | General ability to absorb losses above minima | Standing |
| Countercyclical buffer | System-wide cyclical build-up of credit risk | Time-varying |
| G-SIB / D-SIB or systemic buffer | Externality from systemic importance | Institution/system-specific |

The exact legal stacking order can differ. What does not change is that a buffer is intended to be entered in stress before the bank exhausts minimum capital. That usability is why distribution constraints intensify as buffers are consumed.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Pillar 1 · Pillar 2
This:       CET1 buffers above requirements
Downstream: Distribution constraints · management headroom · stress use
```

### Permanent rule

A buffer is useful precisely because the bank can enter it before breaching minimum capital, with consequences designed to conserve resources.

### Never confuse [Pillar 2 requirement] and [Buffer]

**Pillar 2 requirement** — capital required because Pillar 1 does not adequately capture a bank-specific risk or weakness.

---

**Buffer** — CET1 cushion maintained above requirements for conservation, cyclical or systemic purposes.

### Permanent trap

Calling every amount above Pillar 1 a "buffer" hides the distinction between required institution-specific capital and deliberately usable cushion.

### Jurisdiction variants

#### IN
**As at August 2026:** RBI applies a 2.5% CET1 capital conservation buffer above domestic minimum ratios, plus applicable countercyclical and systemic requirements.

#### UK / EU
**As at August 2026:** combined-buffer structures and firm-specific Pillar 2 arrangements follow local rules. The UK SDDT regime is final but has a future commencement of **1 January 2027** for eligible firms.

#### US
**As at August 2026:** large-bank capital uses the existing stress capital buffer architecture rather than reproducing the EU combined-buffer stack.

<Recall
  q="Why does a buffer sit above Pillar 1 and Pillar 2 requirements?"
  a="It provides usable CET1 that can absorb stress before hard capital requirements are breached."
/>

<Recall
  q="What makes the countercyclical buffer different from the conservation buffer?"
  a="It changes with system-wide cyclical risk rather than remaining a standing fixed cushion."
