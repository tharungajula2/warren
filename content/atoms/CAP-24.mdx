An **internal stress test** is designed and governed by the bank. It should reflect the institution’s particular concentrations, products, funding structure and strategic risks. Its value lies in decision-making: capital planning, risk limits, contingency actions and identification of vulnerabilities that broad industry scenarios may miss.

A **supervisory stress test** is conducted or specified by a prudential authority. Basel distinguishes banks’ own internal stress testing, bank-run supervisory tests and supervisor-run tests. Common scenarios or methodologies can improve comparability across institutions and allow supervisors to evaluate system-wide vulnerabilities.

The distinction is not simply “private versus published”. A bank may execute calculations itself using a supervisory scenario, while the authority determines methodology or evaluates results. Conversely, an internal exercise can use public macroeconomic variables but remain a management-owned stress test.

Consequences can also differ. An internal test may lead management to change limits or capital targets. A supervisory exercise may inform formal capital requirements, buffers, supervisory expectations or remediation depending on the jurisdiction.

The United States provides a clear example: the Federal Reserve’s supervisory stress test feeds the stress capital buffer architecture for large banks. In February 2026 the Board maintained then-current stress capital buffer requirements until 2027 while model feedback is considered. That is a jurisdiction-specific capital consequence, not a universal feature of all internal tests.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
A bank cannot outsource understanding of its vulnerabilities to the supervisory stress test; internal stress testing must remain tailored to its own risk profile.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Treating the supervisory scenario as the bank’s only severe scenario can leave material institution-specific concentrations unstressed.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Internal stress test" b="Supervisory stress test">
**Internal stress test** — management-owned assessment designed for the institution’s risks and decisions.

---
**Supervisory stress test** — authority-run or authority-specified assessment used for prudential oversight and, in some regimes, capital calibration.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Supervisory consequences differ, but Basel expressly recognises internal, bank-run supervisory and supervisor-run stress tests as distinct uses of the same stress-testing discipline.
</Rule>
