### Core mechanism

| Prudential input | Effect on required provision |
| :--- | :--- |
| Standard versus NPA status | NPA generally attracts higher minimum provisioning |
| Sub-standard/doubtful/loss class | More severe or seasoned classes attract stronger provisioning |
| Secured versus unsecured portion | Realisable security can affect the applicable minimum |
| Time spent in doubtful status | Seasoning can increase the required provision |
| Entity type | Banks, NBFCs, HFCs and cooperative banks can sit on different schedules |

The applicable provisioning percentages sit in the current entity-wise RBI Directions. The exact post-consolidation reference and full percentage schedule for every entity are not established here as at 31 August 2026. They remain UNVERIFIED rather than being copied from the legacy pack.

That means this atom teaches the invariant mechanism without inventing a number: classification, security and seasoning determine the prudential floor. A lender may hold a higher provision under internal policy or accounting rules.

One-line reminder: ECL measurement and staging belong to IMP.

### Permanent rule

RBI prudential provisions are minimum regulatory requirements; a lower accounting estimate does not automatically eliminate the prudential floor.

### Never confuse [Prudential provision] and [ECL allowance]

**Prudential provision** — RBI-prescribed minimum buffer linked to the prudential classification framework.

---

**ECL allowance** — accounting impairment measured under Ind AS 109 for entities to which it applies.
