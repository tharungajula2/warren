IFRS 9 defines a financial asset as **credit-impaired** when one or more events have occurred that have a detrimental impact on estimated future cash flows. Evidence can include significant financial difficulty of the borrower, breach of contract such as default or delinquency, a concession granted for financial difficulty, probable bankruptcy or disappearance of an active market because of financial difficulties.

Stage 3 therefore represents more than elevated probability of default. Stage 2 says risk has increased significantly but the asset is not yet credit-impaired; Stage 3 says impairment evidence has crystallised. IFRS 9 also contains a rebuttable operational presumption that default does not occur later than 90 days past due unless reasonable and supportable information justifies a more lagging criterion. The accounting definition still cannot be reduced to one DPD threshold.

Both Stage 2 and Stage 3 carry lifetime ECL, but Stage 3 has an additional interest consequence. For a financial asset that was not credit-impaired when originated but later becomes credit-impaired, IFRS 9 calculates interest revenue by applying the original EIR to **amortised cost**, meaning the gross carrying amount net of the loss allowance, rather than to the gross carrying amount used for non-credit-impaired assets.

That distinction prevents recognition of interest income on amounts the accounting measurement already expects not to collect. It is separate from the question of whether contractual interest remains legally due.

### Permanent rule

Credit impairment concerns detrimental effects on expected cash flows, not merely a high risk score.

### Permanent trap

Equating Stage 3 mechanically with “90 DPD” misses borrowers that become credit-impaired before 90 days and misunderstands the default backstop.

### Never confuse

**SICR** — material deterioration in default risk since initial recognition.

---

**Credit-impaired** — evidence exists that events have detrimentally affected estimated future cash flows.

### Jurisdiction variants

**IN** — Current commercial-bank IRACP uses NPA, substandard, doubtful and loss-asset classifications rather than IFRS Stage 3; common NPA mechanics include 90-day overdue/out-of-order criteria. RBI’s ECL Directions from April 1, 2027 introduce Stage 3 while retaining prudential floors and specific income-recognition rules. [RBI 2025 IRACP; RBI 2026 ECL Directions]

**UK** — UK-adopted IFRS 9 applies the credit-impaired definition and Stage 3 lifetime ECL/interest mechanics. [IFRS 9 Appendix A and 5.4.1]

**EU** — EU-adopted IFRS 9 applies the same accounting definition; prudential default concepts remain separate regulatory concepts even where operational alignment is sought. [Regulation (EU) 2023/1803; EBA guidance]

**US** — CECL has no Stage 3 allowance category. US nonaccrual and regulatory classification practices are separate from Topic 326’s lifetime expected-loss measurement. [ASC 326; Interagency CECL policy]
