Default capital is calculated like any credit exposure: EAD × PD × LGD (for IRB banks) or risk-weighted under standardized. CVA capital (also called “Market risk” for CVA) is designed to capture losses from sudden widening of credit spreads on derivatives. Basel frameworks include a standardized CVA charge (and previously an advanced CVA VaR approach) to ensure banks hold capital against this risk. Notably, hedges (like single-name CDS) can reduce CVA capital if recognized. 

Thus banks need both: default capital (part of credit risk RWA) and CVA capital (part of market risk RWA in trading or CVA desks). These are distinct: a low-default-probability counterparty could still incur large CVA losses if its credit spreads move.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Always compute capital for CCR exposure under credit risk rules *and* for CVA risk. Don’t double-count (deduct CVA exposures when already in default RWA).
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Thinking credit risk capital is enough for CCR. CVA capital is separate for mark-to-market movements. Some banks once tried to net CVA and default exposures – Basel forbids netting them for capital.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Default Risk Capital" b="CVA Capital">
**Default Risk Capital** — Capital against expected loss from default (PD × LGD).  
---  
**CVA Capital** — Capital for mark-to-market credit spread risk on counterparty exposures.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** The need for two charges is a global standard. The methods (basic vs standardized CVA) vary slightly: final Basel rules now use standardized CVA capital for all banks.
</Rule>

<Recall
  q="Why is CVA capital separate from credit capital?"
  a="Because CVA capital accounts for fluctuations in the value of the mark-to-market portfolio due to credit spread changes, not just default events."
/>

<Recall
  q="Can CVA capital be hedged?"
  a="Yes, certain instruments (like CDS on the counterparty or proxies) can reduce CVA capital under rules, up to prescribed limits."
/>
