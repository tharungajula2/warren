The FATF issues non-binding recommendations that form the global standard for anti-money laundering (AML) and countering the financing of terrorism (CFT).  It has 40+9 Recommendations, covering CDD, reporting, supervision, international cooperation, etc.  According to FATF, these “set an international standard, which countries should implement through measures adapted to their circumstances”.  In practice, each country enacts its own laws (e.g. the UK’s MLR, India’s PMLA/KYC Regulations, US BSA) to meet FATF standards.  

FATF also evaluates jurisdictions’ AML/CFT regimes.  Every few years, member and key countries undergo peer review (“mutual evaluation”) to check compliance.  The FATF maintains two important lists: (1) **High-Risk Jurisdictions (Call for Action, “black list”)** – these have serious strategic deficiencies and require countermeasures (e.g. a ban on new correspondent accounts). (2) **Jurisdictions under Increased Monitoring (“grey list”)** – these have deficiencies but are working with FATF to improve.  For example, being grey-listed signals a need for enhanced due diligence on customers from that country.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Always check the FATF list statuses of jurisdictions. If a country is grey- or black-listed, apply enhanced due diligence to counterparties from that country.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Assuming FATF recommendations are law. They are **not legally binding**; instead each country implements its own legal framework based on them. Confusing grey list with black list (they imply different urgency) is a common error.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Grey List (increased monitoring)" b="Black List (high-risk)">
**Grey List (increased monitoring)** — jurisdictions with strategic AML gaps, under FATF follow-up.  
---  
**Black List (high-risk)** — jurisdictions with serious AML deficiencies requiring sanctions or countermeasures.  (Newcomers often misunderstand grey-list countries as “black.”)
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** The FATF standards and lists apply globally. Every member/territory is subject to the same evaluation methodology and must heed the same grey/black list status.
</Rule>
