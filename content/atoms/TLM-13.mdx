The Liquidity Coverage Ratio requires a stock of **high-quality liquid assets (HQLA)** against stressed net cash outflows. Basel describes HQLA as assets that can be easily and immediately converted into cash at little or no loss of value.

Three questions therefore precede the label. First, is the asset intrinsically liquid enough under the regulatory eligibility criteria? Second, is it **unencumbered**, meaning it is not already pledged or otherwise unavailable for monetisation? Third, can the bank operationally sell or repo it when needed? A qualifying security trapped in the wrong legal entity, pledged elsewhere or operationally inaccessible is not equivalent to immediately usable liquidity.

Basel distinguishes levels of HQLA because assets have different liquidity characteristics. Level 1 assets receive the most favourable treatment; lower HQLA levels are subject to haircuts and composition caps. A **haircut** reduces the amount of liquidity value recognised relative to the asset’s market value. Its purpose is to allow for value uncertainty and monetisation risk.

HQLA management therefore involves more than holding a target nominal amount. Treasury monitors market value, regulatory value after haircuts, encumbrance, currency, legal-entity location, settlement access and concentration. The buffer must also remain usable in stress: a liquidity regime that requires assets never to decline would defeat the purpose of a stress buffer.

India adds a distinctive interaction because qualifying government securities held for statutory liquidity purposes can, within RBI-defined MSF and FALLCR carve-outs, also be recognised as Level 1 HQLA for LCR purposes. The overlap is governed rather than automatic. RBI also revised valuation of Level 1 government securities for LCR purposes from 1 April 2026 to incorporate applicable LAF/MSF haircuts.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
HQLA status is based on stressed monetisability under regulatory conditions, not merely credit quality.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Using the market value of an eligible security as its LCR value without checking encumbrance, haircut and composition treatment.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="HQLA" b="Liquid asset in ordinary language">
**HQLA** — assets meeting prudential liquidity eligibility and operational requirements.
---
**Liquid asset in ordinary language** — any asset thought readily saleable; it may not qualify as regulatory HQLA.
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="IN">
— RBI allows specified government securities within mandatory SLR to count as Level 1 HQLA through MSF/FALLCR carve-outs; revised Level 1 government-security haircut treatment is effective from 1 April 2026. [RBI/2022-23/25; RBI/2025-26/27]
</Delta>

<Delta j="UK">

</Delta>

<Delta j="EU">

</Delta>

<Delta j="US">
— US LCR rules use regulatory HQLA categories and apply the LCR according to the tailoring framework rather than uniformly to all banks. [OCC Bulletin 2019-52]
</Delta>
