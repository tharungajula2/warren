A **contingency funding plan (CFP)** begins before the crisis. It defines early-warning indicators such as deposit outflows, widening funding spreads, falling unencumbered collateral, unusual drawdowns, reduced market access or rapid deterioration in liquidity metrics. Thresholds escalate the issue to named decision-makers rather than relying on ad hoc judgement.

The plan then maps actions to stress severity. Early actions might shorten asset deployment or increase secured funding. More severe stages may involve monetising liquidity buffers, raising term funding where markets remain open, mobilising collateral, reducing new commitments or using available central-bank facilities subject to eligibility and internal governance.

Each funding source needs an operational dossier: amount, currency, haircut, settlement time, legal entity, pre-positioning requirement, documentation, counterparty capacity and any constraint created by using it. The same asset cannot support two independent actions simultaneously.

Governance is as important as funding capacity. A usable CFP identifies who can invoke it, who executes transactions, who communicates with supervisors and counterparties and how information is produced during fast-moving stress. Periodic testing should verify that access channels, collateral movements and communication trees work in practice.

Basel’s sound liquidity principles call for robust, operational contingency funding plans tied to stress-test results. In the US, Regulation YY requires covered bank holding companies to maintain a CFP addressing identified stress events, available and alternative funding sources, invocation triggers, a liquidity-event management process, communication, early-warning monitoring and periodic operational testing.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
A contingency funding source is useful only to the extent it can be operationally accessed in the stress in which it is being counted.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Listing “sell securities” or “raise secured funding” without identifying haircuts, settlement time, collateral availability and responsible execution teams.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="CFP" b="Liquidity stress test">
**CFP** — the executable response plan for liquidity stress.
---
**Liquidity stress test** — the analytical scenario that quantifies the liquidity problem the CFP must address.
</NeverConfuse>
