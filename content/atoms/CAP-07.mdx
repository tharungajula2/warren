### Core mechanism

```text
Expected credit loss increases
    ↓
Loss allowance increases
    ↓
Credit impairment expense or opening-equity adjustment increases
    ↓
Retained earnings decrease
    ↓
Accounting equity decreases
    ↓
CET1 usually decreases
    ↓
Apply any separate prudential treatment
```

The same economic deterioration can also raise RWA through rating migration, default treatment or changed exposure characteristics. Capital ratios can therefore weaken through both numerator and denominator.

The prudential framework then asks a separate question: how much of the accounting provision is eligible for a specific regulatory-capital treatment? Under IRB, that question becomes the expected-loss-versus-eligible-provision comparison owned by CAP-08.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   IMP-01 allowance versus capital · impairment expense · retained earnings
This:       Accounting-to-capital transmission
Downstream: Provision shortfall/excess · ECL transition · stressed capital ratios
```

### Permanent rule

The allowance affects capital mainly by changing earnings or equity; any regulatory recognition of provisions is a second prudential step.

### Never confuse [Allowance effect on CET1] and [Allowance counted as capital]

**Allowance effect on CET1** — accounting loss recognition reduces retained earnings and therefore the CET1 base.

---

**Allowance counted as capital** — a separate and limited prudential treatment that exists only where the capital rules permit it.

### Permanent trap

Saying "provisions increase capital because they absorb losses" misses the first-order accounting effect: creating the provision generally reduces profit and CET1.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta in the balance-sheet transmission. The regulatory treatment of provision shortfalls, excess provisions and transitional relief differs by approach and jurisdiction.

<Recall
  q="What is the first capital effect of a new impairment charge?"
  a="Lower earnings or retained earnings, which normally lowers CET1."
