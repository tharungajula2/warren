A usable capital buffer creates a design problem. If a bank can distribute capital freely after entering the buffer, shareholders or instrument holders may receive resources that supervisors intended to remain available to absorb losses. Conversely, if entering the buffer immediately creates the same consequences as breaching a minimum, management may avoid using the buffer even during legitimate stress.

Basel solves this by imposing **capital conservation constraints** as CET1 falls through the conservation-buffer range. The deeper the bank moves into the buffer, the greater the share of earnings it must retain. Basel describes the buffer as usable while business continues, with automatic distribution restrictions increasing as capital approaches the minimum.

In UK and EU terminology, the related calculation is commonly called the **maximum distributable amount**, or MDA. The MDA limits specified discretionary distributions when the combined buffer is not met. UK PRA materials identify affected distributions such as bonuses and AT1 coupons, while EU CRD Article 141 links failure to meet the combined-buffer requirement with restrictions on distributions.

The important sequencing is therefore: first the bank uses excess management headroom; then it may enter regulatory buffers; distribution constraints intensify; only after further deterioration does it approach minimum capital requirements. A buffer is consequently neither “free capital” nor a second minimum.

The US stress capital buffer reaches a similar objective through a different capital architecture, so European MDA terminology should not be mechanically imported into US requirements.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Distribution restrictions are a conservation mechanism triggered before capital minima are exhausted.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Treating MDA as the amount of capital a bank is permitted to lose confuses a distribution limit with a capital stock.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="MDA" b="Capital buffer">
**MDA** — the maximum amount permitted for specified distributions under the applicable conservation regime.

---
**Capital buffer** — the CET1 cushion whose depletion can cause the MDA restriction to apply.
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="IN">
— RBI applies graduated capital-conservation ratios to earnings as CET1 enters the prescribed buffer bands. [RBI Capital Adequacy Directions 2025, capital conservation standards]
</Delta>

<Delta j="UK">
— Ordinary-regime firms face MDA restrictions when they do not meet the combined buffer; the SDDT capital regime effective 1 January 2027 uses a simplified structure and removes the ordinary automatic-MDA architecture for SDDTs. [PRA CP9/24; PS4/26 and finalised Strong and Simple policy]
</Delta>

<Delta j="EU">
— CRD Article 141 requires an MDA calculation and distribution restrictions when the combined-buffer requirement is not met. [CRD Article 141 / EBA]
</Delta>

<Delta j="US">
— The large-bank framework uses a stress capital buffer derived from supervisory stress testing rather than the EU-style combined-buffer/MDA architecture. [Federal Reserve Annual Report]
</Delta>
