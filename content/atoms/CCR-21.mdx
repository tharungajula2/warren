The **Basic Approach** is a simpler formula (often a flat rate on net expected exposures). The **Standardized Approach** is more risk-sensitive, effectively charging against CVA volatility at the 99% quantile. Eligible hedges (credit derivatives) get offsets. Only standardized methodology is allowed for large internationally active banks now. Small banks or some portfolios may use the BA.

Key point: under Basel’s finalized CVA framework, banks must hold capital for CVA risk using these global formulas. EU/UK transposed these in CRR3/UK Basel 3.1 (effective ~2025) and the US is updating its rule. Hedging rules (what qualifies and to what extent) follow Basel’s main text.

### Permanent rule

When calculating CCR capital, CVA risk is a separate add-on: follow the prescribed CVA framework (basic or standardized) based on bank size and model usage. CVA hedges can reduce CVA RWA only as explicitly permitted.

### Permanent trap

Mixing CVA and default exposures incorrectly. For example, one should not double-count risk reduction. Also, misidentifying which hedges are recognized (only certain counterparty CVA hedges).

### Never confuse

**Basic CVA Approach (BA-CVA)** — Simple standardized calculation (like fixed factor * EAD).
---
**Standardized CVA Approach (SA-CVA)** — Basel’s detailed formula or CVA VaR-based method.

### Jurisdiction variants

**IN** — RBI’s draft CCR circular focuses on CEM add-ons; India has not yet mandated a separate CVA capital charge for banks. (NBFCs under Ind AS would follow global norms, but banks follow RBI norms).
**UK** — Basel 3.1 includes the revised CVA framework (implemented by mid-2025, final CRR3 expected Jan 2025).
**EU** — CRR3 (approved by end-2023) implements Basel’s new CVA rules from Jan 2025.
**US** — Agencies have proposed changes (Nov 2022) to align with Basel 3.1 CVA rules, but finalization is pending.

<Recall
  q="What can banks use to reduce CVA capital?"
  a="Eligible CVA hedges (e.g. single-name CDS on the counterparty) up to limits (typically 50% offset) under standardized approach."
/>

<Recall
  q="Are interest rate or equity hedges allowed in CVA capital?"
  a="No. Only credit derivative hedges on the same counterparty or covered indices count."
