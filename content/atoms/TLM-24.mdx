A non-maturity deposit can be withdrawn quickly but may provide persistent aggregate funding. Crediting it purely at an overnight FTP rate ignores that stable funding value. Crediting the entire balance at a long-term rate assumes stability the bank may not actually possess.

A common conceptual approach begins by segmenting the portfolio and separating a **volatile** component from a **core or stable** component. The volatile portion receives short-tenor treatment. The stable portion is assigned to a ladder or **replicating portfolio** of maturities based on estimated balance persistence and repricing behaviour. Its FTP credit becomes a weighted blend of transfer rates across those maturities.

The model must distinguish balance stability from deposit-rate behaviour. A balance can remain in the bank for years but reprice rapidly when market rates change. Such a deposit supplies stable funding but less interest-rate benefit than one whose administered rate moves slowly. Combining the two assumptions into one “duration” obscures the economics.

This methodology also links FTP to IRRBB. If the NMD desk is credited with a five-year funding value, treasury effectively accepts the corresponding behavioural position and must manage the resulting interest-rate exposure. The NMD model used for FTP, ALM and risk should therefore be reconcilable even when the exact purposes require different conservative overlays.

FSI better-practice work states that non-maturing products require blended marginal transfer rates rather than a single average funding rate. The modelling challenge is not to find one objectively “correct” maturity; it is to establish a stable, validated and stressable representation of how the portfolio actually behaves.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
NMD transfer value must come from behavioural assumptions that are explicit, governed and consistent with the risk transferred to treasury.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Rewarding a deposit franchise with long-term FTP credit while ALM independently models the same deposits as short-lived, creating profits from inconsistent assumptions.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Behavioural tenor" b="Contractual tenor">
**Behavioural tenor** — modelled persistence used to represent how long funding effectively remains.
---
**Contractual tenor** — legal maturity; for an NMD it may provide almost no information about aggregate funding persistence.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Regulatory behavioural constraints may influence model boundaries, but the FTP problem itself is universal.
</Rule>
