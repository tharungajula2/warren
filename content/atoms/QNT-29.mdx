### Core mechanism

| Tool | Calculation idea | Main limitation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Calibration curve | Compare average prediction with observed event rate by bin | Depends on binning and sample size |
| O/E ratio | Compare total observed events with total expected events | Can hide offsetting segment errors |
| Brier score | Average squared probability error | Mixes calibration and discrimination information |
| Statistical calibration test | Compare observed departures with sampling variability | Large samples can flag tiny differences; sparse samples have low power |

```text
Predicted probabilities
    ↓
Group comparable predictions or evaluate globally
    ↓
Compare predicted event mass with observed events
    ↓
Separate level error from sampling noise
```

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Probability model · Sampling uncertainty · Observed outcomes
This:       Calibration curve · O/E · Brier · Statistical tests
Downstream: Recalibration · Monitoring · Challenger comparison
```

### Formula

```text
Expected events = sum over loans of predicted probability
O/E ratio = observed events / expected events
Brier score = sum over loans of (observed outcome - predicted probability) squared / n
```

### Permanent rule

A model can discriminate well and calibrate badly, or calibrate the portfolio average while being badly miscalibrated within important segments.

### Never confuse [Calibration-in-the-large] and [Segment calibration]

**Calibration-in-the-large** - total or average predicted event level matches total or average observed level.

---

**Segment calibration** - predicted and observed frequencies align across relevant risk bands, products, vintages or other groups.

### Permanent trap

Declaring a model calibrated because O/E is near 1 for the whole portfolio. Overprediction in one segment can cancel underprediction in another.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta. Calibration mathematics is universal; MRM owns governed interpretation of test results.

<Recall
  q="What does O/E greater than 1 indicate?"
  a="The portfolio produced more observed events than the model predicted in aggregate, although sampling uncertainty and composition should be examined before calling the difference systematic."
/>

<Recall
  q="Why is Brier score not a pure calibration statistic?"
  a="Squared probability error rewards both assigning appropriate probability levels and separating event from non-event cases, so discrimination also affects the score."
/>

<Recall
  q="Can perfect portfolio-level O/E coexist with poor calibration?"
  a="Yes. Opposite errors across grades or segments can cancel in total while individual probability bands remain materially wrong."
/>
