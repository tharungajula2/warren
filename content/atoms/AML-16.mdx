Sanctions screening is a separate discipline from general transaction monitoring. Banks must screen customers at onboarding and periodically re-screen accounts against sanctions lists: UN Security Council, US Treasury’s OFAC, EU, UK, etc.  For transactions (especially cross-border payments), the payment’s beneficiary and originator names are also screened in real time to block any transfer involving a sanctioned party.  Fuzzy or “approximate” matching is used to account for misspellings or transliterations. For example, a wire transfer to “Mohammed Salim” would be checked against “Mohammed Suleiman” using phonetic match logic.  

Sanctions screening is mandated by law. In the US, OFAC requires blocking any transaction involving a person on the Specially Designated Nationals (SDN) list. Similar rules exist globally. Unlike AML alerts, a sanctions match has no ambiguity – if the payee is on a list, the payment must be rejected or frozen.  Thus, sanctions screening focuses on lists and name-matching, whereas transaction monitoring focuses on behavioral patterns.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Maintain up-to-date sanctions and PEP lists (daily at least). Screen all new customers and every transaction against these lists using reliable matching software. Do not rely solely on exact spellings – use fuzzy logic to catch variants.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Assuming exact name match is enough. Criminals often use aliases or misspellings; failing to catch those will let them slip by. Also, treating sanctions screening as part of AML monitoring – it’s a distinct process legally and technically.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Sanctions screening" b="Transaction monitoring">
**Sanctions screening** — matching names against official sanctions/terrorism lists.  
---  
**Transaction monitoring** — looking for suspicious activity patterns. The two are often conflated but sanctions screening simply verifies if transaction parties are blacklisted, whereas monitoring checks transaction *behavior*.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Sanctions regimes are country-specific in content (different lists), but all jurisdictions require compliance with UN sanction lists. In practice: **IN** follows UN and domestic (e.g. ISIS list) sanctions via RBI circulars; **UK** implements UN/EU/UK lists via HM Treasury; **EU** has Union lists; **US** has its own OFAC list (e.g. North Korea, Iran). Screening processes are similar globally but lists differ.
</Rule>
