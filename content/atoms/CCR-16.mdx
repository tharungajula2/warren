Under CEM, each trade’s PFE was a percentage of notional (e.g. 1% per year on interest rate swaps, up to caps). CEM did not recognize hedging across asset classes or margin. The Standardized Method applied broad risk weights or a CEM variant. Neither captured netting or actual collateral effects well.

Basel Committee’s 2014 standard explicitly “replaced both the Current Exposure Method and the Standardized Method” with SA-CCR. Thus, for capital calculations, banks must use SA-CCR (or IMM if allowed). CEM and SM are now obsolete except as possible fallbacks for small institutions until SA-CCR is implemented.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
After SA-CCR’s effective date, do not use CEM or SM for capital purposes. Any regulatory references to CEM/SM are historical or transitional.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Mistakenly applying CEM factors or SM calculations. For example, calculating add-ons on notional as under CEM will understate capital if SA-CCR is mandated.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Current Exposure Method (CEM)" b="Standardized Method (SM)">
**Current Exposure Method (CEM)** — Old CCR method: RC + fixed add-ons on notional.
---
**Standardized Method (SM)** — An older Basel approach for CCR (e.g. index-based factors).
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** All Basel members have moved to SA-CCR for regulated banks. Any national capital rules referencing CEM/SM should be updated.
</Rule>

<Recall
  q="What did CEM use for PFE?"
  a="Flat percentages on notional by product and maturity, without netting or margin."
/>

<Recall
  q="Why replace CEM/SM?"
  a="They were too crude: CEM ignored hedging/netting effects, and SM was overly simplified. SA-CCR is more risk-sensitive."
/>
