**Retail credit** consists of large numbers of comparatively small exposures with repeatable product structures and observable behavioural histories. These characteristics support statistical segmentation: borrowers with similar attributes and behaviour can be grouped and modelled using scorecards, transition models and pool-level parameters.

Basel's IRB architecture reflects this idea by requiring retail exposures, among other criteria, to be managed as part of large pools rather than purely as individually managed wholesale credits. US Advanced Approaches rules similarly define certain retail exposures as managed within segments of exposures with homogeneous risk characteristics.

**Wholesale credit** covers corporate, sovereign, bank and other individually assessed exposures under relevant frameworks. A single exposure can be large enough that borrower-specific financial statements, industry structure, management, collateral, covenants and legal arrangements materially alter risk. The sample of comparable defaults may also be sparse. Wholesale systems therefore commonly combine quantitative analysis with expert judgement and assign obligors to rating grades.

"Retail is statistical; wholesale is judgemental" is a useful orientation, not a law. Retail underwriting still uses policy rules, overrides, fraud controls and expert model governance. Wholesale rating systems can use sophisticated statistical models and external information. The real distinction is the **unit and density of evidence**: mass portfolios support estimation from repeated patterns; large idiosyncratic obligors demand greater case-specific interpretation.

The modelling level follows the object hierarchy. Retail performance can often be assessed at facility or pool level under regulatory rules, while wholesale default is generally obligor-centred. This difference affects default tagging, validation samples and parameter estimation.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Choose the modelling unit and method to match the economic homogeneity, data density and materiality of the exposures.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Applying a mass-retail scorecard mechanically to a small set of large heterogeneous corporate borrowers creates statistical confidence without genuine comparability.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Retail modelling** — pool- and segment-oriented modelling supported by repeated observations.

**Wholesale rating** — obligor-centred assessment combining structured quantitative evidence and case-specific judgement.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Regulatory boundary definitions differ, but the economic reason for statistical retail treatment versus more individual wholesale treatment is universal.
</Rule>
