### Core mechanism

| Dimension | Retail tendency | Wholesale tendency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Number of exposures | Large | Smaller |
| Individual exposure size | Comparatively small | Often material |
| Homogeneity | Higher within designed segments | Lower |
| Primary modelling unit | Pool / segment / facility behaviour | Obligor rating plus facility analysis |
| Data evidence | Repeated behavioural observations | Financial statements, sector, management, structure, expert judgement |
| Default application | Can permit facility/pool treatment under applicable rules | Generally obligor-centred in Basel-style wholesale frameworks |

“Retail = model” and “wholesale = judgement” is too crude. Retail systems still need policy, overrides and fraud controls; wholesale ratings can use powerful quantitative models. The difference is how much **repeatable population evidence** versus **case-specific information** can support the decision.

<Flow>
Exposure population
    ↓
Size + homogeneity + data density + materiality
    ↓
Retail-like → Pool / segment statistical treatment
Wholesale-like → Obligor-centred rating + facility analysis
</Flow>

<Flow>
Upstream:   Exposure type · Population size · Homogeneity · Data density · Materiality
This:       Choose pooled statistical treatment or obligor-centred assessment
Downstream: Segmentation · Scorecards · Ratings · PD/LGD/EAD estimation · Validation
</Flow>

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Choose the modelling unit and method to match the economic homogeneity, data density and materiality of the exposures.
</Rule>

<NeverConfuse a="Retail modelling" b="Automated lending">
**Retail modelling** — pool-oriented estimation using repeated observations with similar risk characteristics.

---
**Automated lending** — an operational decision process; retail credit can include manual review, and wholesale credit can use automation.
</NeverConfuse>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Applying a mass-retail scorecard to a small, heterogeneous set of large corporates creates apparent statistical precision without a genuinely comparable population.
</Trap>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta in the economic logic.** Regulatory definitions determine which exposures qualify for retail or wholesale treatment and can impose size, pooling or management criteria.
</Rule>

<Recall
  q="Why is statistical modelling especially natural in retail portfolios?"
  a="Large numbers of similar exposures create repeatable observations."
/>

<Recall
  q="Why does wholesale analysis rely more on borrower-specific evidence?"
  a="Each exposure can be material and the population of truly comparable obligors is smaller and more heterogeneous."
/>

<Recall
  q="Does wholesale modelling exclude quantitative models?"
  a="No. Models can be central, but case-specific information and judgement usually carry more weight."
/>
