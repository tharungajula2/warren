Under the *Co-Lending Model* (CoL), a bank and an NBFC enter a formal agreement. They extend credit to a common borrower at a predetermined ratio. One pattern is 80:20 (bank:NBFC) or 70:30, but RBI’s 2023 updates mandate minimum bank share of 20% for this scheme. In practice, many big banks take 80% and partner with smaller NBFCs (especially MFIs) that originate and service the loan to micro customers.

All documentation must be in both names, and collections are shared. For example, the bank provides funds to NBFC (or directly to borrower), but NBFC handles field appraisal. The repayments are then split per the agreed ratio. This model aims to leverage NBFC outreach (last-mile distribution, quick appraisals) while giving banks a strong share of safer assets. Both parties must decide upfront on interest rate split, service fees to NBFC, and NPA handling.

Operationally, co-lending adds complexity: two sets of ledgers (bank’s book and NBFC’s), reconciliation of cashflows, dual KYC checks, etc. If a borrower defaults, both share the loss per their ratio. RBI requires co-lending accounts to be reported correctly in their prudential returns (assets doubled: one on each book).  Tax and accounting rules also reflect that a co-lent loan is partly a loan to NBFC (on bank book) and partly bank’s loan to borrower.

The CoL framework was revised: initially introduced in 2018, it was formalized by Master Directions in 2021 and updated in 2023. The 2023 revision relaxed some earlier restrictions (like making NBFC proportion flexible up to 80%). It also allowed banks to lend their own share through partnerships (bank fraction can be higher). These policies aim to expand affordable credit (especially to underserved borrowers of NBFCs) by leveraging bank deposit base.

### Permanent rule

In any RBI-approved co-lending arrangement, the bank must retain at least 20% (and typically holds the majority) of the credit amount. Both parties share risk and ownership accordingly.

### Permanent trap

Misunderstanding who owns the loan: both do. A common mistake is booking the entire amount on one balance sheet; instead, each books its share. Also, forgetting that two payment schedules exist – banks must get their share.

### Never confuse

**Co-lending** — joint financing by bank + NBFC on a single loan to a borrower. Each funds part (min. 20% by bank) and shares title.

---

**Syndication** — multiple banks lending separate portions to one borrower, each with own loan. (Co-lending keeps one loan, two lenders, not a consortium of loans.)

### Jurisdiction variants

**IN** — RBI’s co-lending model is unique (though other countries have bank–nonbank partnerships, they are usually simpler).
