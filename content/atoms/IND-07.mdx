SMAs serve as a watchlist under RBI’s framework. When a loan installment is overdue for 31–60 days, it is an **SMA-0**.  Overdue 61–90 days becomes **SMA-1**. Overdue 91–180 days is **SMA-2** (by then it is an NPA if &gt;90 days). These buckets help banks report accounts approaching NPA status. For example, a loan 45 days past due is SMA-0. Any payment beyond 90 days makes it automatically NPA (substandard category). SMAs capture loans at risk of slipping: if a borrower is regularly late or temporarily stressed, the account is flagged as SMA even if NPA threshold not crossed yet.

RBI introduced SMAs to improve risk monitoring and discourage “evergreening” accounts (extending new loans to cover interest on old ones). Banks must report SMA balances to RBI daily. Regulators use SMA data to assess sectoral asset quality and systemic risk.

No additional provisioning is mandated for SMAs (they remain performing assets) but banks should increase watchful provisioning as accounts progress through SMA categories. When an SMA-2 account crosses 90 days, it is upgraded to NPA (sub-standard). Thus, SMAs act as intermediate flags.

```
Loan Days Past Due -&gt; Classification
------------------------------------
0–30 days        | Standard (Performing)
31–60 days       | SMA-0 (Special Mention 0)
61–90 days       | SMA-1 (Special Mention 1)
&gt;90 days        | SMA-2 (Special Mention 2) -&gt; NPA (Sub-std)
```

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Accounts must be flagged in increasing SMA categories as soon as they cross due-date thresholds (31, 61, 91 days past due). This daily reporting is mandatory.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Ignoring SMA flags or delaying them.  SMA classification is not a choice; banks must stamp accounts promptly at 31+ days overdue. Treating a stressed loan as “still standard” before 30 days beyond due violates rules.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**SMA-0** — overdue 31–60 days (early warning, pre-NPA).
**SMA-1** — overdue 61–90 days.
**SMA-2** — overdue 91–180 days (immediately becomes NPA if not resolved)

SMAs are *not* regulatory categories with prescribed provisioning; they’re managerial flags. Once past 90 days, any SMA becomes NPA (Sub-Standard asset).
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="IN">
— RBI’s SMA classification is used; similar watch-lists exist in some countries but not all regulators formalize 30/60/90 day buckets.
</Delta>
