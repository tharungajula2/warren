**Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)** is an Indian statutory liquidity requirement based on Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL). **LCR** is the Basel-style ratio of recognised HQLA to 30-day stressed net cash outflows. They answer different questions: SLR requires specified liquid-asset holdings; LCR tests short-term stressed liquidity adequacy.

The crucial interaction is HQLA recognition. RBI permits government securities held **above** mandatory SLR to qualify as Level 1 HQLA subject to the rules. It also permits government securities **within** mandatory SLR to be counted as Level 1 HQLA to the extent allowed under the **Marginal Standing Facility (MSF)** and the **Facility to Avail Liquidity for Liquidity Coverage Ratio (FALLCR)**.

In April 2022, RBI noted that the MSF component had fallen to 2% of NDTL and increased FALLCR from 15% to 16% of NDTL. The total HQLA carve-out from mandatory SLR therefore became 18% of NDTL: 2% through MSF plus 16% through FALLCR.

This does **not** mean “SLR equals LCR”. The denominator, purpose and operational rules remain separate. Nor does it mean every rupee of statutory holdings automatically enters LCR at full value. The asset must satisfy applicable HQLA conditions, and RBI’s April 2025 final amendments—effective 1 April 2026—require Level 1 government securities to be valued for LCR at no more than market value adjusted for applicable LAF/MSF haircuts.

The practitioner’s mental model is overlap, not addition: the same qualifying security can help satisfy a statutory holding requirement and, within RBI’s defined accessibility framework, contribute to the LCR numerator.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
SLR and LCR are separate requirements; RBI’s MSF/FALLCR rules determine how specified securities within mandatory SLR can also count as LCR HQLA.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Adding “18% SLR liquidity” to the HQLA buffer as though they were separate pools when the same securities have already been counted through the MSF/FALLCR overlap.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="SLR" b="FALLCR">
**SLR** — India’s statutory liquid-asset holding requirement linked to NDTL.
---
**FALLCR** — the RBI facility permitting specified securities within mandatory SLR to be reckoned as HQLA for LCR purposes; it is not a second SLR requirement.
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="IN">
— RBI permits an 18% of NDTL HQLA carve-out from mandatory SLR through 2% MSF plus 16% FALLCR; government-security LCR valuation is subject to the revised haircut treatment effective 1 April 2026. [RBI/2022-23/25; RBI/2025-26/27]
</Delta>

<Delta j="UK">
— no SLR/FALLCR construct equivalent to this Indian RBI mechanism; UK firms follow the PRA liquidity/HQLA framework. [PRA SS24/15]
</Delta>

<Delta j="EU">
— no Indian-style statutory SLR/FALLCR overlay exists in the CRR LCR architecture; EU HQLA treatment follows CRR and Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/61. [CRR Part Six]
</Delta>

<Delta j="US">
— no Indian SLR/FALLCR construct applies; US HQLA and LCR requirements are governed through the US liquidity rules and tailoring framework. [OCC Bulletin 2019-52]
</Delta>
