Regulators mandate that fair-valued trading assets must be marked down conservatively.  For example, the EU’s CRR/CRD rules (via RTS on prudent valuation) require AVAs to cover risks like market price uncertainty and model risk.  Banks quantify AVAs (often at a 90% confidence level) for items such as bid-ask spreads, close-out costs, and other uncertainties.  The total AVA reduces Tier-1 capital.

Separately, XVAs (e.g. CVA for counterparty credit, FVA for funding) adjust valuation for trading derivatives.  These are usually calculated under accounting standards or supervisory frameworks.  While CVA (Counterparty Value Adjustment) was specifically addressed in Basel III/CRR2, other XVA components (DVA, FVA) reflect economic value adjustments.  Overall, prudent valuation and XVA policies ensure the bank’s book reflect worst-case or “fiduciary” values, protecting capital.

### Permanent rule

Always include regulatory valuation adjustments (AVAs) on marked-to-market positions to account for uncertainty.

### Permanent trap

Relying solely on model or mid-market prices for capital without AVAs, understating risk.

### Never confuse

**Fair value** — market-based exit price; **Prudent value** — lower value after applying AVAs (90% CI). AVAs include factors like bid-ask and model risk.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Prudent valuation standards (including AVA requirements) are embedded in CRR/CRD in EU and similarly in other advanced frameworks.
