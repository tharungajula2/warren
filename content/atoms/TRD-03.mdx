Coupon interest accrues economically between payment dates. Suppose a bond pays a coupon every six months. The seller who has held the bond for part of the coupon period has earned part of the next coupon even though the issuer will pay the full coupon to whoever owns the bond on the payment date. Settlement therefore compensates the seller for accrued interest.

The standard relationship is:

Dirty price = Clean price + Accrued interest

The clean price is useful for price comparison because it removes the predictable saw-tooth movement caused purely by coupon accrual. The dirty price, sometimes called the full price, is the value used to determine the cash consideration for settlement, subject to market-specific conventions and transaction charges.

Accrued interest depends on the coupon amount and the fraction of the coupon period that has elapsed. That fraction is determined by a day-count convention such as Actual/Actual, 30/360 or Actual/365. Two instruments can therefore accrue differently even when they have the same coupon rate and calendar dates.

Around a coupon payment date, the mechanics can look counter-intuitive. Immediately before coupon payment, accrued interest is high. After the coupon is detached and paid, accrued interest resets, while the clean price should not mechanically jump by the same amount merely because time passed. This is why analysts distinguish clean and dirty measures.

The distinction also matters in reconciliations. A trader may discuss a clean-price move, the risk system may mark a full price, and the settlement instruction may reflect principal amount plus accrued interest. If systems disagree on the field definition, a correct trade can appear to have a valuation or cash break.

Always identify whether a bond price field is clean or dirty before comparing valuations or settlement amounts.
</Rule>
