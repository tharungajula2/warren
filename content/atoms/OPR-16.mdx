Business continuity (BCP) and disaster recovery (DRP) are key elements of resilience. A **BCP** is a high-level strategy covering all aspects of the business: it identifies essential functions and minimum acceptable service levels, and outlines procedures (e.g. relocation, manual processes) to maintain those functions. A BCP will include communication plans, alternate work sites, and human resource arrangements to cope with events like natural disasters or pandemics.

A **DRP** is a subset of BCP that deals specifically with IT recovery. It defines how to recover systems, networks, and data (e.g. via backups, failover to secondary data centers) after an outage. For instance, after a cyberattack, the DRP would guide restoring customer databases and applications.

Both plans require regular testing (drills, simulations) to ensure readiness. For example, a bank might simulate a data center blackout and try to operate from the backup site. Regulatory expectations (e.g. RBI’s BCM guidelines, FFIEC in US) mandate routine tests and board oversight of these plans.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Maintain up-to-date BCP and DRP, and test them at least annually (or when significant changes occur). Ensure all staff know their roles. Back up critical data offsite and verify restore capability.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Do not prepare a plan and forget it. Plans must evolve with the business. A common mistake is outdated contact lists or assuming the “test was enough.” Also, don’t rely solely on IT solutions – people and process continuity must be addressed.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Business Continuity Plan (BCP)" b="Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP)">
**Business Continuity Plan (BCP)** — ensures business operations continue (includes people, processes, systems).
---
**Disaster Recovery Plan (DRP)** — detailed IT recovery procedures after an incident.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** All banking regulators require both BCP and DRP.  For example, RBI mandates annual BCM tests; Fed/OCC have similar guidance.
</Rule>
