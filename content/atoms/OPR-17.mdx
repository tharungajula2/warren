Cyber risk (often called information/cybersecurity risk) involves potential losses from attacks like hacking, malware, or ransomware. At the governance level, it means ensuring the bank has proper oversight over this risk. This includes: appointing a Chief Information Security Officer (CISO), having a board committee for cyber, setting a risk appetite for cyber breaches, enforcing policies (access controls, encryption standards), and running incident response drills.  For example, regulators expect boards to understand major cyber threats and ensure management addresses them (e.g. Sarbanes-Oxley and Fed guidance highlight board responsibility for cybersecurity).

Organizations increasingly treat cybersecurity as a business-risk issue, not just IT’s job. Recent high-profile outages (AWS, Azure) and cyberattacks underscore this point. Firms must integrate cyber defense into risk management (e.g. cross-functional risk committees, regular audit of cyber controls). Ultimately, effective cyber risk governance means embedding cyber into the risk culture – emphasising that a human or process failure (e.g. poor patching, phishing success) is as much an operational risk as a market shock.

### Permanent rule

Board and executive management must have clear oversight of cyber security.  Risk committees should review cyber metrics (KRI) and ensure incident plans exist.  All major system changes or third-party integrations need cyber risk assessment.

### Permanent trap

Technology teams should not be left as isolated silos for cyber.  A common error is delegating all responsibility to the IT department.  Instead, cyber risk must be owned at the enterprise level.

### Never confuse

**Cybersecurity (tech focus)** vs **Cyber risk governance (management focus)**. Cybersecurity involves tools and processes (firewalls, encryption). Cyber risk governance means leadership sets strategy and ensures controls are in place.

### Jurisdiction variants

**IN** — RBI’s Cyber Security Framework for banks requires boards to approve cyber strategies.
**UK/EU** — Regulatory requirements (e.g. NIS regulations, DORA) push firms to enhance cyber resilience.

**US** — SEC, OCC and FFIEC expect robust cyber governance (board-level oversight).
