The Institute of Internal Auditors' current framework is called the **Three Lines Model**, updating the older "three lines of defence" terminology. It places the governing body, management and internal audit in related but distinct roles and rejects an overly rigid interpretation in which functions operate as isolated silos.

The **first line** consists of management roles closest to delivering products, services and operations. First-line management owns and manages the risks arising from those activities and maintains the controls needed to keep risk within approved boundaries.

The **second line** comprises management roles providing specialist expertise, support, monitoring and challenge on risk-related matters. Risk management and compliance functions are common examples. The second line can set frameworks, monitor exposures, challenge decisions and escalate concerns, but that does not transfer ownership of business risk away from first-line management.

The **third line** is internal audit. Its defining feature is independence from management responsibilities so that it can provide objective assurance and advice regarding governance, risk management and internal control. The third line should not become the owner of controls it later audits; doing so can compromise its ability to assure them independently.

The **governing body** sits above this operating distinction. It is accountable for governance and oversight and receives information and assurance from management and internal audit. Cooperation among the lines is desirable, but accountability and independence must remain clear.

The model is therefore less about organisational charts than about answering: who owns the risk, who supports and challenges management, and who independently assures the governance and control system?

```text
+--------------------------+
| Governing body           |
| oversight + accountability|
+--------------------------+
             |
             v
+--------------------------+
| FIRST LINE               |
| business / management    |
| owns and manages risk    |
+--------------------------+
             |
             v
+--------------------------+
| SECOND LINE              |
| expertise, monitoring,   |
| support and challenge    |
+--------------------------+
             |
             v
+--------------------------+
| THIRD LINE               |
| independent internal     |
| audit assurance          |
+--------------------------+
```

### Permanent rule

Management retains responsibility for managing risk; internal audit must remain sufficiently independent to provide objective assurance.

### Permanent trap

Writing a control failure off as "second line's responsibility" because the risk function monitors it, even though the business process creating the risk sits in the first line.

### Never confuse

**Second line** — management functions providing expertise, monitoring, support and challenge.

---

**Third line** — independent internal audit assurance; it is not simply a stronger version of second-line risk management.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** The IIA Three Lines Model is a governance model rather than a jurisdiction-specific banking rule; local supervisory requirements can add role-specific obligations.
