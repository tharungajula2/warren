**PD**, probability of default, measures the likelihood that the obligor defaults over the prescribed horizon. **LGD**, loss given default, measures the proportion of exposure lost if default occurs, after appropriate recoveries and costs under the regulatory definition. **EAD**, exposure at default, measures the amount expected to be outstanding if default occurs. **Maturity**, where applicable, adjusts for the period over which credit deterioration can affect the exposure. Basel specifies these as core IRB risk components.

The function also includes a **correlation** assumption. Intuitively, two borrowers whose fortunes move closely with the same systematic economic factor create more common-tail risk than two independent borrowers. Basel does not ask each bank to choose this portfolio correlation freely; the supervisory function embeds prescribed relationships.

The formula is intentionally not just expected loss. PD × LGD × EAD is a useful expected-loss intuition under simplified assumptions. IRB capital targets the unexpected-loss portion of the distribution at a high adverse confidence level, with maturity and systematic dependence affecting the tail.

That is why a small change in PD need not produce the same percentage change in capital at every starting PD. The regulatory mapping is nonlinear.

**Perishable — India’s final Standardised Approach becomes effective on 1 April 2027. The formula described here is therefore not the Indian domestic banking-book credit-capital method.**

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
IRB capital is a supervisory portfolio-tail calculation using modelled inputs; it is not the arithmetic product of PD, LGD and EAD.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Using expected loss as a proxy for IRB capital misses correlation, maturity and the unexpected-loss calibration embedded in the risk-weight function.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Expected loss" b="IRB capital requirement">
**Expected loss** — a central loss estimate often simplified as PD × LGD × EAD.

---
**IRB capital requirement** — the regulatory unexpected-loss result generated by the prescribed function and applicable adjustments.
</NeverConfuse>
