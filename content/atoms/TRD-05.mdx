Duration is both a cash-flow timing concept and a practical risk measure. Macaulay duration is a present-value-weighted average time to receive cash flows. Modified duration converts that concept into approximate percentage price sensitivity to a small parallel change in yield. If modified duration is 5, a 1 percentage-point rise in yield implies roughly a 5% price fall before allowing for convexity.

The approximation is:

Percentage price change ≈ − Modified duration × yield change

The minus sign reflects the inverse price–yield relationship. Duration is local: it is most accurate for small changes and for instruments whose cash flows do not change when rates move.

Convexity captures curvature. A plain option-free bond normally has positive convexity, meaning the price gain from a given fall in yield is larger than the price loss from an equal rise in yield. Adding a convexity term improves the approximation:

Percentage price change ≈ −D × Δy + ½ × Convexity × (Δy)^2

Embedded options can change the shape materially. A callable bond may exhibit reduced or negative effective convexity when rates fall because the issuer becomes more likely to call it, capping price appreciation. For instruments whose expected cash flows change with rates, effective duration and effective convexity are more useful than measures assuming fixed cash flows.

Duration is not maturity. A 10-year high-coupon bond generally has lower duration than a 10-year zero-coupon bond because more value is received earlier. Duration is also not credit spread duration unless the shock being measured is explicitly a spread change.

At portfolio level, duration supports hedging and risk aggregation, but parallel-shift duration misses changes in curve shape. Key-rate durations or bucketed sensitivities are needed when different maturities move by different amounts.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Use duration for local slope and convexity for curvature; neither is a substitute for understanding the cash flows and options.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Quoting a single duration number as if it captures every possible yield-curve movement.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Maturity" b="Duration">
**Maturity** — time until contractual principal repayment.

---

**Duration** — price sensitivity to yield changes, derived from the timing and present value of cash flows.
</NeverConfuse>

**No delta.**
</Rule>
