Key Risk Indicators (KRIs) and Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) often confuse people but serve different purposes.  A **KRI** is a risk-focused measure, intended to alert the bank to increasing risk before losses occur. For example, a KRI might be the percentage of exceptions in a control process, or days since the last system patch (longer time = higher risk). KRIs usually have warning thresholds: if exceeded, management investigates potential failures.

A **KPI** is a metric of business performance (e.g. profit margin, new accounts opened).  A KRI gives an early warning of possible risk events, while a KPI tells you if you met your targets.  In short: “KRIs measure exposure to risk; KPIs measure progress toward goals”.  For instance, falling customer satisfaction could be a KRI for conduct risk; growth in assets under management is a KPI for business success.

Always align KRIs to actual risk scenarios identified (e.g. RCSA or loss data).  And distinguish them clearly from KPIs so risk and business metrics are not mixed.

### Permanent rule

Define KRIs with clear thresholds and action triggers.  Link each KRI to a specific risk scenario, and review them regularly.  KRIs should be meaningful and measurable (e.g. error rates, incident counts).

### Permanent trap

A frequent mistake is tracking a KPI and calling it a KRI (or vice versa).  For example, using branch transaction volume (a KPI) to measure processing risk is misleading.  Always double-check if a metric is truly risk-related (KRI) or just performance (KPI).

### Never confuse

**KRI** — a risk exposure metric that should rise *before* losses (e.g. late detections).
---
**KPI** — a performance metric of outcomes (e.g. revenue growth).

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** The concept of KRIs vs KPIs is consistent across banking regulations. All risk frameworks emphasize forward-looking indicators.
