CICs operate under the Credit Information Companies (Regulation) Act, 2005. By law, any institution granting loans above certain thresholds must report data (including repayment behaviour) to CICs. Thus, a “CIBIL report” may contain: outstanding loans, EMI payment history, credit inquiries, defaults or write-offs. A high credit score (e.g. 750+) typically leads to faster loan approval. However, some segments (like unsecured or newer forms of credit) still rely on alternate data, since CIC coverage has gaps (informal lending not always reported).

RBI mandates that lenders consult at least one CIC before sanctioning any loan. Lenders are also regulated on sharing the score with customers (ECIR/ ECI guidelines requiring justification for rejection). Over the past decade, refinements have been made: e.g. CRILC (Central Repository of Information on Large Credits) to flag large defaults, and securitisation of card limits for bureau reporting. Debenture data (various corporate loans) is less visible in consumer credit reports.

For corporate or MSME borrowers, there is no single “CIBIL score” standard. Instead, banks rely on financial statements, promoter history, and sometimes the promoter’s personal credit scores. Commercial credit intelligence (like Dun &amp; Bradstreet ratings) complement it.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Banks and NBFCs must submit borrower data to CICs and use bureau reports in credit decisions. Borrowers have a right to one free credit report per year from each bureau.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Over-reliance on the numeric score. A common error is to treat a 750 CIBIL score as guarantee of repayment; lenders must still do underwriting. Conversely, a borrower’s poor score may have valid reasons (data errors or past minor defaults) – blindly rejecting such cases without manual review can be wrong.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Credit report** — detailed history of all borrowed accounts and repays (with any defaults) held by a CIC.
**Credit score** — a numerical summary (e.g. CIBIL score) of that report.
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="IN">
— System of multiple CICs (CIBIL, HDFC ERGO, etc.) is similar to US/UK bureau systems. But India’s credit scoring scale (300–900 for CIBIL) is proprietary (others use different ranges).
</Delta>
