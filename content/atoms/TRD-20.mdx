An overnight index swap, or OIS, is an interest-rate swap whose floating leg is linked to an overnight reference rate compounded or averaged over the accrual period according to the contract. Examples include SONIA, SOFR and €STR-linked swaps. The fixed OIS rate is the market rate that equates the present value of fixed and overnight-based floating legs at inception.

OIS rates matter beyond the product itself. For a collateralised derivative, the appropriate discount rate is closely connected to the rate earned or paid on posted cash collateral under the collateral agreement. Post-crisis markets therefore moved away from using a single unsecured IBOR curve for both projected cash flows and discounting. Multi-curve valuation separates the curve used to project a floating benchmark from the curve used to discount collateralised cash flows.

This distinction can feel abstract but has a direct P&amp;L effect. Suppose a swap pays a term benchmark but is collateralised daily in cash remunerated at an overnight rate. Future floating coupons may be projected from one curve while discounted using an overnight-based curve. Changing the collateral currency or remuneration convention can change discounting and therefore fair value.

An OIS rate is not the same as the overnight fixing itself. The fixing is one observed overnight rate; an OIS rate is a market price for a future sequence of overnight outcomes over the swap period.

Operationally, compounding details matter: observation dates, lookbacks, lockouts, holidays, publication times and negative-rate handling can all affect the floating amount. The exact ISDA floating-rate option and version should therefore be treated as contractual data.

The mental model is two questions: which curve generates the future cash flow, and which curve discounts that cash flow back to today? They may be related, but they are not automatically identical.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Projection and discounting are separate valuation functions.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Calling an OIS curve merely the “risk-free forecast curve” and ignoring the collateral agreement that makes its use economically relevant.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Overnight fixing" b="OIS rate">
**Overnight fixing** — observed overnight reference rate for a specific date.

---

**OIS rate** — fixed rate on a swap against compounded/averaged overnight rates over a future period.
</NeverConfuse>
