PSI is calculated as Σ (actual - expected)*ln(actual/expected) over bins of the distribution.  It quantifies how much the data have shifted.  A common practice (from industry “rules of thumb”) is: **PSI &lt;0.10** = little change, **0.10–0.25** = moderate change, **&gt;0.25** = significant change requiring investigation.  For example, if the score distribution has more high-risk borrowers now than at development, PSI will rise.  CSI is a variant used when variables are categorical (like rating transitions).  

Banks use these metrics in monitoring (MRM-10).  If PSI breaches its threshold, it often triggers a model review or recalibration.  Regulators expect banks to track such stability measures for key variables and scores.  (EBA/ECB Pillar 2 expectations and TRIM reviews included similar migration/stability checks.)  

It is important not to treat PSI/CSI breaches as automatic model failure.  They are signals that something changed – perhaps the portfolio mix or economy – and model performance must be reassessed.  A sudden PSI increase could also come from a one-time data issue.  Nonetheless, including PSI/CSI in validation reports is good practice.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Monitor variable and score shifts with PSI/CSI.  Significant drift (e.g. PSI &gt;0.25) should always prompt model review or action.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Relying on fixed PSI thresholds without context.  A PSI just above 0.25 in a benign economic situation may not be as urgent as the same PSI during a crisis.  Also, using too many bins can artificially inflate PSI; ensure bins are consistent.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Population Stability Index (PSI)" b="Characteristic Stability Index (CSI)">
**Population Stability Index (PSI)** — measures shift in the model *score* or numeric input distribution between time periods.  
---  
**Characteristic Stability Index (CSI)** — measures shift in a *categorical* characteristic distribution (e.g. rating classes).  
Both flag distribution shifts, but PSI is for continuous scores/inputs, CSI for discrete categories.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** PSI/CSI are industry-standard techniques. Supervisors generally expect banks to check stability of model inputs and outputs over time.  ECB’s internal-model audits (TRIM) and Pillar 2 guidance include similar stability monitoring.
</Rule>
