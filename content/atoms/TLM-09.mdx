### Core mechanism

For a small yield move:

ΔP / P ≈ −Dmod × Δy

| Component | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| P | current economic value |
| Dmod | modified duration |
| Δy | change in yield |
| ΔP / P | approximate percentage value change |

If modified duration is 5, a +1 percentage-point yield move gives a first-order price change of approximately −5%, before convexity and other effects.

At bank level, asset and liability value sensitivities can be compared, but four limits matter:

1. **Local approximation** — large shocks need curvature/convexity effects.
2. **Curve shape** — one duration number is closest to a parallel-shift intuition.
3. **Behavioural cash flows** — prepayment and NMD assumptions make effective duration scenario-dependent.
4. **Basis** — equal durations against different curves do not create a perfect hedge.

### Flow

```text
Expected cash flows
    ↓
Discount to present value
    ↓
Weight by timing
    ↓
Duration sensitivity
    ↓
Add curve + convexity + basis + options
    ↓
Full EVE scenario result
```

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   EVE perspective · PV foundations
This:       First-order economic-value sensitivity
Downstream: NMD duration · optionality · IRRBB stress and hedging
```

### Formula

```text
ΔP / P ≈ −Dmod × Δy

Dmod ↑ → value becomes more sensitive to a given small yield move.

|Δy| ↑ → linear approximation becomes less reliable as curvature matters more.
```

### Permanent rule

Duration is a sensitivity approximation, not a complete IRRBB model.

### Never confuse [Duration] and [Maturity]

**Duration** — present-value-weighted sensitivity to rates.

---

**Maturity** — contractual or behavioural time until principal/cash flow returns; it does not directly measure price sensitivity.

### Permanent trap

Treating a small aggregate duration gap as proof the balance sheet is hedged. Curve twists, basis and behavioural options can leave large residual EVE risk.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta. Duration mathematics is universal.
