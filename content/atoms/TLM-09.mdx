A fixed-rate loan may permit a borrower to repay principal early. When market rates fall, refinancing becomes attractive, so prepayments may accelerate precisely when the bank would prefer to retain the higher-yielding asset. The bank receives cash back sooner and may have to reinvest it at lower rates. This is **prepayment risk**.

A term deposit can contain the opposite option. If market rates rise and early withdrawal is permitted at an acceptable penalty, the customer may redeem the old deposit and seek a higher rate. The bank loses funding earlier than the contractual maturity assumed in a static schedule. This is **early-redemption risk**.

**Pipeline risk** arises before expected business is fully booked. A bank can commit to lend at a fixed rate while the customer retains a period in which to draw or complete the transaction. If market rates change before funding or hedging is finalised, the economic value of the commitment changes. Forecast origination volumes can also fail to materialise, leaving anticipatory hedges larger than the eventual exposure.

These are forms of optionality because the amount or timing of future cash flows changes with rates or customer choices. Modelling requires an exercise function: how prepayment, withdrawal or drawdown probability changes under different scenarios. Purely historical averages are insufficient when current incentives differ from the historical sample.

The practitioner must also distinguish **automatic options**, whose contractual payoff follows a formula, from **behavioural options**, whose exercise depends on human decisions and operational frictions. Basel and RBI IRRBB frameworks explicitly require embedded options and behavioural assumptions to be captured and stressed. RBI’s forthcoming framework addresses loan prepayments, term-deposit redemption and option-sensitive cash flows.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Where exercise behaviour depends on interest rates, cash-flow timing must be scenario-dependent.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Projecting prepayments or deposit redemptions from a single historical average and holding that rate constant across all interest-rate scenarios.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Prepayment risk" b="Pipeline risk">
**Prepayment risk** — an existing asset returns principal earlier than expected.
---
**Pipeline risk** — expected or committed future business changes exposure before final booking or funding.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Option exercise economics are universal; prescribed regulatory modelling parameters can vary.
</Rule>

<Recall
  q="Why do falling rates often increase prepayment risk on fixed-rate assets?"
  a="Borrowers gain an incentive to refinance and repay higher-rate debt early."
/>

<Recall
  q="What creates pipeline risk?"
  a="A gap between commitment or hedging and the eventual timing, amount or completion of new business."
