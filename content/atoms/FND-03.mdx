Consider a new loan of 100 whose proceeds are credited to the borrower's account at the lending bank. At origination, the bank can record **+100 loan asset** and **+100 deposit liability**. Its balance sheet expands by 100 on each side. Commercial bank lending is therefore capable of creating deposit money; lending need not mechanically wait for a specific existing depositor's money to be reassigned first. The Bank of England describes bank lending as creating deposits in this manner.

That mechanism is frequently stretched into the false claim that a bank needs no funding. Suppose the borrower immediately pays 100 to somebody who banks elsewhere. The deposit created at the lending bank may leave it. Settlement between banks then requires an asset acceptable for interbank settlement, ordinarily central-bank money within the relevant payment system. The originating bank must manage the resulting liquidity position and may replace lost deposit funding with other deposits or wholesale funding.

Lending is also constrained by more than settlement cash. The bank must expect the loan to compensate for funding costs, operating costs, credit losses and the use of scarce balance-sheet resources. Prudential capital requirements constrain how much risk can be supported by loss-absorbing resources, while liquidity requirements constrain vulnerability to cash outflows. Risk appetite and underwriting standards impose further limits. Basel standards therefore contain separate capital and liquidity frameworks rather than treating deposit creation as an unlimited process.

"Loans create deposits" describes an accounting and monetary mechanism. "Banks need funding" describes the continuing requirement to finance assets and survive payment outflows. Both statements can be true at the same time.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Loan origination can create a deposit, but a bank must still fund and settle the balance sheet that remains after customers move money.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Interpreting "banks create deposits by lending" as "banks can lend without capital, liquidity, funding costs or settlement constraints".
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Deposit creation" b="Funding">
**Deposit creation** — the simultaneous balance-sheet creation of a loan asset and deposit liability.
---
**Funding** — the liabilities and equity supporting assets over time, including after newly created deposits are transferred elsewhere.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** The double-entry mechanism is universal; local monetary, capital and liquidity rules determine the constraints around it.
</Rule>
