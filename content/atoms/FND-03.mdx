### Core mechanism

Suppose the bank grants a new loan of 100 and credits the proceeds to the borrower's deposit account:

| Bank entry | Change |
| :--- | ---: |
| Loan asset | +100 |
| Deposit liability | +100 |
| Total assets | +100 |
| Total liabilities | +100 |
| Equity at origination, before fees/costs | No mechanical change |

The key next step is payment. If the borrower pays someone who banks elsewhere, the newly created deposit can leave the originating bank. Interbank settlement then requires the originating bank to transfer settlement assets, normally central-bank money within the relevant payment system. The bank must manage the liquidity and funding position left behind.

### Flow

```text
Loan approved
    ↓
+ Loan asset
+ Deposit liability
    ↓
Borrower spends
    ↓
Same-bank payment → deposits move internally
Other-bank payment → settlement outflow
    ↓
Funding / liquidity position must be managed
```

### Permanent rule

Creating a deposit at loan origination and funding the resulting balance sheet are compatible truths, not competing theories.

### Never confuse [Deposit creation] and [Funding]

**Deposit creation** — the simultaneous increase in a loan asset and deposit liability when lending is booked in this form.

---

**Funding** — the liabilities and equity that support assets over time, including after customers transfer newly created deposits elsewhere.

### Permanent trap

Turning “banks create deposits by lending” into “banks can lend without constraint”. A bank that cannot settle payments, fund its assets, absorb losses, satisfy constraints, or earn an adequate return cannot sustainably expand lending.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** The double-entry mechanism is universal. Payment-system design and prudential calibration differ, but those differences do not alter the accounting logic.
