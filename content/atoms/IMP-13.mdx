A **PD term structure** distributes default risk through time. This matters because ECL depends not only on whether default eventually occurs but also on when it occurs: exposure, recoveries and discount factors can all differ by period.

Let cumulative PD to year 1 be 2%, to year 2 be 5%, and to year 3 be 9%. The unconditional probability of default specifically during year 2 is approximately `5% − 2% = 3%`; during year 3 it is `9% − 5% = 4%`. Those are **marginal** or interval default probabilities on an unconditional basis. A conditional hazard rate asks a slightly different question: given survival to the start of the interval, what is the probability of default during it? The denominator therefore excludes exposures that already defaulted.

The distinctions matter operationally. Adding cumulative PDs across years double-counts earlier defaults because a borrower counted in the year-2 cumulative PD remains included in year 3. ECL engines normally need mutually exclusive default timing probabilities when they sum period-specific loss contributions.

IFRS 9 does not mandate a `PD × LGD × EAD` implementation, but where that architecture is used, lifetime PD must represent default risk over the relevant lifetime and be consistent with forward-looking information. IFRS 9 also warns that changes in 12-month default risk are only an approximation for changes in lifetime risk when the circumstances make that approximation reasonable; concentrated risk beyond twelve months can make the shortcut inappropriate.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Default probabilities used across periods must be mutually consistent so that an obligor cannot be counted as newly defaulting more than once.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Summing one-year cumulative PD figures to construct lifetime PD overstates default risk by repeatedly counting defaults already included in earlier horizons.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Cumulative PD" b="Marginal PD">
**Cumulative PD** — probability of default at any time from the starting date up to a stated horizon.

---
**Marginal PD** — probability allocated to a particular future interval rather than all preceding intervals combined.
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="IN">
— Current commercial-bank IRACP does not require lifetime PD term structures. RBI’s ECL Directions effective April 1, 2027 require 12-month PD for Stage 1 and lifetime PD for Stage 2 under a PD–LGD approach, and prohibit mechanically extending Stage 1 PD parameters without adjustment for lifetime and higher-risk characteristics. [RBI 2026 ECL Directions, paragraphs 45–46]
</Delta>

<Delta j="UK">
— IFRS 9 governs the measurement objective but does not prescribe one PD-term-structure methodology. [IFRS 9 5.5 and B5.5]
</Delta>

<Delta j="EU">
— EU-adopted IFRS 9 has the same accounting position; EBA supervisory work addresses sound ECL methodology. [Regulation (EU) 2023/1803; EBA/GL/2017/06]
</Delta>

<Delta j="US">
— CECL may use PD/LGD methods but does not require them; any selected method must estimate lifetime expected collectability consistently with Topic 326. [ASC 326; Interagency CECL FAQ]
</Delta>

  q="Why cannot cumulative PDs simply be added across years?"
  a="Because later cumulative PDs already include defaults counted in earlier years."
