RBI’s 2025 Commercial Banks – Prudential Norms on Capital Adequacy Directions prescribe an ongoing minimum CET1 ratio of 5.5% of RWA, minimum Tier 1 of 7% and minimum total capital of 9%, plus a 2.5% CET1 capital conservation buffer and other applicable requirements. These are domestic binding minima; Basel’s 4.5%, 6% and 8% global minima must not be substituted for them.

The leverage-ratio rule is also explicitly higher than the Basel 3% baseline: RBI prescribes **4% for a Domestic Systemically Important Bank and 3.5% for other banks**.

For banking-book credit risk, RBI issued final *Commercial Banks – Capital Charge for Credit Risk – Standardised Approach Directions, 2026* on 27 April 2026, effective **1 April 2027**. The Directions state that banking-book exposures are risk weighted using prescribed categories or eligible external ratings as applicable, and that RWA is the product of the standardised risk weight and exposure amount. **IRB must not be taught as the Indian domestic commercial-bank credit-capital regime.**

Accounting is a separate issue. RBI’s 22 March 2019 circular deferred Ind AS implementation for scheduled commercial banks until further notice. Indian scheduled commercial banks therefore must **not** be described as being on Ind AS 109 merely because expected-loss concepts arise in banking regulation. Relevant NBFCs required to implement Ind AS are subject to RBI guidance interacting with Ind AS 109, which is a different institutional perimeter.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
For an Indian commercial bank, start with RBI’s domestic directions: Basel minima, IRB teaching or Ind AS 109 cannot be substituted for the applicable RBI banking rules.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Writing “India = Ind AS 109 + IRB” commits two distinct errors: scheduled commercial banks have not implemented Ind AS under the deferred timetable, and RBI’s final 2027 credit-capital framework selects the Standardised Approach.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="RBI Standardised Approach" b="Ind AS 109">
