  Common SQL data quality checks ensure tables have no missing (NULL) values in
  mandatory fields, no unexpected duplicates, and maintain referential
  integrity. For **completeness**, verify required columns have no NULLs:
  `SELECT COUNT(*) FROM Loans WHERE AccountID IS NULL;`. For **duplicates**,
  check for duplicate keys (e.g. `SELECT LoanID, COUNT(*) FROM Loans GROUP BY
  LoanID HAVING COUNT(*)>1;`). For **referential integrity**, ensure every
  foreign key matches a primary key: `SELECT fk.col FROM ChildTable fk LEFT JOIN
  ParentTable pk ON fk.col=pk.col WHERE pk.col IS NULL;` (rows returned have
  broken links).

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Ensure primary/unique keys have no duplicates (they enforce uniqueness) and foreign keys match existing parent rows.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Mistaking legitimate multiple entries (e.g. multiple payments per loan) for duplicates. Always confirm the correct key: a *primary key* versus a *natural unique*.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="PRIMARY KEY" b="UNIQUE constraint/index">
**PRIMARY KEY** — key defined in schema, cannot have duplicates.
---
**UNIQUE constraint/index** — also enforces uniqueness, but (unlike primary key) usually allows one NULL.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Data quality principles (completeness, duplicates, referential checks) are universal.
</Rule>

<Recall
  q="How to check for missing mandatory fields?"
  a="Use `WHERE mandatory_col IS NULL`."
/>

<Recall
  q="How to find orphaned foreign keys?"
  a="LEFT JOIN child to parent, filter where parent key is NULL."
/>
