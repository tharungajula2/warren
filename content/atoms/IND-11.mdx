### Core mechanism

| Role | What it does | What it does not become |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Regulated Entity (RE) | Sanctions, funds or owns the regulated credit exposure | It does not shed responsibility by outsourcing |
| Lending Service Provider (LSP) | Performs one or more customer acquisition, underwriting-support, servicing or recovery functions under contract | It is not automatically the lender |
| Digital Lending App (DLA) | Customer-facing digital interface operated by or for an RE/LSP | The app itself is not the regulatory creditor |

The legal and prudential loan remains with the RE. RBI therefore focuses heavily on outsourcing accountability, disclosure of the RE, permitted data collection and customer treatment.

An LSP can influence underwriting or servicing operationally, but the RE must perform due diligence on the LSP and remains responsible for compliance. A technology layer cannot be used to create an unregulated pass-through lender.

### Permanent rule

The regulated lender remains responsible for acts performed through its LSP or DLA within the lending arrangement.

### Never confuse [LSP] and [Lender]

**LSP** — a service provider acting for a regulated lender under a contractual arrangement.

---

**Lender** — the regulated entity that owns or extends the credit under the applicable RBI perimeter.

### Permanent trap

Assuming a fintech brand shown to the customer is necessarily the balance-sheet lender. The underlying RE must be identified.

### Jurisdiction variants

#### Banks and NBFCs
Both can use LSPs and DLAs, but their underlying prudential treatment remains entity-specific.

#### Non-regulated technology firms
They may act as service providers where permitted, but they do not obtain lending powers merely by operating an app.
