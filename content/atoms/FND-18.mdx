### Core mechanism

| Facility component | What it means |
| :--- | :--- |
| Drawn amount | Amount already advanced or otherwise outstanding |
| Undrawn commitment | Contractual capacity not yet drawn |
| Total commitment | Drawn plus undrawn contractual amount |
| Exposure measure | Amount treated as at risk under the stated purpose and measurement date |

### Formula

```text
Total commitment = Drawn + Undrawn
Total commitment = contractual facility amount in this simple identity
Drawn = amount already used by the customer
Undrawn = committed amount still available to draw

The identity does not mean the full undrawn amount automatically equals exposure under every framework. The governing exposure methodology determines how undrawn commitments are treated and at what horizon.

One-line reminder only: eventual loss severity also depends on what is recovered; recovery and loss-severity mechanics belong to the credit-risk pack.
```

### Flow

```text
Contract / position
    ↓
Drawn amount + possible undrawn component
    ↓
Choose measurement date / horizon
    ↓
Apply governing exposure definition
    ↓
Exposure amount
```

### Permanent rule

Exposure is an input to loss measurement. It is never automatically equal to realised or expected loss.

### Never confuse [Exposure] and [Loss]

**Exposure** — amount or position subject to the specified risk event.

---

**Loss** — adverse outcome after the event under the relevant loss definition; foundations does not own the downstream severity mechanics.

### Never confuse [Drawn amount] and [Total commitment]

**Drawn amount** — already used.

---

**Total commitment** — drawn plus contractual undrawn capacity; downstream methods decide how much undrawn capacity enters a risk exposure measure.

### Permanent trap

Using current outstanding principal as the complete exposure for a revolving or committed facility without checking whether further drawings can occur before the measurement event.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Specific regulatory exposure definitions and conversion factors can differ, but the distinction between amount at risk, current drawings, and contractual undrawn capacity is universal.
