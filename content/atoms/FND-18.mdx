Take a modelled loss variable \(L\). A 99th-percentile loss quantile is a threshold such that roughly 99% of modelled losses lie at or below it and roughly 1% lie above it, subject to the precise quantile definition and whether the distribution is continuous or discrete. NIST illustrates percentiles through this cumulative-probability interpretation.

This is the foundation of **Value at Risk (VaR)**-type thinking: a high loss quantile gives a boundary associated with a selected confidence level. But the quantile says little by itself about the severity of losses once that boundary has been breached.

Consider two hypothetical loss distributions that both have a 99% quantile of 10. In one, the worst remaining 1% clusters around 11. In another, the remaining 1% includes losses of 100 or more. Their 99% quantiles can look similar while their tail severities are radically different.

**Tail risk** concerns these extreme outcomes and their distribution. A heavier loss tail places relatively more probability or severity in extreme losses than a lighter-tailed alternative. Measures focusing on loss conditional on being beyond a quantile can therefore provide information that the quantile alone does not.

A quantile also depends on horizon and model. A one-day 99% loss quantile and a one-year 99% loss quantile answer different questions. Changing the probability distribution, dependence assumptions or data window can change the estimated threshold.

The phrase "99% confidence" is frequently miscommunicated as "there is only a 1% chance of any loss". That is wrong. It means approximately 1% of modelled outcomes exceed the stated loss threshold, not that the other 99% have zero loss.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
A quantile identifies a distribution threshold; it does not cap outcomes beyond the threshold.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Describing a 99% VaR-type number as "the most that can be lost with 99% certainty".
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Quantile" b="Tail risk">
**Quantile** — a threshold associated with a cumulative probability level.
---
**Tail risk** — the probability and severity of extreme outcomes beyond a chosen region; a single quantile does not describe the whole tail.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Quantiles and probability tails are mathematical concepts independent of jurisdiction.
</Rule>
