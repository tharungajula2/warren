### Core mechanism

Loan rate = benchmark + contractual spread

Benchmark = the reference rate specified by the applicable RBI framework or lender methodology.

Spread = borrower/product margin added to the benchmark under the lender's approved pricing policy.

| Question | Regulatory control |
| :--- | :--- |
| What benchmark is permitted? | Depends on entity and product |
| Can spread change arbitrarily? | No; changes must follow the permitted contractual and policy framework |
| What happens at reset? | Rate or instalment/tenor implications must be handled under applicable borrower-protection rules |
| Can prepayment attract charges? | Treatment depends on loan type, borrower class and current RBI directions |

A draft Reserve Bank of India (Interest Rates on Loans and Advances) Directions, 2026 consultation was issued in August 2026. As at 31 August 2026 it is a proposal, not a rule in force. This atom therefore teaches the extant entity/product framework and does not treat the consultation text as implemented law.

### Permanent rule

A floating-rate loan must follow the benchmark/reset mechanics that govern that product; "floating" does not mean the lender may reprice without a defined rule.

### Permanent trap

Teaching the August 2026 consultation as already effective. Its proposed consolidation is future-facing as at 31 August 2026.

### Jurisdiction variants

#### Scheduled commercial banks
External-benchmark or other bank-specific benchmark requirements apply by product class.

#### NBFCs and HFCs
Pricing remains subject to their fair-practice and board-approved pricing framework; bank benchmark rules should not be imported automatically.
