Upon successful resolution of an NPA (e.g. borrower makes good on arrears or loan is restructured), banks may upgrade the asset to Standard. RBI’s 2015 clarification mandates a “cooling period”: for a restructured account to be classified as Standard again, repayments must meet conditions (like no defaults for two quarters).  Without that probation period, an upgrade is not allowed. This rule was highlighted by RBI when it cautioned banks against prematurely upgrading SME accounts in 2014-15.

**Write-offs** occur when a loan is deemed unrecoverable. RBI allows two types: *prudential write-off* (for banks, as per regulatory guidelines) and *technical write-off* (often a bookkeeping exercise after 2 years of NPA, for income recognition, but banks continue recovery efforts). After prudential write-off, a bank can still pursue the borrower or recover via SARFAESI/IBC. A write-off does not necessarily extinguish the debt legally (banks still have claim on assets). RBI’s rules require certain conditions (security realization attempts, etc.) before prudential write-off. Many NBFCs (like some housing finance) have their own write-off timelines but must adhere to RBI’s broad norms.

Practically, write-off reduces a bank’s taxable income (since provisions were already made) but is a regulatory decision to clean balance sheets. The outstanding balance after write-off may be reported as ‘Outstanding written-off assets’ for supervisor attention. Only after full write-off can a new loan be given to that borrower under banking laws.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
An NPA cannot be downgraded to Standard until RBI’s post-default performance period is satisfied. Banks may only write off loans after exhausting all recovery options and meeting prudential criteria.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Improper early upgrade. A common error is reclassifying a restructured NPA as Standard immediately. RBI’s circular forces a waiting period – skipping it breaks rules. Another mistake is treating a write-off as forgiveness: even after write-off, banks pursue the borrower.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Upgrade** — reclassification of an NPA to Standard after borrower cures. Requires RBI-mandated wait period (e.g. no default for 2 quarters).
**Write-off** — formally removing the loan from active books (after full provision); often a tactical cleanup, but the borrower’s liability remains.
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="IN">
— RBI’s detailed upgrade criteria are India-specific. Many jurisdictions similarly prevent immediate NPA cures, but RBI’s exact timelines (e.g. 2Q after restructure) are local.
</Delta>
