Terrorist financing (TF) involves raising or moving money to fund terrorist activities. Crucially, TF funds can come from both illegal and completely legitimate sources (donations, charities).  For example, a charity donation may be diverted to extremist groups; the donor’s money wasn’t criminally obtained, so “fund placement” isn’t needed, only disguise of the destination.  FATF and the Egmont Group stress that terrorists typically aren’t as concerned with hiding *where* money came from, but they do hide where it’s going and what it will buy.

For banks, this means focusing on transaction patterns that suggest support for terrorism: e.g. frequent small payments to charities or NGOs based in conflict zones, purchases of military gear or specific supplies, or international transfers to entities with extremist ties.  Unlike money laundering, the key risk factors include connections to known extremist groups, donations to shell charities, or unexplained funding of persons on watchlists.  Financial institutions must report any transaction suspected to finance terrorism via Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs/STRs) just as for laundering.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Apply enhanced scrutiny to cross-border transfers, new high-risk charities, or foreign nationals sending money to known conflict regions. If funds flow towards suspects of terrorism, report immediately.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Ignoring a transaction just because the money source seems legitimate (e.g. a salary or donation), forgetting that TF risk is about *destination and use*, not origin.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Terrorist financing" b="Money laundering">
**Terrorist financing** — providing funds (legal or illegal) to support terrorism.
---
**Money laundering** — disguising illicit origins of criminal proceeds. They get mixed up because both are predicate offences requiring detection, but TF can involve fully legal funds, while ML always involves criminal proceeds.
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="IN">
— PMLA covers TF financing; banks must report suspicious TF under FIU-IND guidelines (as STR).
</Delta>

<Delta j="UK">
— Terrorism financing is criminal under Terrorism Act 2000; UK firms file SARs to NCA for TF-related activity.
</Delta>

<Delta j="EU">
— EU AMLD obliges Member States to criminalize terrorist financing (including from lawful sources); STRs must include TF suspicions.
</Delta>

<Delta j="US">
— Under U.S. law (18 U.S.C. § 2339A/B), giving material support or funds to terrorists (even from legal sources) is illegal; banks file SARs for TF (covering both domestic and foreign terrorists).
</Delta>
