Unlike credit or market risk, operational risk is hard to quantify with statistical models. The main issue is the loss distribution: it tends to be *fat-tailed*, meaning a handful of enormous losses (think $100m+ fines or frauds) dominate the worst-case quantiles.  Most of the time losses are small or zero, so the tail is driven by outliers.  This irregular, heavy-tail pattern means that conventional models (which assume “normal” or thin-tailed losses) understate risk.  You often see statements like “99.9% VaR is dominated by just a few big events”.

Another challenge is **sparse data**. Firms may have only a few decades of internal loss history and any particular risk type (e.g. cyber fraud) might have zero or very few data points.  Basel’s AMA recognized this by requiring scenario analysis and external data supplements, but regulators later noted that these inputs can be subjective.  In practice, even global consortia like ORX (for external loss data) still yield few non-zero data because severe events are rare.

These factors create capital volatility and uncertainty.  For example, risk experts have shown that a small change in a tail parameter can double the capital estimate. This instability was a key reason Basel ditched advanced models.  In summary, operational loss modelling requires special care with tail-fitting, often relying on conservative, simple approaches (or on SMA) to avoid underestimation.

### Permanent rule

Always model operational losses as heavy-tailed.  Focus on tail estimation (e.g. extreme quantiles) and acknowledge data scarcity.  Use all available data (internal + external + scenario inputs) to capture possible large losses.

### Permanent trap

Do not assume a normal or light-tailed loss distribution.  Underestimating tail risk or over-relying on few data points (or ignoring large single events) can lead to severe capital undercoverage.

### Never confuse

**Tail of distribution** vs **bulk data**.  The bulk (small losses) should not be mistaken for the tail. Operational risk capital depends on the tail.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Heavy tails and sparse data are universal challenges.  All banks must use conservative modeling (or the standardized multiplier approach) to account for them.
