**Regulation** establishes binding or otherwise formally applicable requirements under a legal mandate. In banking these can concern capital, liquidity, governance, risk management, permissible activities, reporting or customer treatment. **Supervision** is the continuing process of assessing institutions against applicable requirements and supervisory objectives, including through data, meetings, inspections, examinations and judgement.

The two functions reinforce each other. A capital rule might specify a minimum ratio; supervision asks whether the bank calculates it correctly, manages the underlying risks, maintains credible controls and can remain resilient under stress. The Federal Reserve describes supervision as evaluating institutions' financial condition, governance, controls and ability to manage risks, complementing the regulatory framework.

A regulator or supervisor can also possess **authorisation or licensing** powers that determine whether an institution may conduct regulated business, and **enforcement** powers to require remediation or impose consequences where legal conditions are met. Exact powers differ by statute and agency. The OCC, for example, charters, regulates and supervises national banks within its remit and conducts examinations focused on safety, soundness and compliance.

Not every body called a "regulator" performs every function. The EU's EBA develops the Single Rulebook and promotes supervisory convergence, while institution-level supervision involves competent authorities under the EU architecture. Accounting standard-setters are different again: they prescribe financial-reporting standards rather than supervising bank safety and soundness.

### Permanent rule

Regulation defines applicable requirements; supervision assesses institutions and their risk management against law, rules and supervisory objectives.

### Permanent trap

Assuming a bank is safe because it met a reported minimum ratio on one date, while ignoring supervisory assessment of governance, controls, concentrations and risk management.

### Never confuse

**Regulation** — the body of legally or formally applicable requirements made under the relevant mandate.
---
**Supervision** — ongoing assessment of institutions and their practices; it tests reality rather than merely publishing rules.

### Jurisdiction variants

**IN** — RBI exercises statutory regulation and supervision of banks under powers including those derived from the Banking Regulation Act, with on-site and off-site supervisory mechanisms. [RBI Department of Supervision; Banking Regulation Act framework]
**UK** — The PRA is responsible for prudential regulation and supervision of firms within its remit, focused on safety and soundness and related statutory objectives; the FCA separately carries major conduct responsibilities. [Bank of England/PRA prudential regulation; FCA mandate]
**EU** — The EBA develops and maintains the Single Rulebook and supports supervisory convergence; it should not be mistaken for the direct supervisor of every EU bank. Institution-level supervisory responsibility depends on the EU and national competent-authority architecture. [EBA Single Rulebook; EBA Missions and Tasks]
**US** — Banking supervision is multi-agency. The Federal Reserve supervises institutions within its statutory remit, while the OCC charters, regulates and supervises national banks and federal savings associations. [Federal Reserve supervision framework; OCC mandate]
