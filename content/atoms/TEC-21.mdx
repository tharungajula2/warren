Always be careful: operations skip `NaN` by default (e.g. `sum()` treats `NaN` as zero, but `mean()` ignores it). Deciding to drop or fill depends on context. For instance, missing credit score might be imputed or flagged.

### Permanent rule

Detect missing explicitly with `isna()`. Always decide on a strategy: either drop records or fill them (imputation).

### Permanent trap

Mixing `None` and `NaN`: some pandas methods distinguish them, but generally `isna()` catches both. Beware that `df['col'] == np.nan` does not work (use `isna`).

### Never confuse

**NaN** — pandas float “Not a Number” for missing.

---

**Empty string** — a valid string, not missing. `isna()` returns False for `''`.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** pandas missing-value handling is the same everywhere.
