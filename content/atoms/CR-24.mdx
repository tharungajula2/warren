**Single-name concentration** occurs when one obligor represents a material share of the bank's exposure or capital capacity. **Connected-group concentration** extends the same logic to entities whose risks are linked through control or economic dependency.

**Sector concentration** occurs when many legally independent obligors depend on the same industry conditions. A portfolio with 1,000 borrowers can still be highly concentrated if almost all rely on the same commodity price, property market or economic activity.

Basel's supervisory framework explicitly treats concentrations to individual counterparties or connected groups, sectors, regions and other common risk factors as concentration risk.

Concentration matters because loss capacity is nonlinear. Ten equal exposures to ten genuinely independent obligors provide more diversification than one exposure of the same total size to a single name. Likewise, 100 companies whose revenues fail together are less diversified than their legal-entity count suggests.

A concentration metric must therefore declare its denominator and grouping rule. Exposure can be measured against total portfolio exposure, eligible capital or a specific subportfolio. Sector taxonomies must be stable enough that moving a borrower between labels does not become a way to manage the metric cosmetically.

Large-exposure regulation places hard prudential constraints on some single-name and connected-group concentrations. Internal risk appetite normally goes further by addressing sectors and common risk factors that may remain below formal large-exposure limits.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Count common sources of failure, not merely legal names.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
A portfolio can appear diversified after splitting one economic group across subsidiaries or one industry across several narrow sector codes.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Single-name concentration** — excessive dependence on one obligor or connected risk.

**Sector concentration** — excessive dependence on a shared industry or economic factor across multiple names.
</NeverConfuse>

<Delta j="IN">
— RBI's Large Exposures Framework imposes limits and aggregation rules for single counterparties and groups of connected counterparties; internal concentration management must also address risks not exhausted by those legal limits. [RBI Commercial Banks Large Exposures Framework Directions 2025]
</Delta>

<Delta j="UK">

<Delta j="EU">
— CRR large-exposure provisions aggregate connected clients and impose exposure constraints, while broader concentration risk is addressed through supervisory risk management. [CRR Articles 387–395]

<Delta j="US">

</Delta>
