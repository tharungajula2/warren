### Core mechanism

| Risk source | Example | Diversification effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Idiosyncratic | Fraud, plant failure, loss of one customer | Falls as many independent names are added |
| Sector systematic | Commodity-price collapse, industry disruption | Persists across borrowers in the same sector |
| Macro systematic | Recession, unemployment, rate shock | Can affect many sectors and borrowers together |
| Recovery dependence | Property prices fall during mortgage defaults | Defaults and LGDs can worsen together |
| Utilisation dependence | Revolvers draw in stress | Defaults and EAD can worsen together |

Correlation does not require equal PDs. A 1% PD firm and a 5% PD firm can still become riskier at the same time because both respond to recession, rates or a common customer base.

### Flow

```text
Idiosyncratic shock → One / few obligors → Diversification impact ↓

Systematic shock → Many obligors deteriorate together → Correlated defaults
    ↓
Clustered losses → Portfolio tail ↑ → Capital pressure ↑
```

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Individual PDs · Common economic factors · Sector/geography links
This:       Common shock → joint deterioration/default → clustered losses
Downstream: Portfolio loss tail · Stress testing · Diversification benefit · Capital
```

### Formula

```text
For default indicators `D₁` and `D₂`, positive dependence means:

`P(D₁ = 1 and D₂ = 1) &gt; P(D₁ = 1) × P(D₂ = 1)`

in the simple comparison to independence.

Positive dependence ↑ → probability of clustered losses ↑ → tail loss ↑, all else equal.
```

### Permanent rule

Adding exposures diversifies risk only to the extent that their failure drivers are not the same.

### Never confuse [Correlation] and [Concentration]

**Correlation** — dependence between credit outcomes.

---

**Concentration** — disproportionate exposure to names or common drivers; concentration magnifies the consequence of dependence but is not the same concept.

### Permanent trap

Assuming legal independence implies default independence systematically understates loss clustering during common economic stress.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Correlation and systematic risk are economic and statistical properties; prudential frameworks differ in how dependence is parameterised, stressed or capitalised.
