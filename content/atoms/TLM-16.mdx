### Core mechanism

| Risk | Question | Typical stress |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Funding liquidity | Can we raise the cash/currency when due? | deposit runoff, failed rollover, draws, collateral calls |
| Market liquidity | Can we sell/repo the asset at tolerable value and size? | wide spreads, low depth, higher haircuts |

### Flow

```text
Funding outflow ↑
    ↓
Need to borrow or monetise assets ↑
    ↓
Market depth ↓ / haircut ↑
    ↓
Cash raised per asset ↓ + realised loss ↑
    ↓
Capital/confidence ↓
    ↓
Further funding outflow ↑

This loop is why “the asset has a quoted price” is not enough. What matters is executable monetisation size, timing, haircut and settlement under stress.
```

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Solvent-bank cash failure · structural FX
This:       Two interacting liquidity mechanisms
Downstream: Liquidity inventory · cash-flow ladders · stress · collateral
```

### Permanent rule

Funding liquidity is about obtaining cash on time; market liquidity is about converting positions into cash at tolerable value.

### Never confuse [Funding liquidity risk] and [Market liquidity risk]

**Funding liquidity risk** — inability to meet expected or unexpected cash and collateral needs efficiently.

---

**Market liquidity risk** — inability to offset or monetise a position near prevailing value because market depth or functioning is inadequate.

### Permanent trap

Calling a security liquid because it traded yesterday. A stress buffer must work at the size, speed and market conditions in which the bank expects to use it.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta. Regulatory definitions and monitoring templates differ, but the economic distinction is universal.
