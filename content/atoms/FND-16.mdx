### Core mechanism



### Formula

```text
Var(X) = E[(X - mu)^2]
Var(X) = variance of X
X = random variable
mu = mean of X
E[ ] = expected value operator

SD(X) = square root of Var(X)
SD(X) = standard deviation of X

Variance has squared units; standard deviation returns to the original units. In finance, “volatility” usually means a standard-deviation measure over a stated variable and horizon.
```

### Flow

```text
Possible outcomes
    ↓
Expected value / mean
    ↓
Deviation from mean
    ↓

    ↓

    ↓

    ↓

```

### Permanent rule

A volatility number is incomplete without the underlying variable and its time horizon.

### Never confuse [Variance] and [Standard deviation]

**Variance** — squared dispersion around the mean.

---

**Standard deviation** — square root of variance, restoring the variable's units.

### Never confuse [Volatility] and [Downside loss]

**Volatility** — dispersion in both directions under the chosen measure.

---

**Downside loss** — adverse outcome; high positive outcomes can raise volatility without being losses.

### Permanent trap

Comparing daily and annual volatility as if they were the same object merely because both are percentages.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** These statistical relationships do not vary by jurisdiction.

<Recall
  q="Why is standard deviation easier to interpret than variance?"
  a="It returns to the original units of the variable."
/>

<Recall
  q="Does volatility measure only downside?"
  a="No. Standard deviation counts dispersion on both sides of the mean."
/>

<Recall
  q="Can two distributions have equal volatility but different tail risk?"
  a="Yes. The same dispersion measure can coexist with very different extreme-outcome shapes."
/>
