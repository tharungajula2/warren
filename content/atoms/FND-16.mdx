A **probability** is a number between 0 and 1 representing the likelihood assigned to an event under a specified probability model. A probability of 0.01 corresponds to 1%; a probability of 1 corresponds to certainty within that model.

An **expected value** is a probability-weighted average of possible numerical outcomes. For a discrete random variable \(X\) with outcomes \(x_i\) and probabilities \(p_i\):

\[
E[X]=\sum_i p_i x_i
\]

Suppose a position loses 100 with probability 1% and loses zero with probability 99%. Expected loss is:

\[
0.01\times100+0.99\times0=1
\]

Nothing in that calculation predicts that a realised loss will equal 1. In an individual period, the simplified position loses either 0 or 100. The value 1 describes the probability-weighted mean across the modelled distribution or across repeated comparable situations.

That is why probability and expected loss must carry units correctly. Probability is dimensionless: 1%, 5%, 20%. Expected loss is measured in currency or another loss unit. Multiplying a probability by a loss amount can contribute to expected loss; the probability itself is never the amount of loss.

Expected value is also sensitive to the modelled outcome set and assigned probabilities. Missing a severe state or mis-estimating its probability changes the answer. A mathematically correct weighted average does not rescue incorrect assumptions.

This distinction forms the base for credit-risk reasoning. Default probability describes likelihood. Exposure describes what is at risk. Loss severity describes what portion is lost if the event occurs. Combining such quantities can create an expected-loss measure, but each answers a different question.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Probability is a likelihood; expected value is a probability-weighted amount.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Reporting "1% expected loss" when the calculation actually produced a 1% probability of a monetary loss event.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Probability" b="Expected value">
**Probability** — likelihood of an event under the stated model.
---
**Expected value** — the probability-weighted average amount across possible outcomes.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Probability axioms and expected-value mathematics do not depend on jurisdiction.
</Rule>
