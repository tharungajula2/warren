The DRC addresses the gap that credit spread shocks (in the sensitivities method) do not capture actual default.  For instruments like bonds or CDS in the trading book, banks must calculate an additional capital charge representing a sudden default loss.  The specifics follow a default table of risk weights.  

The RRAO is for “any instruments bearing residual risk” beyond delta/vega/curvature.  Typical examples: exotic options whose risk isn’t well represented by the buckets, correlation-trading positions, or multi-underlying products.  FRTB requires a fixed charge or stress scenario calculation for these.  Together, SA capital = SB method (sensitivities) OR (DRC+RRAO), whichever is higher.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Always add DRC for credit default risk and RRAO for remaining exposures to the sensitivities-based capital.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Treating the sensitivities formula as sufficient and ignoring DRC or RRAO, which can underestimate capital for credit and exotic positions.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Default Risk Charge** — covers jump-to-default credit losses; **Residual Risk Add-On** — covers other risks not in SBM (like exotic instrument exposures).
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Both DRC and RRAO are defined by Basel and apply universally in SA.
</Rule>
