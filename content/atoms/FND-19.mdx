Return to a simple position that loses 100 with 1% probability and zero otherwise. Its expected loss is 1. But no actual outcome in that example equals 1: realised loss is either 0 or 100. The gap between the average and adverse realisations is the intuition behind **unexpected loss**.

Expected loss can be written generically as the probability-weighted mean of possible losses:

\[
EL=E[L]
\]

In simple credit-risk shorthand, later frameworks may approximate expected loss using components for probability of default, exposure and loss severity. That decomposition does not change the fundamental definition: expected loss is an average over a loss distribution.

Unexpected loss is not ordinarily a second amount that simply gets added to every loan's invoice. It describes variability around expected outcomes, particularly adverse states for which the institution needs resilience. The Basel credit-risk framework explicitly states that risk-weighted assets under its internal-ratings-based architecture are designed to address **unexpected losses**, while expected-loss amounts and provisions receive separate treatment.

This makes the distinction foundational to banking. Expected losses should influence economics such as pricing and loss recognition under the applicable framework. Capital exists to provide loss-absorbing capacity when actual losses are worse than expected, though precise prudential treatment is more complex than "provisions equal EL and capital equals UL" in every circumstance.

The distinction also prevents a dangerous error in risk reporting: a portfolio can have a modest expected loss but a severe tail. Low average loss does not imply low capital need, because concentration and dependence can make rare outcomes extremely damaging.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Expected loss describes the centre of a loss distribution; resilience must also address the possibility that realised losses are materially worse.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Subtracting expected loss from a high quantile and treating the result as a universally defined accounting or regulatory "unexpected loss" number without checking the framework.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Expected loss" b="Unexpected loss">
**Expected loss** — probability-weighted average loss.
---
**Unexpected loss** — adverse variation around expected loss, especially the severe outcomes for which additional resilience is required.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** The statistical distinction is universal; national prudential and accounting frameworks determine exactly how provisions and capital interact with it.
</Rule>
