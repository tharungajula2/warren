### Core mechanism

Return to the two-state example: loss 100 with 1% probability, otherwise zero. Expected loss is 1, but the adverse realised state is 100. The difference between the distribution's average and materially worse outcomes creates the intuition for unexpected loss.

### Formula

```text
EL = E[L]
EL = expected loss
L = loss variable
E[L] = probability-weighted mean of the modelled loss distribution

There is no single universal foundation formula for “unexpected loss”. Different frameworks express adverse variation through standard deviation, quantiles, stress losses, capital models, or other constructs.
```

### Flow

```text
Loss distribution
    ↓
Probability-weighted centre
    ↓
Expected loss
    ↓
Adverse variation / tail outcomes
    ↓
Unexpected loss intuition
    ↓
Need for resilience beyond the average
```

### Permanent rule

Planning for the average is not resilience. Risk management must also consider outcomes materially worse than the expected value.

### Never confuse [Expected loss] and [Most likely loss]

**Expected loss** — probability-weighted mean.

---

**Most likely loss** — mode or most probable outcome; it can differ sharply from the mean.

### Never confuse [Unexpected loss] and [One universal formula]

```text
**Unexpected loss** — concept of adverse variation around expected loss.

---
**A specific UL metric** — a framework-dependent quantitative construction.
```

### Permanent trap

Taking a high loss quantile, subtracting expected loss, and treating the result as a universally defined “unexpected loss” measure without checking the framework.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** The statistical distinction is universal. Prudential and accounting frameworks decide how expected losses, provisions, and capital interact.

<Recall
  q="Can expected loss be low while tail loss is severe?"
  a="Yes. A low probability-weighted mean can coexist with rare but very large losses."
/>

<Recall
  q="Is expected loss necessarily the most likely realised loss?"
  a="No. The mean of a distribution need not be one of its most likely realised outcomes."
/>

<Recall
  q="Does “unexpected loss” have one universal calculation?"
  a="No. Frameworks can represent adverse variation using different statistical or prudential methods."
