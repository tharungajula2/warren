### Core mechanism

```text
Model-based / nominated-approach RWA
                  versus
Standardised-approach RWA × applicable floor percentage
                  ↓
Higher amount
                  ↓
RWA used for capital requirements and buffers
```

Example:

Model-based RWA = 60
Standardised RWA = 100
Floor percentage = 72.5%
Floor amount = 72.5
Binding RWA = 72.5

If model-based RWA were 80, the floor would not bind.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Standardised RWA · modelled RWA · input constraints
This:       Aggregate model backstop
Downstream: Pillar 1 ratios · buffers · jurisdiction implementation
```

### Formula

```text
Floored RWA = max(model-based RWA, floor percentage × standardised RWA)
```

### Permanent rule

The floor percentage multiplies the prescribed standardised RWA benchmark, not each IRB exposure.

### Never confuse [Output floor] and [Risk weight]

**Output floor** — aggregate lower bound on model-based RWA.

---

**Risk weight** — exposure-level or formula-derived scaling factor used within an RWA methodology.

### Permanent trap

Applying 72.5% to every modelled exposure discards the aggregate comparison the floor is designed to perform.

### Jurisdiction variants

#### UK (PRA)
**As at August 2026:** final rules commence **1 January 2027**. The transitional multiplier is scheduled at **60% in 2027**, **65% in 2028**, **70% in 2029**, reaching **72.5% from 1 January 2030**. These are future phase-in steps under final rules, not proposals.

#### EU
**As at August 2026:** CRR3 is in force. The floor is **55% during 2026**, then **60% in 2027**, **65% in 2028**, **70% in 2029**, and **72.5% from 1 January 2030**.

#### US
**As at August 2026:** the 19 March 2026 large-bank proposal is not finalised. Do not teach a Basel output floor as an implemented US rule.

#### IN
**As at August 2026:** RBI has selected the Standardised Approach for the covered commercial-bank credit-risk framework effective 1 April 2027; an IRB-versus-SA credit output-floor mechanism is therefore not the domestic credit-risk architecture.
