### Core mechanism

| Layer | What it contributes | Can satisfy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| CET1 | Highest-quality, permanent going-concern loss absorption | CET1, Tier 1 and Total Capital requirements |
| AT1 | Additional going-concern capacity meeting stricter permanence and loss-absorption rules than ordinary debt | Tier 1 and Total Capital, not CET1 |
| Tier 2 | Lower-quality subordinated gone-concern capacity | Total Capital only |

```text
CET1
  + AT1
  = Tier 1
      + Tier 2
      = Total Capital
```

Basel global minimum ratios are themselves nested: 4.5% CET1, 6% Tier 1 and 8% Total Capital, before buffers and any jurisdictional or firm-specific additions. A bank therefore does not add 4.5 + 6 + 8 and hold 18.5% merely because three ratios are reported.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Going-concern versus gone-concern absorption
This:       Capital quality hierarchy
Downstream: CET1 eligibility · AT1/Tier 2 eligibility · minimum ratios
```

### Formula

```text
Tier 1 = CET1 + AT1

Total Capital = CET1 + AT1 + Tier 2

Each component is measured after applicable regulatory adjustments.
```

### Permanent rule

Capital quality nests upward: CET1 can meet a broader requirement, but Tier 2 cannot meet a CET1 or Tier 1 shortfall.

### Never confuse [Tier 1] and [Total Capital]

**Tier 1** — CET1 plus eligible AT1.

---

**Total Capital** — Tier 1 plus eligible Tier 2.

### Permanent trap

Adding the CET1, Tier 1 and Total Capital minimum percentages as separate capital layers triple-counts the same higher-quality capital.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta in the hierarchy. Jurisdictions may set higher ratios or additional layers above the Basel minima.
