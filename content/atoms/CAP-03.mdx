Loss absorption has a timing dimension. **Going concern** means the institution continues operating: deposits are serviced, lending continues and the legal entity has not crossed into resolution or failure. Capital that absorbs losses at this stage must be sufficiently permanent and subordinated that losses can be imposed without creating an ordinary payment default.

Common Equity Tier 1 is the clearest example. Losses reduce retained earnings immediately, so CET1 bears deterioration continuously. Additional Tier 1 is also classified within Tier 1 and is designed for going-concern loss absorption, although its contractual and regulatory mechanisms differ from ordinary equity. Basel’s capital definition labels Tier 1 as going-concern capital and Tier 2 as gone-concern capital.

**Gone concern** begins conceptually when ordinary private operation is no longer viable without resolution action or extraordinary support. Tier 2 is subordinated and designed to absorb losses before senior unsecured creditors and depositors in failure or resolution. The exact legal mechanism depends on national resolution law and instrument terms, so “gone concern” does not mean that every Tier 2 instrument automatically converts at the same accounting ratio.

The distinction is about when and how reliably loss-bearing capacity is available. A funding instrument can be deeply subordinated yet still fail regulatory-capital eligibility if its maturity, redemption rights, incentives or contractual terms undermine the required permanence and loss absorption. Basel therefore uses detailed eligibility criteria rather than simply asking whether an instrument is called “equity” or “subordinated debt”.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
A capital instrument’s regulatory value comes from genuine loss-absorption capacity and permanence, not its accounting label or marketing name.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Describing Tier 2 as a normal operating-loss buffer equivalent to CET1 ignores the reason regulators distinguish going-concern from gone-concern quality.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse a="Going-concern capital" b="Gone-concern capital">
**Going-concern capital** — capital capable of absorbing losses while the institution remains viable and operating.

---
**Gone-concern capital** — loss-absorbing resources designed principally to protect creditors and support resolution after non-viability.
</NeverConfuse>

<Rule label="NO DELTA">
**No delta.** Legal resolution mechanisms differ, but Basel’s conceptual distinction between Tier 1 going-concern capital and Tier 2 gone-concern capital is common.
</Rule>
