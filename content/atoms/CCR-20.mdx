In practice, banks often compute FVA and MVA when pricing uncollateralized or margined trades, particularly since replacement to central clearing became common. KVA is a newer concept: it estimates the present value of future capital costs from holding exposures. Collectively, these adjustments ensure a derivative’s price covers not only market risk, but also funding, liquidity and capital costs.

Importantly, regulators do not yet require capital for FVA/MVA/KVA. These are internal or accounting adjustments. They are included here for completeness of CCR context. CVA (and DVA) are the primary regulator-view adjustments. FVA/MVA are aligned to CVA in cost-of-carry models; KVA is still largely theoretical.

### Permanent rule

Banks should clearly separate risk capital from valuation adjustments. XVAs beyond CVA are modeling/ALM issues, not additional capital requirements.

### Permanent trap

Confusing FVA with regulatory charges. Banks sometimes thought FVA could be offset by CVA profits; regulators clarified that XVA hedges only apply to CVA capital.

### Never confuse

**FVA** — Cost of funding derivative exposures.

---

**Capital Charge** — A regulatory cost (separate from FVA). KVA is an internal pricing layer for this charge.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** XVA concepts are primarily driven by internal risk management and accounting. No jurisdiction requires KVA hold.
