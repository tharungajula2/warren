### Core mechanism

```text
Basel standard
    ↓
Jurisdiction chooses legal implementation
    ↓
National discretions / deviations / transitions
    ↓
Effective date and institutional scope
    ↓
Supervisory permissions / firm-specific requirements
    ↓
Binding treatment for the bank
```

Use this workflow:

1. **Jurisdiction** — which legal regime applies?
2. **Status** — final rule, future final rule, proposal, consultation or guidance?
3. **As-at date** — what was true on the date being analysed?
4. **Commencement** — already in force or future?
5. **Scope** — which bank, exposure and consolidation level?
6. **Transition** — fully loaded or phased?
7. **Methodology** — standardised, modelled or special regime?

Basel's Charter states that the Committee has no formal supranational authority and its decisions do not have legal force. Members commit to implement the standards domestically.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   Basel three-pillar architecture
This:       International standard → local law
Downstream: UK/EU · India · US implementation
```

### Permanent rule

Never answer a bank-specific capital question from the Basel text alone when a domestic implementation exists.

### Never confuse [Basel standard] and [Binding rule]

**Basel standard** — international benchmark agreed by BCBS members.

---

**Binding rule** — domestic or regional legal requirement applicable to the institution.

### Permanent trap

Saying "Basel is effective on date X, therefore every bank must use it on date X" ignores domestic transposition and transition.

### Jurisdiction variants

**As at August 2026:** EU CRR3 is already substantially in application; UK Basel 3.1 final rules have a future 1 January 2027 commencement; India's revised SA and ECL rules have a future 1 April 2027 commencement; the US March 2026 capital package remains proposed after its comment period closed.
