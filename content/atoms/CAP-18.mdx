### Core mechanism

```text
Internal estimate
    ↓
Regulatory eligibility and scope
    ↓
Input floor / parameter constraint
    ↓
Permitted regulatory input
    ↓
IRB function
    ↓
RWA
```

Specialised lending branch:

```text
Project / object finance exposure
    ↓
Qualifies for supervisory slotting?
    ↓
Assess prescribed transaction factors
    ↓
Assign supervisory category
    ↓
Prescribed risk-weight / EL treatment
```

Input floors operate before the risk-weight function. The output floor in CAP-19 operates after risk calculations at aggregate RWA level. Slotting is also different from an external rating: it is a prudential classification under the specialised-lending framework.

### Where it sits

```text
Upstream:   IRB function · specialised-lending exposure
This:       Input constraints and slotting
Downstream: Modelled RWA · output-floor comparison
```

### Permanent rule

Regulatory modelling is bounded twice: first through model scope and input constraints, then through the aggregate output floor.

### Never confuse [Input floor] and [Output floor]

**Input floor** — lower bound or constraint on a model parameter before the risk-weight calculation.

---

**Output floor** — aggregate lower bound on model-based RWA relative to standardised RWA.

### Never confuse [Slotting category] and [External rating]

**Slotting category** — supervisory classification of qualifying specialised lending.

---

**External rating** — assessment issued by an eligible external credit assessment institution.

### Permanent trap

Calling 72.5% a "PD floor" moves an aggregate RWA backstop into the wrong stage of the calculation.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta in the conceptual distinctions. IRB scope, parameter floors and specialised-lending implementation depend on local transposition and permissions.
