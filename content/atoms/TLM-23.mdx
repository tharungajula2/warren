An asset consumes funding because cash leaves when the asset is originated and returns over time. The longer or less liquid the exposure, the greater the funding commitment it can impose. A committed but undrawn facility creates a different cost: there may be no cash outflow today, but stress can trigger a draw exactly when market funding is least available.

FTP should therefore trace each material funding or contingent-liquidity requirement back to its originating activity. The transfer charge then enters product pricing, profitability metrics, limits and new-product approval. Federal Reserve guidance explicitly expects FTP costs and benefits to be allocated based on funding risk and contingent liquidity risk and incorporated into product pricing and business metrics.

A useful sequence is: identify the cash-flow or contingent exposure; determine effective tenor and stressed liquidity behaviour; select the matched FTP rate; add the contingent-liquidity component where appropriate; then measure the customer business margin after those charges.

This attribution creates the correct question for a product manager. The question is no longer “does the customer yield exceed the coupon on deposits?” It becomes “does the customer return compensate for credit risk, operating cost, capital, matched funding and liquidity consumption?” Risks allocated elsewhere under the bank’s framework should not be double-counted, but none should disappear.

Central treasury remains responsible for aggregation because a bank has natural offsets. One business can produce stable deposits while another originates loans. Central funding lets treasury recognise those offsets. FTP ensures that the economic benefit of the funding source and cost of the consuming asset are visible to the appropriate businesses even though no external borrowing is matched transaction by transaction.

### Permanent rule

The central location of treasury execution does not justify centralising the economic cost away from the business that creates it.

### Permanent trap

Charging funded loan balances for liquidity while giving undrawn commitments zero charge because they have not yet produced a cash outflow.

### Never confuse

**Risk execution owner** — the central function that funds or hedges aggregate exposure.

---

**Risk-originating business** — the activity whose product decision created the funding or contingent-liquidity need.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.** Allocation design is institution-specific; the economic incentive principle is universal.
