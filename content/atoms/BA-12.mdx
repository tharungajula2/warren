Non-functional requirements (NFRs) cover attributes not captured by functional specs. Key NFRs in banking projects include:
- **Performance:** e.g. “System must handle 1000 concurrent users with response &lt;2s.”
- **Security &amp; Compliance:** data encryption, access controls, KYC/AML checks, GDPR or similar data privacy.
- **Availability:** e.g. “99.9% uptime required.”
- **Data Retention:** how long data (or logs) must be kept (often 5–7 years or more).
- **Auditability:** ability to trace actions – full logging of transactions and changes. Regulators explicitly require audit trails on changes. For example, a BA noted for a bank onboarding project that auditability (full action logs retained 7+ years) was a key NFR.

Non-functional requirements are critical: even if the functionality is correct, failure in security or audit logging can invalidate a project. In regulated change, treat NFRs like compliance: put them in the requirement list and test for them.

<Rule label="PERMANENT RULE">
Always capture NFRs explicitly (security, performance, reliability, legal/policy requirements). In banking, *auditability* (e.g. detailed logs and retention policies) is first-class: include it as a requirement early.
</Rule>

<Trap label="PERMANENT TRAP">
Assuming NFRs will be “obvious” or handled later. A common error is neglecting to require sufficient audit logging or forgetting to enforce retention, only to fail compliance checks later.
</Trap>

<NeverConfuse>
**Functional Requirement** vs **Non-Functional Requirement**:
A *Functional Requirement* describes what the system does (specific features). A *Non-Functional Requirement* describes constraints or qualities (like speed, security). For example, “generate monthly report” is functional; “report generation shall complete within 5 minutes” is non-functional.
</NeverConfuse>

### No delta

The categories of performance, security, retention and audit requirements are the same across banks worldwide. Regulatory laws differ (e.g. GDPR vs CCPA), but the concept of NFRs is universal.
