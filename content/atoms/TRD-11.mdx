Covered interest parity, or CIP, compares two ways to obtain a hedged return across currencies. An investor can invest domestically, or convert into a foreign currency, invest there and lock the future reconversion with an FX forward. In an ideal frictionless market, the hedged returns should match; otherwise an arbitrage would exist.

Using S as spot and F as forward under a consistent quote convention, the simplified one-period relation can be expressed as:

F / S ≈ (1 + domestic rate × time) / (1 + foreign rate × time)

The exact formula depends on quotation and compounding conventions. Forward points are commonly F − S, often scaled into market “points”. If domestic and foreign rates differ, the forward generally departs from spot even when nobody has a directional view on the future currency level.

This is why “currency at a forward premium” must be handled carefully. Premium and discount depend on which currency is the base and which is the quote. A sign that looks intuitive under one quotation reverses under the reciprocal quote.

In real markets, CIP can deviate. Since the global financial crisis, cross-currency basis has persisted because dealer balance sheets, capital, liquidity, credit and hedging demand make the textbook arbitrage costly. The basis is economically the extra adjustment required to reconcile FX-swap-implied funding with direct cash-market funding.

For risk and valuation, forward points need the correct spot date, forward date, two discount curves and quotation convention. A one-day date error around holidays can change the interest accrual and therefore the expected points. The core test is not “where will spot go?” but “what relative funding conditions and basis are embedded between these two dates?”

```text
Domestic cash
    |
    +--&gt; invest domestically ------------------+
    |                                          |
    +--&gt; buy foreign FX -&gt; invest foreign -&gt; forward hedge
                                               |
                                               v
                                  hedged returns compared
                                               |
                                      no-arbitrage/CIP
```

### Permanent rule

Forward points are first a relative funding price; do not read them as a standalone currency forecast.

### Permanent trap

Using CIP as if it must hold exactly after transaction costs, credit, capital and balance-sheet constraints.

### Never confuse

**Forward points** — difference between forward and spot FX rates under the market quote convention.

---

**Cross-currency basis** — deviation indicating that FX-implied funding differs from direct cash-market funding after the standard parity relationship.

### Jurisdiction variants

**No delta.**
