### Core mechanism

| Lens | NII / ΔNII | EVE / ΔEVE |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Object | earnings over projection horizon | present value of future rate-sensitive cash flows |
| Main driver | what reprices during horizon, margins, replacement assumptions | full cash-flow timing, discounting, options |
| Typical horizon | finite earnings horizon | remaining life of positions |
| Can improve while other worsens? | Yes | Yes |

Example mechanism without assuming a universal sign:

Rate shock
    ↓
Asset and liability repricing speeds differ
    ↓
Near-term interest margin changes → ΔNII

Rate shock
    ↓
Discount factors + expected behavioural cash flows change
    ↓
Present value of assets, liabilities and OBS positions changes → ΔEVE

A bank can improve short-run NII because assets reprice upward faster than deposits while losing EVE because long-dated fixed cash flows fall in present value. The reverse pattern is also possible. Disagreement is information, not an error to be reconciled away.

### Formula

```text
ΔNII = NII_shock − NII_base

ΔEVE = EVE_shock − EVE_base

NII_shock/base — projected net interest income under shock/base assumptions.

EVE_shock/base — economic value under shock/base curves and cash flows.

More positive ΔNII does **not** imply more positive ΔEVE.
```

### Permanent rule

A sound IRRBB view needs both earnings and economic-value perspectives because each can hide risk visible in the other.

### Never confuse [ΔNII] and [ΔEVE]

**ΔNII** — change in projected interest earnings over a stated horizon.

---

**ΔEVE** — change in present economic value of rate-sensitive banking-book cash flows.

### Permanent trap

Choosing whichever metric shows less risk. A low ΔNII does not neutralise a large EVE loss, and a low ΔEVE does not guarantee acceptable earnings volatility.

### Jurisdiction variants

No delta in concept. Calibration, horizon and prescribed modelling assumptions differ by regulator.
